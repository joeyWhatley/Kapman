2. THE ROUTE MAP (2D chart) — route_map/route_map.tscn  (main scene, F5)
==============================================================================
Direction: sleek, inky, sharp lines; minimal HUD (ship name, hold, silver, date), details on
hover/select; one modern typeface (Inter), no medieval fonts; smooth motion.

Voyages
- New voyage → pick your vessel → click a destination → a direct route is drawn → you edit it
  (drag waypoints) to avoid weather, dangerous waters and cursed areas; subtle warnings.
- Route charters are planned before leaving and sailed automatically; interrupted by events,
  battles and negotiations. Start / pause / fast-forward.
- Trade by hand at ports (port panel), shipwright repairs and hiring crew.
- Battles (battle_rules.gd, battle_screen): crew surrenders at 2 or fewer; morale rules.
- Music player in a corner (human-made, copyright-free, PS2-style — shortlist still to do).

Fog of war
- Everything starts fogged; the fog shows a rougher, vaguer version of the map; hazards stay
  hidden until explored (fog.gd, fog_mask.gdshader).

The world
- Map scaled up ~10x: Greenland to the Volga, Lofoten to Constantinople ("Constantinople is the
  edge"). CK3-like scale with many more navigable rivers.
- Projection: Lambert conformal conic (geo.gd), 1 map unit ≈ 1.112 km.
- Navigable rivers (Rhine, Seine, Elbe, Vistula, Neva, Volkhov, Lovat, Dnieper, Don, Volga,
  Danube, Dvina…) and 4 portages (Lovat–Dnieper, Volga–Don, Hollingstedt–Hedeby, Vyshny
  Volochyok). Route finding with AStarGrid2D across sea, rivers and portages.
- Period corrections as editable polygons: Wantsum Channel, Bosporus, Dardanelles.
- World ports added: Miklagard, Cherson, Bolghar, Atil, Parisius, Rotomagus, Colonia, Hammaburg.
- All 24 routes from Dorestad verified valid (e.g. Kiev via Neva–Volkhov–Lovat–portage–Dnieper).
- Fixed a scanline bug in build_coast.gd that had put a false wall of land across the Black Sea.

Look and feel
- Coastline overhaul: tiled, smoothed, level-of-detail coast drawing, hairline water-lines.
- Living ink sea shader, drifting cloud shadows, day/night with port lights.
- Idle life: gulls, other sails, port life, ship at anchor.
- Sailing model: points of sail for a square sail, rowing into the wind, currents, moving
  weather systems, river flow, portage speed.
- 2.5D tilt when zoomed in; "old Frisian" chart style (vellum and iron-gall ink), keeping Inter.
- Performance: ~190 fps idle and sailing.

Editable layers (route_map/nodes, route_map/layers, route_map/ui)
- Ports, hazards, land patches, currents, borders, cartouche, region labels, marginalia are all
  nodes you can place and edit in the Godot editor. UI scenes: HUD, port panel, music player,
  incident card, battle screen, theme.tres.


==============================================================================
3. THE 3D WORLD — route_map/world3d/sea_view.tscn  (open it, press F6)
==============================================================================
Goal: "insanely detailed zoomed all the way in, 3D, interactive realistic sea, the trading vessel
to scale rocking on the waves, and a flat 2D map when zoomed out." Style chosen: A — stylised
realism (Sea of Thieves / Tiny Glade feel), painterly, old Frisian palette.

History
- First: a low-poly 3D preview from ETOPO1 (route_map/terrain3d/) — judged too rough.
- Then: a high-detail Dorestad vertical slice (47 m AWS Terrain Tiles).
- Now: the whole map, streamed from Copernicus 30/90 m + EMODnet seabed (section 4).

What's in the scene (every part is a node you can edit)
- SeaView: settings — start position, wind, sea state, cloud cover, cloud shadows, mist, zoom
  distances, render scale, where the terrain tiles are.
- Look: cel shading throughout (toon.gdshaderinc) with ink outlines (Camera/Outlines).
- WorldEnvironment + Sun: the North Sea mood — overcast sky shader with moving layered clouds,
  a warm break low toward the sun, sea mist, muted colour grading ("wholesome, dark, dreary but
  hopeful").
- Terrain and Sea: geometry clipmaps (fine near the camera, coarse far away), 1 unit = 1 metre,
  floating origin so nothing jitters. Their look lives in terrain_material.tres / sea_material.tres.
- Sea: Gerstner waves shaped by the wind (the same maths moves the ship), calm in shallows, clear
  water over sandbanks, foam on crests and surf, a wake behind the ship, cloud shadows. Rivers
  and lakes are the same water surface at their own levels, joining the sea seamlessly.
- Land: real heights plus made-up fine relief (never at the shoreline), coastal dune ridges,
  land cover of 800 AD — beaches, grey-green dunes, salt marsh, wet meadow, peat bog, heather,
  oak/alder/birch woods. Stylised relief shading, darker wood edges.
- Plants (Plants/Trees, TreesMid, TreesFar, Bushes, Grass, Reeds): placed on the GPU by the same
  rules as the ground colours. Trees keep their spot at every distance: detailed models near
  (with shadows), simpler ones to 750 m, bigger sparser ones to 1.5 km, then a "canopy shell" —
  the woods become a raised, painted canopy so forests read as forests from any height.
  Oak / alder / birch from one model shaped per species. Meshes in world3d/meshes/*.res — swap
  in your own models.
- Ship: an 18 m Frisian cog at true scale — tarred planking, barrels and cloth bales, crew,
  steering oar, striped wool sail that fills and braces to the wind. Floats on the real waves
  (heave, pitch, roll, heel), sails by the chart's points-of-sail rule, runs aground in shallows,
  sails up rivers. Hull and Sail are nodes (cog_hull.res, cog_sail.res).
- Ship marker: an ink glyph with name and heading when the ship is too small to see (all the way
  out onto the chart); an arrow with distance at the screen edge when she's off-screen.
- Zoom: one continuous move from the waterline to the 2D chart — the camera levels to top-down,
  north-up, and the real route_map chart fades in exactly aligned.
- Rendering: supersampled (render scale 1.33), about 70 fps at 1080p on an RTX 4070.
- Controls: wheel zoom · right-drag orbit · left-drag pan · F follow ship · A/D steer ·
  W/S sail · 1–4 wind · Q/E turn wind.

Key files (route_map/world3d/)
- sea_view.gd (wires everything), clipmap.gd, terrain.gdshader, ocean.gdshader, sky.gdshader,
  world_height.gdshaderinc, landcover.gdshaderinc, clouds.gdshaderinc, waves.gd, cog.gd,
  sail.gdshader, plants.gd, scatter.gd, scatter.gdshader, scatter_place.gdshader,

4. TERRAIN DATA PIPELINE (G:\kapman_data — kept off OneDrive)
==============================================================================
- Choice: "balanced" — Copernicus GLO-90 (90 m) for all land, GLO-30 (30 m) on every 1° tile
  with sea coast, EMODnet 2024 for the seabed (~115 m). About 12 GB raw.
- download.ps1: resumable background downloader (queue.txt, progress in status.txt).
  Downloaded: 1929 GLO-90 tiles, 726 GLO-30 tiles, 58 EMODnet tiles (H1 skipped).
- export_vectors.gd → vectors.txt: the chart's coastline, lakes, rivers and period corrections.
- Baker (baker\Baker.cs, C# 5, built with the .NET Framework compiler — no extra installs):
  reads the GeoTIFFs directly, reprojects into the game's map projection, and bakes a tile
  pyramid tiles\L0–L8 (30 m … 7.7 km per pixel, 256×256 tiles; height, water level, distance to
  shore). It shapes the data with the chart: smooth true coastline, lakes at their surface level,
  every river cut in with a channel, levees and its own water level, every river mouth joined to
  the sea. Hand-traced rivers of 800 AD: the Oude Rijn (Dorestad–Utrecht–Katwijk, the Rhine's main
  mouth then) and the Vecht.
- bake_all.ps1 runs the whole bake unattended; progress in bake_status.txt ("BAKE DONE").
- terrain_stream.gd streams nine levels around the camera into the 3D scene.
