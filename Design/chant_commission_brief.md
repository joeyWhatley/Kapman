# Brief: recording the chant (and the lyre) for Kapman

**Why a commission.** Every clean modern recording of plainchant is in copyright, and the only free ones are the 1904
Rome recordings on 78s. Your rule is public domain or CC0 only, no invented sound. A recording you pay for and own
outright is a real recording of a real voice, so it keeps the rule, and it is the only way to get the sound of a
Frisian or English church of 800 without crackle. See Design/research_music_books_culture.md for the research.

## 1. What to record

One trained singer, unaccompanied, Latin, Solesmes-style free rhythm (the Liber Usualis of 1961 as the score: its
scans are on the Internet Archive and its edition rights have run out). No organ (Winchester had one by 990, nowhere
else in your world), no harmonies before the Winchester pieces. Order of value to the game:

| # | Piece | Use in the game | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ave maris stella** (Vespers hymn, Marian feasts) | the sailors' hymn; crew at sea on Mary's feasts; Marian churches ashore | 2-3 min (7 verses; also a single verse) |
| 2 | **Te lucis ante terminum** (Compline hymn) | every nightfall in port | 1 min |
| 3 | **Veni Creator Spiritus** (Rabanus Maurus, c. 809) | Pentecost; Utrecht and Fulda | 2-3 min |
| 4 | **Rorate caeli** (Advent introit) | Advent in port | 2 min |
| 5 | **Puer natus est** (Christmas introit) | Christmas | 2 min |
| 6 | **Resurrexi** (Easter introit) and **Haec dies** | Easter | 2 + 2 min |
| 7 | **Spiritus Domini** (Pentecost introit) | Pentecost | 2 min |
| 8 | **Media vita in morte sumus** (antiphon, attr. Notker, St Gall) | Lent; a burial at sea; the storm | 2 min |
| 9 | **Salve Regina** (Hermann of Reichenau, d. 1054) | late-game Marian antiphon | 2 min |
| 10 | **Victimae paschali laudes** (Wipo, c. 1040) | Easter after 1040 | 2 min |
| 11 | **Psalm 4 and Psalm 90 (Vulgate numbering), Compline psalms**, simple tone 8 | the bedrock of every evening; can loop | 3 + 3 min |
| 12 | **Kyrie XVI, Sanctus XVIII, Agnus Dei XVIII** (the simplest ferial Mass chants) | any Mass heard through a wall | 1 min each |
| 13 | **A Winchester Troper organum** (two voices, optional, a second singer) | Winchester and England after 980 | 2 min |

Also record, as raw material: a single held note on each degree; "Alleluia" alone; "Amen"; the Compline versicles;
a bell-free "Deus in adiutorium". Those become the small sounds a port needs without a whole piece.

## 2. How to record

- A dry, quiet room, not a church (reverb is added in the game by the building it plays from, through HDRP's or
  URP's audio, so the recording must be dry).
- 48 kHz, 24-bit WAV, mono, one close mic (30-50 cm) and one at 2-3 m if possible.
- Three takes of each piece; the singer picks the best; keep all.
- Pitch: whatever sits well for the voice; the game does not tune it.
- No vibrato beyond the natural; Latin with the Italianate church pronunciation of the Liber (the Frankish Latin of 800
  sounded different, but nobody can hear that and the Liber's pronunciation is what singers know).
- The lyre: a player on a Germanic six-string lyre (Sutton Hoo or Trossingen type) records: open strings plucked and
  strummed each, three 30-second improvisations in a pentatonic mode, a slow pattern to speak over (Bernlef's
  recitations), a quick dance pattern, tuning noises. A bone flute player, the same way, if one is at hand.

## 3. The deal

- A written **work-for-hire / full buyout**: all rights in the recordings to you, worldwide, all media, in perpetuity;
  the performer's consent to use and to edit; a waiver of moral rights where the law allows; credit by name in the
  game if they want it. One page is enough; have it signed before the session.
- Pay a fair session fee. A guide, UK 2026: a cathedral lay clerk or an early-music postgraduate £150-300 for a half
  day plus £100-200 for the buyout; a lyre player similar. Through SoundBetter or Fiverr with an explicit buyout,
  about the same. Keep the invoice and the signed page with SOURCES.md.
- Where to find them: a cathedral's lay clerks (they sing chant daily), a conservatoire's early-music department, the
  Schola Gregoriana of a university chaplaincy, a Benedictine house's guest-master (some monasteries will record for a
  donation, but get the written buyout all the same).

## 4. Files and credit

- Name files `chant_<piece>_<take>.wav`, keep the raw and a trimmed copy; put the trimmed copies under
  `Assets/Kapman/Audio/Chant/` (ignored by git if large; list them in SOURCES.md).
- SOURCES.md line: `Chant: <piece>, sung by <name>, recorded <date>, commissioned, all rights assigned (contract on file).`

## 5. Meanwhile, free

Until the session: the 1904 Gregorian Congress 78s (Mocquereau's choir; Internet Archive, pre-1926 so public domain) as
a far-off, found-object sound; Musopen and the Open Goldberg for the menu; nothing invented.
