# Kapman script: the editor's report

## In short

The script reads fast, knows its history and is often funny. It is funny in one way only. The single thing most wrong: there is one voice. Lender, alewife, bishop, steersman and teller all speak two or three clipped sentences and close on a dry remark, so ninety-two port people come down to about a dozen. Your own rules feed this: with plain words and no shortened words, rhythm is the only way left to tell people apart, and everyone has the same rhythm. The lines to keep are the ones where nobody is clever: someone wants a thing, or sees it, and says only that.

## The habits to break

Both critics found these, each in a different part of the script. Most damaging first.

**1. The dry last word.** The speaker gives his news, then adds a short flat sentence that undercuts it. At least 60 in the cards; 410 of the 744 port lines end on a sentence of eight words or fewer.

- `tollman.memory_honest` "You hid nothing last time. I looked."
- `daventre.einbert rumour 2` "Buy rivets. Nobody was ever hanged for rivets."
- `dorestad.hartwin farewell` "Michaelmas. I shall be here. I am always here."

Rule: one dry last word per port, owned by one person.

**2. One breath for everybody.** Everyone talks in two or three clipped sentences, the shortest last. Nobody runs on. 630 of the 744 port lines are exactly two or three sentences.

- `rhine_frisia.dorestad.kings_mint.greet` "Put it on the pan. All of it. I do not talk to silver in a purse."
- `chain_riddle_trail.s05.hand_hroald` "Not a skald. A skald makes. Hroald kept."
- `wijnaldum.sicco greet` "You came in on the wrong side of the stake. It does not matter today. It will matter at springs." His sheet says he talks all the time.

Rule: choose each speaker's breath before his words. The lender in scraps, Ravn in one long sentence that loses its end.

**3. One mark, many mouths.** A trait meant for one person is given to all. Counting, the lender's quirk, is used of at least 16 people. There are 43 home-made proverbs, riddles from 12 askers, and one short-cloak story told by 8 people in 6 ports.

- `rhine_frisia.traiectum.vecht_hand.short` "I counted your crew as they came up the bank. I count everybody."
- `crew.home.michaelmas.mate` "A man kept against a promise rows like a sack."
- `emuthon.tetta saying` "A short cloak is a long cloak sold to a Frank."

Rule: one mark, one mouth. Only the lender counts aloud, only Ravn has sayings and riddles, and a fact is told once.

**4. Not this. That.** The speaker denies what nobody claimed, or says he is "only saying", and then makes his point. 26 denials in the cards, 30 disclaimers in the chains and ports, 8 more in Ravn's lines.

- `rhine_frisia.wijnaldum.bare_mound.asks` "I do not want it back. I want to know where it went."
- `chain_barrow.s08.steersman_spoiled` "I do not say take it. I say that is how it is."
- `chain_old_gods.s02.hand_door` "I only say what I see."

Rule: say what it is. Never open on what it is not.

The critics disagree on who may keep "I only say": the first gives it to the steersman, the second to Beornwine. I believe both, because these are two quirks. Aldger gives both sides (`crew.death.body.steersman` "Now I have said both."). Beornwine defers (`chain_storm_vow.s06.lookout_send` "I only say what was said."). Ravn loses it.

**5. The trait is announced.** A caption, the teller or the speaker himself says what the person is like, and the lines do not do it. Aldger says "The worst first." five times. The chains say eight times that Ravn talks.

- `traiectum.schoolmaster farewell` "Keep it dry. Everything I say to anyone ends with that."
- `chain_riddle_trail.s09.hand_warning` "I will say it once, and you know I am not a man who says a thing once."
- `chain_barrow.s03.out_back` "The Northman says nothing for once, which from him is thanks."

Rule: a quirk is something the speaker does not know he does. Cut the name and keep the doing.

**6. The teller smirks.** Narration ends on a knowing remark, a "which" that explains the gesture, or a note of who did not speak. About a third of the cards' scene lines; 46 of the 180 outcomes in the chains.

- `crew.bored.calm.fact` "The sea is so flat that the boat's own reflection is tiresome."
- `chain_storm_vow.s04.out_piece` "goes without a word of thanks, which is the right way to go."
- `chain_barrow.s09.out_sale` "Nobody says anything. That is the men's answer."

Rule: the teller sees and does not smile. End on the thing seen.

**7. Stock hellos and goodbyes.** Greetings come from four stock openings, strangers are met by what they do "before" they greet you, and goodbyes are errands. 13 people say "You again". 30 of 93 farewells are "if you pass".

- `doccinga.northman greet` "A merchant. Good." The same words open `traiectum.schoolmaster greet` and `walichrum.harald greet`.
- `harbour.heath_iron.daventre` "He looks at your hands before your face."
- `witla.thiadlint farewell` "If you pass Ghent, tell the cellarer the cows are alive."

Rule: before a hello or a goodbye, decide what this person wants from the boat today.

**8. The footnote in the mouth.** A law, a book or a dated event is recited by someone with no cause to know it or to say it. 26 lines in the cards, 13 of them the steersman's at sea.

- `crew.fight.blows.steersman` "By our law a grip on the hair is two shillings and a ducking is four, at three pennies to the shilling."
- `traiectum.cellarer greet` "Frisia gives no oil and no wine, as a better man than you has already said."
- `dorestad.aldswith saying` "The black merchant shut his door on a poet, and the poet wrote it down."

Rule: a fact comes in only as something a person here heard, fears or is owed.

**9. Threes with a joke third.** A list of three whose third item is the turn. At least 25 in the cards, about 14 in the chains and ports.

- `rhine_frisia.traiectum.vecht_hand.him` "He poles, he can splice, and he owes me for the winter."
- `dorestad.aldswith crew` "One is good, one is thirsty and one is my cousin."
- `crew.hire.second_son.fact` "Big hands, bare feet, and a bundle that is mostly cheese."

Rule: a list is two things or four.

One more, from the first critic alone: in the crew cards the scene line states the situation and the first speaker says it again. In `crew.pay.promise_due.mate` the scene ends "This is the next landing." and the mate says "This is a landing." It counts only if scene lines are shown to the player.

## Fix these first

Ravn's opening lines carry the keys of the trial file.

1. `opening.6` "The clock is at the very top: Space stops it, and the numbers hurry it." Cut the whole paragraph. If he must speak: "Look about you before we go. I will wait."

3. `opening, the boat shown` "She floats. He has watched her do it." Write: "Your boat." The lender has just made this joke.

## Where a player is lost

1. **Who is owed.** In the opening the lender sells the boat: "For her, eight pounds of silver." At `dorestad.folcbert greet` a count says "You have my boat under you and my silver in her." And "the count's day" reads as the lender's counting. The first critic saw two creditors. The second, who did not read the opening, saw only two sums. Both hold. Fix: one holder of the stick, one name, one sum.
2. **The riddle trail** (`chain_riddle_trail.s02` to `s07`). The player never reads a riddle or picks an answer; one button answers. At `chain_riddle_trail.s08_salhaugar` the ending is chosen and nothing says so. Fix: print the riddle and offer three answers. Change "Say there is a head in your boat that already holds half of it" to "Say his pupil is standing behind you."
3. **Riddle cards answer themselves.** The scene of `crew.riddle.hail`: "left hail in the bilge. The Dane has a riddle for that too". Fix: no card names its answer.
4. **The brackets at sea.** "(sure)", "(likely)" and "(doubtful)" are never explained, and in `sea.fog.bank` all three choices are "(sure)". Fix: drop them where nothing is risked.
5. **The toll man** (`tollman.greet`). The toll is "a tenth of what you sell here, taken when you sell it. Not before", and then the choice is "Declare the cargo". Fix: he says what he is searching for now.
6. **Questions with no way to answer.** 31 of 92 first greetings ask one, such as "Do you play?" (`doccinga.northman greet`). Fix: the speaker reads the answer off the boat.
7. **Names.** Four Gerbrands (`doccinga.gerbrand`, `dorestad.gerbrand`, `wijnaldum.gerbrand`, `witla.gerbrand`), and Thiadburg, Thiadbod and Thiadlef in one port. Fix: no name twice, and a trade with each name.

## Slips

- The debt is "eight pounds" at `dorestad.folcbert debt` and "four hundred and eighty" at `dorestad.hartwin greet`.
- The opening says "Six oars". `sea.calm.falls.say.oars` says "We have four oars and a heavy boat."
- `opening, the log` "Dorestat to Traiectum, half a day." `rhine_frisia.dorestad.book_for_utrecht.book` "One day down the river."
- Places and coin are named two or three ways: Dorestat and Dorestad, Traiectum and Utrecht; "pence", "pennies" and "d".
- The lookout is English in the crew cards and tagged Frisian at sea. The teller calls Ravn "the Northman", "the hand" and "Ravn", chain by chain.
- One joke, three times: `sea.gale.breaks.say.worse` "I have been in worse." `sea.water.foul.say.worse` "I have drunk worse." `chain_old_gods.s14.hand_row` "I have rowed for worse."
- One line, two craftsmen: `walichrum.wilbraht greet_again` "The pin held, then. They do." `daventre.einbert greet_again` "Your rivets held, then. They do."
- Out of its time: Ravn in 793 says "Space stops it" (`opening.6`). Unless tied to later years, so are `crew.faith.white_shirt.dane` "the emperor's Easter court" (no emperor before 800) and `crew.pay.wage_or_share.fellowship` "memorial stones" (two hundred years late). Our own century speaks in `rhine_frisia.dorestad.aldswiths_bench.pay_first` "After is a different conversation".
- `chain_barrow.s08.out_great_success` lets the player empty the ship burial on the Deben, which lay whole until 1939. Your rule is that known history is kept.
- The quoted verse shortens words against your rule: "o'er" (`crew.riddle.wind.dane`), "call'd" (`crew.riddle.anchor.lookout`).

## The opening

The lender's seven paragraphs are the best-built part of the script: in about 150 words the player has the debt, the day, the boat, the cargo and the threat. His five questions are his weakest words, and he does not count once while asking them. Nothing on screen says what "the Wokerer" means. Then the best joke is told three times in two minutes, and the five jobs are given three times: across Ravn's paragraphs, in his last one, and in the list on screen. Ravn's caption says "Talks a great deal and means well", yet four of his eight paragraphs are instructions. He says "He does not say much, that one" of a man who has just spoken seven paragraphs. Both men say "mind". From the last question to the first thing the player may do there are fifteen paragraphs in a row. Cut Ravn to four: himself, the sail, the men, the wine. End on "I forget things. That is how I know where it is kept."

## The four stories

- **The riddle trail.** The best. It builds from a stick to a blind man's farm, and its ending pays what card five set up. Its fault is above: the player never answers a riddle.
- **The barrow.** The best built. Each card adds a claimant to the gold, and each ending costs something you can see. Two closing cards, `chain_barrow.s11_end_church` and `s12_end_king`, offer one button.
- **The storm vow.** The saint road is the best use of choice in the script, because the bishop reads back every corner you cut. The Thor road is two cards long, and the Gyrwe cards belong to another story.
- **The old gods.** The weakest. It is the storm vow told again: a storm, a measured candle, "Forty days on bread, salt and water" (`chain_old_gods.s09.priest_term`), a feast at winter's start. Cut it down to the stone.

## Ravn, somewhat rural

No, not with 'ere. Rural, yes, by lighter means.

The trial costs reading: 441 apostrophes against 7. `chain_storm_vow.s14.hand_thor` "'E's a thing in 'is tarn that 'e didn't 'ave afore." has to be decoded. Its country words put him in the north of England, not Norway: `landing.empty` "With nowt to sell, mind." It makes a clown of him where he is in earnest: `chain_riddle_trail.s10.hand_stone` "It is not nothing." becomes "'Tisn't nowt." And it leaves his breath alone: `opening.2` is still scraps, "'E don't say much, that one. 'E counts."

Three things in the trial work. The shortenings, which nobody else has: `chain_riddle_trail.s09.hand_spade` "I'll not carry one. Not into that ground." The flat words that cost no apostrophe: `chain_old_gods.s06.hand_counts` "The sea counts slow, that's all." The old words kept clean: `rhine_frisia.dorestad.first_question.ask` "It hinders words, and yet it rouses them."

He is rural already in what he notices: `quay.rumour.3` "The rooks at the graves told me, and my knee agreed with them."

How far: keep I'll, don't, that's, "I'll not", "mind", slow and quick. Drop the dropped h, -in', "me" for "my", "were" for "was", "a man as", nowt, owt, summat, 'tis, afore, yon, mebbe and aye. When he is in earnest take even the shortenings out: `chain_riddle_trail.s11.hand_low` "Do not help me." is stronger than the trial's "Don't help me." I agree with the second critic: the two Ravns then become one man with two gears.

## Where to start

1. Settle the facts before any line: who holds the stick and for how much, six oars or four, one Northman, one name for each place and coin.
2. Rewrite the opening.
3. Write the four crew, each with one breath and one quirk, the same man in cards, chains and at sea. Ravn first.
4. Rewrite the chains, the riddle trail first. Make the teller plain as you go.
5. Then one port at a time, asking of each person what they want from the boat today. Count the stock phrases in the other four fifths first, and start where they are thickest.

## The ten changes to make first (the chief critic's list)

1. Settle who is owed and how much (Wursing or the count; 1,920 pennies or 480) and call him one thing everywhere.
2. Make Ravn one man: let him run on when nothing is at stake and say every word whole when it matters; fold "the Dane" and the Danish hand of the cards into him.
3. Cut Ravn's button paragraph and his recital of the five jobs from the opening, and speak prices as "twelve pennies", never "12 d".
4. Leave one dry last word per port, with one person; end every other line when its news is said.
5. Give each speaker a breath of their own; in every port let one person run on and one say four words.
6. Take back the lent quirks: only the lender counts aloud, only Ravn has sayings and riddles, and each fact is told by one mouth.
7. Strike "I only say" and "I do not say X, I say Y" from everyone but Aldger and Beornwine; never open a line on what a thing is not.
8. Make the teller plain: end on the thing seen, one silence to a chain, and no scene line that says what someone wants, says or feels.
9. Print the riddles and let the player choose the answer; no riddle card names its own answer.
10. Rewrite every greeting and farewell from what that person wants from the boat today; keep "if you pass" for real errands.
