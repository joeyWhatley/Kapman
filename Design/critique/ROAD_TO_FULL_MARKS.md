# The road to full marks

The user, 7 October: "We need to get the review to 100/100, 10/10 and 5/5 asap."

## The short of it

1. **By the critics' own figures the whole list lands at about 88 of 100, 8.5 of 10 and 5 of 5.** The newcomer's own
   five changes say "the low 80s"; the rest of the 88 is my adding of his one-point cons. The systems critic says
   8.5 "with all five". The words critic says five stars "for the readers it is for, and still a three for those it
   is not". No list reaches 100 or 10. Section 2 says what the gap is made of.
2. **What is in hand today moves the scores little past "as planned": about 72, 6 and 3.5 to 4.** The critics
   already counted the queue as built when they gave 68, 6 and 3. The scores move with rows nobody has yet.
3. **Five things carry most of it.**
   - The clock of ruling 26 (ruled, not built): about ten of the newcomer's missing points, by his word.
   - The user's own hand through the voice: one whole star of the two missing. Nobody else can do it.
   - The opening stake (the tally card let through, a first payment on the bar): +6 of 100 for under two nights.
   - The card window (a name on each speech, the written end shown): half a star and +4 of 100 for one night.
   - A leg that pays (one number, one soak run, the orders capped): +0.5 of 10.
4. **Three rows can lose points this week** if nobody stops them: the purge of modern words (row 7), bigger type in
   windows that are already letterboxes (row 32), and helpers writing 4,000 choice endings in the one voice the
   words critic marks down (rows 2 and 6).

## How to read the list

- **N** is the newcomer (`review_newcomer.md`, out of 100: 68 as planned, 30 today). **S** is the systems critic
  (`review_systems.md`, out of 10: 6 and 3). **W** is the words critic (`review_words.md`, out of 5: 3 and 1).
  **P** is `player.md` and **D** is `designer.md`, the two earlier critics, who gave no score.
- "N5" is the newcomer's con 5. "N ch.1 +6" is the first of his five score-moving changes and what he said it is
  worth. "S today 2" is the second line of his verdict on the game as it is today.
- **Only the "ch." figures are scores.** N: +6, +5, +4, +4, +3, "about fourteen" together. S: +1, +0.5, +0.5, +0.25,
  +0.25, which is 8.5. W: one star, then half a star four times, which he caps at five. A con's "a point" is the
  critic's word for how bad it is. It cannot be added: the systems critic's cons come to about ten "points" and he
  is four short of ten.
- Where one "ch." figure covers several rows, each row says which rows share it.
- **Who mends it:** a builder (code); the content pass; a data edit; the user's ruling; the user's hand (as writer or
  artist).
- **In hand?** names the item of QUEUE.md, the ruling of 7 October, the file in `Design/fixes` or `Design/checks`,
  or the content pass. **NOBODY** means none of them has it. "In the book only" means `GAME_MAKER.md` plans it and
  no queue item or fix file does: for the work, that is nobody too.
- **Work (g):** XS under an hour. S a night or less. M two or three nights. L four or more. "User" is the user's own
  evenings. "None" is a yes or a no.
- **The order is score gained for work spent.** Rows 1 to 21 are cheap and a critic priced them. Rows 22 to 46 are
  priced and cost more. Rows 47 to 66 are scratches, or have no figure, or wait on the user. Rows 67 to 77 are in
  hand already and the critics counted them in their "as planned" scores.

## What the new clock changes in what the critics wrote

Ruling 26 came at 12:15, after all three critics had finished. Each of them wrote for a clock whose fastest speed
was a game day in six real minutes. Under ruling 26:

| What | Game days | Speed 2 (the old 4x) | Speed 3 | Speed 4 | Speed 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A watch of four hours | 0.17 | 1 min | 10 s | 2.5 s | under 1 s |
| Dorestat to Lundenwic, fair wind | 2.2 | 13 min | 2 min | 33 s | 9 s |
| Dorestat to Lundenwic, foul wind | 4.9 | 29 min | 5 min | 73 s | 20 s |
| A card's week (ruling 12) | 7 | 42 min | 7 min | 1 min 45 | 28 s |
| 1 May to St John's day, 24 June | 54 | 5 h 24 | 54 min | 13.5 min | 3.6 min |

| Twelve sailing seasons, 793 to 805 | 2,328 | 233 h | 39 h | 9.7 h | 2.6 h |

All of these are my sums from the ruling's own rates. What follows from them:

- **N1, S1 and P1 are answered by the ruling, once it is built.** "Fifteen real hours to the first stake" and "230
  hours for a life" were both speed 2 figures. They are 38 minutes and under 10 hours at speed 4.
- **"An hour's trading makes eight pennies" (N2, S2) no longer holds as said.** Eight pennies was ten game days.
  An hour at speed 3 is sixty game days; at speed 4 it is more than a season. What still stands is the count by the
  leg: 13 d for a laden leg in the mean, 55 legs in 100 carrying nothing (`money.md`). Row 20 is about that.
- **The plan that fills a crossing (`crossing.md`, 16 nights) was written for speed 2.** "One thing a watch" is one
  offer every ten real seconds at speed 3. It has to be planned again for a player who is aboard at speeds 1 and 2
  and passing through at 3 to 5. Rows 37 and 45 say so.
- **The newcomer's first change asked for a payment "about ten game days in, which is one hour at 4x".** Ten game
  days are now ten minutes at speed 3. The book's own earnest, 40 d on St John's day, falls 54 real minutes in at
  speed 3: that is his "inside the first hour" with no new date invented (row 4).
- **The systems critic's largest change, "cut the release to one year, about twenty hours", is no longer needed as
  a cut.** The other half of it, an ending, still is (row 44).
- **Ruling 23's line, "there must be far more to meet than the 420 cards", rested on 230 hours for the first life.**
  Ruling 26 corrected that figure. It is now a question to answer by counting, not a rule (section 2, point 2).
- **The length of the game is now set by its stops, not by its clock.** A season is 48 minutes of running clock at
  speed 4. With about 65 port calls a season (the systems critic's count) at a minute or two each, and two cards a
  leg, a played season is nearer two or three hours and the life 25 to 35 (g). So what a stop is worth is the game.

## 2. What a full-marks game of this kind has that none of the three lists asks for

From what the best-reviewed games nearest to Kapman are praised for, as far as I know them. From memory; no review
is quoted.

1. **A question for every year, not only the first.** Sunless Sea asks for an ambition at the start and that is
   what winning means; Crusader Kings gives every ruler something to want next; Roadwarden measures everything
   against its last day. In Kapman the debt is settled at the first Michaelmas, 38 minutes in at speed 4. No list
   says what is at stake from 794 to 805. The pieces exist: the heavy penny of 794 in `chain_house_debt`, fifteen
   dated hours on the twelve ports (`checks/hours_check.md`), and the "ten entries that would most change what a
   merchant does" in `checks/news_first_years.md`. *Done looks like:* a table of the twelve years with each year's
   question, and a Tasks window that always holds one thing with a day on it.
2. **A count of what there is to meet against the hours it must fill.** 80 Days was praised because a second and a
   third journey met cities the first never saw. Sunless Sea was faulted for ports that say the same thing on the
   fortieth call. Under the new clock a life is about 780 port calls (g: 65 a season, twelve seasons) on 420 cards.
   *Done looks like:* a seeded run of a whole life that prints how often each card came, and no card seen more
   than three times unless, like the toll man, it has a state for each time.
3. **A line that says what is waiting, for a clock that runs fast.** Crusader Kings was praised for making a deep
   game learnable: alerts along the top, a list of what needs you, a tooltip on everything. Ruling 26 names what
   drops the clock. No list asks that the player be told before it drops. *Done looks like:* one line on the bar,
   "Next: Sunday in 2 days; bread out in 5; the count's day in 31; a card waits, 6 days left", and at speed 4 the
   clock never drops without that line having said so.
4. **The game does things with its hands.** 80 Days has its globe and its ticking clock, Cultist Simulator its
   timers, Citizen Sleeper its dice, Dredge its catch. The newcomer found Kapman's one such moment, the turning
   line, and asked for no more of them. *Done looks like:* one small movement for each thing the game cares about:
   notches cut on the tally when a payment is made, the scale tipping when silver is weighed at Ripa, the lead
   going down, a word's ink fading as it ages.
5. **Lettering as character.** Pentiment was praised for giving each speaker a hand that showed his schooling, and
   for a plain face behind a switch for those who could not read the hands. Kapman has Gentium and the turning
   line. *Done looks like:* a hand for the church, cut runes for the north, tally notches for sums, and an easy-read
   switch. The fonts must be ones that may be shipped.
6. **A sound that is the game's own.** Pentiment's period music and scratching pens, Disco Elysium's score and
   Dredge's sea were all named in their reviews, as I recall. The lists ask for three event sounds. The user's rule
   is recordings in the public domain or CC0 only; `Design/chant_commission_brief.md` is on disk. *Done looks like:*
   each of the three coasts can be told with the eyes shut, and one sung thing belongs to the game.
7. **A life the player can show someone.** 80 Days ends on the route travelled; Sunless Sea hands something of a
   dead captain to the next. The words critic praised the annal voice (his pro 14) and no list turns it on the
   player. *Done looks like:* at each Martinmas the year written as an annal ("In this year the kapman of Dorestat
   lost his wine on the bar at Witla"), and at the end one picture: the chart with every leg drawn. The wiki that
   fills as you learn (ruling 3) is already what a second life inherits; the New game window should say how many
   pages are known.
8. **A failure worth reading.** Disco Elysium's failed checks were praised as some of its best pages; King of
   Dragon Pass made a bad choice a story. The lists ask for a written end to each choice. None asks that the
   failure be the better read. *Done looks like:* in the first-hour cards, no failure text shorter than its
   success.
9. **Reach, and a build that does not break.** Keys the player can change, a frame that scales from 1280 by 720 to
   a very wide screen, a pad and a handheld, reduced motion, a pause that is always there, no lost save in the
   review build. `popups_and_windows.md` 4.4 and 4.7 plan the pad and the widths. The words critic asks for type
   sizes only.
10. **Strangers have played it.** Every game named above was tuned on watched play. The designer's critique asks
    for it ("a stranger plays the first hour each week while you watch and say nothing") and none of the three
    lists repeats it. It is the only source of the last points.
