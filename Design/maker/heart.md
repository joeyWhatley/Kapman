# The heart: what people will love, and what is weight

7 October 2026. The second apprentice's page for the game maker.

**What I read.** Both critiques. The concept (sections 1 to 6), `CHAINS.md`, `DECISIONS.md`, `living_world.md` 1 to 3,
the fleets plan 1 and 2, `depth_design.md` 2.5 and 2.6. Five chains with their words (house_debt, pilots_secret,
storm_vow, black_stones, silver_road) and house_heir in outline. Three places (Traiectum, Walichrum, Lundenwic).
Twenty-six loose cards taken at even steps from the sea, crew and Rhine decks. The first capture. I ran one count over
all 1,041 cards; its rule is in section 5. I did not run the game. The session's web searches were already spent, so
what I say of other games is from memory and from the project's own research. (M) marks a figure to check before
quoting it.

## The short answer

The vision is not too big. It is in the wrong order. Each large wish has a small form that a merchant feels inside
one leg, and most of those small forms are already written.

The treasure is four things: a word that may be stale, the count's day, people who remember you, and a house that
outlives you. The weight is what was made wide before one leg was fun: fifty ports to sail to, forty-three chains
before one can start, four coinages, and endings that pay 1,500 d in a game about 480.

One finding changes the critics' advice. They call the content a debt. Counted card by card it is two things. The
chains and the port cards are mostly strong. The weak deck is the one a leg needs most: the sea and the crew. Write
there, and nowhere else, until a stranger asks for a second hour.

## 1. The promise

What a player tells a friend:

> "It's a trading game where you never see the real price. You only know what some skipper told you last week, and
> you bet your summer on him."

Three feelings in the first hour:

| The feeling | What gives it | Does it reach the player today? |
|---|---|---|
| **I know something.** I hold a word, and I am acting on it | `Word.cs`, `PriceNotes`, 138 ships that make a word true or stale. The first screen has it once already: Lindisfarne as news "13 days ago" | Half. The word is built. It is not scarce (34 markets known at minute zero, one Talk gives all), and nothing on arrival says whether you were right. Without the reveal the feeling never lands |
| **The day is coming.** A date is ahead, and every leg spends some of it | The count's tally: "They fit now, and they will fit at Michaelmas." The clock that waits while you think | No. The bar says "due in 365 days". No card can cost an hour. Nothing happens on the day |
| **They know me here.** I came back, and someone remembered | Lines that read your past: "You sat on my mud a whole tide last time. I waved. It seemed polite." By my count 560 of the 792 chain and port cards carry such a line | No. No card opens in port in `L_Chart` |

All three are in the project and none reaches the player. That is stage 1, and it is the critics' stage 1 too.

A fourth feeling is the reason to finish, and it comes later: **I will not be here, and the house will.** Do not try
to give it in the first hour.

## 2. Stakes and reward

The game's own scale (`CHAINS.md`): the debt is 480 d. A cask of wine is about 50 d at Dorestat and 80 d at
Lundenwic. An ordinary leg makes 10 to 40 d, a good voyage 100 to 300 d. A small boat is 600 d, a sea-going keel
2,400 to 3,000 d (two tables disagree). She starts with 86 d, four casks and bread for seven days.

| Span | Real time | Can win | Can lose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A leg** | 2 to 5 minutes | 10 to 40 d. 100 to 150 d when the word held and the hold was full | 1 to 3 days. A cask (50 to 80 d: a tenth to a sixth of the debt). A word gone stale: 80 d a cask turned to 60 |
| **A season** | one evening | The debt cleared on the day: the toll eased for 90 days, the count's feeling +15, and his long road opens (the host's stores at a penny a load; the hawk, 200 d sold or 120 d and his friendship given) | The day broken. Three roads, each a loss you live with: a year's grace for the boat's work at his call; the mooring rope on his wall; the stick sold to Thiadbod, and you carry his wine |
| **A life** | 10 to 15 hours | A second hull (the widened logboat 300 d, a coaster 1,200 d). A plot (240 d at Traiectum, 500 d at Lundenwic). A work that pays every year (the salt hearth, 360 d: "Each autumn sixteen sacks of salt wait at the hearth for the house"). An heir with a second tongue | The boat and her cargo in a wreck. A man who has made three crossings with you: the only kind who can steer a second boat. From 834, the home port (to move: a winter and 480 d) |
| **A house** | the campaign | The name: a vow kept is 1 notch, a town above its line 2, a hinge turned 3. Five is "known on the bank", 25 "across the sea" | At each death: a third of the loose silver, half of every friend's feeling, and the deepest feud comes with the heir |

Five rules that make a day matter:

1. **One pace number, always in sight.** 21 June to Michaelmas is 100 days. 480 less 86 is 394 d: about 4 d a day,
   wind-bound days too. Show it as a sentence: "Owed 480. In hand 212. At this pace: short by 90." By `CHAINS.md`'s
   leg (10 to 40 d over two to four days) that is hard and can be done. By the critics' sums a wine crossing clears
   about 150 d and the debt is gone in 25 days. The two scales differ fourfold. Tune to `CHAINS.md`: Michaelmas
   should be in doubt until about day 80.
2. **The best price sits behind a shut door.** It is already written. Lundenwic pays most, and in 793 "their reeves
   turn Frankish keels away". The reeve wants a gift of 20 d (the toll eased 30 days), or three tides lying off, or
   you go round by Hamwic. So the richest run costs silver or days before it pays. That is the tuning lever, not a
   lower price for wine.
3. **A word ages by the day.** This is what an hour at sea is worth. If each day past the word's date raises the
   chance that three Frisians got in first, half a day lost to a card can turn 320 d for four casks into 240. Cards
   need no bigger numbers than that.
4. **Losing the day is a road, not an ending.** house_debt has it right. Pay 480 by tale, or 367 by the moneyer's
   weight and the count likes you less. Take the widow's surety: 120 d now and a third of three cargoes. Or kneel to
   Saint Martin and carry wax and wine when asked. The player loses freedom, not the save. Nobody reloads a loss
   that opens a better story.
5. **Silver does not cross a death whole. Names and knowledge do.** So the question of every late year is what to
   turn silver into: a work, the heir's schooling (60 d and two cakes of wax buy three winters at Saint Martin's,
   and Latin), a share in a keel. This is the user's "growth and development", and it keeps a rich house from being
   a safe one.

Winter is the second squeeze, and it is written too: Martinmas wages 24 or 40 d, tar and moss 8 d, and whatever is
unsold sits till Easter. The true first-year sum is about 530 d in 143 days.

Two numbers must be settled before any of this is tuned. **Wages:** `voyage_rules.json` says a penny a day for four
men, about 140 d a season; the Martinmas card pays 24 d. **The price of a card:** a sea or crew card should stake 10
to 40 d, one ordinary leg. The safe choice costs half a day to a day. The bold one risks a lot or a wound at
"doubtful", and its prize is to arrive while the word still holds.

**A finale pays in doors, not coin.** black_stones can end on "silver +1500" and a charter. The charter is the prize.
The silver pays the debt three times and ends the game's sums. Cap the coin from any one card at 300 d, a good
voyage. Keep every plot, charter, pilot's mark, partner and standing order. The best finale I read pays no coin at
all: ground on the king's bank, held for "a hen at Michaelmas ... One hen. I like to be reminded of the day."

## 5. The content: gold, filler, and a rule a machine can apply

**Gold** is a card where two things the player wants pull apart, and the game remembers which way he went.

- Silver against a name: "Have the moneyer weigh out what was lent, 367 new pennies, and pay that." He saves 113 d
  and the count likes him less.
- Silver against a person: "Say nothing here, and sell the word in the ale-houses up river: the pilot of the Maas is
  going blind." Thirty pennies. On the next card: "Somebody sold my eyes in the ale-houses up river. I do not ask
  who. I ask you to swear, and I will listen to how it sounds."
- A craft the player can keep: "grey sand is the bank, shell is its edge, black mud is the old channel and she has
  left it." And why a pilot's marks are a verse: "when a line is lost you can hear the hole. Nobody can tell when a
  list is short."
- A price that is a person: "You can make another candle. I cannot make another son."

**Filler** is a card that stops the ship and changes nothing.

- `sea.light.ashore`: the safe choice has no effect at all, and the other two move heart or health by 0.03.
- The 43 fitts: "Evening at sea. The lookout has the next part of his song ready, if you want it."
- A good story with no stake. `crew.faith.white_shirt` is the best anecdote I read ("The third year there were more
  of us than shirts, and I got a thing sewn out of a sack"), and its three choices move feeling and heart a little.

Most filler is good writing in the wrong box. Do not cut the white shirt. Move it. A story with no stake is a line
in the log or a leaf in the chest. Only a choice with a price may stop the ship.

**The rule: four marks.** A machine can read all four from the JSON.

1. **Price.** A choice costs or wins 10 d or more, a lot, a man, or (once the verb exists) hours.
2. **Key.** A choice opens only because of something earned: a text, a riddle, a flag from an earlier choice, a
   tongue, a friend's feeling, a good in the hold.
3. **Memory.** A line is spoken only because of the player's past.
4. **Fork.** Two choices lead to different hard effects, not to more or less heart.

Three or four marks: gold. None or one: filler. Two: yours to read.

| Deck | Cards | Gold (3 or 4 marks) | Filler (0 or 1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| The 43 chains | 595 | 418 | 30 |
| Port and region cards | 197 | 151 | 11 |
| Long cards (dated events) | 73 | 30 | 3 |
| The sea | 63 | 10 | 37 |
| The crew | 112 | 14 | 74, of which 43 are the song |

My script, 7 October. Its marks are crude. It cannot tell a good line from a dull one. It can tell a card that asks
something from one that does not.

Two gates come before the marks, and a machine can apply them too:

- **Reach.** The card can open before 797 at a port of the release. Of the loose cards, 68 are tied to twelve home
  ports and 55 of those are gold. Another 177 are tied to no port (sea, crew, long) and 27 of those are gold.
- **Scale.** No more than 300 d of silver from one card. No sea or crew card over 40 d.

So the port cards are in hand and the chains are in hand. The leg is not: about two dozen gold sea and crew cards,
where a season needs forty. That is the only writing the first release needs. Reprice twenty; write twenty.

**On the one voice.** It is true of the sea deck. Where a person has a want, the voice is there. The pilot's
daughter hides her father's failing eyes with chatter: "I talk too much. Everybody says so. It is a great help to
him." Every person in the place files has a `want` and a `fear`. A line that someone without that want could say is
the line to rewrite.

**One thing to decide on purpose.** In silver_road the richest ending is the slave pen: six captives for 1,700 d and
a ship, against 400 to 500 d for carrying the Frisian boy home. It is true to the trade. As written, the worst deed
pays best and costs nothing later. Let the word of it sail as fast as the news and the heirs carry it, or cap it
like the rest.

**One life is the game.** 793 to about 805. The handover to the heir is the ending. A second life is the reason to

**What brings a stranger back after the first evening:**

1. **The evening ends on a date, not on a menu.** The first sitting should stop near day 60 to 80, with the tally
   saying "short by 90" and Michaelmas in sight. He sleeps on a plan.
2. **One word not yet tried.** For example: Ripa weighs silver and pays over for wine; Folkmar said so nine days ago.
3. **Someone waiting.** A notch on the stick with a name: Witla, after Michaelmas, the pilot.
4. **He was wrong once, and the game told him why.** The reveal line is the teacher. A first hour with no wrong word
   teaches nothing.
5. **Winter reads his summer back** in his own entries. That page is the one a player shows a friend.
6. **The second life is not the first.** The ports open in 796. Lundenwic burns in 798, and "a burnt plot goes for
   half". The heir speaks English, or he does not.

Three counts to take at every play test: minutes to the first sale (under 20); whether the tester was wrong at least
once and cast off again; whether he starts a second season unasked.

## Where I part from the critics

I agree with the core: four ports, one debt, cards that cost days, scarce hearing, one good hour first. Four points
of difference:

1. **"Stop writing."** Stop writing wide. Forty sea and crew cards are owed, and they are the leg.
2. **"The Command frame turns the chart into wallpaper."** Not if the measure is its heart. "There and back before
   Michaelmas?" is a question only the chart answers. The frame must always say three things: the day, the tally
   against the debt, and the word she is sailing on. A click on a port must still set the course.
3. **"Real history only in version one."** Keep one changed line a life. It costs one chronicle entry, the endings
   are written, and "change history" is in the user's first list.
4. **"No card over 60 d."** Right for the sea deck. For a chain, cap the coin at 300 d and keep the doors.
