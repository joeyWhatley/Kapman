## The story in five sentences

In 828 a young deacon of Mulinheim comes down the Rhine with a box sewn in waxed linen, and hires a Frisian boat to carry it fifteen days to Saint Bavo's at Ganda: in it are bones of the Roman martyrs Marcellinus and Peter, which his lord Einhard's notary took out of their tomb by night the year before.
A priest of Saint-Medard at Soissons, whose abbot was made to hand back a stolen share of the same martyrs after Easter, offers the shipman two thousand pennies if the box loses its way to the Seine, and the abbey will not ask what road it came.
On the way a seller of bones offers two bundles that weigh the same, the crew begin to watch the stern board, and a blind man from the coast asks whether this is the boat with the saints.
At the Scheldt mouth the deacon goes up to the church for an hour while the ebb runs, and the shipman waits, sails with the box, sends for the priest after dark, or goes up to the church himself.
The martyrs come to Ganda and the blind man sees, as it really was; or they come to Soissons and a book is written about a miraculous wind; or two houses show the same two saints and only a Frisian knows which has them.

## How a player meets it

- It starts by itself in **828, from April to December**, at any of **Mogontia, Colonia, Noviomagus, Dorestad or Traiectum**: card `s01_box_from_the_main` is drawn on the first call there (weight 30, once). A house that trades on the Rhine at all that year meets it. The window is one year because the event is: see "The year" below.
- Every choice on the first card takes the box aboard. A player who wants no part of the story sails to Ganda, is paid, and has the honest ending.
- Steps 2, 5, 6 and the endings come on the next call at their ports (weight 30 to 40). Step 4 is scheduled; step 7 follows step 6 by `then`; steps 3 and 8 are drawn under way and may be passed over; step 9 comes only to a thief who calls at Quentovic or Rotomagus.
- The last card (`s14_the_book`) waits for 830.

## The real history under it

**The night at Rome, 827.** Einhard, Charlemagne's biographer, had built a church on his land and had no saint for it. At the palace he met Deusdona, a deacon of Rome, who promised him relics. Einhard sent his notary Ratleic to Rome with him; Hilduin, archchaplain and abbot of Saint-Denis and of Saint-Medard at Soissons, sent a priest of his own, Hunus, to get a saint for Soissons. When Deusdona delayed, Ratleic went by night to the church on the Via Labicana, opened the tomb of the martyrs Marcellinus and Peter, and carried them off. They came over the Alps, down the Rhine by boat, and to Michelstadt in the Odenwald (autumn 827). There the reliquary was wet with a bloody liquid for days and men dreamed that the martyrs wished to rest elsewhere; in January 828 they were moved to Mulinheim on the Main, later called Seligenstadt. Source: Einhard, Translatio et miracula sanctorum Marcellini et Petri, book I (English: B. Wendell, 1926, https://archive.org/details/historyoftransla00einh ; not quoted here).

**The theft from the thief, 827 to 828.** On the road Hunus took a share of Marcellinus out of the box and carried it to Soissons. Einhard heard of it at the palace from Hilduin himself, and had the share back at Aachen after Easter 828 (Translatio II; the week from memory (?)). Saint-Medard already had Saint Sebastian from Rome, by the pope's gift, since 826 (Annales regni Francorum 826). This is Gozhelm's grievance in card 2, and the reason an abbey of Hilduin's "does not ask how they come".

**Ganda, 3 July 828.** Einhard was abbot of Blandinium at Ganda by 815 and of Saint Bavo's by 819, and ruled both from the palace. On 3 July 828 relics of Marcellinus and Peter reached Saint Bavo's, and the sick were healed there until 30 September. The report from Ganda in Einhard's fourth book names the blind girl Hartlinda of Furnes; Eberald and Gundrada, serfs of the house; Liodold of Alphen, on two crutches; and **Germar, blind, "from the sea-coast of the Frisians near the mouth of the river Scheldt"** (`dossiers/ganda.md` [6], from Translatio IV). Germar is on cards 5 and 10. How he travelled is not said: the boat is ours.

**Fifteen days by boat.** In a letter of 830 Einhard reckons fifteen days by boat from his house at Ganda to the Main (`dossiers/ganda.md` [5]). Who carried the relics in 828, and in what boat, no source says. A Frisian hired on the Rhine is our reading (?); pilgrims rode in merchant boats on the Main (`dossiers/mogontia.md`).

**Einhard's men and his letters.** Erembert the steward (vicedominus) at Ganda is from the letters (`dossiers/ganda.md` [5]); that he held the office in 828 is assumed (?). The same letters order the dues taken "in full and in good silver" and ask for wax because the honey was short: the steward's line in card 11 and the wax order.

**False names of martyrs.** The Admonitio generalis of 789 forbids false names of martyrs (c. 42) and wanderers who deceive the people (c. 79), as cited in `story/unlocks_793.json`. Deusdona went on supplying Frankish abbeys for years, Fulda among them (Rudolf of Fulda (?)). The bone seller of card 4 stands for that market; he and his blank bundles are ours.

**The emperor's writing for merchants, 828.** A formula of Louis the Pious's chancery of 828 takes named merchants who serve the palace under the emperor's protection and frees them of toll everywhere except at Quentovic, Dorestad and the Alpine passes (Formulae imperiales no. 37, MGH Formulae; from memory (?)). It is the hard road's reward, and the steward names the three exceptions.

**Saint-Denis.** Hilduin was abbot of Saint-Denis; the abbey held the tolls of its October fair (chain `wine_fleet`, with its sources). The stolen box is landed there because a sea boat can reach it; Soissons lies inland on the Aisne.

**Afterwards.** Einhard wrote the whole matter down in four books about 830 to 831 (?), and names no boat or shipman for Ganda (true). Hilduin sided with the emperor's sons in 830, lost his offices that autumn and was sent away; he had Saint-Denis again within a year or two (Astronomer, Vita Hludowici c. 45 (?)). Einhard lay sick at Saint Bavo's in the spring of 830 (`dossiers/ganda.md`); that he corrected the Latin of the cures in bed is ours. Einhard was a very small man, and his friends said so in verse (Walahfrid Strabo (?)): Gozhelm's "the little man who is abbot of Ganda".

**The custom.** Stealing a saint was told as the saint's own wish: if he had not consented, no man could have lifted him. Einhard tells the night at Rome in that way himself. P. Geary, Furta Sacra (1978), was the guide for how such stories go; nothing is taken from it in words. That Saint-Medard later claimed relics of Marcellinus and Peter is said of the house's later writings (?). Two houses claiming one saint is common (?).

**The year.** The cards do not say the number of the year aloud. The start is held to 828 from April (after the Easter of the handing back). A player who dawdles may land the box some months after 3 July: the game's summer of 828 bends that far and no further.

**Invented, and said so.** Otbert (Ot- + -bert), deacon of Mulinheim; Gozhelm (Goz- + -helm), priest of Saint-Medard; Lupicinus, the seller of bones (a late Roman name); the cellarer at Saint-Denis, the bishop's clerk, the canon and the monk, unnamed; the offer of two thousand pennies; the two blank bundles; the finger bone held back; the letter to the bishops of the coast.

**No dead ends.** Every card has a choice that costs only time or pride. A player who skips the turn (sails from the Scheldt mouth without calling again) simply arrives at Ganda. The thief can turn back twice (cards 8 and 9) and still ends at Saint Bavo's, without the land. A failed check costs a hurt man (card 8) or 100 d and a marked boat (card 9); the voyage goes on. Both dangers warn first: the men speak before the weather is chosen, and the clerk reads the letter aloud before anything is asked.

**History changed.** With `changed_marcellinus_ganda` the Ganda relics of 828 never reach Saint Bavo's (or reach it only as nobody's bones). What would follow, for later cards that read the flag:
- No cures at Ganda in the summer of 828: the Ganda report is missing from Einhard's fourth book, and the feast of Marcellinus and Peter (2 June) is not kept at Saint Bavo's.
- Saint-Medard has both Sebastian and the martyrs: more pilgrims on the Aisne and at the fair of Saint-Denis, fewer on the Scheldt. A later card may make wax and candles cheaper at Ganda and dearer at Parisius.
- When the Northmen burn Saint Bavo's in 851 the canons flee to Laon with Bavo's bones only. Nothing recorded after that depends on the Roman relics.
- About 950 the two abbeys of Ganda fight over which is older, forge charters and invent saints (`dossiers/ganda.md`). With the flag, Saint Bavo's has one true claim the less and one more reason.
- Hilduin still falls in 830. Whether Einhard gets his box back from a fallen man is left open for the user.

Why a merchant of the time could have won each:
- **A plot on the bank at Ganda.** A lay village of boatmen and traders grew on the Scheldt bank by Saint Bavo's from the early ninth century, on abbey ground held for a yearly rent (`dossiers/ganda.md` [7, 12]). The rent in wax is ours.
- **A cloth from the martyrs.** A cloth laid on a saint's tomb was itself a relic and the ordinary gift to a layman who had served the house (brandea: Gregory the Great's letter to the empress Constantina (?)). `unlocks_793.json` already has two of the kind.
- **The emperor's protection.** The formula of 828 exists for exactly such men. Einhard was at the palace and could ask.
- **Saint-Denis's man; a plot at the fair.** The abbey held the fair and its tolls and gave its merchants its name (chain `wine_fleet`).
- **A finger bone.** Hunus kept back more than that. A bishop has not proved it.
- **Saint Bavo's man.** The abbey's immunity of 13 April 819 kept the count off its land and its people (`dossiers/ganda.md` [9]).
- **A leaf of Einhard's book.** One leaf, copied for 20 d by a monk who is copying the book anyway.

## How long it takes

At the least about 80 game days: the passage down the Rhine and through the delta to the Scheldt (Einhard's fifteen days from the Main, less from Dorestad), a day at the Scheldt mouth for the ebb, the river up to Ganda, and sixty days for the abbot's answer. The thief's road is the Channel and the Seine to Parisius instead, three weeks or more in a small boat. The last card waits for 830, so the whole chain lies across two or three sailing seasons and overlaps with ordinary trading, with `mission_north` (829) and with the first years of `dorestad_834`.
