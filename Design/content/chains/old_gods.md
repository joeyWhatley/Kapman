## The story in five sentences

In a gale, some year before 840, the steersman tells the merchant to vow Saint Martin a candle the length of the keel, the Northman who rows for him says to vow the sea a share, and the merchant promises one of them, or nobody.
The same season's gales cut the dune at Walichrum, and a grey stone stands out of the sand: a seated woman with apples, a dog and a ship's prow, green Roman coins at her foot, and among them three silver pennies of the merchant's grandfather's day, which means somebody was still paying her.
A priest of Saint Martin's at Traiectum reads the stone (a Roman pot dealer on the Britain run, thanking a goddess "for goods kept safe", his vow paid "gladly, as was due"), finds her name in none of his books, and wants the thing ended before midsummer; a Danish skipper at Ripa asks the house to the feast at winter's start in the hall at Hleidr, where a horse is eaten, an old man lifts a lot, and a share can be given to the water.
The priest hears of all of it and opens his small book; and a vow that is not paid to the one who was promised comes back at sea as a swell with no wind.
The next summer, on the dune, with the sheep woman, the bishop's clerk and a mattock all waiting, the merchant gives the stone back to the sand (as happened), sets it up for seamen to lay apples at, has a cross cut in it and a chapel built over it, or breaks it for the coin underneath.

## How a player meets it

- It starts by itself **at sea in hard weather** (`storm >= 0.4 or wind >= 9`), any year before 840: card `s01_storm`, 0.15 an hour while the weather lasts. A merchant who sails the North Sea in autumn will meet it in his first year or two.
- After that every step sits at a port with a high weight and comes on the first call there: **Walichrum** (the stone), **Traiectum** (the priest), **Ripa or Sliesthorp** (the Dane), **Hleidr in the tenth or eleventh month** (the feast), **Walichrum again from the fourth to the ninth month** (the choice).
- A man who vowed nothing still finds the stone; a man who never goes north can finish through the priest, and a man who never sees the priest can finish through the hall. Only one of step 4 or step 8 is needed for the choice on the dune.
- Limit: Walichrum leaves the chart in 850, so the start is closed from 840 and the choice on the dune from 849. A house that lets the whole ten years go by keeps its flags and no finale: the only way to lose this chain is not to go back to the island.

The four options of step 10 are one more than the brief likes; the story has four roads and they part there.

## Keeping the vow, and breaking it

- **Kept** means: the one who was promised has been paid and the other has not (the candle for a vow to the saint; the share, at Hleidr or over the side, for a vow to the sea). A man who vowed nothing may pay either one freely and it counts.
- **Broken** means: not paid (step 6 comes back every 120 to 150 days until it is), or paid to the wrong one (a candle for a sea vow leaves the sea unpaid and pleases only the priest; a purse for a vow to the saint leaves the candle owed, and the priest hears of the purse).
- **The hard road** in the buried, church and custom finales is open only to a man whose vow is kept, who took nothing from the stone and who did not lie to the priest. It gives a second grand reward (`ch_old_gods_hard_road`).
- Paying both is allowed, as the Svea trader in `lore/gods_cult.md` 3.5 counts both paid: it stops step 6, and it closes the hard road.

### What follows from the changed endings in later years

- `changed_walichrum_chapel`: there is a priest's place on the island before 837. The raid of 17 June 837 has a church to burn; Bishop Frederick (815/816 to 838) preaches in a building and not on a beach; the churches of Saint Willibrord at Westkapelle and Oostkapelle are rebuildings, not beginnings. In January 1647 the sea gives up the other altars, and one is missing from the count.
- `changed_nehalennia_remembered`: the stone outlasts the houses. When Lothar gives Walcheren to the Dane Harald in 841 the new lord finds a place on the island he understands; the annalist who called that gift detestable has one more thing to write. Later cards at Walichrum can offer `act:offer` at a `shrine` site. The antiquaries of 1647 find a stone their grandfathers' grandfathers had never lost.
- `changed_nehalennia_broken`: nothing comes out of the dune in 1647 at that spot; the name on that altar is read once, by one priest, and never again. Cards after about 800 can have the islanders remember who held the mattock.

### Why a merchant could really have won these

- **The Scheldt mouth and its banks** (`unlock_pilot_scheldt`): the Banjaard shoals lie off the island's north-west corner and a creek ran behind the dunes; such water was learned from the people who lived on it, and they taught whom they chose.
- **A house on the island** (`unlock_factor_walichrum`): Walichrum's trade was wool, hides and cloth from marsh sheep; a Dorestad house with a trusted family there is the ordinary shape of the Frisian trade.
- **Saint Martin's man** (`unlock_charter_traiectum`, in the unlock table): the church of Utrecht held royal privileges and its carriers passed under its immunity.
- **A chip of the signed stone** (`unlock_relic_willibrord_stone`): a contact relic of a hallowed place, of the kind an honest bishop could give; three centuries later a monk of Echternach wrote of just such a stone on this island.
- **Felag and a lord's ring** (both in the unlock table): the rune stones' partnership, and silver worn on the arm as proof of whose friend a man is.
- **A lord's protection** (in the unlock table): the bishop's hand over a free man who has done the church a service; Liudger's shrine-breaking was paid from the same purse.

## How long it takes

- The shortest road: the storm in the ninth month, the stone the same autumn, the priest before Martinmas, a hundred and twenty days' wait, the dune in the fourth month, the finale sixty days on: about **nine months**.
- The full road: the storm one autumn, the stone and the priest that winter, the Dane the next summer, Hleidr in the tenth month, the priest again a month later, the dune the summer after, the finale by autumn: **two years**.
- It overlaps with ordinary trading on purpose: every step but the feast is at a port a Dorestad boat calls at anyway, and the feast pays for the detour in wine.
