## The story in five sentences

In 1018 England weighs out the greatest tribute it ever paid, the fleet goes home, and for the first time one king
holds both shores of the North Sea. The house carries an English moneyer and his irons to Ripa, so that the same
penny passes on the Thames and in Jutland, and takes a bishop's order for Rhine wine. On the way to fill it the
shipman meets the tolls of the age: a toll with a king's writing behind it at the Wantsum mouth, and tolls with only
a rope behind them at Cologne and on the count's water at Flardinga, and he cuts what they take on a stick. In 1026
a priest of the king's household asks what was taken on the roads, and the shipman tells all of it, or tells what
everybody tells, or sells the stick to the men of Cologne. The king's letter comes home from Rome in 1027, and in
1035 the king dies and the house learns what a dead man's peace is worth.

## How a player meets it

- **The start** is `harbour.chain_cnut_1016.s01_the_geld`: any call at Lundenwic, Hamwic, Sarre, Dofras or Gipeswic
  in 1017 or 1018 can draw it (weight 2.5).
- **A second start** is step 2, `s02_irons_for_denmark`: Lundenwic, April to September, 1019 to 1023 (weight 3). It
  does not ask for step 1, so a house that was elsewhere in the tribute years still finds the story.
- Steps 3 to 7 are drawn at their ports once step 2 is done, in any order. None is needed for an ending; each
  one changes what the endings offer.
- Step 8 (June 1026 to February 1027) chooses the road. If the player never meets it, ending 1 still comes: the
  letter was written without him.
- The spine: thread `en_*` flags (`en_paid_share`, `en_followed_silver`, `en_cnut_peace`); cast `cast.cnut` (never an
  actor: a merchant hears him as a letter read out, as the spine says).

**What is ours, and marked (?) in the cards' notes.** That a stranger's keel was asked toward a town's share; that
a moneyer crossed to Ripa in a merchant's boat; that the tolls the letter complains of were met on the Rhine at
Cologne (the letter names the road to Rome and the mountain barriers, not the river); that a priest gathered the
complaints from shipmen (the letter says only that the king spoke of his people's needs); that the letter was read in
the ports and that a reeve gave a merchant the king's writ after it; that a writ of Cnut's bore a hanging seal
(sealed writs are certain only from Edward's reign (?)). No research was done on the web for this chain: every fact
is from the project's own dossiers and from memory, and the ones not found in the dossiers carry a (?).

No words are quoted from a translation. The Chronicle and the letter are in our own rendering.

**What would follow from the changed ending.** In truth the English and the Danes had no freedom at Cologne in
these years, and the men of Cologne none in writing at London beyond the old tariff, until Henry II took them into
his protection about 1157 (?). With `changed_cnut_letter_rhine` set: English and Danish keels pay no third toll
between the sea and Cologne from 1027; English cloth goes up the Rhine a century early, and Rhine wine comes down
cheaper to London and to Ripa; Count Dirk's toll at Flardinga is the emperor's quarrel and the English king's at
once, so the Tiel men have two lords to complain to; and when Gunhild marries the emperor's son in 1036 the grant
has a reason to outlive her father. Later cards may read the flag for: wine cheaper at Lundenwic and Ripa after
1027, the toll man at Colonia cool to this house, the chain `tiel_1020` (being written the same night: its count's
man and its emperor's men stand in the same quarrel), and the reckoning in 1066.

**The costs that last.** `ch_cnut_1016_named` (witness road): the toll men of the Rhine know the boat; the
integrator may start the Colonia toll man's feeling low. `ch_cnut_1016_sold_tally` (sold road): the London cloth men
who paid three tolls know who was on the bank with a stick. `ch_cnut_1016_writ_void` (card 13, lapse): the
integrator should withdraw `unlock_title_kings_protection` and `en_cnut_peace` if this chain alone gave them.
`unlock_charter_colonia`, `unlock_title_emperors_man`, the plot and the factors do not lapse with the king.

**Why a merchant could really have won these.** A king's writ of protection for a house is the commonest favour of
the age, and the letter of 1027 is the first time a northern king bargains for his merchants by name. A reeve's man
or a Cologne cousin keeping goods between sailings is how every stranger traded. Plots on the London hythes were
granted by kings from 889. Toll freedom at one named place is the commonest royal document there is. The emperor's
men's standing at London is in the London tariff. The thegn's right is the law's own promise to a merchant who
crossed the wide sea three times at his own cost. The pilotage of the Rhine is an old man's knowledge, given.

## How long it takes

From the first card to an ending: nine or ten years of the game (1017 or 1019 to the summer of 1027), with eight
steps spread over six ports on both shores and the Rhine, each a single call. The reckoning comes eight years after
that (November 1035 to 1037). The real waits are a ship's passage (nine days to Ripa), a commission (240 days), a
king's journey (step 8 to the letter: five months to a year), and a king's life. A heir may begin it and the same
heir, older, or the next, may end it.
