## The story in five sentences

In the summer of 804 the emperor empties Wihmodia and the land beyond the Elbe: every Saxon there, with wife and children, is walked into Francia and set down far from home, and a Frisian shipman sees them sitting on the Rhine bank waiting for hulls.
At the Ems mouth a family of five that did not come in to be counted lies hidden in a sheep house: Meginward, his wife Aldburg, his mother Ricswind, the boy Folcward and the small girl Gerburg, eleven nights through the marsh, wanting passage to a brother among the Danes.
The count's man Gundhart is short of his number, pays by the head, and keeps a guard boat of twelve oars at the river mouth that looks under sails.
The shipman can give them up, carry them north, take them to the priest Liudger who can ask for them in the open, or make Frisians of them and wait six winters for the war to end.
Whatever he does, a people that has little left but its memory remembers it: as guest right and a partner among the Danes, a bishop's hand over his house, a buried pot of silver on the Weser marsh, or a small mark cut in his stem post that nobody will own to.

## How a player meets it

- **On the Rhine or the IJssel:** `s01_column` is drawn at Diusburg, Colonia or Daventre from July 804 to the end of 805 (weight 2, once). Any house trading wine, querns or grain up the Rhine in those two summers sees it. It sets nothing in motion by force: it gives a name (Aldburg) and a river (the Ems), posts the news, and schedules the next card at Emuthon.
- **At the Ems mouth:** `s02_sheep_house` is drawn at Emuthon in the same years by anyone, whether or not he saw the column (weight 2.5, once). This is the real start.
- **A player who refuses is still in the story.** The count's man asks him what he has seen (step 3), and the family asks again (step 5). All five endings are open to him.
- Later steps are drawn with a high weight (4 to 8) at their ports, so they come on the next call there once their flags and waits allow; the count's man, the night after, the marsh run, the priest (after a failure at sea) and the ending by the count are also scheduled.
- Other chains can read `sx_carried_fugitive` (the spine's flag, set when the five go aboard), `ch_saxon_exiles_804_watched` (the count's men know the boat) and `ch_saxon_exiles_804_saxons_remember` (he gave them up).

## The real history under it

**The deportation of 804.** Annales regni Francorum 804: the emperor wintered at Aachen; in the summer he led an army into Saxony and moved all the Saxons who lived beyond the Elbe and in Wihmodia, with women and children, into Francia, and gave the districts beyond the Elbe to the Obodrites. Einhard, Vita Karoli c. 7: ten thousand men of those who lived on both banks of the Elbe, with wives and children, settled in many groups here and there through Gaul and Germany. Also Chronicon Moissiacense 804, Annales Xantenses 804 (`chronicle_793.json` 804-summer-nordalbingia-emptied). Earlier removals: 795, 797, 798, 799 (`chronicle_793.json` 799-saxons-deported-799). This was the last, and the war of thirty-three years ended with it.

**The fugitives and the Danes.** The same annal: Godfred, king of the Danes, came with his fleet and all the horsemen of his kingdom to the place called Sliesthorp, on the border of his kingdom and Saxony; he promised to come to a meeting with the emperor and, kept back by the counsel of his men, did not; the emperor sat at Hollenstedt on the Elbe and sent an embassy to him about the return of fugitives (`chronicle_793.json` 804-summer-godfred-sliesthorp, 804-summer-hollenstedt). So Saxons did flee to the Danes that summer, as Widukind had in 777 and 782. What Godfred answered is not written. The game lets him keep them, since nothing says any were given back (?).

**The watch on the rivers.** Einhard, Vita Karoli c. 17: a fleet built against the Northmen, and watches set at the harbours and at the mouths of the rivers, from about 800 (`chronicle_793.json` 802-spring-coast-ships-802 for the Channel coast). That this watch was turned on fugitives is the game's reading (?). No capitulary on hiding or carrying the deported survives; the count's man's reward by the head and the penalty in goods are the game's, with the half share to the finder borrowed from Thionville 805 c. 7 on arms (MGH Capitularia I no. 44) (?).

**Lists.** The clerks did keep Saxons by name: the Indiculus obsidum Saxonum Moguntiam deducendorum of about 805 names 37 hostages and the bishops and counts who held them (MGH Capitularia I no. 115) (?). A list of the households of Wihmodia that never reached the road (step 10) is invented in its likeness.

**Liudger and Werden.** Altfrid, Vita Liudgeri I.22-24: teacher of five Frisian districts east of the Lauwers and the island Bant from 787, among them Emisga, where Emuthon lies; then teacher of the western Saxons at Mimigernaford; founder of Werden on the Ruhr, on his own land, about 799; bishop from 30 March 805; died 26 March 809 (spine `cast.liudger`; `dossiers/emuthon.md`). The deported were settled on royal and church land, so a bishop asking for one household for his own house's land is inside the order. No such case is recorded (?). The Old Saxon baptismal vow is `texts_793.json` saxon_vow; the first Saxon capitulary made baptism the price of life.

**Where the five go if they are given up.** The spine sets deported families on abbey land by Rotomagus, and `places/rotomagus.json` has the story of the man who asks every ship for the Weser (`rotomagus.weser_man`). No source names Rouen (?).

**The land afterwards.** Annales regni Francorum 808-811: Godfred falls on the Obodrites and takes two thirds of them under tribute (808); the Franks take the north back and Saxon counts begin the fort of Esesfeld on the Stor on 15 March 809; in 810 two hundred Danish ships take a hundred pounds of silver from the Frisians and Godfred is killed by one of his own men; in 811 twelve men of each side swear peace on the Eider. That deported people were then let home to the land beyond the Elbe is a modern guess ("vermutlich", de.wikipedia, Nordalbingien, read 6 Oct 2026). The Astronomer, Vita Hludowici c. 24, has Louis the Pious give Saxons and Frisians back the right to their fathers' inheritance in 814 (?). Of Wihmodia's people, nothing more is written.

**The small things.** A stranger without a host among the Northmen has no peace (Rimbert, Vita Anskarii c. 24-27, for merchants under a king's or chief's peace; Hávamál's guest stanzas). Oath-helpers clear a man in Frisian law (Lex Frisionum). Bant is now a sandbank; the sea took the last of it in 1780 (`dossiers/emuthon.md`). Silver was buried before a flight (`Design/npc_text_encounters.md` 1.5).

## The endings

1. **end_north, the ones who went north** (`ch_saxon_exiles_804_end_north`). What happened to those who fled: the annal says the emperor asked for them back and does not say he got them. Five Saxons live under Meginfrid's roof among the Danes and say the merchant's name after their father's. Pay: 520 d of cut silver and a timber of marten (about 640 d) with `unlock_title_danish_guest_right`; or five timbers of marten (about 600 d) with `unlock_plot_ripa`.
   **With the forty brought out (the hard road): history changed**, `changed_wihmodia_exodus`. 900 d, four timbers of marten (about 1,380 d), guest right and `unlock_partner_meginfrid`, a sworn partner who sails a second boat. What would follow: a whole Saxon kin, with its own meeting and law, lives among the Danes from 804. When Godfred falls on the Obodrites in 808 and when Danish kings are baptised (826) there are Saxons on that strand who speak both tongues: interpreters for Anskar's mission at Sliaswic about 850, kin on both sides of the Eider when Hamburg burns in 845, and a house that has guest right in Denmark through every heir. Later cards can read the flag to give the house a Saxon-speaking friend at any Danish port.
2. **end_given, counted onto the road** (`ch_saxon_exiles_804_end_given`). What happened to most. The five walk west with the last column and are set down on abbey land by the Seine. Pay: 900 d and `unlock_title_counts_shipman`; or 600 d and `unlock_charter_emuthon`; or 600 d and the title with the household kept together (`ch_saxon_exiles_804_kept_together`), which is the family of the Rotomagus story. **Lasting cost:** `ch_saxon_exiles_804_saxons_remember`, the crew's heart, and Tetta's bread at the stranger's price.
3. **end_church, Werden's people** (`ch_saxon_exiles_804_end_church`). The lawful road: the five are the Church's people on the Ruhr, four days from their woods. Pay: 260 d and nine cakes of wax (about 620 d) with `unlock_title_lords_protection`; or 100 d, ten cakes of wax and six crocks of honey (about 640 d) with `unlock_factor_diusburg`. **The most honest road** (the merchant laid down the price of five heads himself, 120 d): both grand rewards, 420 d, the wax and the honey (about 960 d) and half toll at the port for a year.
4. **end_home, the empty land: history changed** (`ch_saxon_exiles_804_end_home`, `changed_wihmodia_resettled`). After six winters as Frisian shepherds the family is carried home across the Jade in 810 or later and digs up the pot under the hearth stone. Pay: 700 d and `unlock_pilot_weser`; **the hard road** (80 d laid out for seed, iron and a cow): 1,100 d, the pilot's knowledge and `unlock_heirloom_weser_arm_ring`, the cut arm ring that paid the first passage. What would follow: in the record Wihmodia's people are not heard of again; here one hamlet on the Weser marsh is lived in by the people who left it, and by the next harvest there are three smokes. A house of the player's heirs has a friend's landing below Bremen when the bishops there get their market (the charter of 888 for Bremen (?)), Saxon honey and hides at a kinsman's price, and, in 841, a village that has less reason than most to rise with the Stellinga.
5. **end_terp, they stay Frisians** (`ch_saxon_exiles_804_end_terp`). The same road, but the merchant says what Tetta says. Meginward fetches the pot and comes back; his grandchildren will say they were always Frisians. Pay: 360 d and six bolts of cloth (about 650 d) with `unlock_factor_emuthon`.

## Why each grand reward is one a merchant could have won

- `unlock_title_danish_guest_right`: a man of standing who says before the lawman "this one is mine" is what a foreign merchant needed among the Northmen; Meginfrid has a hearth there and says it.
- `unlock_partner_meginfrid`: the felag, a sworn partnership with one purse, is named on later rune stones (?) for 804; partnerships of shipmen are older than the word. Meginfrid already has a boat.
- `unlock_plot_ripa`: booth plots at Ripa were laid out in rows on the bank and held by traders (the excavated plots, from about 705).
- `unlock_title_counts_shipman`, `unlock_charter_emuthon`: a count rewarded service with his word and with freedom from his own toll; Emuthon was the king's own place with a toll (`dossiers/emuthon.md`).
- `unlock_title_lords_protection`: a bishop's letter of protection for a merchant's house; Liudger was a bishop from 805 and the emperor's man in these districts.
- `unlock_factor_diusburg`, `unlock_factor_emuthon`: a man who owes the house five lives and keeps its goods under his roof. Abbeys kept such men at river landings.
- `unlock_pilot_weser`: the gullies of the Jade and the Weser mouth, learned turn by turn from a man born on them.
- `unlock_heirloom_weser_arm_ring`: a silver arm ring with a finger's length cut out of it; hack silver from rings is what the north paid in.

## How long it takes

- **The count's road:** two to three weeks to the telling, then ninety days to the count's court: one season.
- **The road north:** the passage to Ripa, thirty days for the brother to come, forty-five more to the ending: a season. With the forty, add the passage back to the Ems and thirty days: half a year.
- **The priest's road:** the river passage and sixty days for the count's answer: a season.
- **The long road:** hidden in 804 or 805, searched for between 806 and 809, home from 810: five to six years, running under everything else the house does.
