## The story in five sentences

In the 790s the law of the Frisians is in no book: it lives in the heads of a few wise men on the terps, and a merchant
first meets it at Doccinga, where lots are laid on the altar over a man killed in a crowd and his own steersman is one
of the seven named. He carries a word from the wise man Wlemar to the wise man Saxmund beyond the Lauwers, whose
sister's sons plough their dead father's field for the count's man, because the king took the right of inheritance from
all who rose in 793. The wise men of the three lands reckon what a man is worth, and find they have three numbers for
one man. In 802 the emperor's messengers ask at Dorestad who knows the Frisians' law, and the merchant carries the wise
men south by Saint Martin's to the palace above the Waal. There he stands between two old men who speak Frisian and a
clerk with a pen, and what is written, or not written, goes through his mouth.

## Why this story

It is the one great thing the region made in these years that no other chain tells: the Lex Frisionum. Other chains
quote single clauses of it (house_rival the lawsuit, old_gods the shrine, house_marriage the kin's will); none tells how
it came to be written, by whom, and why it looks as it does. It is the player's own law, it ties seven of the region's
ten ports into one errand, and it is a true case of history turning on what one room chose to write down.

**What is invented**, and marked so in every card's note: where Wlemar and Saxmund lived (Wlemar on the terps of the
middle land, Saxmund at the Ems mouth) and how they spoke; the old man of the west and his grandson (the law names no
western wise man); the killing at the wool sale; the widow and her sons; the clerk, the count and the deacon; that the
Frisians were heard at Noviomagus on the way to Aachen (the place of the hearing is not recorded); and that a merchant
stood between the wise men and the pen. Bernlef (`cast.bernlef`) is spoken of once; the bishop is `cast.hamacar` and is
not named. The steersman is `cast.crew.aldger` where the game has him.

## How a player meets it

At **Doccinga, in any year from 794 to 801**: the first card has a weight of 3.0, so it comes on nearly every call
there. Doccinga is on the inland way north from Dorestad over the Almere, and Boniface's day (5 June) brings boats.
Each later card has a weight of 30 and asks for the last flag, so it comes on the first call at its port. A house that
first comes to Doccinga after 801 has missed it: nothing was left to carry.

### What would follow from the changed endings

- `changed_lex_frisionum_finished`: Frisian courts judge "by the writing" from 803. The three scales and the heathen
  judgements are gone from the record: no later card may quote the sand or the three wergilds as living law, and a
  lawman's line can say "the emperor's book" where it would have said "as it was judged". The wise men lose their
  office a generation early; a count's clerk can now tell a Frisian his own law. `changed_lex_frisionum_ship`
  (house_rival) would then be a line added to a finished book.
- `changed_frisian_inheritance_802`: Louis's grace of 814 has nothing left to give the Frisians, and the 814 card of
  this chain does not come. The east owes the old emperor something in the years of Godfred's fleet (810): cards of
  coast_watch_810 could give Emuthon a willing levy. The Stellinga of 841 is a Saxon matter and is not touched.
- `changed_lex_frisionum_unwritten`: Frisian law stays oral until the Frisians write it themselves (in truth the Old
  Frisian law texts are of the 11th to 13th centuries). Herold has nothing to print in 1557. In play: every law card in
  Frisia needs a lawman present, and a house that holds `unlock_heirloom_law_of_three_lands` can say the law itself.

**Why a merchant could have won these.** An oath taken as a landsman's is the whole worth of a man in a law that
proves by oath-helpers (tit. I). A plot and a factor on a terp are what a trusted outsider is given. Toll freedom at
Dorestad was the king's to give and he gave it (779). The palace's own merchants are in the privilege of 828. A sworn
partner with a second boat is the Frisian trading company the sources show at Sliaswic and Birka. A pilot for the
Lauwers mouth is a fisherman's knowledge. The law as an heirloom is what the wise men themselves had: it was inherited
by being said. The silver is "a penny a hearth" from three lands, or the emperor's carriage money.

## How long it takes

From the first card to the hearing is as long as the years make it: the chain can start in 794 and nothing can be
written before the spring of 802. Inside that, the waits are 20 days (after the lots), 30 days (Saxmund's hay), the
voyage from the Ems to Winaldum and to Medemelacha, then, from 802, 20 days for the summons, the voyage south by the
Vecht, 3 to 12 days at the palace, and 60 days for the assembly and the news of it. A house that starts in 801 can
finish in about a year and a half; a house that starts in 794 carries it for nine years between other trade. The last
card comes in 814, twelve years after the hearing, perhaps to an heir.
