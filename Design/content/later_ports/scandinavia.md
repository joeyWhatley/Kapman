# Later ports: Denmark, Norway and Sweden, 793-1066

Written 6 Oct 2026 for `Design/content/later_ports`. The port table (`ports_793.json`) was made for the year 793.
This file lists the trading places of the Danes, the Northmen, the Svear and the Gutes that are **not** in that table
and that a merchant could have put in at in some year between 793 and 1066.

**Already in the table, so not repeated here:** Ripa (Ribe), Sliesthorp (Hedeby), Skiringssalr (Kaupang), Birka, Helgo,
Uppsalir, Paviken, Ahus, Ogvaldsnes (Avaldsnes), Hleidr (Lejre).

**How it was checked.** Wikipedia pages in English, Danish, Swedish and Norwegian were read on 6 Oct 2026 for the
dates and coordinates (listed under each place). Everything else rests on the excavation reports and books named under
"Sources" as the writer knows them; they were not opened today. `(?)` marks a fact that could not be checked or that
the sources leave in doubt. Chapter numbers of Adam of Bremen and Snorri must be checked against an edition before any
is shown to a player. Snorri wrote about 1230: for anything before 1000 he is a late witness.

**The rows.** Each place ends with a row in the exact format of `ports_793.json`. They use only realm, region, kind and
landing words the table already has, so they load as they stand. Where a better word is wanted (a realm for the earls
of Lade, a region for Trondelag) it is said under "Needs" at the bottom and not put in the row. `from` is the first
year a merchant finds a market there; `to` is the last (0 = it goes on past 1066). Coordinates are the place itself;
where the town lies inland of its landing, the landing's coordinates are given too.

## MUST HAVE

### 1. Aros

- **Name then:** Aros ("river mouth"). AROS on coins of Harthacnut and Magnus the Good; Adam of Bremen writes Arhusin (?).
- **Modern place:** Aarhus, east Jutland, Denmark.
- **Lat, lon:** 56.157, 10.211 (the old town on the north bank of the river mouth).
- **Active:** about 770 to now. A town all through the game.
- **Kind:** a small craft and trading place in 793; a walled town with a bishop from the 900s.
- **Who ruled:** the kings of the Danes throughout. 793-810 Sigfred and Godfred; 813-854 Horik I; the 900s Gorm and
  Harald Bluetooth, who walled it; 986-1014 Sweyn Forkbeard; 1018-1035 Cnut; 1035-1042 Harthacnut; 1042-1047 Magnus of
  Norway; 1047-1076 Sweyn Estridsen. A bishop sits here from 948.
- **Traded:** in: Norwegian soapstone pots and whetstones, Rhineland quernstones, glass beads, Frankish and later
  German pots. Out: grain, cattle and hides of east Jutland, combs, iron work. A market for ships changing between
  the Belts, the Sound and Norway.
- **How a boat comes in:** an open bay of the Kattegat, sheltered from the west. A small boat runs up into the river
  mouth and lies on the north bank under the rampart. No tide to speak of.
- **Why a merchant goes:** it is the middle of the Danish waters. Adam says that from Aarhus one sails to Fyn, to
  Zealand, to Skane, or all the way to Norway (?). Prices and news from four seas meet here.
- **Evidence:** pit houses, hearths, glass beads and a road of the late 700s (dig of 2003). An earth rampart of the
  early 900s, perhaps 934 (?), made stronger under Harald Bluetooth. Bishop Reginbrand of Aros at the synod of
  Ingelheim in 948. Six rune stones in and about the town from around 1000; one (the mask stone) names a fight
  "when kings fought" and another a man who "died at Hedeby" (?). Coins struck here from about 1040. Harald Hardrada
  burns the town and its church about 1050.
- **Sources:** Skov, H. (2005), "Aros 700-1100", in Vikingernes Aros, Moesgaard Museum. Adam of Bremen, Gesta II.4
  (the bishops of 948) and IV.1 (the sailings) (?). en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Aarhus (read 6 Oct 2026).

### 2. Hroiskelda

- **Name then:** Hroiskelda ("Hroi's spring"); Adam of Bremen writes Roscald.
- **Modern place:** Roskilde, Zealand, Denmark.
- **Lat, lon:** 55.642, 12.080 (the church hill). The landing below it: 55.650, 12.078.
- **Active:** about 980 to now.
- **Kind:** a king's town with a church, later a bishop and a mint. It takes over from Hleidr (Lejre), 8 km away.
- **Who ruled:** Harald Bluetooth founds it in the 980s and is buried in the church he built (Adam). Sweyn Forkbeard
  986-1014. Cnut 1018-1035: his brother-in-law Earl Ulf is killed in the church at Christmas 1026, and Ulf's widow
  Estrid has it built again in stone (?). Bishops from about 1022 (Gerbrand, consecrated in England). Sweyn
  Estridsen, Estrid's son, makes it his chief seat 1047-1076.
- **Traded:** in: wine, cloth, church goods, English and German silver, Norwegian stone, walrus ivory. Out: Zealand's
  grain, butter, cattle, horses. The king's and the bishop's households are the buyers.
- **How a boat comes in:** forty kilometres up a shallow, crooked fjord from the Kattegat, through narrows that want a
  local man in the bow. Then a beach under the hill. About 1070 the channel at Skuldelev, 20 km north, is blocked with
  sunk ships to guard the town.
- **Why a merchant goes:** the richest buyers in Denmark after 1000, and from 1047 the king himself. A place to seek a
  charter or a commission.
- **Evidence:** Adam of Bremen on Harald's church and burial, and on Roskilde as the seat of the Danish kings and the
  greatest town of Zealand. The five Skuldelev ships: a small coaster built in Denmark about 1040, a sea-going trader
  built in western Norway about 1030, and a longship built at Dublin in 1042. A 36-metre longship of about 1025 found
  in the harbour in 1997 (Roskilde 6). Coins of Cnut and his heirs struck here.
- **Sources:** Adam of Bremen, Gesta II.28, II.55, IV.5 (?). Crumlin-Pedersen, O. & Olsen, O. (2002), The Skuldelev
  Ships I, Viking Ship Museum. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roskilde (read 6 Oct 2026).

### 3. Lund

- **Name then:** Lund ("the grove"); LVND on the coins. A coin of Cnut calls it the Danes' London (?).
- **Modern place:** Lund, Skane, Sweden (Danish until 1658).
- **Lat, lon:** 55.704, 13.193. The landing: the mouth of the Hoje river at Lomma, 55.673, 13.070, 9 km west.
- **Active:** about 990 to now.
- **Kind:** a king's town with churches and the largest mint in Denmark. It takes over from Uppakra, 5 km south.
- **Who ruled:** founded about 990 under Sweyn Forkbeard. Cnut 1018-1035 brings English moneyers. Sweyn Estridsen
  makes it a bishop's seat about 1060 (?).
- **Traded:** in: English and German silver, cloth, wine, quernstones, church goods. Out: Skane's grain, horses,
  cattle, and autumn herring from the Sound. Silver is weighed and struck here.
- **How a boat comes in:** Lund has no water. She lies on the open beach at Lomma or in the river mouth, and the goods
  go up by cart. The Sound is sheltered from the west but open to a north-wester.
- **Why a merchant goes:** good coin. A merchant with foreign or hacked silver can change it for the king's pennies
  here, and the town buys what the king's and bishop's men want.
- **Evidence:** the oldest graves and houses are dated by tree rings to about 990. Several wooden churches before
  1050. Coins from about 1020 with the names of English moneyers. The move from Uppakra is an inference from the two
  sites' dates, not a written fact.
- **Sources:** Cinthio, M. (2002), De forsta stadsborna: medeltida gravar och manniskor i Lund. Andren, A. & Carelli,
  P. on Lund's founding (?). en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund (read 6 Oct 2026).

### 4. Alaborg

- **Name then:** Alabu on a coin of Harthacnut (about 1040); Alaburg in Adam of Bremen.
- **Modern place:** Aalborg, north Jutland, Denmark.
- **Lat, lon:** 57.048, 9.921 (the mouth of the Ostera stream on the south shore).
- **Active:** from the 900s (?) to now. Across the water, the village and grave field of Lindholm Hoje from the 500s
  to about 1100, when blown sand buried it.
- **Kind:** a market at the narrowest point of the Limfjord; a town with a mint by 1040.
- **Who ruled:** the kings of the Danes. Harald Bluetooth builds the great ring fort of Aggersborg 40 km west about
  980 to hold the fjord.
- **Traded:** in: Norwegian iron, soapstone, whetstones and quernstones; later stockfish. Out: grain, malt, cattle,
  horses. Herring in the fjord itself.
- **How a boat comes in:** this is the thing to know. In these centuries the Limfjord is open to the North Sea in the
  west as well as to the Kattegat in the east (the western mouth sands up about 1100-1200 (?)). A small boat from
  Frisia can leave the open sea at the western mouth, sail 150 km of sheltered water across Jutland and come out in
  the Kattegat, and never round Skagen. Aalborg is the last narrows before the eastern mouth. She lies in the stream
  mouth.
- **Why a merchant goes:** the safe road to the Baltic and to Norway. Adam says the crossing from here to Norway is
  short (a day to the Vik (?)).
- **Evidence:** the coin of about 1040. Adam of Bremen about 1075 on Alaburg as the harbour for Norway. The 700
  graves and ship-shaped stone settings of Lindholm Hoje. The saga tells how Harald Hardrada, shut in the Limfjord by
  Sweyn's fleet about 1061, dragged his ships over the neck of land in the west (?).
- **Sources:** Adam of Bremen, Gesta IV.1 and IV.33 (?). Ramskou, T. (1976), Lindholm Hoje gravpladsen. Roesdahl, E.
  et al. (2014), Aggersborg: The Viking-Age Settlement and Fortress. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalborg (read 6 Oct 2026).

### 5. Hladir and Nidaros

One story, two rows: the earls' seat on the headland, then the king's town at the river mouth 3 km away.

- **Name then:** Hladir (Lade). The town: Nidaros ("mouth of the Nid"), or just Kaupangr ("the market town").
- **Modern place:** Lade and the centre of Trondheim, Norway.
- **Lat, lon:** Hladir 63.447, 10.445. Nidaros 63.431, 10.397.
- **Active:** Hladir from before the game to about 1000 as a seat. Nidaros from 997 to now.
- **Kind:** Hladir is a lord's seat with a hall, a heathen holy place and a beach. Nidaros is a king's town.
- **Who ruled:** the earls of Hladir: Hakon Grjotgardsson about 900, Sigurd (burned 962), Earl Hakon the Mighty
  (about 970-995, for a time under Harald Bluetooth). Olaf Tryggvason founds the town in 997 and falls in 1000. Earls
  Eirik and Svein 1000-1015 under the Danish and Svea kings. Olaf Haraldsson builds it again in 1016; he falls at
  Stiklestad in 1030 and lies in the town from 1031. Cnut's son Svein and Aelfgifu 1030-1035. Magnus 1035-1047.
  Harald Hardrada 1046-1066, who strikes coin and builds a Mary church here.
- **Traded:** out: the north's goods, brought down by the men of Halogaland: marten, otter, bear and reindeer skins,
  walrus ivory, walrus-hide rope, down, falcons, later stockfish. In: grain, malt, honey, wine, cloth, swords. The
  Trondelag itself has grain and iron.
- **How a boat comes in:** forty kilometres in from the sea up a wide deep fjord. At Hladir a beach under the
  headland. At Nidaros the river makes a bend round a flat ness: she lies in the river on the town side, out of all
  weather.
- **Why a merchant goes:** the furs and ivory of the north at the nearest place a southern boat can buy them. After
  1031 the pilgrims to Saint Olaf: a town full of people who must eat and who want wax, wine and cloth for the church.
- **Evidence:** Snorri and the older Olaf sagas for the founding of 997. Houses of the late 900s under the town show
  it was not quite empty before (?). The Ore thing at the river mouth, where kings are taken. A wooden church with
  graves from soon after 1000, found in 2016 and held to be Saint Clement's, where Olaf first lay. Pennies of Harald
  Hardrada from about 1047. Olaf Kyrre begins the stone church over the grave about 1070.
- **Sources:** Snorri, Heimskringla: Olaf Tryggvason's saga ch. 70 (?), Saint Olaf's saga ch. 53 (?). Christophersen,
  A. & Nordeide, S. W. (1994), Kaupangen ved Nidelva. Ohthere's account in the Old English Orosius (about 890) for the
  northern goods. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trondheim (read 6 Oct 2026).

### 6. Tunsberg

- **Name then:** Tunsberg ("the fenced hill").
- **Modern place:** Tonsberg, Vestfold, Norway.
- **Lat, lon:** 59.268, 10.408.
- **Active:** Snorri has a trading place here before 900. The dig finds farms and graves of the Viking age and a
  town from about 1000-1100 (?). For the game: a landing market from about 900, a town from about 1000.
- **Kind:** a market under a rock, by the thing mound of Vestfold. Kaupang's heir (Kaupang ends about 960).
- **Who ruled:** the kings of Vestfold, and often the Danish kings over them. Snorri says Harald Fairhair's son Bjorn,
  called the Merchant, sat at Saeheim close by, kept trading ships and was killed there by his brother Eirik about
  930 (?). Danish overlords about 970-995 and 1000-1015. The kings of Norway after 1015.
- **Traded:** out: whetstones of Telemark, iron, soapstone pots, furs and hides from inland, timber. In: grain,
  malt, cloth, pots, wine, silver. Snorri says ships from the north of Norway, from Denmark and from Saxland came here.
- **How a boat comes in:** a narrow sheltered sound between the mainland and the island of Notteroy, entered from
  the south. A beach under the castle rock.
- **Why a merchant goes:** whetstones. Every man with a knife in the North Sea world wants a Norwegian whetstone, and
  they load here. And a king's son who trades is a rare friend.
- **Evidence:** Snorri (late). Viking-age graves under the town, dug in 1987-88. The Oseberg ship burial of 834, 5 km
  north. The Klastad ship, wrecked near by in the 990s with a cargo of whetstones still in her (?). The Haugar mounds
  and thing place.
- **Sources:** Snorri, Heimskringla: Harald Fairhair's saga ch. 35 (?). Christensen, A. E. & Leiro, G. (1976),
  Klastadskipet. Brendalsmo, J. & Molaug, P. B. (2014) on the early Norwegian towns (?).
  en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonsberg (read 6 Oct 2026).

### 7. Oslo

- **Name then:** Oslo; also written Anslo (?).
- **Modern place:** Gamlebyen (the Old Town), Oslo, Norway.
- **Lat, lon:** 59.905, 10.768 (the mouth of the Alna river).
- **Active:** about 1000 to now.
- **Kind:** a small town with a church from about 1000; a king's town from about 1048.
- **Who ruled:** the Vik is the Danish kings' land more often than not until 1015: Harald Bluetooth, then Sweyn
  Forkbeard through the earls. Olaf Haraldsson 1015-1028. Cnut 1028-1035. Magnus 1035-1047. Harald Hardrada, who
  Snorri says set up the market town about 1048, to have a store of food near at hand and a guard against Denmark.
- **Traded:** out: the produce of the richest farmland in Norway, iron and hides from the uplands, timber. In: cloth,
  wine, pots, quernstones, salt.
- **How a boat comes in:** a hundred kilometres up the fjord, through the narrows at Drobak. A flat shore at the
  river mouth; she is pulled up or lies in the river.
- **Why a merchant goes:** the head of the fjord and the road inland. In Harald's years, a king who buys for a host.
- **Evidence:** Christian graves under the oldest church (Saint Clement's), dated to about 980-1030: so a Christian
  settlement stood here before Harald. Snorri for Harald's founding. Hallvard, a local man killed in 1043, is held a
  saint and becomes the town's patron.
- **Sources:** Snorri, Heimskringla: Harald Hardrada's saga ch. 58 (?). Schia, E. (1991), Oslo innerst i Viken.
  Nedkvitne, A. & Norseng, P. (2000), Middelalderbyen ved Bjorvika. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo (read 6 Oct 2026).

### 8. Borg (Lofoten)

- **Name then:** not known. Borg is the farm's later name (?).
- **Modern place:** Borg, Vestvagoy, Lofoten, Norway (the Lofotr museum).
- **Lat, lon:** 68.245, 13.754.
- **Active:** a lord's seat from about 500. The great hall stands from the 700s to about 950, then it is left.
- **Kind:** a chieftain's hall, the largest house known from the Viking age in Norway: 83 metres long.
- **Who ruled:** its own lords, of the kind Ohthere was: rich in reindeer and in the tribute of the Sami, owning no
  king until Harald Fairhair's time (about 900). The Book of Settlements names an Olaf Tvennumbruni who left Lofoten
  for Iceland; that he was the last lord of Borg is a guess (?).
- **Traded:** out: what Ohthere lists as his wealth about 890: marten, otter, bear and reindeer skins, down, whalebone,
  walrus ivory, ship ropes of walrus and seal hide. Dried cod. In: grain, malt, wine, glass, fine cloth, swords.
- **How a boat comes in:** inside the island, on a sheltered lagoon reached by a narrow tidal inlet from the north.
  Boat houses stand on its shore, the largest 26 metres. A stranger needs a pilot and slack water.
- **Why a merchant goes:** the best furs and ivory at their source, at a lord's table instead of a market. It is five
  weeks' sail from Dorestad. The lord pays in goods and in friendship, not in coin.
- **Evidence:** the hall, found in 1981-83. In its floor: pieces of Rhineland glass beakers, two Tating jugs from the
  Rhine (the same ware Dorestad ships), gold-foil figures, English and Frankish metalwork. This is the plain proof
  that Rhine goods reached the top of Norway.
- **Sources:** Munch, G. S., Johansen, O. S. & Roesdahl, E. (eds.) (2003), Borg in Lofoten: A Chieftain's Farm in
  North Norway. Ohthere's account in the Old English Orosius. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lofotr_Viking_Museum (read 6 Oct
  2026).

### 9. Sigtuna

- **Name then:** Sigtuna; SITVN on the coins, some with DEI after it ("God's Sigtuna") (?).
- **Modern place:** Sigtuna, Uppland, Sweden.
- **Lat, lon:** 59.617, 17.723.
- **Active:** about 980 to now. Birka ends about 975: this is where its trade goes.
- **Kind:** a planned king's town: one long street along the shore, a hundred narrow plots, the king's own in the
  middle.
- **Who ruled:** founded under Erik the Victorious (about 970-995). Olof Skotkonung (about 995-1022), the first
  Christian king, strikes the first Swedish coins here from about 995 with English moneyers. Anund Jacob (1022-about
  1050); the mint stops about 1030. Emund, then Stenkil (1060-1066). A bishop about 1060 (?).

### 23. Veey

### 24. Vastergarn

- **Name then:** Garn (?). **Modern:** Vastergarn, Gotland. **Lat, lon:** 57.446, 18.139.
- **Active:** Paviken, the lagoon harbour just north, about 700-1000. The half-round wall of Vastergarn, nearly a

  12th century or after (?). A harbour settlement under it from about 1000 (?).

### 25. Slesvig

## What this means for the rows the table already has

These are for whoever owns `ports_793.json`; nothing was changed.

- `hleidr` (Lejre) has `to: 0`. The hall site is given up about 1000 as Roskilde grows (?): `to: 1000` would be truer.
- `paviken` has `to: 0`. The lagoon harbour ends about 1000: `to: 1000`, or an era note naming Garn (see 24).
- `sliesthorp` has `to: 0`. It is burned about 1050 and again in 1066 (see 25). Its name after 808 was Haithabu to
  the Danes (at Haethum in Old English).
- `uppsalir`: a bishop for Sigtuna from about 1060; the heathen temple still stands at Uppsala in Adam's day (1070s).
- `skiringssal` ends in 960 in the table: Tunsberg (6) and Skida (17) pick up its trade.
- `birka` ends in 975: Sigtuna (9) starts in 980. The five years between are true to the ground.
- `ahus`: the trading place (Transval) ends in the 9th century (?); `to: 0` may be too long.

## Needs (words the shared tables lack; none were added)

- **Realms.** The rows use `norse_west` for Trondelag and Halogaland and `norse_vestfold` for the whole Vik. Truer
  would be a realm for the earls of Lade (to 995 and 1000-1015), and one kingdom `norway` after about 1015. The Vik
  is under Danish kings for long stretches (about 813, 970-995, 1000-1015, 1028-1035): a port's `realm` should be
  able to change by year.
- **Regions.** `norway_north` is used for Nidaros, Hladir and Steinker, but they are Trondelag: a grain and iron
  country that sells the north's goods on. A `trondelag` region in `goods_793.json` would let prices show that.
  Oland is put under `gotland` for want of its own region (?).
- **Kinds.** Uppakra, Tisso and Borg in Lofoten are lords' halls with a market when the lord is at home. They are
  given `royal seat`. A kind such as "hall" would fit better, and a way to say "market only at feasts".
- **Inland towns.** Lund, Uppakra and Viborg have no water. Their rows carry the town's coordinates and the entry
  gives the landing's. The game needs either a landing point per port or the rows moved to Lomma and Hjarbaek.
- **Goods.** Whetstones, soapstone, quernstones, bar iron, stockfish, walrus ivory, walrus rope, falcons and eider
  down are all in `goods_793.json` already. The new ports need to be named as sources: whetstones at `skida` and
  `tunsberg`, stockfish at `vagar`, the northern goods at `hladir`, `nidaros` and `borg_lofot`.
- **Year gates for cards.** No coin struck in Denmark outside Hedeby before about 995; none in Sweden before about
  995 or after about 1030; none in Norway before about 995 and not steadily before about 1047. No bishop north of
  Hedeby, Ribe and Aarhus before 948. No church at Nidaros before 997.
