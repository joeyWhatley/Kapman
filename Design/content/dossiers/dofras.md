# Dofras (Dover): dossier

Port `dofras`: Dofras, modern Dover, 51.125 N 1.315 E; realm kent, region england_south, kind market, landing beach, size 2 [ports_793.json]. Researched 4 Oct 2026. Source keys are spelled out in section 9. (?) marks what I could not check; (memory) is known to me but not opened.

**Read this first.** No text names Dover between King Wihtred's day (about 700) and 1051. For 793 there is only the ground: a wooden minster and houses inside a Roman fort, and two Roman towers on the hills. The rich record is the game's last fifteen years: 1051, 1052, 1066, and Domesday Book looking back at King Edward's time.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What it is.** A minster town at the mouth of the Dour, a chalk stream about 4 miles long [enwp Dour], in the gap between two chalk heights. The Romans' Dubris, from a British word for "the waters"; Old English Dofras [wikt; DM Saxon].
- **Who rules.** Offa of Mercia until 29 July 796; Eadberht Praen, 796-798; Cuthred under Cenwulf of Mercia, 798-807; then Cenwulf; Wessex from 825 [repo chronicle; repo thames]. The archbishop at Canterbury is Æthelheard (792/3-805), Offa's man [spine]. No reeve, priest or lord of Dover is named before 1066.
- **Who lives there.** The canons of St Martin, twenty-two by their house's later tradition [VCH]; families in timber and sunken-floored houses, weavers among them [KARU].
- **How big.** Unknown. Digs under the town centre found 14 Anglo-Saxon buildings of all dates [KARU]; the museum calls it "a major settlement" of Kent [DM Saxon]. Size 2 is the game's guess.
- **Coming in.** The strait is 33.2 km at its narrowest, South Foreland to Cap Gris-Nez, and each shore is seen from the other on a clear day [enwp Strait]. White cliffs stand either side of the gap. On each height is a Roman lighthouse, flanking the old harbour [DM Roman; enwp Dubris]. The eastern one stands yet: eight-sided, of tufa, ragstone and red brick [enwp Castle]. The western one's state in 793 is unknown. No source says either was lit after the Romans; the game keeps Dover dark [repo light]. Spring tides are about 6 m, the stream 2-3 knots toward the strait: modern figures carried back [repo water].
- **Where boats lie.** Not recorded. The Roman harbour was the Dour's estuary, said to be at least 200 m wide and tidal a mile inland [DH Dour, no source given]. Shingle kept closing the mouth [enwp Port] and the estuary silted in the Middle Ages [enwp Dour]. Hasted says the haven at the Conquest still lay "much further within land" than later, and quotes Leland on cables and anchors dug up between the hills [H]. The table's "beach" is a fair guess.

## 2. What stood there

- **The Roman shore-fort**, built about 270 [enwp Dubris]: 200 m of its south and west walls and seven bastions have been dug; the Anglo-Saxon buildings lie inside them [KARU].
- **St Martin's minster**: a church "rebuilt in wood four times from the 7th to 10th centuries" [KARU], west of today's Market Square. First one small room with an altar base at the east end; much lengthened eastward in the late 7th century; a century later the floor re-laid and sealed with "imitation red marble" (the floor of 793, if the dating holds (?)); burnt in the 9th century and rebuilt on the same lines [DH Martin, reporting KARU]. Believed lost in the fire of 1066 [DM Norman]; Odo of Bayeux rebuilt it larger as St Martin-le-Grand [enwp Priory].
- **Houses and workshops**: sunken-floored buildings; one, burnt in the 7th century, held over 200 loom weights [KARU].
- **The eastern lighthouse**, about 80 ft (24 m): four Roman stages survive; a fifth was added when it became a bell tower [enwp StMary; enwp Dubris].
- **St Mary in Castro**, beside it: built about 1000 of flint and Roman tile; cross-shaped, with a central tower whose arches are 28 ft high. A large late Saxon graveyard round it suggests an older church without proving one [enwp StMary; DM StMary].
- **The hilltop stronghold**: earthworks hint at an Iron Age fort, unproven [enwp Castle]. By 1066 there was a "castle" for William's men to burn [DM Norman]. What stood there in 793 is not known.
- **The western lighthouse**, later the Bredenstone or "Devil's Drop of Mortar" [enwp Dubris].
- **Buckland grave field**, on Long Hill east of the Dour: 170 graves found in 1951, 244 more in 1994, with weapons, jewellery, combs, pots and Frankish goods [DM Saxon; enwp Buckland]. Older than the game (about 475-750, memory (?)).
- **A mint** by the middle of the 10th century [DM Saxon]; its house is not found.
- **By 1086**: the burgesses' guildhall, and a mill in the harbour mouth [DB; H].
- **Not known**: any quay, market place, well, toll house or town wall of English build.

## 3. Who could be met

Nobody is recorded at Dover between 793 and 1050.

- Æthelheard, archbishop 792/3 to 12 May 805; fled Kent 796-798; at Rome 801-803 [spine]. Where he took ship is not recorded.
- The canons of St Martin: secular clerks "subject to no one except the court of Rome and the king" [VCH]. None is named before Domesday.
- Edward the Confessor (1042-1066): had two parts of Dover's £18 [DB].
- Godwine, earl (died 1053 (memory)): had the third part [DB]; in 1051 would not harry the town, "loth to injure his own people" [ASC].
- Eustace II, count of Boulogne: the fight of 1051 [ASC; enwp Eustace].
- The householder of 1051, wounded by a Frenchman who wanted his house, who killed him and was killed at his own hearth: unnamed [ASC].
- Harold Godwinson: came in with his father's ships in 1052 [ASC]. William of Poitiers says he swore to hand William the castle of Dover (memory) (?).
- William of Normandy: eight days here in October 1066 [DM Norman].
- Odo, bishop of Bayeux: held the town after 1066 and split up the canons' common fund [DB; VCH].
- Moneyers: Dover pennies name their makers. From memory only (?): the first are Æthelstan's (924-939), and Boga, Cinstan, Ceolwi, Manwine and Eadsige stand on pennies from Æthelred II to Harold II. Check each in the Early Medieval Corpus before use.
- Ordinary people the evidence shows: weavers [KARU]; householders who fight for their doors [ASC]; burgesses crewing twenty ships; a steersman and his helper for hire; the king's reeve taking fines [DB]; canons; after about 1000 a ringer in the old lighthouse [enwp StMary].

## 4. What was traded

No cargo of Dover is recorded for 793.

- **The crossing.** In King Edward's day the king's messengers crossed here: 3 pence for a horse's passage in winter, 2 in summer; the burgesses found a steersman and one helper, and more hands were at the messenger's cost [DB]. In 793 the port opposite is Quentovic, which struck coin until 980 [repo economy]; Wissant is in no document of its own time before the later 11th century [enwp Wissant]. The short crossing ran most of the year [repo seasons].
- **Toll.** The canons held "a moiety of the toll of the port", by their tradition from Wihtred [VCH]; Domesday gives them a half share [DB]. No rate is recorded. A burgess who lived in the town and paid the king's custom was quit of toll through all England [DB].
- **Ship service.** Twenty ships once a year for fifteen days, twenty-one men in each, because the king had given the burgesses sake and soke [DB]: King Edward's time, not 793. The Cinque Ports league is thought to begin in his reign; its first common charter is of 1260 [enwp Cinque].
- **The king's peace** lay on the town from Michaelmas (29 September) to St Andrew (30 November) [DB]: usually read as the herring season (memory) (?). The portsmen later sailed each autumn to the Yarmouth herring fair [enwp Cinque].
- **Coin.** In 793 Offa's broad penny [repo economy]; Dover's own mint by about 950 [DM Saxon].
- **Goods.** Cloth was woven here in the 7th century [KARU]; Frankish things lay in the Buckland graves [enwp Buckland]. For Kent at large: wool in June, cloth and hides out; Rhenish wine, querns, pots and glass in [repo seasons]. None of it is recorded of Dover by name.
- **Ships and fairs.** No ship is found and no fair recorded. In 1052 Godwine took "as many ships and hostages as they chose" here [ASC].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- Before the game: King Eadbald (died 640) sets twenty-two canons in St Mary's chapel "in the castle"; in 696 (Hasted says 691) Wihtred moves them down to St Martin's [VCH; H].
- 796-798: Eadberht Praen's rising; in 798 Cenwulf ravages Kent [repo chronicle].
- 825: Kent passes to Wessex [repo thames].
- 9th century: St Martin's burns and is rebuilt; no cause is recorded [DH Martin].
- 851: the heathen men winter on Thanet and storm Canterbury [repo chronicle]. 865: the army on Thanet overruns east Kent [repo thames].
- 892: 250 ships cross from Boulogne to Appledore, through the strait [repo chronicle].
- By about 950: a mint [DM Saxon].
- 991: Olaf's 93 ships ravage Folkestone and Sandwich [repo chronicle]. Dover is not named.
- About 1000: St Mary in Castro built; the lighthouse made its bell tower [enwp StMary].
- 1009: Thorkell's army lands at Sandwich; east Kent pays 3,000 pounds [repo chronicle].
- 1051 (the Chronicle's E text says 1048): Eustace's men put on mail "some mile or more" short of Dover and demand lodgings. They "slew, as well within as without, more than twenty men. And the townsmen slew nineteen men on the other side." The king orders Godwine to harry Dover; he refuses, raises men and is driven out [ASC; enwp Eustace].
- 1052: Godwine and Harold return by sea and take ships and hostages at Dover [ASC].
- 1066, October: William comes from Hastings. While the people are yielding, his men fire the stronghold; he stays eight days adding defences [DM Norman, from William of Poitiers]. "On his first arrival in England, the town itself was burnt" [DB].
- 1067: Eustace helps the men of Kent try for Dover castle, and fails [enwp Eustace].
- 1086: worth £18 before, £40 now, though the reeve pays £54 [DB].

## 6. Faith and custom

- **St Martin** of Tours, feast 11 November: Martinmas, in slaughter month [repo seasons]. Wihtred is said to have built the church to keep a vow to the saint after a victory [enwp Priory; DH Martin]: house tradition. **St Mary** has the hill [VCH].
- **The canons** are clerks, not monks, sharing a common fund worth £61 a year in King Edward's day [VCH].
- **Wihtred's law**, 6 September 695: the church pays no tax and prays for the king; penalties for gifts to idols and for meat in a fast; a lord who makes his slave work on the Sabbath loses him; a stranger who leaves the track and neither shouts nor blows his horn may be killed or taken [enwp Wihtred]. So the old gods still had offerings in Kent a century before the game. No shrine is known at Dover.
- **Burial**: with weapons and brooches at Buckland in the old days [DM Saxon]; later round the hill church [enwp StMary].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- Hasted's Domesday has one "Sigerius" break the king's peace. The Latin is "si quis", "if anyone" (memory) (?). He never lived.
- "In the entrance of the port of Dovere, there is one mill, which damages almost every ship, by the great swell of the sea" [DB]. Built after 1066.
- A lighthouse that became a bell tower; its fellow became the Devil's Drop of Mortar.
- Six metres under the town lies an oak boat sewn with yew, of 1575-1520 BC, found in 1992 [enwp Boat].
- Hasted boasts that the Danes "never once" dared land under the castle [H]: a boast, not a record.

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- That either lighthouse was lit, or that pilgrims or Æthelheard crossed here: Dover as the crossing is first on record in Domesday.
- A market, fair, toll rate, quay, reeve or named person of 793. The table's kind "market" is a guess; "minster and landing" is what the ground shows.
- A raid on Dover. None is recorded; the 9th-century fire has no cause, and the chronicle table's 991 entry is Folkestone.
- Eadbald's canons and Wihtred's vow as fact: they come from the priory's late registers.
- From the Dover Historian, all unsourced: Wihtred's sea wall, a charter of 1041 and twenty wards, Godwine as "Lord Protector", Harold's stone keep, seven dead in 1051, Alfred's fleet. Leave them out.
- That Alfred the atheling landed here in 1036 (Wikipedia says Sussex), or that the Dour's mill of 762 [enwp Dour] was Dover's.
- Any moneyer's name until checked; the words "Cinque Ports" before 1066; Wissant before about 1050.
- The Dover Museum's William of Poitiers is a modern translation: do not quote it. Hasted (1800) is safe; the Avalon Chronicle is Ingram's and Giles's (memory) (?).
