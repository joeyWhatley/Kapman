# Hleiðr (Lejre, Zealand): dossier

Port `hleidr`: Hleiðr, modern Lejre (the village of Gammel Lejre), 55.605 N 11.97 E; realm danes, region danish_isles, kind royal seat, landing beach, size 2 [ports_793.json]. Researched 6 Oct 2026. Source keys are spelled out in section 9. (?) marks what I could not check; web search was shut for this run, so several details stand on memory and are marked.

**Read this first.** Lejre is two things that must be kept apart. The ground gives great halls, graves and silver from about 500 to about 1000, with no names. The texts give names (Ro, Helgi, Rolf Kraki) and are all written after 1012, most after 1150. No text of 793-1066 written by a Dane names Lejre, and no dated event of those years is recorded there.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What it is.** A great man's seat, "a center of power and likely a royal seat" for more than 500 years [LM]: a hall with its houses on a rise, mounds and a grave field round it. Not a town and not a market [daW; C (?)].
- **Who rules.** Unknown. The king of the Danes named in Frankish annals in these years is Sigfred, then Godfred (d. 810) [task brief; ARF (?)]; no source puts either at Lejre. Whether the lord here was the king, a kinsman or a rival is not known.
- **Who lives there.** A household: the lord, his following, craftsmen, farm people, slaves [C (?)]. No count of heads exists. Size 2 is the game's figure.
- **Coming in.** A skipper does not reach the hall by water. He comes from the Kattegat into the Isefjord, then south up Roskilde Fjord, about 42 km long, mostly about 5 m deep, with many small islands and one hole of 29 m [daW Fjord]. The fjord has almost no tide; wind sets the level [repo water]. Its southern end is Lejre Vig [daW Fjord]. Boats lie on the beach there, by Gevninge, a settlement of 500-1000 read as "the Port of Lejre", an outpost that anyone bound for the hall had to pass [GH]. (English Wikipedia says Gevninge is three kilometres "upriver from Lejre"; it lies downstream, towards the fjord (?).) From the beach it is a walk or a ride of some kilometres up the stream valley to the hall [repo water; (?) for the distance]. Whether a small boat could be poled up the stream is not known.
- **Landmarks.** Old mounds on the skyline, most of them Bronze Age, and Stone Age chamber graves, already ancient in 793 [enW Lejre]. The great mound Grydehøj [daW]. The hall roof itself.

## 2. What stood there

- **The hall at Mysselhøjgård** (the one standing in 793). A run of halls on one spot from the mid 600s to about 1000; five halls counted [daW]. The largest is 61 m long and 12 m wide, called the largest known royal hall in Denmark [daW]. Dug 1986-1988 by Tom Christensen of Roskilde Museum; wood from one hall radiocarbon-dated to about 880, built over an older hall of about 680; the halls about 50 m long by that account [enW Heorot]. The two accounts differ on length: give no figure as exact. Lesser houses, a fence and a smithy stood round it (?) [C].
- **The stone heap.** A great heap of fire-cracked stones with animal bone beside the hall, which the excavator read as a place of cooking for feasts or offering (a "hørg") (?) [C; repo gods_cult lists Lejre among halls with a holy yard].
- **The older halls at Fredshøj**, a little north: two halls of the 500s and 600s [daW]; one marked out at 48 by 11 m, of the 500s, found 1997 [daW]; dug 2004-2005 [enW Heorot]. Gone by 793; a grassed rise.
- **Grydehøj.** A great cremation mound of the 600s with a 22 m layer of ash at its base and gold thread among the remains [daW; enW Lejre]. In 793 it is a hundred years old and somebody's ancestor.
- **The grave field** east of the village: 49 earth graves and 6 cremation graves of the 900s [daW]. One double grave: a man buried with gifts, and above him a beheaded man, read as a slave killed to follow his master [daW; repo gods_cult]. After 793: use from about 900.
- **The ship settings.** Stones set in the outline of ships on the grave field. At least four once; two survive with 33 stones; the largest was 70-80 m long and 15-18 m wide [daW]. Viking Age [enW Lejre]; the exact date is not given in what I read, so do not show them in 793 as certain.
- **Older mounds** called now Hyldehøj, Ravnshøj, Mysselhøj (?), and one called Harald Hildetand's mound, which is a Stone Age chamber grave [enW Lejre]. The names are late.
- **Gevninge**, the landing: a farmstead dug in 2000 [GH].
- **Not there:** no church, no wall, no mint, no quay, no market place is known at any date before 1066.

## 3. Who could be met

- **No historical person can be placed at Lejre between 793 and 1066.** Not one.
- **The legendary kings** (all set by the stories in the 500s, long dead in 793, and a matter for song, not meeting): Ro (Hrothgar), who shared the rule with his brother Helgi, "Ro taking the land and Helghe the water", and was buried at Lejre [CL via enW Hroðgar]; Rolf Kraki, Helgi's son, open-handed, killed in his hall at Lejre by Hiartuar, husband of his sister Skuld, and avenged by Wigg [CL, Saxo via enW Hrólfr]; Harald Wartooth, who took "the traditional royal center in Lejre" [enW Harald].
- **Kings of the Danes in the years**, none tied to the place: Sigfred, Godfred (d. 810), Hemming, Harald Klak, Horik [task brief; ARF (?)]; Gnupa, baptised 934 [repo realms/denmark.json]; Harald Bluetooth, who founded Roskilde in the 980s [enW Roskilde]; Cnut, who had Earl Ulf killed in Trinity church at Roskilde at Christmas 1026 [enW Ulf, from the saga of St Olaf, late].
- **Ordinary people the ground shows:** a lord and men who wore gilded helmets (the makers of the 600s-700s) [daW; GH]; smiths and workers in fine metal (?) [C]; a well-buried man and his beheaded slave (900s) [daW]; a watch at the landing [GH, an interpretation]; whoever owned four silver vessels and a weight and hid them at the end of the 900s [daW].

## 4. What was traded

- **No market, toll, law or fair is recorded.** Goods came to a hall as gifts, dues and plunder as much as by sale; that is the general reading of such seats, not a Lejre record (?) [C].
- **What the finds show came in:** silver (the hoard found 1850: four silver vessels, a weight, a silver disc, perhaps a whetstone, 44 beads, in a clay pot, buried at the end of the 900s; now in the National Museum) [daW]; gold (22 pieces and a copy of a coin of Justinian, 70 g, found 2011; older than our years) [daW]; gold thread [daW]; glass beads [daW]. Where the silver vessels were made I could not check; one is said to be Insular (?).
- **Coins and weights.** Silver by weight; the hoard holds a weight [daW]. No coin was struck here. The game's rule holds: no coinage in Scandinavia before about 825 [BRIEF].
- **What a merchant might bring** (inference from the kind of place, not evidence): wine, glass, good cloth, weapons, quern stones; and take away hides, furs, slaves. Mark every such line as inference.
- **Ships.** The Skuldelev ships, sunk in the fjord and built about 1030-1042, show what came up it by then: a knarr from western Norway (15.8 m, 20 tons), a small Danish coaster (14 m, 4-5 tons), a 30 m warship built in Dublin in 1042 [SK]. None is from 793.

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

Dates from the ground are round; no chronicle gives Lejre a year except 934.

- **About 680.** The first hall at Mysselhøjgård [enW Heorot]. Background.
- **Late 600s to mid 700s.** A gilded bronze eyebrow from a splendid helmet, like those of Valsgärde and Sutton Hoo (found 2024) [daW].
- **793.** The hall stands and is lived in; nothing is recorded.
- **About 880.** The hall rebuilt on the same spot (radiocarbon) [enW Heorot].
- **About 900.** The small silver figure on a throne is made (section 7) [OL].
- **900s.** The grave field east of the hall in use: 55 graves [daW].
- **934.** Henry of Germany defeats the Danes; Thietmar, writing eighty years on, says "our king" then forbade the nine-yearly sacrifice at Lejre [repo realms/denmark.json; Th; Blót]. Hearsay.
- **980s.** Harald Bluetooth founds Roskilde, 10 km (?) east, with a wooden Trinity church and a king's house; no trace of either has been found [enW Roskilde].
- **End of the 900s.** The silver hoard is buried [daW].
- **About 1000.** The last hall goes out of use; the seat ends [daW; enW Lejre].
- **1012-1018 (?).** Thietmar writes of Lejre as a thing past [Blót; Th].
- **1020.** Cnut makes Roskilde a bishop's seat [enW Roskilde].
- **1026, Christmas.** Earl Ulf killed in Trinity church, Roskilde [enW Ulf].
- **About 1030-1042.** The Skuldelev ships are built; they were sunk later to bar the fjord at Skuldelev [SK]. The date of the sinking was not on the page; about 1070 (?).
- **After our years:** about 1170 a clerk at Roskilde writes the Chronicle of Lejre [CL].

## 6. Faith and custom

- **Heathen to the end.** No church is known at Lejre in our years; the church came to Roskilde [enW Roskilde].
- **The great sacrifice.** Thietmar of Merseburg (I.17): at Lederun, the chief place of the kingdom, every ninth year in January, after the day of the Lord's appearing (6 January), they offered "ninety-nine human beings and as many horses, along with dogs and cocks" [Th via Blót]. He is a German bishop writing by hearsay to show the Danes as savage; the numbers may be inflated and the picture borrowed from books [Blót]. No dig has confirmed it [Blót].
- **Burial.** Cremation under a great mound in the 600s; in the 900s mostly unburnt burial in the earth, a few cremations, ship-shaped stone settings, and one killed companion [daW].
- **Feasting.** The hall and the heap of cooking stones are the evidence (?) [C].
- **The law.** Nothing written. An assembly is not attested here.

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **Heorot.** Beowulf never names Lejre, and no Scandinavian source names Heorot [enW Heorot]. But the poem's Hrothgar and Hrothulf are the Ro and Rolf whom Danish writers seat at Lejre, and many scholars place Heorot here "with fair confidence" [enW Heorot]. The halls dug since 1986 are of the right kind; the one of the 500s fits the story's time. Keep it a quiet echo.
- **The watchman.** In the poem a mounted coast-guard challenges the strangers' ship; the outpost at Gevninge, with its piece of a gilded helmet (eye-piece, 8 by 5 cm, 550-700, found 2000), is the real thing of that kind [GH].
- **Kraki.** Rolf got his by-name when a poor lad, Wigg, said the famous king looked like a thin pole (kraki); Rolf gave him a ring for the name, and Wigg swore to avenge him, and did [Saxo, Snorri via enW Hrólfr]. Gold is "Kraki's seed" because he sowed it on the plain at Uppsala to slow the men chasing him [Snorri via enW Hrólfr]. Texts of about 1200 and later.
- **The dog king.** The Chronicle of Lejre says the Danes once had a dog, Raka, for king [CL]. It also hates all things German "in epic proportions" [CL]. Written about 1170: nobody in 793 can quote it, though the tale may be old.
- **Ro and the town.** Ro is said to have founded Roskilde and named it for himself [CL, Saxo via enW Hroðgar]. In fact the town is of the 980s [enW Roskilde].
- **The little figure.** Cast silver with niello and gilding, 18 mm high, 9 g, about 900, found 2 September 2009 by Tommy Olesen: a person on a throne with two birds on the arm-rests. Called Odin with his ravens, but the dress, with apron and four strings of beads, is a woman's; Frigg or Freyja have been proposed [OL].
- **Mannerup,** about 8 km south: more than 3,000 pieces of cut silver, far older than our years (found 2012) [daW].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Who held Lejre in any year from 793 to 1066. Do not seat Sigfred, Godfred, Horik, Gorm or Harald there as fact.
- Do not say "Skjöldung kings ruled here in 793" as history. The Skjöldungs are legend; people of 793 may have told of them, and that is all. The port table's note ("The Skjöldung kings' hall") is tradition, not record.
- Do not state Thietmar's numbers as fact, and never show the sacrifice as seen after 934.
- Do not call the hall Heorot, or let anyone at Lejre have heard of Beowulf.
- Do not give Lejre a market, a toll, a mint, a quay, a wall, a church or a thing-place.
- Do not bring a ship to the hall door. The landing is on the fjord; the way up is by land.
- Do not show Roskilde before about 980, or Lejre as a living seat after about 1000.
- Do not put the Skuldelev barrier, the silver hoard or the 900s graves into 793.
- The hall's length (50 m or 61 m), the date of the ship settings, the stone heap's meaning and the sex of the silver figure are open.
- Ice, the fjord's channels in 793 and the walking distance are the game's estimates [repo water].
