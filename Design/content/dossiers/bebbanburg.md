# Bebbanburg (Bamburgh): dossier

Port `bebbanburg`: Bebbanburg, modern Bamburgh, 55.609 N 1.710 W; realm northumbria, kind royal seat, landing beach, size 2, no mint [ports_793.json; roles]. Researched 4 Oct 2026. Source keys are in section 9. (?) marks what I could not check; (memory) is known to me but not opened.

**Read this first.** A king's rock, not a market: no text or dig shows a merchant, a toll or a fair. Between the annal of 774 and a death in 913 I found no text that names it, so 793 rests on that annal and the ground. Its named people come late: the house of Eadwulf, 913 to 1067.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What it is.** Bede's "royal city, which has taken its name from Bebba, one of its former queens" [Bede III.6]; the Britons' Dynguoaroy before her [Nennius 63]. In 774: "exceedingly well fortified, but by no means large, containing about the space of two or three fields, having one hollowed entrance ascending in a wonderful manner by steps" [HR 774]. That is the only measure of its day; today's castle covers 9 acres of a black dolerite crag, 45 m above the sea [BC; enwp Castle].
- **Who rules.** King Æthelred, restored 790, killed 18 April 796; Osbald for 27 days; Eardwulf from 14 May 796 [repo chronicle]. No source puts a king on the rock in 793, and no keeper is named before 913.
- **Who lives there.** By the ground: wardens in a hall at the gate, smiths, the priests of the hilltop church [BRP Gate; BRP West; HR 774]. No number is known.
- **Coming in.** The rock stands on the shore [repo water]. Off it lie the Farnes: 15 to 20 dolerite islands by the state of the tide, the highest 19 m [enwp Farne], with races and reefs [repo water]; Bede puts Farne "nearly two miles from the city" [Bede III.16]. Holy Island is 8.8 km north-west (computed from the table's coordinates), over flats that flood fast. Tides about 2.7-3 m at neaps, 4.2 m at springs (estimate); sea fog April to July [repo water].
- **Where boats lie.** Not recorded for 793. North of the gate was "a tidal inlet, now cut off from the sea by the developing sand dunes", a harbour for shallow craft, with ships recorded early in the 13th century, before trade moved to Budle Bay [BRP Gate], the mile-wide bay to the north-west [enwp Budle]. The table's "beach" fits.
- **The way up.** St Oswald's Gate, a cleft at the rock's lowest point: 3.4 m wide at the top, 1.6 m at the foot, about 20 m above the sea, the ground outside at 12 m; "the bedrock was worn smooth" [BRP Gate; BRP West].

## 2. What stood there

- **Defences.** Ida "built Bamburgh-Castle, which was first surrounded with a hedge, and afterwards with a wall" [ASC 547]. Dug: timber defences in post-holes cut into the rock, later replaced in stone [BRP Gate].
- **The gate hall.** Timber, 12 m by 7 m, about 7th century, perhaps the gate wardens'; then a smaller stone hall, out of use by the 11th century [BRP Gate; BRP West].
- **St Peter's church**, on the summit, "of very beautiful architecture, in which is a fair and costly shrine. In this, wrapped in a pall, lies the uncorrupted right hand of St. Oswald" [HR 774]; Bede says a silver case [Bede III.6]. A building about 16 m by 8 m under the 12th-century chapel may be this church [BRP Inner].
- **The well**, "on the west and highest point of this citadel, a well, excavated with extraordinary labour, sweet to drink, and very pure to the sight" [HR 774]: 145 feet through the rock [BC].
- **The workshops** (West Ward): cobbled paths, timber halls, a possible smithy, a stone-lined water channel, and a mortar mixer, so some buildings were of mortared stone. A small stone-footed timber building of the later 9th century held a burnt hoard of stycas; round it lay hammerscale from high-heat welding and scraps of decorated gold [BRP West; BRP 2010].
- **Finds.** The Bamburgh Sword: 7th century, 76 cm, welded of six strands where no other blade of its age has more than four; found 1960, then lost in a suitcase in the excavator's garage until 2001 [enwp Sword]. A second pattern-welded sword [BRP West]; a gold plaque, the Bamburgh Beast [enwp Castle]; a piece of a carved sandstone chair of about 800 [BRP Chair].
- **The king's vill and Aidan's church**, "not far from the city": the wooden church where Aidan died on 31 August 651, and the post he leant on, which fire could not burn [Bede III.17]. The village church, late 12th century with older stonework, shows a beam as Aidan's [enwp Bamburgh].
- **The Bowl Hole grave field**, 300 m south in the dunes: bared by a storm in 1816/17, dug 1998-2007; 91 skeletons [BRP Bowl] (110 or 120 by other counts [enwp Bamburgh; enwp Castle]), mostly 7th and 8th century; some crouched, very few grave goods. Few grew up nearby: they came from western Scotland, Ireland, Scandinavia, even the Mediterranean; tall, robust, with terrible teeth [BRP Bowl].
- **Inner Farne**: the cell of Aidan and of Cuthbert, who died there in 687 [enwp Farne].

## 3. Who could be met

Nobody is named at Bamburgh between 774 and 913.

- **Kings**: Æthelred I (790-796); Eardwulf (796-806, 808-810); Ælfwald II (806-808); Eanred (810-841); Æthelred II (841-844, 844-c. 848); Rædwulf (844); Osberht (c. 848-867) and Ælla, both killed at York on 21 March 867 [enwp Kings].
- **Bishops of Lindisfarne**: Higbald (780-803), Ecgberht (803-821), Heathwred (821-830), Ecgred (830-845), Eanberht (845-854), Eardwulf (from 854; left the island in 875) [enwp Bishops; HR 875].
- **Kings "beyond the Tyne"** under the Danes: Ecgberht (867 to about 873), Ricsige (to 876), Ecgberht II [HR 867, 876].
- **Eadwulf**, died 913: "king of the Northern English" in the Irish annals, "actor of the town called Bamburgh" in Æthelweard, a favourite of King Alfred [enwp Eadwulf].
- **Ealdred**, his son: driven out by Ragnall, fled to Constantine of the Scots; fought at Corbridge, 918; bowed to Edward in 920 and to Æthelstan at Eamont, 12 July 927; dead about 933 [enwp Ealdred; ASC 926]. "Adulf mcEtulfe", died 934 [enwp Rulers].
- **Oswulf**, the first called high-reeve of Bamburgh: charters 946-950; with one Maccus betrayed King Eric in 954; then earl [enwp Oswulf; HR 1072].
- **Eadwulf Yfelcild**, "Evil-child" (charters 968-970) [enwp Evil].
- **Waltheof** (charter of 994): too old to fight the Scots, he "shut himself up in Bebbanburc" [DOD].
- **Uhtred**, his son, earl 1006-1016: married a bishop's daughter, then a rich York man's, then King Æthelred's; killed with forty men by Thurbrand Hold [DOD; enwp Uhtred].
- **Eadwulf Cudel**, "cuttlefish" (1016 to the 1020s); **Ealdred** (to 1038), who killed Thurbrand and was killed by Thurbrand's son Carl; **Eadwulf** (1038-1041), killed by Siward [enwp Cudel; HR 1072].
- **Siward** (1041-1055), married to Ealdred's daughter Ælfflæd; **Tostig** (1055-1065); **Oswulf** son of Eadwulf (1065-1067) [HR 1072; enwp Siward; enwp Oswulf2].
- **Wynegot**, who carried Oswald's arm off to Peterborough between 1006 and 1055 [VCH]; **Swartebrand**, monk of Durham, who had often seen the hand here and died after 1081 (memory) [LDE].
- **Ordinary people the evidence shows**: gate wardens; smiths who weld blades; a goldsmith; mortar-makers; priests of the shrine; men and women born over the sea. No moneyer, merchant or reeve is named.

## 4. What was traded

- **Nothing is recorded**: no market, fair, toll, weight or merchant in any text or report I read. A king's rock lives on food-rents and gifts (memory).
- **Made here**: iron weapons, pattern-welded swords among them; fine gold; copper melted for re-use; mortar [BRP West; BRP 2010].
- **Coin.** The styca, first struck for Æthelred I: base silver, copper from about 837, ended about 855, in use until 867; the king's or archbishop's name on one side, the moneyer's on the other; Northumbria's alone [enwp Styca]. One Bamburgh styca is of good silver, "c. 790 onwards"; another is the moneyer Monne's [BRP Styca]. No northern coin is known after 867.
- **Goods of the country**: wool and hides out; herring August to October; wine, querns, glass and Rhenish pots wanted [repo seasons]. The dig gave glass, worked bone, pottery and much animal bone [BRP West; BRP 2010]. 793 is a famine year [repo chronicle].
- **Ships**: none found. In 774 a king went from here to the Picts; in 796 another took ship from Lindisfarne [HR 774; repo chronicle].
- **A man's price** by the North People's law, in thrymsas: king 30,000; archbishop and ætheling 15,000; bishop and ealdorman 8,000; hold and high-reeve 4,000; thegn 2,000; ceorl 266 [enwp Laga, which prints 200; repo english].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- Before: 750, King Eadberht holds Bishop Cynewulf captive here; 774, King Alhred flees here, then to the Picts [HR 750, 774].
- 793, 8 June: Lindisfarne sacked, within sight of the rock. What the fortress did is not recorded.
- 796: Æthelred killed; Osbald sails to the Picts; Eardwulf king [repo chronicle].
- 800, 24 December: a gale; the sea floods the land [repo chronicle].
- 806-808: Eardwulf driven out and brought back [repo chronicle].
- About 855: the styca ends; later the workshop hoard burns [enwp Styca; BRP 2010].
- 867, 21 March: both kings die at York; Ecgberht reigns "over the Northumbrians beyond the Tyne" [HR 867].
- 875: Halfdan "destroyed all the monasteries"; Cuthbert's people leave Lindisfarne [HR 875].
- 913: Eadwulf dies. 918: Corbridge. 927: Eamont [enwp Eadwulf; enwp Ealdred].
- 954: Eric killed; "the province was committed to earl Osulf" [HR 1072].
- 993: "This year was Bamborough destroyed, and much spoil was there taken" [ASC 993]; the Danes "carried off all that they found in it" [HR 993].
- 995: Cuthbert's see moves to Durham [enwp Bishops].
- 1006: the Scots besiege Durham; Uhtred breaks them [DOD, which dates it 969; enwp Waltheof].
- 1016: Uhtred killed [ASC 1016]. 1018: the men of Bamburgh beaten by the Scots at Carham [HR 1018; enwp Cudel].
- 1038: Ealdred killed [enwp Ealdred2]. 1041: Harthacnut "betrayed Eadulf, under the mask of friendship" [ASC 1041].
- 1061: Malcolm of the Scots ravages the earldom and breaks Cuthbert's peace on Lindisfarne [HR 1061].
- 1065: Tostig driven out; Morcar gives the land beyond the Tyne to Oswulf [HR 1072; enwp Oswulf2].
- After the game: 12 March 1067, Oswulf beheads his rival Copsi at a church door, and dies that autumn on a robber's spear [HR 1072].

## 6. Faith and custom

- **Saints.** Peter has the hilltop church. Oswald, killed 642, feast 5 August: his head went to Lindisfarne, his hands and arms to "his royal city" [Bede III.12; enwp Oswald]. Aidan, 31 August [Bede III.17]. Cuthbert, 20 March [spine].
- **The relic.** At an Easter dinner Oswald sent his food and its silver dish out to the poor, and Aidan took his hand: "May this hand never perish" [Bede III.6, as in enwp Oswald]. Bede does not say where.
- **Burial.** Few grave goods: Christian by the look [BRP Bowl]. Whether the Bowl Hole was still used in 793 is not settled.
- **Sanctuary.** In 750 a king's kinsman, starving, was dragged from Cuthbert's relics and killed; the king shut the bishop up in Bamburgh [HR 750; LDE].
- **The old gods**: nothing is recorded here in the game's years.

## 7. Stories and oddities

- Penda pulled down the villages round about, heaped their beams, wattles and thatch against the rock and lit them. Aidan saw the smoke from Farne and prayed, and the wind turned [Bede III.16, as in enwp Aidan].
- Peterborough built a watch-tower so that a monk could guard the stolen arm at all hours [enwp Oswald].
- Uhtred set the best-looking Scottish heads, "with braided locks", on stakes round Durham; four women washed them, "to each of whom he gave a cow for her trouble" [DOD].
- Cuthbert is said to have made rules in 676 to protect Farne's eider ducks (?); grey seals pup there in late autumn [enwp Farne].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- That the king's men saw the raid of 793 and did not come [spine]: the game's invention. Only the line of sight is real.
- That any king, or Higbald, was at Bamburgh in a given year.
- A market, mint, toll, quay, moneyer or reeve. The diggers floated a mint as a theory only [BRP 2010]. "High-reeve" is a 10th-century title.
- A "king" here after 867, except in Irish annals (913, 934): a "king's gleeman, 871-1066" serves a lord.
- A raid on the rock before 993, or its fall to Halfdan in 875.
- A stone keep, towers or the word "castle"; today's wide dunes.
- Uhtred in Alfred's day: that is a modern novel, not to be copied.
- "Ida the Flamebearer" [BC] and Din Guarie as a capital from 420 [enwp Castle]: unsourced.
- Uhtred at Carham in 1018 [HR] against his death in 1016 [ASC]: the sources disagree.
- Quote only Stevenson's Symeon (1855), Ingram's Chronicle (1823) and Giles's Nennius (1848). The Fordham Bede names no translator (?).
