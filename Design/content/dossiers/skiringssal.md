# Skíringssalr (Kaupang, Tjølling, Vestfold): dossier

Keys in brackets are listed under Sources. (?) marks what I could not check on a page I opened on 6 Oct 2026: no web search was to be had, and the dig reports themselves (Skre 2007, 2008, 2011) were not opened, so what is given from them is from memory and marked. Pages opened were read through a summary tool; numbers that two pages give differently are both given.

## 1. The place in 793

- **Name.** *Skíringssalr* is the hall and its district; *kaupangr* means only "market" (the same word as English Chipping, Swedish köping) [WS; NOD]. *Sciringes heal* in Ohthere, who calls it "a port" [WO]. Farms "in Skirikx Saal" and "Skirisall" stand in Tjølling in papers of the 1400s [NOW].
- **In 793 there is no town.** The hall at Huseby stands (built mid or late 8th century) [WS; NOW]; the thing heath and the holy lake are in use [WS]; the oldest graves at Bikjholberget are put at about 760 [NOW]. The first shore posts of the market are tree-ring dated to about 803, the first buildings to 800-810 [NOW; WS]. English Wikipedia says "780s" [WK]; the game's row opens the port in 780 with a (?). The writer should make 793 a chieftain's landing below a hall, and bring the market in about 800.
- **Rule.** The lord of the hall at Huseby, about 1 km north of the market [SNS]. Over him, in all likelihood, a Danish king: the Frankish annals for 813 call *Westarfolda* the furthest north-west part of the Danish kings' realm [WH]. Dagfinn Skre holds that the market was founded at Danish bidding [NOW]. Which king is not known.
- **People and size.** At its height about 500 people [SNL], or 400 to 600 [NOW], or up to 1,000 [WK]. The town ran about 750 m along the shore and 20 to 90 m back from it [NOW]; about 5.4 ha (?) (the page opened says "54", surely a slip). It was laid out in regular plots with fixed lanes, and lived in the year round [SNL]; Skre found a first few seasons of summer camping before the plots (?).
- **Land and water.** The town lies on Kaupangkilen, an arm of the Viksfjord, about 5 km east of Larvik, sheltered behind the island of Lamøya [NOW]. The shore then stood about 3.5 m higher than today [NOW], so the bay was wider and deeper and Lamøya and the low ground were more cut up by water (?). The way in is among skerries and islands (?); no source opened names a bar, a channel or a mark. Ohthere: south of Sciringes heal "a very great sea" runs up into the land, too broad to see across (?) [OH]. The tide in the Skagerrak is slight (?).
- **Where boats lie.** Two timber jetties with paths were dug; the "stone jetties" once claimed are now read as stone-laid paths across the muddy shore [NOW]. Small craft were drawn up (?).

## 2. What stood there

- **The hall at Huseby** (*Skíringssalr* itself, as Kåre Hoel argued in 1986 [NOW; SNS]). Dug 2000-01: 32 to 35 m long, 9 to 11.5 m wide, with tapering ends, on a platform partly man-made over a levelled grave mound of the 3rd or 4th century; standing from the mid or late 8th century, out of use about 900 [WS; NOW; SNS]. It looks straight down on the town and harbour [NOW].
- **The thing place, Þjóðalyng** ("the people's heath"), now Tjølling. Things were still held there in 1557 [WS]. The stone church on it is of about 1150 [WT]: no church in the game's years.
- **The holy lake** just north of Tjølling, about 1.1 km by 500 m then (now Vittersetjønna and Lilletjønna), ringed by place-names of worship [WS].
- **The town plots.** Small houses with two pairs of roof posts, an earth floor, a hearth in the middle and a door in the gable facing the sea, like the houses of Norse Dublin [NOW]. Blindheim dug five whole buildings and part of a sixth; Skre's team four more possible houses, hearths and pits [NOW; WK]. Fenced plots (?). One house is read as a Frisian trading household of the 9th century [NOR, citing Skre 2010] (?).
- **Six wells** and store pits, some wattle-lined [NOW].
- **Workshops.** Bronze casting (crucibles, brass and lead bars, clay moulds), a jeweller's work, lead casting, glass-bead making, cloth-making, bone and antler work [NOW; SNL]. Amber working and a smithy (?).
- **The grave fields.** Perhaps a thousand graves once [SNL]. *Nordre Kaupang*: barrows along the road north; Nicolaysen recorded 115 mounds in 1867 and opened 71 (79 by other counts), mostly cremations, many of the 10th century [NOW; WK; SNL]. *Bikjholberget*, a rock by the harbour: 74 graves dug 1950-57, about 760 to 940, unmarked, many laid in boats, with full weapon sets, swords and bronze brooches, three of them rich women [NOW]. *Lamøya*: small mounds dug by Gustafson in 1902 [NOW; SNL]. Also Søndre Kaupang, Hagejordet, Bjønnes, Vikingholmen (?).
- **No mint, no church, no wall** is known. Hedeby and Birka had ramparts; none has been found here (?).

## 3. Who could be met

No person is recorded at the market by name. Those tied to the district:

- **Harald (Klak) and Reginfrid**, joint kings of the Danes, who took an army to Westarfolda in 813 because "its princes and people refused to submit to them" [WH, from the Royal Frankish Annals]. Those unnamed princes of Vestfold are the nearest thing to a named lord of the hall.
- **Godfred**, king of the Danes (died 810), who moved Reric's merchants to Sliesthorp in 808: a possible founder, by Skre's reasoning, but no text says so (?).
- **Ohthere (Ottar)** of Hålogaland, who came down the coast and told King Alfred of it about 890 [WO]. A rich man by northern measure: 20 cattle, 20 sheep, 20 swine, horses to plough with, and the Finnas' tribute of marten, reindeer and bear skins, feathers, and ship ropes of whale and seal hide 60 ells long [WO].
- **Halfdan Whiteleg**, an Yngling king of legend, who "died in Toten and was buried in a mound at Skiringssal" (*Ynglingatal*) [WS; WHH]; the saga names the spot Skereid (?). Dead before the game begins.
- **Halfdan the Black** (9th century): his head was buried at Skiringssal, says *Fagrskinna*, a 13th-century book [WS]. Late.
- **Þjóðólfr of Hvinir**, poet of *Ynglingatal*, by tradition Harald Fairhair's skald [WY]. Not placed here by any source.
- **Harald Grenske**, king in Vestfold 976-987 [WV], after the market's end.

Ordinary people the digs show: bead-makers, bronze casters and jewellers, weavers, comb-makers, men who weighed silver; foreign traders, some Frisian (?); women buried with rank; a merchant and craft people unlike the farmers around them [NOW; SNL].

## 4. What was traded

- **Out.** Iron, soapstone pots, perhaps fish [WK]; whetstones of Eidsborg schist from upper Telemark, at Kaupang from about 800 [repo ECO] (?); and the north's goods come down the coast: furs, walrus tooth, hide rope, down [WO].
- **In.** Pottery from the Rhineland, Frisia and the Slav shore of the Baltic [SNL]; glass and beads by the hundred [WK]; Frankish and English metalwork (?); carnelian and rock-crystal beads from the east (?); grain and malt (?).
- **Silver.** Weighed, not counted. Islamic dirhams, mostly cut, and Frankish deniers; scales; lead weights [SNL; WK]. About a hundred coins and some four hundred weights (?) [repo SIL]; the turn from barter to weighed silver falls about 850-875 (?). A gold coin from Dorestad [WK].
- **Tolls and law.** Nothing is recorded. The thing on the heath would have judged (?).
- **Seasons and ships.** Ohthere: from Hålogaland one could not sail here in a month, camping at night, with a fair wind each day; from here to Hedeby five days, three with Denmark to port and open sea to starboard, two among the Danish islands [WO; OH]. So northern cargo arrives in high summer [repo SEA]. Clinker-built cargo ships: the Klåstad wreck, 20 to 22 m long, 4.5 m broad, oak with pine top strakes [WKL].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- about 750-800: the hall at Huseby built [WS; NOW].
- about 760: first graves at Bikjholberget [NOW].
- about 800-803: the market founded; shore posts felled about 803 [NOW].
- 800-810: the first buildings; plots laid out [WS; SNL].
- 808: Godfred moves Reric's merchants to Sliesthorp, the next market south [repo row `sliesthorp`].
- 813: Harald and Reginfrid lead the Danish host into Vestfold against its princes; the first time Vestfold is written down [WH; WV].
- 834: the Oseberg ship burial, further up the Vestfold coast [WV].
- about 850: the latest buildings that survive the plough; later layers are gone [WS].
- about 890: Ohthere tells Alfred of the port [WO].
- about 900: the hall at Huseby goes out of use [WS]; the Gokstad burial, near by [repo NOR] (?).
- about 930: the town given up [NOW]; "early 10th century" [WK].
- about 950-970: some activity again [NOW]; then none. The game closes the port in 960.
- 998: the Klåstad ship built; she sank in Klåstadkilen, the next inlet, with Eidsborg whetstones aboard [WKL].
- about 1050-1150: a large building at Huseby, perhaps 11th century [NOW]; the stone church at Tjølling about 1150 [WT].

## 6. Faith and custom

- Heathen throughout. No church, priest or mission is recorded [all pages opened].
- Burial is the best evidence: cremation under mounds at Nordre Kaupang; unburnt burial in boats and in flat graves at Bikjholberget, with weapons, tools and brooches [SNL; NOW].
- A yearly sacrifice at Skiringssal, attended by all of Viken, "in the time of Sigurd Hring": so says *Sögubrot*, a late and legendary text [WS]. Colour, not fact.
- The holy lake and the names round it [WS]. Which gods, no source says.
- Law was spoken at the thing; nothing of its content survives from these years (?).

## 7. Stories and oddities

- An amulet of amber carved as a foot [SNL].
- A boat grave at Bikjholberget with a man, a woman and a second woman seated at the stern with a staff, read as a seeress (?).
- The whetstone ship of 998, lost within sight of the dead town [WKL].
- The name's first half defeats the scholars: "bright", "clear water", a god; Stefan Brink calls every reading "practically impossible" [WS]. Hoel: "the shining hall" [NOW].
- The town was lost so wholly that men argued over where Skiringssal lay until P. A. Munch placed it in 1850 and Blindheim dug it in the 1950s [SNL; NOW].
- About 100,000 finds in all [WK].
- The houses are like Dublin's [NOW]: the same people built both (?).

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- That Sciringes heal is Kaupang: most hold it, Birgisson and Masdalen deny it [WS; WK].
- Who founded the market, or that Godfred did. No king is recorded here in person.
- That the Ynglings ruled here: poem and saga, written or shaped later.
- Any name of a trader, craftsman or chieftain; any toll, law or fair day.
- A market before about 800. A church, mint or rampart at any date.
- Why it ended: the silting bay (Blindheim) or the ebb of Danish power (Skre) [NOW].
- The bar, channel and sea-marks. The shore of 793 is 3.5 m above today's: do not draw the modern bay.
- The numbers marked (?), above all the coin and weight counts and the area.
