# Winaldum (Wijnaldum): dossier

Port `wijnaldum`: Winaldum, now Wijnaldum (Frisian Winaam), 53.21 N 5.46 E; franks, westrachia, frisia, royal seat, beach, size 2, years 0 to 850 [ports]. Researched 4 October 2026. Source keys are in section 9; (?) marks what I could not check. The row's kind, end year and name do not fit the evidence (section 8).

## 1. The place in 793

- **What.** Seven terps (house mounds built of sods) in a row over 2 km long on a low salt-marsh ridge in northern Westergo [JALC; fywp; PA91]. One has been dug: Tjitsma, east of the present village (1991-1993, the centre only) [PA91; RIV]. All buildings and finds below are from it.
- **Not a king's seat in 793.** The gold is 7th-century: a jeweller's workshop and a garnet brooch of about 630 [PA92; JALC]. Even for then the excavators claimed only a chieftain of more than local weight [PA91]. In 793 the terp is in its Carolingian phase (750-850), still rich in imports, with houses like any terp's [RIV; GLS; JALC].
- **Who rules.** The Frankish king, since Charles Martel's victory on the Boorne in 734 won the land between Vlie and Lauwers [nlwp Boorne]. Westergo is a gouw of the realm, "Westracia" in the annals [nlwp Westergo; AX 846]. There is no Frisian king [wording]. No count is named (?).
- **Who lives there.** In the dug part always one farmstead: a main house, outbuildings and sunken huts, a leading family with its dependants [PA91; PA92]. Numbers are unknown. The mound is about 2 m high today, ploughed below its 7th-century surface [fywp Tsjitsma; PA91].
- **Coming in.** Westergo is one wide salt marsh without dikes, flooded at times [JALC; FCT2]. From Dorestad: the Vecht, the Almere, then the Vlie ("Fli" about 800), the law's border between West and Middle Frisia [traiectum; nlwp Vlie]. A tidal channel north of the terps was shut by a marsh ridge about 500, but the Ried creek may have kept an opening to the sea, perhaps a dug one [RIV, after Vos 1999]. The tide at Harlingen, 4 km south-west (my measure), is about 2 m today and the flats dry [water]. No bar, sea-mark, jetty or landing is known: the waterside was never dug [RIV].

## 2. What stood there

- **The terp Tjitsma.** Begun in the 2nd century as a sod platform; empty about 350-425; eight periods, the last 850-900/950 [PA91; RIV; JALC].
- **Sod houses**, walls of stacked turf, in every period [PA92; MIG].
- **Sunken huts**, small dug-in sheds, until about 850; loom weights and spindle whorls gather in them [JALC; PA92].
- **Refuse pits**, the mark of 750-850 [JALC].
- **Wells**, mostly 850-950; a baby's grave was later cut through one [JALC; BAB].
- **Smithy.** A floor of hammer-scale, scrap and charcoal, dated 136-386 [SMI]; iron was forged from bars and scrap into the 10th century [RIV].
- **Jeweller's workshop** (6th-7th century): gold drops, melted silver, ingots, touchstones, a die for patterned gold foil [PA92; BN97]. By 793 a memory.
- **Bead-making**: a crucible with yellow glass, a yellow mosaic cube, many yellow beads; Merovingian and Carolingian [GLS; JALC].
- **The dead.** No grave field is known [PA91]. Found: an early 6th-century urn with an adult (?) and a child, beside the terp [CRE]; two skulls with the brooch; three newborns, two of them 8th-century or later [BAB]; a woman of about 475 [TL].
- **The village terp**, under the present church: a rune-cut antler piece, found 1914 [RUN]. St Andrew's is of about 1500; nothing older is recorded [nlwp Andreas].
- **Not found:** church, churchyard, hall, wall, market, mint, toll, jetty, shipyard.

## 3. Who could be met

No one is named at Wijnaldum in these years [nlwp Wijnaldum]. These are tied to it by lordship or district only.

- Charlemagne, king 768-814: has the Frisians' law written about 802 [enwp Lex; chron].
- Wlemar and Saxmund, the "wise men" whose judgments are added to it; nothing else is known of them [Lex; enwp Lex].
- Bishops of Utrecht, from Hamacar (about 790-806) to Hunger (about 854-866) [dossier medemelacha]. That Westergo was theirs is unchecked (?).
- Rorik the Dane: Dorestad "and other counties" from 850; driven from Frisia in 867 by the people "newly called Cokingi" [chron; AB 867]. No text gives him Westergo.
- Rodulf the Northman: in June 873 he demands tribute in Count Albdag's Oostergo and falls with 500 or 800 men; the Frisians' leader was a Northman turned Christian [AF 873; AB 873; enwp Rodulf; enwp Albdag].
- Godfrid the Dane: Frisia in fief 882, killed 885 [chron].
- Liudolf of Brunswick, count of Westergo 1006-1038; Bruno, 1038-1057, whose pennies were struck at Bolsward, Dokkum, Leeuwarden and Stavoren [nlwp Midden-Friesland; nlwp Bruno].

Ordinary people the finds show: a resident smith [PA92]; goldsmiths and comb-sellers who came and went [JALC]; a bead-maker [GLS]; women spinning and weaving [PA92; Lex Add. II 24]; net-menders and fishers [JALC]; keepers of sheep, cattle and horses (bones: sheep 2,881, cattle 1,601, horse 86) [JALC table 3]; a player of strings (an antler tuning pin); dicers; skaters on bone [JALC].

## 4. What was traded

- **In.** Rhineland and Eifel pottery, an unusually high share [PA91]. Rhenish glass: thirteen Carolingian cups and funnel beakers [GLS]. Iron bars of Scandinavian kinds [RIV]. Red deer and elk antler [JALC]. Raw amber, knapped into beads here [PA92]. A silver dirham in a mount, undated [PA92].
- **Out.** Probably wool cloth, though loom weights do not prove export [PA92]. Fulda drew 855 Frisian cloaks a year about 830 [FCT3]; Werden's Frisian lands owed 1,000 cloths (?) [PA92]. By the bones also sheep, cattle, horses bred here, and fish [JALC; BAB].
- **Coin.** In 1991 alone: three 7th-century gold coins, seven sceattas, fifteen to twenty Carolingian pennies [PA91]. Series D sceattas (about 700) were "minted in Friesland, possibly at Wijnaldum" [JMP]. Lothar I's Dorestad pennies were copied on this coast long after his death: 2,600 or more lay in a hoard at Tzummarum, 7 km north-east (my measure) [DON; nlwp Tzummarum].
- **Law.** Between Lauwers and Vlie three new pennies make a solidus; a noble's wergild is 80 solidi, a free man's 53 and a penny [Lex I]. Stealing a horse or ox, or breaking into a weaving hut (screona): death, or buy back your life [Lex Add. I 3]. Blocking a public way on a river: 12 solidi [Lex Add. VI].
- **Toll, fairs, seasons.** None recorded.
- **Ships.** 81 clench nails and roves in layers of the 5th to 9th century: lapstrake planking, from repairs or old ship timber burnt. The authors hold that Frisians then sailed clinker boats, not Utrecht-type hulls [RIV].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 734: the Franks take Westergo and burn the shrines [nlwp Boorne].
- 750-850: the Carolingian phase on the terp [RIV; JALC].
- about 802: the Lex Frisionum is written down [enwp Lex; chron].
- 810: 200 Danish ships on the Frisian islands; 100 pounds of silver paid [chron].
- 26 December 838: storm flood, 2,437 drowned in Frisia [chron].
- 845: Frisians are said to have killed over twelve thousand heathens [AX 845].
- 846: "Norhtmanni Ostraciam et Westraciam vastaverunt": the one annal that names Westergo [AX 846]. Danes take tribute and hold nearly the whole province [AB 846].
- 850: Rorik is given Dorestad and other counties [chron].
- 852: 252 ships take payment in Frisia and leave [AB 852].
- 850-900/950: the last phase: wells, skates, no more amulets [RIV; JALC].
- 867: the Cokingi drive Rorik out [AB 867].
- June 873: Rodulf killed in Oostergo [AF 873; AB 873].
- later 9th century (?): the Tzummarum hoard is buried [DON].
- 900-950: the dug part of Tjitsma falls out of use; but younger layers are ploughed away and the mound grew to 2.5 (?) or 5 ha by the 11th century [JALC; PA91].
- 10th century: a ring dike is built round Pingjum, 9 km south (my measure) [nlwp Pingjum].
- 1006: Liudolf of Brunswick holds Westergo [nlwp Midden-Friesland].
- 1275: "Wynaldum" first written [fywp; nlwp Wijnaldum].

## 6. Faith and custom

- **Christian by law.** Sunday work costs 4 solidi; a man in feud has peace in church; lots are laid on the altar when a man is killed in a crowd [Lex XVIII, Add. I 1, XIIII].
- **The old gods in the same law.** "Qui fanum effregerit ... ducitur ad mare, et in sabulo, quod accessus maris operire solet, finduntur aures eius, et castratur, et immolatur diis quorum templa violavit": the robber of a shrine is led to the sand the tide covers, his ears are slit, he is gelded and given to the gods [Lex Add. XI].
- **Newborns.** The law lets a mother kill a child fresh from the womb without payment [Lex V]. Three were buried on the terp; the excavators will not call them sacrifices [BAB].
- **Amulets** (pendants, worn knucklebones) stop after 850, which the specialists link to Christianity taking hold [JALC].
- **Burial and church.** Where the dead of 793 lie is unknown [PA91]. No church, saint or feast is recorded before the late-medieval St Andrew's [nlwp Andreas].
- **Woden.** The brooch shows him between two wolves or bears [nlwp Fibula; PA01].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **The brooch.** In 1953 a farmer found the foot plate in a heap of clay; it lay on his sister's window sill until the family doctor saw what it was [FCT1]. 17 cm long, over 300 garnets, traced to India in 2012; the last piece turned up in 2009 [FCT1; nlwp Fibula].
- **Finn.** Its publisher of 1990-1991 called it "Finn's fibula", after the Frisian king of Beowulf and Widsith; nothing ties Finn to the place [PA91; enwp Finn; FCT2].
- **Audulf.** Gold coins of about 600 read AVDVLFVS FRISIA [enwp Audulf]; a die naming him is said to have been found here in 2006 (?) [fywp Tsjitsma].
- **Runes.** A gold pendant of about 600 is read hiwi, "mother of the household", or as a name [RUN].
- **The law's three craftsmen.** Hurt the hand of a harper, a goldsmith or a woman who weaves fresum and you pay a quarter more [Lex Add. II 23-24]. All three crafts are in the finds [PA92; JALC].
- **Small things of 750-850:** an unfinished flute of sheep's shin; a little bone box; a porpoise's skull and jaw [JALC; WHA]. Fifteen enamelled disc brooches 11 mm across, a Carolingian-Ottonian type [FIB].
- **Two unborn foals** buried one on the other, not thrown to the dogs (Migration period) [BAB].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- No king, court, hall or "royal seat" in 793: that reading rests on 7th-century gold. Use "the lord's man", as [wording] says.
- No end in 850: the dug terp was lived on to 900-950 and the village stands today. No raid on Wijnaldum itself is recorded, only on Westergo in 846.
- "Winaldum" is not attested before the 13th century.
- No landing, toll, market, mint, church or graveyard is known, nor whether the Ried was open to the sea. Boats grounding on the creek bank is a guess.
- Unknown: numbers of people and houses; the other six terps; whether ships were built, cloth shipped or goldsmiths settled here; who was count before 1006.
- The babies are not sacrifices; Finn and Audulf are not lords of this terp.
- Not read: Besteman et al. 1999, Nieuwhof 2020, Nicolay and Aalbersberg 2018, which hold the house plans, pottery counts and coin list.
