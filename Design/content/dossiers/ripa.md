# Ripa (Ribe, south-west Jutland): dossier

Keys in brackets: see Sources. (?) = not checked on a page I opened (written from memory of the literature; check before it ships).

## 1. The place in 793

- **Name.** *Ripa* is first written about 870, of events about 855 to 860 [Rim 32]. What the Danes called it in 793 is not recorded. One Ribe archaeologist (Sovso) derives the name from Latin *ripa*, "bank" [Wda].
- **What.** A market on a river bank, laid out in one act and dated by a well timber to between 704 and 710 [Wda; VSM]. In 793 it is about ninety years old and the oldest town of the Danes [Wda]. Not yet ditched: the first boundary ditch is early ninth century [Wen].
- **Rule.** A king is assumed behind the planned plots and the controlled coin; none is named for Ribe [Wda; Wen]. The Franks' annals name Sigfred as a king of the Danes in these years (?) [RD].
- **People.** Craftsmen and traders on plots along the water, Frisian in building, coin and goods [Wda]. Once read as a summer market only [VSM]; newer work shows houses lived in on part of the market from its first decades [Wem]. No count of heads is given.
- **Water.** Ribe river (Ribe A) winds in several arms through flat marsh to the Wadden Sea; ships used it until the late 1600s [Wda]. The modern outlet (Kammerslusen) is about 5 to 6 km below the town (?). Outside lie tidal flats and the islands Mando and Fano (?). Where the shore and the mouth lay in 793 I could not check.
- **Landmarks.** None recorded: low sand at the marsh edge (?).
- **Where boats lie.** Along the market bank: the booths stood in a strip some 400 m long and narrow, on the north side of the river [Wda]. No jetty or quay is reported on the pages I opened.

## 2. What stood there

- **The market strip.** Plots side by side at right angles to the river, on a spread layer of sand, each about 6 to 8 m wide, perhaps 40 to 50 in all (?) [Fev]. Booths and workshops, floor on floor, 8th and 9th centuries [Wda; Wen].
- **The first well**, lined with oak felled 704 to 710; other wells lined with wine casks from the Mainz country [Wda; VSM].
- **Dung layers** under and in the earliest market: cattle stood here, read as a cattle market (about 705 to 725) [Wda; Fev (?)].
- **Bead-makers' floors.** Glass waste, rods, failed beads; mosaic cubes brought as raw glass (?) [Wda; PG].
- **Other floors.** Bronze-casters' clay moulds (buckles); raw amber, mostly unworked; smiths' slag; 117 whetstones, 17 of Eidsborg stone from Norway; hundreds of loom weights and sheep bones [Wda].
- **Comb-makers' floors.** Antler splinters by the thousand [Wda]; some antler is reindeer, so men from Norway were here before 793 (?) [Ash].
- **Heathen grave field** beside the market (the Danish page puts it west of the strip) [Wda]. Cremations and burials of the 8th and 9th centuries (?).
- **The town ditch.** Early 9th century: 2 to 3 m wide, around 12 hectares, a boundary and not a defence. Later in that century a moat 6 to 7 m wide replaced it [Wen; VSM].
- **Anskar's church**, about 855 to 860, timber, on a plot the king gave, with leave for a priest to live there [Rim 32]. Its site is not known; perhaps under the cathedral, on the other bank from the market [Wdk; Wda].
- **Christian graveyard** by the cathedral (Lindegarden), dug 2008 to 2012: graves from the 9th century on, 2,000 to 3,000 in all [Wen; Wda].
- **The offering at Damhus**, south of the town: 262 fresh-struck coins laid down together in empty ground, found 2018, read as a gift to the gods [Wda]. Said to be 9th century; type and date unchecked (?).
- **A mint**, by name, only from Cnut (1018 to 1035) [VSM].

## 3. Who could be met

- **No one is named at Ribe before about 855.** Every person in 793 must be invented.
- **Horik the younger**, king from 854, last heard of 864: gave the church plot [Rim 32; WH]. Whether he came to Ribe is not said.
- **Anskar**, archbishop of Hamburg-Bremen (died 3 February 865), asked for and got the plot [Rim 32]. That he stood in Ribe is likely, not stated.
- **Burghard**, kinsman and counsellor of both Horiks, spoke for Anskar [Rim 32].
- **The first priest**: allowed, not named [Rim 32]; later tradition gives Rimbert the charge [Wdio] (?).
- **Leofdag (Liafdag)**, first bishop, consecrated by Archbishop Adaldag about 948, at the synod of Ingelheim that year; by his name not a Dane, perhaps a Frisian or German [Wdio; Kno].
- **Odinkar the younger**, bishop about 1000 to 1043, a Dane of great family and wealth, schooled abroad under Cnut (?) [Wdio; Ad II]. Said to have won a promise that the see would stay in his kin [Wda].
- **Folkbert**, bishop 988 to 1000 (?); **Val**, 1043 or 1044 to about 1060; **Odder**, 1060 to 1085; Val and Odder from the Saxon coast [Wdio; Wda].
- **Kings whose coins name Ribe:** Cnut, Harthacnut (1040 to 1042), Sven Estridsson (1047 to 1074) [VSM]. The moneyers' names I could not check.
- **Ordinary people the digs show:** bead-makers, comb-makers, bronze-casters, amber-workers, smiths, weavers, cattle drovers, Frisian skippers, Norwegians with antler and whetstones, a rune-carver [Wda; Wsk].

## 4. What was traded

- **In, from the Rhine and Frisia:** glass (beakers and raw glass), pottery, lava quernstones, wine in casks [VSM; Wda; PG]. Cloth is likely and not proven on the pages I opened (?).
- **In, from the north:** whetstones and reindeer antler from Norway [Wda; Ash (?)]. Amber from the Jutland shore or the Baltic [Wda; FA].
- **Out:** beads, combs, cast bronze, cattle and hides (the dung), goods passed on across Scandinavia [VSM; Wda]. Captives are likely and leave no trace; the Danish page says so plainly [Wda].
- **Coin.** Of some 300 sceattas found in Denmark, 216 (or 218) are from Ribe: nearly all the "Wodan and monster" kind (a bearded facing head, a curled beast), about 1 g of silver, thought struck here from about 720 [Wen; Wda; Wsc]. They lie singly in the floors, so they passed by count inside the market, under somebody's control [Wda]. They ran on into the early 800s (?) [Fev]. From Cnut, pennies with Ribe's name [VSM].
- **Tolls and law.** Nothing written for Ribe. Otto's privileges of 965 and 988 freed the Danish bishops' churches from dues; the bishop's half of the king's income from Ribe is ascribed to Cnut, with no charter [Wda].
- **Season.** The market months are summer [VSM]; some households stayed the year [Wem].
- **Ships and routes.** About 1075 one sailed from Ribe to Frisia, England and Saxony [Ad IV.1], and a note gives a sea road from Ribe by Flanders and England to Acre [Ad sch] (?).

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- **About 790.** Cheap beads from the Caliphate reach the north; Ribe's bead-makers fade (?) [PG].
- **Early 800s.** The boundary ditch, 12 ha [Wen].
- **Early 800s.** The Damhus offering (?) [Wda].
- **854.** Horik the elder and almost all his kin killed; a boy, Horik, is king [Rim 31; WH].
- **About 855 to 860.** The church at Sliaswich shut, then opened, with a bell; a plot and a priest allowed at Ripa, "a second church in Denmark" [Rim 31-32; Ad I.28 in Rob].
- **864.** The pope writes to Horik; he is not heard of again [WH].
- **Later 800s.** The wide moat [Wen].
- **After 888.** The church said to be destroyed in a heathen turn after Rimbert's death (a late claim) (?) [CE].
- **About 900 to 1050.** Little found in the ground; the town may have shrunk or nearly ceased [Wen].
- **948.** Leofdag bishop of Ribe at Ingelheim, with Schleswig and Aarhus [Wda; Wdio].
- **1018 to 1035.** Cnut's pennies struck at Ribe [VSM].
- **1043.** Wends harry Jutland as far as Ribe; King Magnus beats them at Lyrskov heath (?) [RD].
- **1060.** Jutland north of the Kongea divided into four sees; Ribe is one [Wdio].
- **Late 1000s.** The market is busy again [Wen].

## 6. Faith and custom

- **793:** heathen. Odin and Tyr are called on in runes here [Wsk]; the coin is read as Woden's face [Wen]. Coins were given to the gods at Damhus [Wda].
- **Heathen feeling:** the gods are angry at "another and an unknown god"; a bell is unlawful [Rim 31-32].
- **Christians before the church** (told of Sliaswich, not Ribe): traders baptised at Dorestad or Hamburg; many "signed with the cross", keeping baptism for the deathbed [Rim 24].
- **From about 855:** a priest, Mass, Christian burial on the south bank [Rim 32; Wda].
- **Dedication:** the later cathedral is Our Lady's [Wdk]; that of Anskar's church is not recorded.
- **Feasts:** Anskar, 3 February; Leofdag is given the same day [Kno].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **The skull charm.** A piece from the top of a human skull, 6 by 8.5 cm, with a bored hole, found 1973 in market layers, about 725. The runes name "Ulf and Odin and High-Tyr" and ask help against a dwarf and a she-dwarf (older reading: "against this pain"). The hole shows no wear from a cord: it may never have been sold [Wsk].
- **Leofdag at the ford.** The later tale: a king's housecarl ran him through with a spear as he forded the river [Wdio]. Adam of Bremen, who praises him, does not know it; it is probably a later invention [Kno].
- **The saint who would not be made.** In the 1160s Bishop Radulf raised Leofdag's bones to the altar; the church burned at once, and no cult took [Kno].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- No king, reeve, toll, law, priest or trader is named at Ribe before 855. Do not put Sigfred or Godfred in a hall here.
- No hall, mint house, toll house, quay or temple has been found. In 793 there is no church, wall or ditch; the stone cathedral (from 1110 to 1150) and Riberhus castle are later still [Wdk; Wen; Wda].
- Whether the Wodan coins were struck at Ribe is argued, not proven; "minted here" is the common view [Wen; Wsc].
- "The first church in Scandinavia" is wrong: Rimbert makes it the second in Denmark, after Sliaswich; Birka's is earlier too [Rim 32; Wda].
- No raid on Ribe, and no fire, is recorded between 793 and 1042.
- The tenth-century gap may be a gap in digging. Do not say the town died.
- Leofdag's martyrdom, its year (948 or about 980) and the 888 destruction are late tradition.
