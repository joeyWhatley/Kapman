# Lundenwic (London): dossier

Port row `lundenwic`: London (the Strand), mercia, emporium, beach, size 5, no end year. Codes: section 9. (?) = unchecked.

## 1. The place in 793

An open trading town of 55-60 ha on the gravel terrace north of the Thames, Trafalgar Square to Aldwych (1 km) and up to Shorts Gardens (600 m), 1 km upstream of the empty Roman city; no wall [N]. Bede's "mart of many peoples coming by land and sea" [N]. Mercian since about 670; Offa (757-796) holds it [N,Of]. In 811 Coenwulf's council sits "in the famous place and royal town, the wic of London" [S168]. A king's wic-reeve witnesses sales; warranty is vouched "in the wic at the king's hall" (Kentish law, 673-685) [HE]. Dwellers: weavers in almost every house, comb-makers, smiths, horn and leather workers, butchers; Frisians probably run the Rhine wine trade. Past its best: the peak was before 750, and by 793 some plots are rubbish dumps [N].

A skipper waits at the Nore for the flood and rides it 60-70 km up (inferred) [Nm]. No bar, no bridge. To port, marsh and eyots; to starboard the Roman riverside wall, St Paul's on its hill, then the Fleet's tidal mouth, over 90 m wide. Beyond, the town stands on the skyline 13 m above a gravel and cobble beach on the outside of the Charing Cross bend. Ships are run onto the hard at high water (springs about +1.6 m OD) and sit dry at low; larger ones ride off. The bank is short timber fronts 1 m high with mooring posts; trade is done on the shore [N].

## 2. What stood there

- The Strand beach: stake-and-wattle fronts (York Buildings, oak felled 670-690), then oak pile and plank; behind, ground raised to +1.6-2.0 m with gravel yards and a burnt sill-beam house (Adelphi). Found: clench bolts from a large clinker ship broken up there, a silver-fir wine-cask head, 300 kg of Roman tile and ragstone [N].
- The Strand, the Roman road on the bluff top; a 5 m metalled road north from it (Royal Opera House, over 60 buildings); parallel roads under James Street and Bow Street. Thatched wattle-and-daub houses; barrel-lined wells [N].
- Workshops in houses: combs (Bedford Street pit, waste of 65-70 combs, about 730-750), smithing at Shorts Gardens, a jewellery workshop, looms everywhere [N].
- Edges: gravel quarries west (National Gallery, 2.75 m deep); stock pens and a boundary ditch north-east (Parker House); 9th-century defensive ditches at Maiden Lane (2 m deep) and Long Acre [N].
- Graves: early 7th-century burials on the ridge, built over (a shield burial at Cubitt's Yard); St Martin-in-the-Fields, Roman sarcophagi (one of about 410) reused for Saxon graves with glass palm cups, perhaps the church site. No church is known inside the wic [N,SM].
- The king's hall and the mint: certain from law and coin, sites unknown [HE,Co].
- "Ceolmundingchaga", a plot "not far from the west gate", sold in 857 to Bishop Alhhun of Worcester with measure and weights "as is the custom in the port" [S208].
- The walled city: ruins inside 2 miles of wall, robbed for rubble; St Paul's (founded 604) [N].
- Treasury hall, Whitehall: porched, 5.6 m wide, about 800, burnt. Thorney Island beyond [N].
- After 886, inside the walls: "Hwætmund's stone", an old stone building by the "trading shore", given to Bishop Wærferth of Worcester (889); Æthelred's hithe, plots for Wærferth and Archbishop Plegmund with "ship stations" before the wall (898/9); Billingsgate; the bridge; St Andrew's "old wooden church" above the Fleet (959 (?)) [S346,S1628,C,SA].
- Westminster: Dunstan's monastery, about 959, a dozen monks; Edward's church, hallowed 28 December 1065 [WA]. St Clement Danes, 11th century [N,SC].

## 3. Who could be met

- Kings: Offa (d. 29 July 796), Ecgfrith (141 days), Coenwulf (796-821), Ecgberht of Wessex (829-830, coining here), Beorhtwulf (put to flight 851), Burgred (852-874) [Of,C,Co,Ec,ASC]. From 886 Ealdorman Æthelred of Mercia (d. 911), then Edward the Elder and the kings of England [C,ASC]. Æthelred II died here 23 April 1016; Edmund Ironside was chosen here; Cnut; Harthacnut ("died at Lambeth, as he stood drinking", 8 June 1042); Edward; Harold II [ASC,C].
- Bishops: Coenwealh (to 793x796), Eadbald, Heathoberht (d. 801), Osmund, Æthelnoth, Ceolberht, Deorwulf, Swithwulf, Heahstan (d. 897), Wulfsige, Æthelweard, Leofstan, Theodred (909x926-951x953), Brihthelm, Dunstan (957x959-959), Ælfstan (to 995/6), Wulfstan (996-1002), Ælfhun, Ælfwig (1014-about 1035), Ælfweard (1035-1044), Robert of Jumièges (1044-1051), Spearhafoc (1051, unconsecrated), William (1051-1075) [B,Td]; Brihthelm to Wulfstan from memory (?).
- Offa's moneyers, very probably of London: Ciolhard, Diola, Eama, Ibba, Ludoman, Wilhun, Winoth; probably Ealhmund, Eadhun, Wulfhath [Na].
- Port-reeves: Wulfgar (1042-1044); Ulf, who with his wife Cynegyth gave Westminster land and a wharf; Esgar the staller, who defended London in 1066 [S1103,S1119,An]. Osgod Clapa, staller: his daughter Gytha married Tovi the Proud at the Lambeth feast where Harthacnut died [Os].
- Ordinary people: comb-makers, weavers, smiths, butchers [N]; tollmen ("thelonearii") [S143]; Tatberht, a name in runes on a sheep's backbone (?); the fleet's "naval men" of the 1030s [ASC]; the peace-guild's brothers, in tens and hundreds [At]; Ginna and Toki, who laid a rune stone at St Paul's [R2].

## 4. What was traded

In: wine in silver-fir casks from the Rhine; Mayen lava querns; Badorf, Tating and north French pots (8-12% of sherds); glass; Norwegian hones; figs and grapes; Ipswich ware [N]. Out: cloth and cloaks, combs, hides; wool likely; slaves once (Imma, section 7) [N].

About 789 Charlemagne closed his ports to English ships over a marriage offer; Gervold, abbot of St Wandrille and toll-gatherer from Rouen to Quentovic, helped mend it [Of,Ge]. In 796 he wrote to Offa of the "black stones" the English wanted and the cloaks the Franks wanted at the old length [Of], and promised merchants protection (?).

Coin: Offa's light penny (about 1.30 g), from 792/3 the heavy penny (about 1.45 g) [Na]. Coenwulf's gold mancus, 4.33 g, worth 30 pennies, reads DE VICO LVNDONIAE [Co]. Æthelstan gives London eight moneyers and forbids buying over 20 pence outside a port [HE].

Tolls: the king took toll by the ship; Æthelbald freed one ship each for Thanet's minster (Offa confirmed it, 761-764) and Rochester (733; confirmed 844-851) [S86,S143,S88]. In 889 trade "on the public street or the trading shore" paid the king; the bishop kept "the urn and scales" in his court [S346]. About 1000 at Billingsgate: a small boat a halfpenny, a sailing ship a penny, a keel or hulk four; Rouen's men pay on wine and blubber-fish; the Emperor's men give at Christmas and Easter two grey cloths and one brown, ten pounds of pepper, five pairs of gloves, two kegs of vinegar [C,Th]. No fair is recorded. Boniface paid his fare here in 716 and sailed to Dorestad [Wb].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 796: Offa dies; Coenwulf king by December [C].
- 798: London burns with great loss of life; Leo III refuses to move the archbishopric here [C,Co].
- 801: Bishop Heathoberht dies; much of London burns [C].
- 842: "great slaughter" at London, Quentovic and Rochester [ASC 839].
- 851: 350 ships storm London and Canterbury [ASC].
- 871-2: the army winters at London; Worcester sells land for the tribute, "the year the pagans sat in London" [ASC 872,S1278].
- 886: Alfred occupies London and gives it to Æthelred; the town moves inside the walls; the wic becomes Ealdwic [C,N].
- 893-896: Londoners help storm Benfleet and take the Danish ships on the Lea [ASC 894,896].
- 961: St Paul's burns and is restored. 982: London burns [ASC].
- 994, 8 September: Olaf Tryggvason and Swein, 94 ships, driven off [C].
- 1009: Thorkell's army: "oft they fought against the city of London; but glory be to God, that it yet standeth firm" [ASC,C].
- 1012: Ælfheah, killed at Greenwich 19 April, buried at St Paul's; moved to Canterbury 1023 [ASC,Ae].
- 1013: London submits to Swein. 1014, 28 September: the great sea-flood [ASC].
- 1016: Cnut's ships dig round the bridge and besiege the town. 1018: London pays 10,500 pounds [ASC].
- 1052: Godwin's fleet waits at Southwark for the flood and passes the bridge by the south bank [ASC].
- 1066: Edward dies 5 January; William crowned at Westminster at Christmas [C].

## 6. Faith and custom

St Paul's is the bishop's church; Earconwald (bishop 675-693, feast 30 April) lies there [Er]. Offa's synods sat upriver at Chelsea: in 787 he vowed 365 mancuses a year to Rome [Ch]. Relics lodged here: St Edmund's body (1010-1013) and Ælfheah's (1012-1023, feast 19 April) [Ed,Ae]. Danes of Cnut's time lie in St Paul's churchyard under a rune stone; St Clement's is theirs by tradition [R2,SC]. Law: a Kentish buyer proves "at the altar" that he bought openly in the wic [HE]; by the peace-guild's rules (about 930-939) a thief over twelve years old who took over twelve pence dies, and the brothers ride out together after stolen stock [At]. Heathen worship here in these years: no evidence found.

## 7. Stories and oddities

- Imma, the captive thegn no fetter would hold, sold "at London, to a Freson", who let him ransom himself (679) [Bd].
- Earconwald, lame with gout, went about in a small wheeled chair; the Londoners carried off his body from the monks of Chertsey [Er].
- The gold mancus "from the wic of London", found at Biggleswade in 2001, sold for £357,832 [Co].
- Saint-Denis forged a charter of Offa (dated 790) for land "in the port called Lundenuuic" [S133].
- A fisherman is said to have seen St Peter on Thorney, hence the abbey's yearly salmon [WA].
- A widow who drove iron pins into a man's image was drowned at London Bridge (963-975 (?)): its first mention [At].
- Guild values: an ox a mancus, a cow 20 pence, a pig 10, a sheep a shilling, a horse or a slave half a pound; dues fourpence a year [At].
- Spearhafoc, goldsmith and bishop-elect, vanished in 1051 with the gold and gems for the king's crown [Sp].
- Harold Harefoot's body, thrown in a fen in 1040, was found by fishermen and buried by the Danes [HH].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- No wic-reeve, tollman or priest of 793 is named: the spine's Wighelm is invented, the office real. No market place is known.
- Eoba struck at Canterbury; the spine puts him at Lundenwic [Na,Cy].
- Population. No quay or jetty: it was a beach.
- The fires of 798 and 801 rest on the 12th-century Historia Regum [N].
- The embargo's dates are loose (about 789 to before 796); no reeve is recorded turning Franks away. Frisians in 793 are inferred (Imma, the casks).
- In 793: no bridge, Tower, Westminster church, Southwark or Billingsgate; inside the walls only ruins and St Paul's, its form unknown.
- After 886 the port lies inside the walls, 2 km east; the row has no end year.
- Olaf pulling down the bridge (1014) is saga only [LB]. Offa's palace at St Alban Wood Street and All Hallows' 675 are late traditions [SW,AH].
- The 842 annal's "Canterbury" in old translations is Quentovic.
- "Peace-guild" is the 930s' own word (the brief bars guilds before 1000).
- Do not quote Attenborough (1922) or Fordham's Boniface (Talbot 1954): not free.
- The tolls of about 1000: checked against the game's table and Thornbury only.
