# Andoverpis (Antwerp): dossier

Port id `andoverpis`: Andoverpis, modern Antwerp, 51.222 N 4.398 E, realm franks, region flanders, kind fort, landing riverbank, size 2, no end year [ports_793.json]. Spellings: Andoverpis (Life of Eligius) [MT], ANDERPVS (coin, about 650) [W-G], Antwerpis (726) [MT], Andwerpa (836) [AF], Antwerf (1008) [MT]. Researched 4 Oct 2026. Keys in square brackets are listed in section 9. (?) marks what I could not check.

## 1. The place in 793

- The digs show no fort in 793: old ploughland on a sand rise by the river, and the first timber buildings going up (a post-built house, stake fences, a round pen, a plank-lined well), all late 8th or 9th century [B]. The rampart comes about 900 [B].
- The texts add a church of St Peter and St Paul, a toll, and a place the charter of 726 calls *castrum Antwerpis* [MT]. Nothing of that *castrum* has been found [B].
- Ruler: King Charles. The district is the *pagus Renensium*, the land of Rien [MT][W-Rien]; no count is named. The bishop is Hildoard of Cambrai (about 790 to 816) [W-Camb][W-Mi]. The church had been given to Willibrord and his abbey of Echternach [W-G]; whether the abbey still held it in 793 I did not check (?).
- People: farmers and herdsmen who eat mostly pork, and hunt red deer, roe, boar, hare and beaver [B]. Size unknown: scattered buildings, the centre seemingly near the landing [B]. The later walled ground is a little over two hectares [B].
- The lie: the right bank of the Scheldt at the mouth of the Schijn, where two sand ridges cross above wet river ground; north, the low peat of the Schijn delta [B]. Open country, fields and meadow, few trees [B].
- Where boats lie: the bank is ragged, with points and inlets. The points are natural landings; the chief one is the *werf* [B]. The inlets are the later town ditches (*ruien*) [B]. Bellens supposes jetties and stakes at the werf; none were dug [B].
- The water: tidal, grey-brown, mud flats and salt marsh on the way up. The way in from the sea is the Eastern Scheldt; the Honte is a narrow inlet. The range here about 800 is a guess, 2.5 to 3.5 m [north_sea_core].
- A ferry crossed the Schijn mouth at Dambrugge toward Merksem; later it was the abbey of Lobbes's (?) [W-G].

## 2. What stood there

- **The dark layer**: 20 to 40 cm of grey sand full of worn Roman sherds, fields worked from Roman times into the early 9th century [B][OE-W].
- **A post-built house** (south plot): two deep posts, stake rows, a trench; 770-900 [B].
- **A round pen**, just under 4 m across, some thirty pointed stakes; 770-900 [B].
- **A square timber well**, 60 cm inside, planks slid into grooved corner posts; 9th century [B].
- **Houses A, B and C**, 9th century: sill beams laid crosswise, 5 to 6 m wide and perhaps 8 m long, close side by side, some timber reused [B]. Van de Walle noted reused ship's timber in the oldest houses he dug [B].
- **The church of St Peter and St Paul**, built by Amandus, given to Willibrord in 726 [MT]. Its site is unknown. Guesses: inside the later castle, on the Koraalberg just east of it, or under the later St Michael's to the south [B][W-G][W-Mi].
- **The Koraalberg graves**: two burials of the 9th century, probably more; perhaps a chapel (Bellens's proposal) [B].
- **The toll**: a third of it went with the church in 726 [MT].
- **Palisade and rampart**, about 900: post rows, then a bank of turves and spoil, D-shaped and open to the river like Hedeby's, 13.5 m wide, still 4 m high in 1887 [B].
- **Corduroy paths** along the rampart's inner foot, 10th century: split logs on long bearers, 1.5 m and 1 m wide [B].
- **The 10th-century plots**: long and narrow; houses 5 to 7 m wide, short end to the street, wattle of hazel and willow on oak posts, ash strewn on the floors, wood chips on the paths [B]. On the Mattestraat Van de Walle found five road levels, three house levels and a three-aisled storehouse [B].
- **Stables and a smithy**: dung and dung beetles in the lowest layers, dwellings over them by the mid 10th century; over 12 kg of slag [B].
- **The stone wall**, Tournai limestone, with towers and gates on four sides, 7.7 m high where best kept. Long dated 1200-1225; oak piles at its foot were felled 1007-1015, and charcoal in its mortar dates 890-985 [B][OE].
- **St Walburga's chapel**, mid-castle, probably 11th century, over older timber houses [B].
- **A toll house, a mint house and the margrave's timber hall** in the castle; date not given [OE-S].
- **Houses outside the wall** from the 10th century (Guldenberg, Gorterstraat) [OE][B].

## 3. Who could be met

- Dead before 793, but named with the place: Eligius, bishop of Noyon (here about 645) [W-G]; Amandus; Rauchingus and his wife Bebelina, givers of 726; Willibrord (died 739) [MT][W-R].
- Bishops of Cambrai: Hildoard, about 790 to 4 July 816; Halitgar, 817 to 830 or 831; later Gerard I (1012-1051) and Lietbert (1051-1076) [W-Camb]. No visit is recorded.
- Beornrad, abbot of Echternach 775-797, archbishop of Sens, Alcuin's cousin; then Ado [W-Beo][W-Ech].
- 836: the Northmen who burned it; no leader named [AF].
- Godfrey of Verdun (about 930 to 998), listed "with reserve" as first margrave; Ansfried, count of Toxandria, bishop of Utrecht after 994, whose county joined the march [W-Mk].
- Gothelo I (about 967 to 19 April 1044), margrave from 1005 or 1008. A charter of 1008 has "the county of Count Gozilo, which is called Antwerf" [W-Goz][MT].
- Gothelo II, 1044-1046 [W-Mk]. Frederick of Luxembourg (1003 to 28 August 1065), duke from 1046, margrave from 1049 (1046 in [W-Mk]), besieged here in 1055 [W-Fr][MT]. Godfrey the Bearded, 1065-1069 [W-Mk].
- Baldwin V of Flanders, who took the march in the war of 1047-1049 and came back in 1055 [W-B5][MT].
- Ordinary people: priests serving the church, herdsmen, bound tenants (charter of 726) [MT]; toll takers [MT]; antler workers making combs, perhaps travelling men; spinners and weavers; leather workers from about 900; a smith; carpenters; hunters and fowlers [B]. No lay person is named between 793 and 1008 in what I read.

## 4. What was traded

- In, about 793: Badorf ware, relief-band amphorae, Vorgebirge or Eifel ware, all from the Rhine [B][OE].
- In, 10th century: white Meuse ware first, red-painted Rhenish ware second, with northern French, Paffrath and shell-tempered wares. Imports are about half of all sherds; the Meuse outweighing the Rhine is unusual [B]. Tournai stone comes by the Scheldt [B].
- Made here: antler combs, pins, needles, spindle whorls (found: 18 combs, 13 pin beaters, 11 whorls, 10 "tridents"), the whole chain from sawn stag skulls to finished comb; probably cloth [B]. Antler work starts before the rampart, leather work after [B].
- From the back country: old plough oxen for meat, young pigs and sheep, game, wheat, rye, barley, peat for fuel. Bones before 980: pig 46 in a hundred, cattle 36, sheep and goat 18; after: 36, 36, 28. Fish is one bone in a hundred; sea fishing comes after 1000 [B].
- Coin: tremisses of the moneyers Chrodigisilus (ANDERPVS, about 650) and Theudegisilus [B][W-G]. No coin or mint of 793-1066 is known to me. No hack-silver, amber or foreign coin was found [B].
- Toll: taken by 726 [MT]; from 805 only old tolls are lawful [chronicle_793]. A Scheldt toll "about 950" is unsourced (?) [W-G].
- Ships: none found. The project's table gives one coaster [traffic_793].
- Seasons: nothing recorded here. Regionally, Zeeland salt in autumn, wool, eels [seasons]; floods December to March [north_sea_core].
- Scale: far smaller than Dorestad or Quentovic, and later: it flowers in the 10th century [B].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 793: famine across Francia [chronicle_793].
- 28 December 809 (or 808): a great flood recorded at Saint-Amand, up the Scheldt [chronicle_793].
- 836: "The Northmen burned the town of Antwerp, and likewise Witla" [AF; Latin in section 9].
- 837, 841: Walcheren raided, then given to the Dane Harald [chronicle_793].
- 843: Verdun; the middle kingdom is Lothar's [chronicle_793].
- About 850-900: the first close-set houses [B].
- 879-880: the great army winters at Ghent and raids the Scheldt [chronicle_793].
- About 900: palisade, then rampart [B].
- 923-927: Antwerp, chief place of Rien, passes to the eastern kingdom; the Scheldt is the border [W-G].
- About 974 to 980: the march of Antwerp; the date is not settled [B][W-enSt].
- 1005 or 1008: Gothelo margrave [W-Goz].
- 1007-1015: oak piles driven along the castle wall; great earthworks in the south part [B].
- 1047-1049: Baldwin V takes the march and gives it up [W-B5].
- 1055: the Flemings besiege Frederick in the castle, fail, and burn the houses round it [B][MT].
- No end: the place goes on.

## 6. Faith and custom

- Christian since the 7th century: Eligius pulled down shrines here and converted Suevi [VE]; Amandus built the church [MT].
- Days: Peter and Paul, 29 June (my knowledge); Amandus, 6 February [W-Am]. Walburga, patron of the later castle church, is called on by sailors in storms [W-Walp].
- The gift of 726 asks for incense and lights, and for the priests' prayers for the givers' sins [MT].
- Burial: the Koraalberg, 9th century, outside the later rampart [B].
- St Michael's, south of the castle, is the mother church by the 11th century, with twelve canons [W-G][W-Mi].
- Which people's law ran here I did not check (?).

## 7. Stories and oddities

- The charter's curse on whoever breaks it: the fate of Judas, of Ananias and Sapphira, the leprosy of Gehazi, and two pounds of gold and five of silver to the treasury [MT].
- Rauchingus got the church by exchange from Firminus, abbot of "Quortolodra", for his hamlet Tumme [MT].
- A Roman coin of Constans (348-350, Trier) lay in the rampart; Roman tile and stone were built into 10th-century houses [B].
- One flea, and 48 kinds of wood and leaf beetle; hardly a horse bone [B].
- The name: probably "at the thrown-up ground", silted land in a river bend; or Celtic, "those on both banks" [W-Ant].
- The giant Antigoon cut off the hands of skippers who would not pay toll; Brabo threw the giant's hand in the river. Told in a 15th-century chronicle; how much older I did not check (?) [W-Brabo].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- A rampart, wall, castle or Het Steen in 793. Rampart about 900; stone about 1000 at the earliest; Het Steen 1200-1225 [B][W-St]. The table's "fort" fits 793 only as the charter's word: let it grow by eras.
- Where the church stood. A chapel of 727 in the castle, destroyed in 836, is tradition [W-Wal].
- What the fire of 836 destroyed: no burnt layer is tied to it in what I read.
- That Northmen founded or held Antwerp. Bellens leaves it open; that Harald and Rorik ruled the Scheldt until 876 is unsourced [W-G].
- Frisians living here. The table lists them; the only text is the 7th-century Life of Eligius [VE].
- A mint or market before 1066; a town seal or town rights in 1008 [W-G].
- The founding date of the march; the date of the stone wall.
- That the giant, the hand, Semini (name first printed 1859 [W-Sem]) or Lohengrin belong to these years.
- The game's chronicle cites "AB 836" for the burning; it is in the Annals of Fulda.
- Do not quote [VE] in the game: the translation is modern. The Latin is free.
