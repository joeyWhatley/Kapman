# Birka (Björkö, in the Mälar bay): dossier

Keys in brackets: see Sources. (?) = not checked on a page I opened. "R 19" is Rimbert's Life of Anskar, chapter 19. Rimbert gives no years: the years below are his translator's (Robinson 1921) or the diggers'. Adam of Bremen was read only as quoted in [WE].

## 1. The place in 793

- **Name.** *Birca* in Rimbert and Adam; no Norse text names it [WE]. Either "birch island" (*Bjǫrkey*) [WS] or from *birk*, a market place with its own rules [WD]: scholars differ.
- **What.** The Svear's one market town, a generation or less old: founded about 750 [WE; WS] or about 790 [M; WD]. Which king founded it is not known [M; WS]. In 793 there is no church, no coin and no priest.
- **Rule.** A king of the Svear, name unknown in 793, whose own hall is not here but at Hovgården on Adelsö, the next island north [M; HG]. A "prefect of the town", a counsellor of the king, keeps it for him (first named in 829) [R 11; M]. The house plots are the king's ground; holders pay him a due [M].
- **People.** About 7 hectares of town; not more than about 700 people at its largest, 500 to 1,000 by other counts, fewer in 793 [M; WE]. Mostly craftsmen's households, with merchants and strangers lodging [M]. Whether they stayed all year or only the summer half is not known [M].
- **Water.** The Mälar is an arm of the Baltic, not a lake: the water stood about 5 m higher than now, and the way in from the sea was by the narrows at today's Stockholm and Södertälje [WD; M; BJ]. Björkö was much smaller than now and its rocky south end was an island of its own [M; BJ]. Adam (about 1075): a harbour facing north, the safest in the Svear's waters, but "risky for those who are careless or ignorant", the inlet blocked with hidden rocks for over 100 stadia; five days' sail from Skåne, five more to the Rus [A].
- **Landmarks.** The fort rock, the island's highest point, dropping 30 m sheer into the water on its west side; watchmen there could signal to Hovgården [M]. Grave mounds ring the town on the higher ground [M]. A great standing stone on a grave of the early 700s stood on the rock, in sight of the town [BB].
- **Where boats lie.** At a row of timber jetties on footings of big stones, running out from the shore below the houses [M]. A line of piles stood in the water off the town to stop an attack; its date is not given [M]. Bays named Kugghamn, Korshamn and Salviksgropen are called harbours (?) [WS].

## 2. What stood there

- **The Black Earth.** The town itself: 7 ha of dark soil. Houses about 8 by 5 m in rows running up from the shore, wattle and clay or plank, a hearth on the floor, each plot fenced with wattle, narrow lanes between; refuse swept into the lanes for pigs and dogs, planks laid over the mud [M]. Dug 1871-81 (Stolpe), 1969-71, 1990-95 (Ambrosiani) [M; WS]. A radar survey in 2018 found the first town packed with small workshops inside an inner bank, nothing over 20 square metres; larger houses come only towards 900-950 [WS].
- **Jetties and stores.** Stone-footed jetties; goods were probably stacked near them [M]. No market square is known: the only "market place" was the ice in winter [M].
- **Workshops.** Bronze-casters by the shore (moulds for oval and equal-armed brooches), comb-makers working elk antler, bead-makers, smiths, weavers, fur-dressers [M]. A mould and its brooch were found a century apart [M].
- **Shipyard.** A stone-lined hollow on the old shore with a timber slip, unused and used boat rivets, slate whetstones, woodworking tools; announced 2022 [WE].
- **Terraces.** Above the town to the east, longhouses of a better sort; one 20 m long, built about the time the town began. Under its door lay a middle-aged man with weapons and half an elk antler by his head, and over him a beheaded younger man, probably a slave [M].
- **The fort (Borg).** A half-ring bank about 350 m long, 7-8 m wide, with a timber breastwork and three gates, one called the King's Gate; the cliff closes the west [BB; M]. Built in two stages, the first slight; burned at least three times, last about 1000 [M; BB]. Under it a grave of the early 700s: an older man and a small stallion [M; BB].
- **The garrison.** On the slope north-west of the fort: a terrace with arrowheads, spearheads, shield bosses, mail and scale armour, many locks and keys, comb cases, coins, and nothing of women's; about 50 men, horse and foot [M; BB]. Arrowheads outside its fence show an attack from the water [BB]. Its hall and date: tenth century (?).
- **Town rampart.** 450 m survive, with six gaps; built in two stages with timber on top. Over fifty graves of about 900 were built into it when it was enlarged early in the 900s [M]. In 793 it is not there in this form.
- **Grave fields.** Some 3,000 graves in six fields: Hemlanden (over 1,600 mounds), north of Borg, Borgs hage and Kvarnbacka, Grindsbacka, Kärrbacka, Ormknös (its largest mound raised about the birth of Christ) [M; WE]. About 1,100 dug: over half burnt; some 500 unburnt, a fifth of them children [M].
- **Churches.** Three in the text, none found (Arbman looked in 1932-34): Herigar's on his family land, about 830; Gautbert's, begun about 832; a hall for prayer on a plot the king granted in 852, and a yard with a house Anskar bought for the priest [R 11, 14, 28; M].
- **The thing place.** "An open plain" with a stage and a shelter of branches, near enough to Herigar's church to be carried there [R 19].
- **Hovgården, Adelsö.** The king's hall; a stone-faced quay about 35 m long behind a 200 m line of piles; three "king's mounds" (the largest 45 m across), a thing mound, and Skopintull, where a man and woman were burnt with seven dogs and three horses, with gold-thread crosses and a lock of hair in a bronze bowl [HG].

## 3. Who could be met

- **Björn**, king; received Anskar in 829 and sent the emperor a letter "in characters fashioned after the Swedish custom" (runes) [R 11, 12].
- **Herigar** (Hergeir), prefect and king's counsellor; baptised 829-30; died about 851 [R 11, 19].
- **Anskar**, monk of Corbie, here 829-31 and about 852-54; died 3 February 865. **Witmar**, his companion in 829 [R 10-12, 26-28; M].
- **Gautbert** (Simeon), bishop to the Svear from about 832; bound and driven out about 845. **Nithard**, his kinsman, killed then [R 14, 17].
- **Anund** (Anoundus), an exiled king who came with the Danes, 845-51 [R 19].
- **Ardgar**, a hermit priest, about 851-52 [R 19, 20].
- **Frideburg**, a rich, old Christian woman, and her daughter **Catla** [R 20]. Magnus calls them Frisian (?) [M]; Rimbert says "amongst the Swedes".
- **Olof** (Olef), king in 852 [R 26-28, 30]. **Eric**, a dead king the heathens proposed to make a god [R 26].
- **Erimbert**, priest, Gautbert's nephew, from 852; **Ansfrid**, a priest of Danish descent, three or four years; **Rimbert**, a priest of Danish forebears. **Ragenbert** was killed by robbers on the road to Schleswig and never arrived [R 28, 33].
- **Unni**, archbishop of Hamburg-Bremen, died here 17 September 936; **Ring** was then king [UN; WS; A].
- **Adalvard the Younger**, bishop of Sigtuna, visited the ruins in the 1060s; **Hiltin (John)**, a bishop ordained "for this city" [A].
- At Hovgården about 1070: **Tolir**, the king's steward (*bryte*), his wife **Gyla**, and a king **Håkon** [U11; HG].
- **Ordinary people.** No craftsman's name survives [M]. The evidence shows comb-makers, bronze-casters, bead-makers, smiths, weavers and seamstresses (needle cases), fur traders, the fort's horsemen in eastern coats, slaves, Christian captives, and women who owned weights and dirhams [M; R 11]. Partners who owned a ship and cargo together were *félagar* [M]. Sami hunters supplied furs and antler [M; WE].

## 4. What was traded

- **Out:** iron from the forest country to the north-west, sledged in over winter; furs (squirrel in winter coat, fox, beaver, marten, hare, ermine; perhaps seal, bear, wolf); elk and reindeer antler and combs; slaves [M; WE].
- **In:** Rhineland glass beakers and tin-foiled jugs; wine; silk, carnelian and rock-crystal beads and spices from the east; glass rods and beads (Ribe's "wasp beads" lie in the oldest layers); honey and perhaps salt in grey Slav pots; amber [M; WE; WD].
- **Direction.** Until about 850 the far trade ran mostly south and west, to Dorestad; then it turned east, by Ladoga [M; R 27].
- **Coin and weight.** No king ever struck coin here. Silver went by weight on a folding balance with small weights, on an eastern standard; dirhams were cut as needed [M]. Most coins found are dirhams; Hedeby coins from the 800s; English and Frankish ones are rare, though grave 66 held a penny of Charlemagne [WE; M]. A hoard of 450 dirhams and silver jewellery lay on an iron dish [M].
- **Dues and law.** A share of cargo to the king through the prefect is likely, not recorded [M]. "The control of public business of every kind should rest with the whole people and not with the king" [R 26]. The later town laws called *Bjärköarätt* may or may not be named from here [M; BR].
- **Seasons.** Fur dealers did their business at winter markets on the ice; bone skates, ice-picks and crampons for men and horses were found [M]. Ships came in summer.
- **Ships.** Danes, Northmen, Slavs and Sembi (Prussians) each year, says Adam [A]. From the Schleswig side, "nearly twenty days in a ship" in 852 [R 26].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- c. 750-790: the town is laid out [WE; M].
- 793: a young, heathen craft town under an unnamed king.
- c. 800 on: dirhams arrive (?) [M].
- 829: Svear envoys ask Emperor Louis for priests. Anskar's merchant convoy is taken by pirates; nearly forty books are lost; he arrives partly on foot [R 9-11].
- 829-31: a year and a half of preaching; Herigar baptised; his church built [R 11, 12].
- c. 832: Bishop Gautbert comes and starts a church [R 14].
- c. 845: a crowd, "not by command of the king", kills Nithard and drives Gautbert out [R 17].
- 845-51: nearly seven years with no priest [R 19].
- 845-51: Anund comes with 21 Danish ships and 11 of his own; the king is away; 100 pounds of silver paid; the lots send the Danes to a Slav town; Anund gives the silver back [R 19].
- c. 851: Ardgar arrives; Herigar dies; Frideburg dies; Ardgar leaves [R 19, 20].
- c. 852: Anskar's second visit; the lots and two things say yes; a plot for a chapel [R 26-28].
- c. 853-54: Olof's Svear burn Seeburg and take ransom at Apulia in Courland [R 30].
- Before 856: the abbot of Fulda sends two bells (?) [WS].
- 854-65: Erimbert, Ansfrid, then Rimbert the priest [R 33].
- 865: Anskar dies; the mission lapses [M; WS].
- Early 900s: the town rampart is enlarged over older graves [M].
- 936: Unni dies here; body buried here, head taken to Bremen [UN].
- c. 940-50: chamber grave Bj 581 by the garrison [581].
- c. 960: the latest coins [WE].
- c. 970-75: the people leave. No sign of sack or fire in the town; the fort burns about then [M; WE].
- c. 970-80: Sigtuna is founded [WD].
- 1060s: Adalvard finds "hardly a vestige", and not Unni's grave [A].
- c. 1065-80: Tolir's rune stone at Hovgården's harbour [U11].

## 6. Faith and custom

- Heathen throughout: "Birka was never a Christian town" [M]. Thor, Frey and Odin; kings sacrifice [M].
- **Lots** are cast in the open before any great decision, by king and by raiders alike [R 19, 26, 27].
- **Burial.** Most are burnt and laid in a pot under a mound; small flat loaves go on the pyre (64 found). Chamber graves hold riders with horses, weapons and silk. Plain east-west graves without goods are probably Christian [M]. The dead were asked for counsel at the mound [M].
- Hammer rings of iron in many graves; ten silver crosses and a crucifix (grave 660, Christ bound, not nailed) from tenth-century women's graves [M; WE].
- The few Christians: captives, and traders baptised at Dorestad [R 11, 27]. Anskar's feast is 3 February [M].
- After the lots favoured Christ in Courland, Svear kept a forty-day fast from meat [R 30].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **The rain.** At the thing Herigar wagered his god against theirs: the heathens were soaked and the leaves of their shelter fell on them; he and one small boy stayed dry [R 19].
- **The leg.** Lame and jeered at, he was carried to his church and walked out [R 19].
- **The ransom.** The Danes said "each individual merchant in the place had more" than the hundred pounds [R 19].
- **Frideburg's wine.** With no priest, she kept bought wine three years to be dropped in her mouth at death [R 20].
- **Catla's bag.** At Dorestad she gave her mother's wealth to the poor, spent four pennies on wine when tired, and found the bag full again, less the four pennies [R 20].
- **A new god.** In 852 a man said the gods had met and would take dead King Eric among them if the Svear wanted more gods [R 26].
- **The old man's speech.** "Some of us who on various occasions have been to Dorstadt have of our own accord adopted this form of religion" [R 27].
- **The ring.** A woman's silver ring with violet glass cut in imitation Arabic script that says nothing [WE].
- **Bj 581.** A woman, seated, with sword, axe, 25 arrows, two horses, three weights, three dice and 28 gaming pieces; what she was is disputed [581].
- **The Birka girl.** About six, buried in the 900s with a brooch, beads, a knife and a needle case [BF].
- **Mead.** Human dung held lime-flower pollen: honey from south or east of the Baltic [M].
- The town was found again in 1871 by an insect man looking for amber [M].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- The king in 793, the founder, any person at all before 829. Invent ordinary people only.
- No rampart, garrison hall, church, cross or coin in 793. The garrison and Bj 581 are tenth century.
- Robinson's notes call the "neighbouring town" of R 19 Sigtuna: wrong, Sigtuna is of the 970s. It is taken to be the fort [WE; AN].
- Where the churches, the thing place and Unni's grave lay. That the mission lasted: it did not.
- Any toll rate, any written law, any named moneyer or craftsman. *Bjärköarätt* is of the 1100s and after; its link to this island is doubted [BR].
- Why the town ended: Sigtuna, the rising land closing the Södertälje way, or an attack are all guesses [WS; WE]. Do not show a sack.
- That silk or the ring prove Muslims here; that Bj 581 was a commander.
- Rimbert's 7,000 and 15,000 fighting men in Courland are not counts [R 30].
- Rimbert never says the king lived at Hovgården; that is the diggers' reading [HG; M].
