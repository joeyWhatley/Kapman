# Hī (Iona): dossier

Port `hi`: Hī, modern Iona, 56.334 N 6.392 W; realm dalriata, region dalriata, kind monastery, landing beach, size 2, no end year [ports_793.json]. Researched 6 Oct 2026. Source keys are in section 9. (?) marks what I could not check; (memory) is known to me but not opened. Web search was shut for this task and the Annals of Ulster would not open, so annal numbers are from the repo's own tables [repo irish; repo chronicle] or marked.

**Read this first.** The island is not emptied by the raids. An abbot and monks are on it in every generation to 1099 [enwp abb]. What leaves is the headship (to Kells, 807-814) and the relics (849, 878). The stone abbey seen today is from about 1200 and later: none of it stands in the game's years.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What it is.** The mother house of Columba's family of churches, founded in 563 by Columba with twelve companions [enwp Abbey]. Lindisfarne is its daughter (section 6). Its abbot is the "successor of Colum Cille", head of houses in Ireland and Britain [enwp abb].
- **The name.** Bede writes Hii; Adomnán writes Ioua insula; in Irish it is Í, or Í Coluim Cille. "Iona" is a later misreading of Ioua (n for u, about 1274) and must not be said in the game's years. The oldest sense may be "yew-place" [enwp Iona].
- **Who rules.** The abbot: Bresal son of Ségéne, abbot thirty-one years, dies 801 [repo chronicle, AU 801.4]. A retired abbot, Suibne (resigned 771), also dies in 801 [enwp abb]. The island lies in Dál Riata, whose king-list breaks off with Donncoirce's death in 792; after that nobody can say for sure who is king [enwp DR]. The Pictish king Constantín son of Fergus is thought to be over-king (memory) (?). The abbots are of Columba's own Irish kin, Cenél Conaill [enwp DR].
- **Who lives there.** Monks, their working brothers, guests and penitents. Kings come to end their days: Niall Frossach, king of Tara, died here in 778, and Artgal of Connacht in 791 (memory) (?). How many monks is not recorded; sixty-eight could be killed in 806 and a house remain [repo irish, AU 806.8]. Bede rates the island at "about five families" (HE III.4, memory) (?).
- **How big.** About 6 km long and 2 km wide. The highest point is Dùn Ì, 101 m, at the north end [enwp Iona].
- **The water.** The Sound between the island and the Ross of Mull is 1.1 km at its narrowest, mostly 2 to 8 m deep, 23 m at most, with islets in it: Eilean nam Bàn and others [enwp Sound]. White sand under it, pink granite on the Mull side; the stream 1 to 2 knots, springs about 3.6 m, both estimates [repo water]. The island shelters the Sound from the west [repo water].
- **Coming in.** From the south, the Torran Rocks: some 39 square km of reefs and skerries, 3 km south-west of the Ross of Mull [enwp Mull]. Then the pink skerries of the Ross, the tidal island of Erraid at the Sound's south mouth, and the low green island with one hill [enwp Mull; enwp Sound]. Ben More on Mull, 966 m, is the far mark [enwp Mull]. Staffa and the Treshnish Isles lie north [enwp Mull].
- **Where boats lie.** On the sand of the east shore, below the monastery: the bay now called St Ronan's Bay [enwp Iona]. Just south is Martyrs' Bay, by tradition the landing of the dead, from which the Street of the Dead runs to the graveyard [HES; (memory) (?)]. People on the Mull shore shout across the Sound and a boat is sent [VC, Reeves I.20 and elsewhere]. No quay is known.

## 2. What stood there

- **The vallum.** A great bank and ditch round the holy ground, in part older than the monastery [HES; enwp Abbey]. It encloses about 8 hectares (memory) (?). Long stretches still stand north and west of the abbey.
- **The church and the houses.** Timber, wattle and thatch: a church, a refectory, a writing house and library, monks' cells, a guest house [enwp Abbey]. Adomnán has the guest house made ready and water drawn to wash strangers' feet [VC I.4]. About 800 the wooden church was replaced in stone [enwp Abbey] (?: no dig cited there).
- **Tòrr an Aba**, "the abbot's hill": a rocky knoll facing the church where Columba's writing hut stood, by Adomnán. Dug in 1956-57: charred wood and a socket for a cross [enwp Abbey]. Radiocarbon dates on the charcoal, published 2017, fell in Columba's lifetime (memory) (?).
- **Saint Columba's Shrine.** A very small stone chapel by the later church door, perhaps of the 9th or 10th century, by tradition over his grave [HES].
- **The high crosses**, 8th to 9th century, four of them, standing before the church [HES]. Saint Martin's, 8th century, still stands where it was set [enwp Abbey]. Saint John's has arms so wide that it broke; the stone ring may have been invented here to hold such arms [enwp HC; (memory) (?)]. Saint Oran's and Saint Matthew's survive in pieces [HES]. Some of the stone came 50 sea miles by boat from Loch Sween [enwp Abbey].
- **Reilig Odhráin**, Oran's graveyard. Digging in the 1970s found a timber building of the Columban age under it [HES]. Saint Oran's Chapel in it is 12th century: not in the game [enwp OC].
- **Sràid nam Marbh**, the Street of the Dead: the old paved way to the graveyard and church [HES].
- **Workshops.** Wood-turning, metal-working, and glass for windows and beads, perhaps from the 7th century [enwp Abbey]. The ditch kept leather shoes and turned wooden bowls (memory) (?).
- **The farm.** Adomnán names a kiln, a barn, the grinding of corn, a western plain where the brothers reap (the machair), cattle, a white horse that carried the milk pails, and an islet "where our young seals are brought forth and nurtured" [VC I.22, I.29, I.33, I.35; III.23 (memory)].
- **Grave slabs and crosses**: over a hundred early carved stones, some asking a prayer for a named man in Irish (memory) (?).
- **Not found, or not there**: a market, a mint, a toll, a wall of stone, a harbour work. The nunnery is of about 1200.

## 3. Who could be met

- **Bresal son of Ségéne**, abbot 772-801 [repo chronicle; spine `cast.bresal`].
- **Connachtach**, abbot, died 802 [enwp abb]; the annals call him a choice scribe (memory) (?).
- **Cellach son of Congal**, abbot 802-814: builds Kells, resigns when its church is done, dies 815 [repo chronicle, AU 814.9; enwp abb; spine `cast.cellach`].
- **Diarmait**, "fosterling of Daigre", abbot from 814: carries the relics to Alba in 818 and 829 and to Ireland in 831; alive through the raid of 825; a leader of the Céli Dé, the strict "clients of God" [enwp Diar].
- **Blathmac son of Flann**, of royal birth, killed 825 (section 5) [repo irish; enwp Bla].
- **Indrechtach**, abbot: brings relics to Ireland in 849; killed among the English on his way to Rome, 12 March 854 [repo irish, AU 854.3; enwp Ind].
- **Cellach son of Ailill**, abbot of Kildare and Hī, died 865; **Feradach**, died 880; **Flann son of Máel Dúin**, died 20 April 891 [enwp abb].
- **Máel Brigte son of Tornán**, successor of Patrick and of Columba together, died 22 February 927: he sat at Armagh, not here [enwp abb; repo irish, AU 927.1].
- **Cáenchomrac**, died 947; **Mugrón**, died 980 or 981 [enwp abb].
- **Amlaíb Cuarán**, king of Dublin, came as a penitent after Tara and died here, 980 or 981 [repo irish, AT 980; enwp Aml].
- **Máel Ciaráin**, abbot, killed 24 December 986 [enwp abb].
- **Later abbots on the island**: Máel Brigte son of Rímid (died 1005), Flandabra (1025), the grandson of Báethéne (1070), Donnchad (1099) [enwp abb].
- **Lords of the sea round it**: the sons of Aralt, Maccus and Gofraid, "king of Innse Gall", killed 989; Earl Sigurd of Orkney from about 990; Earl Thorfinn from about 1035; Echmarcach of Dublin and the Isles [enwp Isles; repo irish]. The Orkney dates rest on sagas (?).
- **Ordinary people the evidence shows**: a scribe, a mason and cross-carver, a wood-turner, a smith, a glass-worker [enwp Abbey]; a baker and a gardener named in Adomnán (memory) (?); a boatman at the ferry; seal-keepers; a guest-master; pilgrims and penitents; the saint's steward who gathers the fines of Adomnán's law [repo irish]. A Norse family of the 10th or 11th century cut a rune stone here for a brother [enwp ScSc; names (memory) (?)].

## 4. What was traded

No market, fair, toll or cargo is recorded. Like Lindisfarne, this is a house that receives, and a merchant comes as the abbot's guest: in Irish law a stranger from over sea has no standing until a king or abbot gives him protection [repo irish].

- **In.** Wine for the Mass, by sea from Gaul [repo seasons]. Adomnán has "Gallican sailors" from "the provinces of Gaul" bringing news within the year [VC I.22]; Dunadd, Dál Riata's royal fort, has Gaulish glass and wine jars [enwp DR]. Timber: the island has none to build with; oak and pine were towed over by boat (memory) (?). Stone for crosses from Loch Sween [enwp Abbey]. Iron, salt, wax, oil (?).
- **Out.** Nothing recorded as sold. What the house makes: books, crosses, fine metalwork such as the house-shaped shrine of about 750 now in Edinburgh (wood, silver and copper alloy, 112 mm wide) [enwp Mon]; sealskin and seal oil from its own islet [VC I.33].
- **The great Gospel.** Most scholars hold the Book of Kells was begun here about 800 and finished at Kells: 340 calfskin leaves, 330 by 250 mm. Its colours are ochres, a copper green, lichen purple and woad; no lapis and no shell purple [enwp BoK]. So calfskin, lichen and woad are true wants; lapis is not.
- **Coin and weight.** No coin is struck in Ireland or Dál Riata in these years. Worth is counted in cattle, in the cumal (a slave woman's worth) and in ounces of silver [repo irish]. A hoard of some 350 pennies, mostly English, was hidden here about 986 (memory) (?).
- **Law.** Adomnán's law (697, renewed 727): the fines for killing or carrying off women, clerics and children go to this community [repo irish; enwp Cain]. From 887 no loading or selling on a Sunday in an Irish king's land [repo irish].
- **Ships.** Hide boats (currachs) and wooden boats under sail and oar, in Adomnán (memory) (?). Dál Riata's levy was 28 oarsmen from every twenty houses, two seven-bench boats [enwp DR]. From 794 the long ships of the heathens.
- **Seasons.** Columba's feast, 9 June, falls in the sailing season [repo saints]. Stay out of the Hebrides 794-840 and 986-989 [repo irish].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 794: "all the islands of Britain" wasted by heathens [repo irish, AU 794.7; enwp ScSc].
- 795: Hī plundered for the first time; Skye and Rechru the same summer [repo chronicle, AI 795.2].
- 801: Bresal dies [AU 801.4]. 802: Hī burned by heathens; Connachtach dies [AU 802.9; enwp abb].
- 804: Kells given to the community "without a battle" [repo irish, AU 804.12].
- 806: sixty-eight of the community killed by heathens [AU 806.8]. The annal names no place; "Martyrs' Bay" is tradition [enwp Abbey].
- 807-814: the new house built at Kells; Cellach resigns; Diarmait abbot [AU 807.4, 814.9].
- 818, 829, 831: Diarmait travels with the relics [enwp Diar; repo irish].
- 825: Blathmac killed. Walafrid Strabo, abbot of Reichenau, put it in verse: Blathmac had the shrine buried under turf, said Mass, refused to tell, and was cut down [repo irish, AU 825.17; enwp Bla; poem (memory)].
- 849: the relics shared: Indrechtach brings some to Ireland; King Kenneth takes some to Dunkeld [repo irish, AU 849.7; enwp Ken].
- 858: Kenneth son of Alpín dies; later king-lists bury him here [enwp Ken] (?).
- 878: Columba's shrine and other relics taken to Ireland "in flight from the foreigners" [enwp Kells; AU 878.9 (memory)].
- 927: the headship of Columba's churches is held with Armagh's [AU 927.1].
- 980 or 981: Amlaíb Cuarán dies here in penance [AT 980].
- 986, Christmas night: Danes plunder Hī and kill the abbot and fifteen elders [repo irish, AU 986.3].
- 987: 360 of those who plundered it are killed [repo irish, AU 987.3].
- 989: Gofraid son of Aralt killed in Dál Riata [AU 989.4].
- 1007: at Kells the great Gospel of Colum Cille is stolen for its shrine and found months later "under a sod" [enwp BoK].
- 1040, 1057: Duncan and Macbeth, by later lists buried here [enwp Iona] (?).
- 1070: the abbot, grandson of Báethéne, dies [enwp abb]; killed, by the annals (memory) (?).

## 6. Faith and custom

- **Saints and days**: Columba, 9 June (died 597); Adomnán, 23 September [repo saints]; Oran, 27 October [enwp Oran].
- **Easter and the tonsure**: the house kept its own reckoning until 716, then took Rome's [repo saints, HE V.22]. In 793 that quarrel is three generations old.
- **Daughter houses**: Lindisfarne was founded from here about 634 [dossier lindisfarena]; Derry, Durrow, Kells, Raphoe, Dunkeld are of the family [enwp abb].
- **The rule**: fasts on Wednesday and Friday, broken for a guest [VC I.20]. Under Diarmait the stricter Céli Dé way [enwp Diar].
- **The chronicle**: a year-by-year record was kept here from after 563 to about 740; the Irish annals grow from it [enwp CoI].
- **Burial**: kings and pilgrims want this ground. Graves are marked with small crosses and slabs [enwp Abbey].
- **The old gods**: none recorded on the island. The heathens of 795-825 are; by the late 10th century Norse lords come as penitents and are buried as Christians [enwp Aml; enwp ScSc].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- Columba told a brother to watch the west shore for a crane blown over from Ireland, and to feed it three days as a guest [VC I.48 Reeves (number (?))].
- A thief from Colonsay hid in the sand hills under his boat, covered with hay, to cross by night and kill the monks' seals. Columba had him caught, then sent him away with sheep so he need not steal [VC I.33].
- On his last day the old white horse laid its head on Columba's breast and wept; he blessed the island from the little hill and said kings of foreign peoples would honour it [VC III.23 (memory)].
- His pillow was a stone, kept by his grave as a monument [VC III.23 (memory)].
- Oran, the legend says, was buried alive so the church walls would stand, and when dug up said hell was not as reported; Columba had him covered again quickly. A late tale, not in Adomnán [enwp Oran].
- A reckoning of 1549 counted 48 Scottish, 8 Norwegian and 4 Irish kings in the graveyard. No such grave can be shown [enwp Iona].
- Walafrid, writing by Lake Constance, calls the island "Eo" (memory) (?): news of it reached the Rhine.

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Who the raiders were or where they came from; what was taken; where the sixty-eight died; whether the church burned in 825 (Wikipedia says so; the annal does not) [enwp Abbey].
- That the island was abandoned in 807, 849 or 878. The Kenneth article calls it "an abandoned abbey" [enwp Ken]; the abbot list says otherwise [enwp abb].
- That the Book of Kells was made here: "widely accepted", not proved [enwp BoK]. Nor that the Edinburgh shrine is Columba's Brecbennach: now doubted [enwp Mon].
- That any king named is buried here: the lists are late.
- Who ruled Dál Riata between 792 and about 843; the repo keeps realm `dalriata` throughout [repo irish].
- No marble trade (the quarry is 18th century) [enwp Iona]; no nunnery; no stone abbey; no "Iona"; no "cathedral"; no quay or market.
- The saying about cows and women, and the women's island: later folklore (memory) (?). Do not give it to Columba.
- Quotable: Adomnán in the 1874 Edinburgh translation (Reeves's edition) is out of copyright. The annals in CELT's modern English are not: paraphrase.
