# Uppsalir (Gamla Uppsala, Uppland): dossier

Port `uppsalir`: Uppsalir, modern Gamla Uppsala, 59.898 N 17.634 E; realm svear, region svealand, kind royal seat, landing riverbank, size 2 [ports_793.json]. Researched 6 Oct 2026. Source keys are in section 9. (?) marks what I could not check on a page I read.

**Read this first.** Three layers must be kept apart. The ground: mounds, a hall, a grave field, all older than 793, with no names. One foreign text near our years: Adam of Bremen, about 1075, who never came here. The Icelanders (Snorri, about 1225) and the Uppland law (1296): **late**. No text written between 793 and 1066 by anyone who stood here survives.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What it is.** The Svear kings' estate, their holy place and the place of the great thing: a ridge with three huge mounds, a hall terrace, a thing mound and a wide grave field, among farms [enW GU; svW F]. Not a town; no craft quarter of traders, no jetty found. The market is Birka [repo realm].
- **Who rules.** "The king at Uppsalir"; no king's name is safe for 780-828 [repo realm]. The estate was the head of the kings' lands, later called Uppsala öd (the name and the law are medieval) [Öd].
- **Who lives there.** The household of the estate, farmers, weavers and smiths: more than 30 pit houses with traces of weaving and metalwork were dug in 2012 [svW F]; their date within 500-1050 I could not check (?). They ate cattle, pig, sheep, goat, horse, elk, roe deer, crane, and sea fish (cod, herring) brought up from the coast [svW F]. No count of heads exists.
- **Coming in.** From the Mälar bay (salt water then) north into the arm called Ekoln, then up the Fyris. With water about 6 m higher the bay probably reached the south edge of today's Uppsala; Uppsalir lay about 5 km further up a small clay-grey river [repo water, an estimate]. The plain between, Fyrisvellir, was wet meadow that flooded; the name is from *fyrva*, to ebb (the river itself was only named Fyris in the 17th century: say "the river") [Fv]. Travellers left their boats and walked [Fv, uncited there (?)]. The land was rising and the place slowly lost its water [enW GU]. A light boat may have gone higher: boat graves lie at Valsgärde, on the river 3 km north [Val]; that is inference.
- **In winter** the way is by sledge over the ice [repo realm, **late**].
- **Landmarks.** The three mounds on the ridge, 7-11 m high and 55-70 m across [svW F], seen far over the flat plain; hundreds of lesser mounds; the hall roof (?).

## 2. What stood there

- **The three great mounds.** Raised about 550-625 [svW F; enW GU], so about 170-240 years old in 793. East mound, dug 1846 by B. E. Hildebrand by a 25 m tunnel: a clay pot of burnt bone, a woman and a boy (or youth), bronze panels, gold, glass beakers, a tafl set, a comb, a hone [enW GU]. West mound, dug 1874: a man and animals, a Frankish sword with gold and garnets, ivory gaming pieces, Frankish cloth with gold thread, four cameos from the Middle East [enW GU]. Helmet fragments [svW F]. The middle mound is undug (?). The names Aun, Egil, Adils (and Odin, Thor, Frey) are modern labels [enW GU].
- **The thing mound** (Tingshögen): low and flat-topped, by the church [enW GU; svW F]. Its age is not known to me (?).
- **The hall on the terrace** (Kungsgårdsplatån): a built-up clay terrace carrying a hall 50-60 m long and 12 m wide, double walls lined with clay, doors 3 m wide in each long side, iron spirals laid in the post holes. It burned so hot the clay turned to brick; the diggers read the fire as deliberate [svW F]. Built about 600 and burned about 800 (?) [from memory, Ljungkvist's digs; not on the pages read]. **So in 793 it may stand, and soon after it may be a burnt terrace: do not fix a year.**
- **The post rows.** A line of great posts about 1 km long, 144 posts 5-6 m apart, each perhaps 7 m tall, and a second line of half a kilometre, found 2012-2013 [Temple]. Dated mid 400s [Temple] or about 600 [svW F]: either way old in 793; whether any still stood is unknown.
- **The grave field.** Once 2,000-3,000 mounds; a map of 1669 shows 669; about 250-300 remain [enW GU; svW F]. A pyre found in 2012 with human, horse and dog bone [svW F]. Unburnt graves of the Viking Age and **boat graves at the vicarage** [svW F; enW GU]; number and dates not checked (?).
- **Buildings under the church.** Post holes found in 1926 by Sune Lindqvist, once drawn as Adam's temple; radar in 2005 (Price and Alkarp) showed more than one building phase [Temple]. Read now as halls or an early wooden church [enW GU].
- **Valsgärde**, 3 km north on the river: boat graves from the 500s to the 1000s, a great family's burial ground, dug from the 1920s [Val].
- **The stone church** is after our years: begun about the mid 1100s; archbishop's seat 1164; burned 1240; see moved 1273 [enW GU; svW GU].
- **Not there before 1066:** no stone church, no wall, no mint, no quay. A well or spring and a grove are in Adam only [Adam].

## 3. Who could be met

No person is placed here by a writing of his own time. These are the nearest:

- **Bjorn**, king of the Svear, received Anskar at Birka in 829-830 [R 11]. At Birka, not here.
- **Anund**, a king driven out, who brought Danes against Birka (840s) [R 19, repo realm].
- **Olaf**, king about 852, who put Anskar's request to lots and to two assemblies [R 26-27, repo]. The text does not name Uppsala.
- **Eric**, a dead king whom the gods offered to take among them (about 852) [R 26, repo lore].
- **Eric the Victorious** (died about 995), who beat his nephew Styrbjorn on the Fyris plain about 985 [Fy; sagas, **late**].
- **Thorvald Hjaltason**, Icelandic skald, who made verses on that battle and was rewarded by the king [Fy, from a late tale].
- **Olof Skötkonung** (about 995-1022), baptised about 1008 (?) [repo]; Adam says he wished to pull the temple down and was stopped [Adam II.58 (?)].
- **Anund Jacob** (1022-about 1050), **Emund** (to about 1060), **Stenkil** (about 1060-1066) [repo realm].
- **Adalvard the Younger**, bishop at Sigtuna in the 1060s, and **Egino**, bishop in Skåne, who wanted the temple closed or burned; Stenkil refused, and Adalvard had to leave for Västergötland; in Bremen June 1069, dead by 1072 [Adal; Adam IV.30, repo].
- **Sigvid, "England-farer"**, who raised a stone for his father Vedjarf, early 1000s, now in the church wall (U 978) [U978].
- **Toki Gormsson**, a Dane who "did not flee at Uppsala" (DR 295, Hällestad, Skåne) [Fy].
- **Legendary only** (dead centuries before 793, matter for song): Aun, Egil, Adils; Hrolf Kraki [Fv; enW GU].
- **Ordinary people the ground shows:** weavers, smiths [svW F]; a goldsmith setting garnets on the terrace (?); farmers; the people of a hall; men who bring cod and herring inland [svW F]; in late winter, everyone (the thing).

## 4. What was traded

- **No toll, mint or market law is recorded for our years.** Silver goes by weight [BRIEF]. The first Svea coins are Sigtuna's, about 995 [repo].
- **The Disting.** Snorri: in heathen times the chief sacrifice was at Uppsala in the month of Gói (late February to March), for peace and the king's victory; all the things of the Svear and a market were held with it for a week; after the faith came it was moved to Candlemas and lasts three days [Dis; repo lore; **late**]. The name is from the *dísablót* [Dis]. What was sold is not recorded; furs and iron by sledge is inference.
- **The levy.** The Uppland law (1296) has the fleet levy for the summer settled at this thing: ships, crews, steersmen [enW GU; **late**].
- **Dues, not sales.** The estate lived on what the land round it owed in kind [Öd, medieval].
- **What the graves show came in** (all before 793): Frankish sword and cloth, eastern cameos, ivory, glass [enW GU].
- **Ships.** None of size. River boats and sledges.

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- **About 550-625.** The great mounds are raised [svW F]. Background.
- **793.** Nothing recorded. The mounds are old; the hall stands or has just gone (?).
- **About 800 (?).** The great hall burns, perhaps on purpose [svW F; date from memory].
- **829-830.** Anskar at Birka under King Bjorn [R 11]. Uppsala is not named.
- **840s.** The exiled King Anund comes against Birka with Danish ships [R 19].
- **About 852.** King Olaf, the lots, the two assemblies; the gods offer to adopt dead King Eric [R 26-27].
- **800s-900s.** Boat graves at the vicarage and at Valsgärde [svW F; Val].
- **About 985.** The battle of the Fyris plain: Eric beats Styrbjorn [Fy]. Doubted by L. Weibull; two rune stones and the verses speak for it [Fy].
- **About 1008 (?).** Olof Skötkonung baptised, in Västergötland; the Svear keep their feasts [repo].
- **Early 1000s.** Sigvid's stone (U 978) [U978].
- **1060s.** The bishops' plan against the temple fails [Adal].
- **1066.** Stenkil dies; strife follows [repo realm].
- **About 1075.** Adam writes: the feast is still held [Adam].
- **After our years:** the 1080s, King Inge driven out for refusing the sacrifice; the temple's end under him is tradition with no witness of the time [enW GU; Temple]. 1164 archbishop; 1273 the see and the name move to Östra Aros [enW GU].

## 6. Faith and custom

- **Heathen throughout.** No church is known before 1066.
- **Adam's account (about 1075, second hand):** a temple with three images, Thor in the middle, Wodan and Fricco beside him; Thor for weather and crops, Wodan for war, Fricco for peace and marriage; every ninth year a feast of all the Svear about the spring equinox, nine days long; nine males of each kind offered and hung in a grove where every tree is holy; a spring into which a living man is put; unseemly songs; nobody excused, and Christians pay to stay away; a note says a Christian saw 72 bodies; another speaks of a gold chain on the gable [Adam IV.26-27, repo lore; Temple].
- **How far to trust it.** No such building is found. Simek hears Solomon's temple in it; Janson reads it as church politics of 1075; Orchard cannot say how much is fact [Temple]. The feast, the hanging and the buying-off are likely; the gold is not [repo lore].
- **The dísablót** in late winter [Dis, **late**].
- **Burial.** Burning under a mound is the old way; unburnt graves and boat graves come in our years [svW F].
- **Kings.** The thing mound "may" have had a part in taking a king [svW F]; no rite is recorded.

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **The seed of the Fyris plain.** Hrolf Kraki, fleeing King Adils, strewed gold on the plain and the Svear got down to pick it up; poets call gold "the seed of the Fyris fields" [Fv].
- **Frey's gift.** Snorri says Frey built the temple and gave it all his lands and dues: the start of Uppsala öd [Öd, **late**].
- **The king buried with a game.** A tafl set went on the pyre in the east mound; ivory pieces in the west [enW GU].
- **The mile of posts.** 144 tall posts in a row, with hearths beside them, perhaps lit [svW F; Temple].
- **"He did not flee at Uppsala."** A Danish stone for a man who lost [Fy].
- **A house fired to brick** [svW F].
- **Easter egg, far future:** Anders Celsius lies in the church (d. 1744); a pope said Mass at the mounds in 1989 [enW GU]. Not for the game's world.

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Do not draw Adam's golden temple, least of all in 793. Do not give figures for victims as fact (72 and 27 are both in the tradition).
- Do not say the hall stands, or lies burnt, in a named year.
- Do not put Bjorn, Olaf, Anund or Anskar here: Rimbert places them at Birka.
- No king's name for 793. No priests by name; a priesthood is Adam's guess [repo lore].
- "Disting", "Fyris" and "Uppsala öd" are late words. The market and thing in late winter are likely, not proven for 793.
- Where a boat was left, and how far up a boat could go, is unknown.
- Whose bones are in the mounds is unknown.
- Unchecked: the hall's dates; the garnet workshop; the number and dates of the vicarage boat graves; Adam II.58; the pit houses' date.
