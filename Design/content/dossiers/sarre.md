# Sarre (Serræ, the Wantsum): dossier

Port `sarre`: Serræ, modern Sarre, Thanet, 51.336 N 1.236 E; realm kent, region england_south, kind market, landing beach, size 2 [ports_793.json]. Researched 4 Oct 2026. Source keys are in section 9. (?) marks what I could not check.

**Read this first.** Sarre between 793 and 1066 is known from one charter, written thirty years before the game starts, and from what sailed past it. Nothing of those years has been dug there. The rich finds are from a grave field already old in 793.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What it is.** A landing and king's toll place on the Thanet shore of the Wantsum, the tidal strait between Thanet and Kent. In 763 or 764 it is "the place called ad Serrae", and with Fordwich one of the "public places" where the king's toll-collectors take toll on ships [S29]. Forms: Serrae, Seorre [S29]; later Serre, Sarra [H].
- **Who rules.** Offa of Mercia, who rules Kent himself from 785 to 796 [K]. The toll is the king's own right [S29].
- **How big.** Not known. No house of 700-1066 is reported dug here in anything I read (?). All Thanet was 600 hides about 731 [Bede].
- **The water.** At its fullest, some 2,000 years ago, the strait was 4 km wide at the north mouth, 3 km at the south, and narrowest at Sarre, about 1,800 m; up to 12 m deep in mid-channel, with islands, and saltings bare at low water [P]. About 731 Bede calls it about three furlongs wide and crossable in two places only [Bede]. So in 793: some hundreds of metres of water between mud and salt marsh (my reading; no figure is recorded).
- **Coming in.** The north mouth (Northmouth) opens just east of Reculver; the south mouth opens by Ebbsfleet, past Richborough, with the shingle bank of Stonar in it [B63; P]. The Stour comes in nearly opposite Sarre [B63]. The tides flow in at both mouths and meet at Sarre [H]; the ebb runs harder north to south (reasoned from modern tides) [P]. It was "the usual passage for the shipping to and from London", saving the North Foreland [H; P].
- **Landmarks.** North, Reculver's minster inside the Roman fort; south, the walls of Richborough [R; P]; above the landing, the chalk down with the old grave field, "commanding the country for miles around" [B63]. Reculver and Minster are each about 5.5 km off (my measure).
- **Where boats lie.** "Under the chalk cliffs facing the west and south" [B63]; "a place where the shipping often lay at anchor" [H].
- **The crossing.** The road from Canterbury crosses into Thanet here [wpS]. The ferry is first drawn about 1414, a boatman carrying a passenger on his shoulders out to the boat [P; B63]. A boat crossing in 793 is likely, not recorded.

## 2. What stood there

- **The toll place.** No building known. The king's toll-collectors (thelonearii) worked here in 763/4 [S29].
- **The grave field on Sarre Down** (51.3396 N 1.2470 E) [C]. Found 1843 and 1860; dug by John Brent for the Kent Archaeological Society, 1863-64: 274 graves, "very far from the total"; more found in 1982 and 1991 [B68; C]. 6th and 7th centuries [C]; one grave held gold coin-pendants copying Maurice, Heraclius and Chlothar II (613-628) [RS]. Found:
  - 26 long swords, one to every ten graves, more than in any cemetery Brent knew; weapons in a quarter of the graves; a Frankish barbed spear; a snaffle-bit [B68].
  - Grave 26, a man with shield and spear: a bronze balance, 19 weights (some of them Roman coins ground down), a lock and keys, a purse mount [B66].
  - Grave 4, a woman: six gold pendants, gold braid, 133 amber beads, a crystal ball slung in silver-gilt, a garnet-set spoon pierced with nine holes in a cross, a glass cup, a fossil sea-urchin [B63].
  - Grave 6: about fifty bone gaming counters. Grave 198: forty more, and two dice [B66; B68].
  - The Sarre Brooch: silver-gilt, three doves on it, 5th century; British Museum [Q].
  - Roman pits with pottery, burnt bone and many oyster shells [B63].
- **Not there in the game's years.** A church (St Giles is scheduled as 11th-century [C] (?) and is not in Domesday [B63]); a mint [repo roles]; a wall; a chartered market (Henry III) [H]; the bridge (1485) [P].
- **Near, and real:**
  - **Minster-in-Thanet**: St Mary's, and the church of Peter and Paul that Abbess Eadburh built before 748 for St Mildrith's body [S91]. Its ships: one bought from a man named Leubucus [S91]; one "lately built in the minster" by 763/4 [S29].
  - **Reculver**: St Mary's minster, founded 669; some fifty sceattas found, matched in England only at Hamwic; its own ship toll-free at Fordwich from 747 [R; S1612].
  - **Salt and fish**, 1086: a salt-pit and two fisheries at Minster, a fishery and a salt work at Monkton [HM; HMo].

## 3. Who could be met

No person of Sarre is named in any text from 793 to 1066. Tied to the strait and the island:

- **Kings over Kent**: Offa (died 29 July 796) [repo chronicle]; Eadberht Præn, 796-798; Cuthred, 798-807; Coenwulf, to 821; Baldred, driven out 825; Ecgberht of Wessex and his son Æthelwulf, from 825; Æthelstan, under-king, 839-851 [K].
- **Archbishops**: Æthelheard, 793-805, who fled Eadberht in 796 [repo chronicle]; Wulfred, 805-832, who fought Coenwulf for Minster and Reculver [W].
- **Abbesses of Minster**: Selethryth, abbess of Lyminge in 804 [repo chronicle], said to have held Minster about 800 (?) [MP]. Cwenthryth, Coenwulf's daughter, named 811 to about 827, abbess of Winchcombe, Reculver and Minster, who lost the Kentish houses to Wulfred by 827 [Cw]. Leofrun, taken by the Danes at Canterbury in 1011 [ASC], counted the last [M]. The abbess of 793 is not known.
- **Ealhhere**, ealdorman of Kent, named 839-853: took nine ships at Sandwich with King Æthelstan in 851; killed on Thanet in 853 with Huda of Surrey [ASC; E].
- **Edgar**, 969; **Thorkell**, 1009; **Cnut**, 1027-1030; **Lothen and Yrling**, 1048; **Godwin and Harold**, 1052 (section 5).
- **Ordinary people the evidence shows**: the king's toll-collectors and agents (thelonearii, actionarii) [S29]; the minster's shipmen and the wrights who built its ship [S29]; nuns and priests; fishermen and salt-boilers (1086) [HM; HMo]; ploughmen on Thanet's "fruitful fields" [T]; later, ferrymen [P].

## 4. What was traded

- **No cargo is recorded at Sarre**, only toll on ships. Eadberht II gives the nuns of Minster "the toll on the carriage of two ships" that was his by right at Sarre, and on a third "all tribute and toll" his toll-collectors rightly demand at Fordwich and Sarre [S29, my translation]. Toll went by the ship, as at London [S86].
- **The king's first look.** If the third ship is replaced, whatever the nuns gain "by their trading in goods of various kinds" they must offer to the king, with the ship, at Fordwich [S29].
- **The run.** The minster was freed at London too: one ship about 733, half the toll on another in 748, confirmed by Offa 761-764 [S86; S91; S143]. Its ships worked Thanet, the Wantsum and the Thames.
- **Goods**, from the game's regional table, not from Sarre: wool, cloth, hides out; wine, querns, pottery, glass in [repo trade]. Of Thanet: corn [T]; salt and fish (1086) [HM].
- **Coin.** No mint [repo roles]; Offa's heavy penny from about 792/3 [repo chronicle].
- **Law.** A man of Kent buying in Lundenwic needs two or three good witnesses or the king's wic-reeve [HlE].
- **Seasons, fairs, ships.** None recorded; the 14 October fair is of Hasted's day [H]. The game's traffic table guesses two to six ships passing a day in season (?) [repo traffic].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 763 or 764: the toll charter [S29].
- 796-798: Eadberht Præn holds Kent; Coenwulf lays it waste in 798 [repo chronicle].
- 811-827: Wulfred against Coenwulf, then Cwenthryth, over Minster and Reculver [W; Cw].
- 825: Kent passes to Ecgberht of Wessex [K].
- 851: nine ships taken at Sandwich; the heathens winter on Thanet for the first time [ASC].
- 853: Ealhhere and Huda fight on Thanet; "many men slain and drowned on either hand" [ASC].
- 865: the army sits on Thanet, takes a promise of money, then steals inland by night and overruns east Kent [ASC].
- 949: Eadred gives Reculver, with its land in Thanet, to Christ Church [S546; R]; some doubt the charter.
- 969: "King Edgar ordered all Thanet-land to be plundered" [ASC]. Roger of Wendover says 974, and why: the islanders had robbed merchants from York [Ed].
- 980: Thanet overrun [ASC].
- 1009: Thorkell's army lands at Sandwich; East Kent pays 3,000 pounds [repo chronicle].
- 1011: Canterbury falls; Abbess Leofrun is taken [ASC].
- 1023: Cnut grants the port of Sandwich to Christ Church [S959]; the text as it stands is held a later forgery.
- 1027-1030: Cnut gives Minster's lands and St Mildrith's body to St Augustine's; she is moved in 1030 against fierce local opposition [SA; M].
- 1048 (Chronicle E, 1046): Lothen and Yrling, 25 ships, strip Sandwich, then go about Thanet, where "the land's-folk strenuously withstood them, and denied them as well landing as water" [ASC].
- 1052: Godwin and Harold go from Sandwich "to North-mouth, and so toward London" [ASC]: through the Wantsum past Sarre, as Brent and Perkins read it [B63; P].
- After the game: salt works in mid-channel about 1150-75; the channel dry by the 16th century [P; Wa].

## 6. Faith and custom

- **Church.** Thanet is Christian since Augustine landed at Ebbsfleet in 597 [Bede; T]. Western Thanet belonged to Reculver's minster, and Sarre's later parish, St Nicholas, was cut out of Reculver's in 1310 [R]; so Sarre in 793 probably looked to St Mary's at Reculver (my inference).
- **Saints.** St Mildrith of Minster, 13 July; her translation, 18 May, from 1030 [M]. St Eadburh, in December [MP].
- **The old ways.** The dead on the down lie with swords, amber, a crystal ball and a sea-urchin charm, to the mid 7th century [B63]. Wihtred's law of 695 fines gifts to idols and meat eaten in a fast, and frees a slave made to work on the Sabbath [Wi].
- **The stranger's horn.** By the same law a stranger who leaves the track and does not blow his horn may be killed or taken [Wi].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **The hind.** King Ecgberht paid for two murdered princes with as much of Thanet as their sister's tame hind could run round. Thunor, the king's man who killed them, was swallowed by the earth. The hind's track still parted Minster's land from Monkton's [KRL; HM; T].
- **No snakes.** Isidore of Seville, about 620: Thanet is named from the death of serpents; it has none, and its earth carried to any land kills them there [T].
- **The money-changer.** The editor of 1866 on the man of grave 26: his weapons were "no doubt, a very essential part of his stock-in-trade" [B66].
- **The ship clause.** The charter provides for a ship "broken and shattered, or worn out with great age, or, which God forbid, lost by shipwreck" [S29, my translation].
- **Ruim**: the Britons' name for Thanet [T]. **Mildrith's footprint**: in a rock at Ebbsfleet, where she stepped ashore from Francia [KRL].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Any building, person, cargo, price, rate of toll or market day at Sarre between 793 and 1066. The toll stands in one charter of 763/4; nothing says it was still taken in 793, or that it had stopped.
- Which toll lay where. The catalogue's summary puts the two ships at Fordwich; the Latin says Sarre. I follow the Latin.
- The ferry-toll gift. Lewis, Hasted and Brent read the charter as the toll of two ferry boats, by "Egbert" or in 726. The reading now is toll on two ships, Eadberht II, 763/4 [S29].
- A "port reeve" at Sarre: Hawkes's phrase (1982), cited by Perkins [P], not opened. The charter names toll-collectors only. Name no reeve as historical.
- The ferry before about 1414; Bede's "two places"; the width in 793; what the name means.
- The scales, swords and jewels are of the 6th and 7th centuries, not of 793. No mounds are recorded.
- That raiders came by the Wantsum to Canterbury in 839 [Wa; repo water]: I could not find it in the Chronicle (?).
- That "North-mouth" in 1052 is the Wantsum's: Ingram prints "the Nore" [ASC].
- When Minster was burnt: late tradition gives 978, 988 and 1011 [HM; HT], another "about 855", yet Asser about 893 writes of a minster still there [MP].
- Reculver's twin towers are 12th century (memory (?)): not to be drawn.
- An end. Sarre has none in the period. Sandwich (Sondwic, first named 851 [Sw]) grows into the port at the south mouth; Sarre is later a limb of it [H].
