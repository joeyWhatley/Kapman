# Duiblinn (Dublin): dossier

Port `duiblinn`: Duiblinn, modern Dublin, 53.344 N 6.27 W; realm irish, region ireland, kind monastery, landing beach, size 1 [ports]. Researched 6 Oct 2026. Source keys are in section 9. **Read this first.** There are four places under one id. To 841: an Irish church by a pool and a ford village, with no town, no coin and no market. 841 to 902: the heathens' ship-fort and its kings. 902 to 917: empty of Northmen. 917 to 1066: Dyflin, a town of plots and plank paths, the richest on its sea, under Irish over-kings from 980 and an Irish king from 1052 [repo sea].

## 1. The place in 793

- **What it is.** Two settlements. **Áth Cliath**, "the ford of hurdles", a village of farmers and fishermen on the ridge where two of the great roads cross, about 100 m from the ford. **Duiblinn**, "black pool", a church settlement about half a kilometre south-east, founded by the early 7th century: a pear-shaped enclosure of about 5 hectares, one of the largest of its kind in Ireland [T795]. Its line survives in St Peter's Row, Whitefriar Street and Stephen Street; some now place it at St Michael le Pole instead [T795].
- **Who rules.** The Liffey is believed to have been the border between Leinster and Mide [T795]. South of it the Uí Dúnlainge hold Leinster's kingship (738 to 1042); the church probably served a small sept of theirs, the Uí Fergusa [T795]. King of Leinster in 793: Bran Ardchenn, burned in a house with his queen in 795 by Fínsnechta, who follows him (?). North of the river is Brega, under the king of Tara: Donnchad (d. 797), then Áed Oirdnide, who wastes Leinster twice in a month in 804 [repo sea] (?).
- **Is the church alive in 793?** Doubtful. No abbot or bishop is named after Siadal (Sedulius), whose death the Four Masters give under 785; the house may have been given up before the Northmen came [ESD; T795].
- **How big.** Not known. A village and a church enclosure; no figure exists. Size 1 is fair.
- **Coming in.** A wide open bay of sand with a shifting bar; no Bull Island, no walls [repo water]. Howth head (Benn Étair) stands to the north, with the island of Rechru (Lambay (?)) beyond it [ESD]. The river is tidal between soft banks, perhaps 100 to 150 m wide at high water, tea-brown from the bogs; springs range about 3.85 m (modern); the tide runs about 4 km above the pool, to Islandbridge [repo water]. The Poddle comes in from the south and widens into the dark pool, where the Castle gardens now are [T795].
- **Where boats lie.** Not recorded for 793. The pool and the strand below it are the natural places (my inference). The ford, of hurdles, lay about 100 m west of the present Father Mathew Bridge [T795]: nothing with a keel passes it at low water (my inference).

## 2. What stood there

- **The church enclosure of Duiblinn** (to 841 at latest), by St Peter's "on the Mount" or St Michael le Pole [T795].
- **St Michael le Pole**: a church said to exist by the late 7th century, later with a round tower [T795].
- **St Bride's**: graves from the 8th century [T795]. **St Columba's**, where St Audoen's stands, and **St Mo Lua's chapel** at the road junction [T795]. **St Martin's**: the one old church later inside the walls [CC].
- **The ford and the roads**: Slige Midluachra from Ulster crossing at the hurdles, Slige Mhór from the west, Slige Chualann south, Slige Dála south-west [T795].
- **Neighbour churches, a morning's walk**: Cell Maignenn (Kilmainham), there by 606 [KM]; Tallaght, Máel Ruain's house, land perhaps granted 774 [MR]; Finglas, Clondalkin, Swords [ESD]; Glasnevin and Lusk (?).
- **The ship-fort, 841.** No trace of it has been dug; candidates are the Castle and pool, Temple Bar West and Usher's Island [ESD].
- **Warrior graves by the pool**: five young men at Ship Street Great and South Great George's Street, radiocarbon 9th century; two grew up in Scandinavia, two in the British Isles [ESD].
- **The grave fields of Kilmainham and Islandbridge**: dozens of heathen burials with goods, 9th century, the largest such cemetery in western Europe outside Scandinavia [KM]. Swords, spearheads, oval brooches, folding scales and weights, a smith's tongs and hammer; found in gravel and railway works from the 1830s (?). About a hundred Norse burials are known around Dublin in all [ESD].
- **Amlaíb's fort at Clondalkin**, burned in 867 with a hundred of his men killed [ESD].
- **The town, 917 on: Wood Quay and Fishamble Street** (dug 1973 to 1981 by Patrick Wallace). About a hundred houses on four acres; over two hundred across the town [WQ; ESD]. Post-and-wattle houses of 10 to 50 square metres, a central hearth, two side benches, four roof posts [ESD]. Fourteen fenced plots along Fishamble Street, rebuilt on the same lines some thirteen times between about 920 and 1100 (?).
- **The banks.** An earthen bank of about 950; a stone wall about 1100 [WQ]. Earlier and later banks, about 900 and 1000 (?).
- **The Thingmote**: the assembly mound, 40 feet high and 240 feet round, south of the river; levelled in 1685 [HD]. Written of only later [repo saints]. **The Long Stone (Steyne)**, set up at the landing east of the town, stood into the 17th century (?).
- **The mint**, from about 995 [KD]. No building is known.
- **Holy Trinity (Christ Church)**, founded probably after 1028 by King Sitriuc with Dúnán as first bishop; of wood; on the height over Wood Quay; endowed with Baldoyle, Raheny and Portrane [CC]. **St Olave's**, after 1030 (?).
- **The weir of Clontarf** and **Dubgall's Bridge** over the Tolka, named in the tale of 1014 [Clon].
- **A shipyard**: the 30 m, sixty-oar warship Skuldelev 2 was built in the Dublin area about 1042, of oak, proved by tree rings [Sk].

## 3. Who could be met

**Before 841.** Siadal, abbot of Duiblinn (d. about 785 to 790) [T795]. Máel Ruain of Tallaght, "bishop and soldier of Christ", died 792; Airerán follows him [MR]. Óengus the Culdee, his pupil, writing his calendar of saints about 800 [MR] (?). Dublitir of Finglas, died 796 (?). Cellach, abbot of Iona, passing to Kells from 807 [repo spine]. Ordinary people: monks, the church's tenants, fishermen, herdsmen, a ferryman at the ford (inference; none is named).

**Kings of the Foreigners** [KD]: Amlaíb (c. 853 to 871), Ímar (c. 857 to 873), Auisle (c. 863 to 867); Bárid (873 to 881); Sichfrith, Sitriuc; Ímar II, driven out 902. Sitriuc Cáech (917 to 921); Gothfrith (921 to 934); Amlaíb son of Gothfrith (934 to 941); Blácaire (941 to 945); **Amlaíb Cuarán**, "sandal" (945 to 947, 952 to 980), baptised in England 943 [AC]; Glúniairn (980 to 989); **Sitriuc Silkbeard** (989 or 995 to 1036; died 1042), son of Amlaíb Cuarán and Gormflaith, husband of Brian's daughter Sláine [SS]; Echmarcach (1036 to 1038, 1046 to 1052); Ímar son of Aralt (1038 to 1046); Murchad son of Diarmait (1052 to 1070).

**Others.** Turgeis, drowned 845 [repo sea]. Cerball of Leinster and Máel Finnia of Brega, who took the fort in 902 [ESD]. Niall Glúndub, king of Tara, killed at Islandbridge 919 [KM; repo sea]. Máel Sechnaill, victor of 980 and 989; Brian, in the town at New Year 1000 [repo sea]. Gormflaith (d. 1030 (?)). Bishop Dúnán (d. 1074 (?)) [CC]. Diarmait of Leinster, lord from 1052; Harold Godwinson, wintering 1051 to 1052 [repo sea]. The poet Cináed ua hArtacáin (d. 975), who praised Amlaíb Cuarán (?). The Icelander Gunnlaug Serpent-tongue at Sitriuc's hall, about 1002, by a 13th-century saga (?).

**Ordinary people the digs show**: comb-makers, amber and jet workers, leather workers and shoemakers, coopers and turners, smiths, shipwrights, weavers, merchants with scales [WQ] (?). Moneyers sign the pennies; Færemin is one name (?).

## 4. What was traded

- **In 793: almost nothing by sea.** No town, no coin, no toll; a stranger trades only under a king's or abbot's protection, for a gift and first choice [repo sea]. Worth is counted in cows, séts and ounces [repo gaelic]. Wine for the Mass comes from Gaul by the western road [repo sea]. The fairs are inland: Tailtiu at the start of August, Carman in Leinster [repo sea].
- **841 to 902.** Captives and church silver out; grain and wine wanted [repo sea]. In 871 two hundred ships bring English, Britons and Picts [repo sea]. Silver goes by weight, as arm-rings and cut pieces [repo coin].
- **917 to 1066.** Out: slaves, hides and leather, wool cloaks, combs, ships. In: wine, salt, English and French pots, walrus ivory, amber, soapstone, jet, silk caps and scarves [WQ; repo cloth; repo furs]. Lead weights to a northern ounce of about 26 g (?). Partners: Chester (four pence a load, the king's first sight of marten skins), Bristol, York, the Isles, Norway, Iceland [repo sea; repo furs].
- **Coin.** Pennies from about 995 to 997, copies of Æthelred's, reading SIHTRIC REX DYFLIN (?) [KD; SS]; lighter after about 1020 and staying at home [repo sea].
- **Dues.** The king's man on the strand, amount unknown; from 989 an ounce of gold from every plot each Christmas night to the king of Tara [repo sea; repo law].
- **Law.** No Sunday loading in an Irish king's land from 887; no Irish slaves held after 980 [repo sea].
- **Season.** Captives come in autumn after the raiding; hides in winter after the slaughter [repo seasons; repo food].

## 6. Faith and custom

- **To 841**: the Irish church. Patrick (17 March), Brigid (1 February), Columba (9 June), Kevin (3 June) [repo saints]; Máel Ruain (7 July) [MR]. Tallaght's reformers, the Céli Dé, are strict; Máel Ruain forbade his monks to go abroad as pilgrims [MR]. Burial unburnt, head west, no goods [repo gaelic]. Church ground is sanctuary; Adomnán's law guards women, clerics and children [repo sea].
- **841 to about 950**: heathen. Graves with swords and scales [KM]. Thor is "Tomar": his ring, his wood [repo gods].
- **After**: Amlaíb Cuarán baptised 943 and dead a penitent on Iona [AC]; a bishop from about 1028, answering for a time to Canterbury, not to the Irish church [CC].
- **Irish law**: a stranger from over sea has no honour-price without a protector; a bargain without sureties is void [repo sea].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- The Irish and the Norse each took one of the two names: Dyflin from Duiblinn [KD], Áth Cliath for the Irish to this day.
- In 989 the besieged drank sea water [repo sea].
- Ímar died in 873 of a "sudden horrible disease" [ESD].
- Children's toy boats, and runes on wood and bone, came out of Wood Quay [WQ].
- The tide of 1014 was reckoned in 1860: high water about 5.30 in the morning and 5.55 in the evening, as the tale needs [Clon].
- A ship built here ended on the bottom of Roskilde fjord [Sk].
- The old tale that the pool is named for a drowned woman, Dub, and the hurdles laid for a Leinster king (?).

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Where the fort of 841 stood, and whether the church was still alive in 793 [ESD].
- Any name for an abbot after Siadal; any quay, market or toll before 841; any population figure. "Several thousands" for the fort is a guess [ESD].
- The "bishops of Dublin" Livinus, Disibod, Wiro, Rumold and the Cormac of 890 are late legend [T795]. Patrick baptising a king of Dublin is 12th-century invention (?).
- The name Baile Átha Cliath is not written before the 14th century [T795].
- Do not call the Northmen Vikings, or the place a town before 917. No Christ Church before 1028, no coin before 995, no Thingmote stated as fact before the later texts.
- The tide, the wall-watching and the bridge at Clontarf come from a 12th-century tale, not the annals [Clon].
- That Irish slaves were never again held after 980 is the annalist's boast, not proof.
