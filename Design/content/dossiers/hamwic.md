# Hamwic (Southampton, the St Mary's shore): dossier

Keys in brackets: see Sources. (?) = not checked on a page I opened. Chronicle years are corrected; the annal's own number follows the key. SOU numbers are the city's excavation codes.

## 1. The place in 793

- **Name.** *Hamwic* is the trading shore, *Hamtun* the king's estate and head of the shire: one place [R; M]. "Hamwih" is a German scribe's spelling; *Suðhamtun* is first written about 1050 [R].
- **What.** The West Saxons' one great trading town: open, timber-built, on the Itchen's west bank around St Mary's [M]. A "mart" by about 721 [HW]; "the royal vill called Hamtun" in 840 [S288]. 42 ha for certain, perhaps 52, when villages were 2-4 ha [M].
- **Rule.** King Beorhtric of Wessex (786-802), husband since 789 of Offa's daughter Eadburh [W4; CHR]. A king's reeve is assumed; none is named [M]. Bishop: Cyneberht of Winchester [W5].
- **People.** At most 2,000-3,000 [M]. Craftsmen and traders, not farmers, fed on old plough oxen and old wool sheep sent in, probably from the king's estates; almost no game [M; BC; EA].
- **Water.** A low tongue of brickearth between Itchen and Test, both tidal for 6 km. They meet in Southampton Water, which reaches the Solent 12 km on, behind Wight [M]. From Calshot up: 13 km of "good and even soundings", soft mud flats either side "safe for any body to rest on", no rocks (a seventeenth-century account) [M]. The tide is double: high water stands for hours, the ebb takes under four hours in twelve; range about 4 m [M]. Bede knew the two tides that meet off the Hamble [B].
- **Landmarks.** No tower. The Roman walls of Clausentum stand on the east bank in the river's bend, 1.4 km upstream [M]. South of the town: salt marsh, a shingle spit, a small lagoon [M].
- **Where boats lie.** Drawn up on a shelving beach, the *wic-hythe*, east of St Mary's at the foot of what is now Chapel Road [M; EA; S1012]. No quay or jetty is known; the waterfront is undug [M].

## 2. What stood there

- **Streets.** Gravel, straight, about 5 m wide, laid before the houses, re-metalled up to five times. A main street north-south (St Mary Street and St Mary's Road; perhaps 15 m wide at Six Dials), cross streets, three streets down to the river, a strand street along the shore [M].
- **West ditch.** 3 m wide, 1.5 m deep, dug soon after 700 and soon filled. In 793 nothing defends the town [M; G].
- **Houses.** 10-12 m by 4-5 m: earth-fast posts, wattle and daub, one storey, thatch, a hearth, the long side to the street, an open shed for work. Door keys, lamps of pot and stone [M; AH2]. Six Dials alone gave some 60-68 buildings, 21 wells, 500 pits [W1; W2] (?).
- **Wells and pits.** Wells up to 6 m deep, three lined with old casks; cess and rubbish pits behind every house [M; AH2; EA]. A spring at Houndwell, 330 m west [M].
- **Workshops**, house by house, not by quarter [M]. Smithies: two at Six Dials, slag at six other spots; iron came in as bloom [M]. Bronze: ingot moulds, crucibles and pin wire near Chapel Road [M]; a silver crucible at Cook Street [G]. Bone: by Chapel Road 1,668 offcuts, mostly cattle bone, and a whale's backbone used as a chopping block; combs, pins, whorls [M; EA]. Weaving everywhere: loom weights, pin-beaters, wool combs [M].
- **Shore church** (SOU 13). Timber, two rooms, 12.9 by 4.2 m, perhaps glazed, in a graveyard of about a hundred, its rows dug over five times; perhaps a standing cross before it [M].
- **St Mary's.** Where the streets meet above the hythe: probably the main graveyard from about 750, the *mynster æt Wic* of 1045, mother church of Southampton since [M; EA; S1012]. Nothing early has been seen [M].
- **Old grave plots**, built over by 793: SOU 32 (a ring-ditched mound, a coin by one head); SOU 20 (two men with spears, one with a long seax); Cook Street (nine graves, three ring-ditches, mounds left standing) [M; G]; the stadium site (burials and cremations, said to be seventh-century with weapons and gold) [W11; W2] (?).
- **SOU 47**, north end: bronze spoons, a fork-spoon; guessed to be the king's enclosure; lived in into the tenth century [M].
- **Not found:** market place, mint, hall, toll-house [M].
- **Later.** From about 900 a ditched town of 6 ha on the Test shore, thriving after 950; perhaps a fort with 188 m of wall at Clausentum (Burghal Hidage, 150 hides) [M; W6].

## 3. Who could be met

- **Kings.** Beorhtric (786-802) [W4]. Ecgberht (802-839), at *Omtune* on 26 December 825 [S273]. Æthelwulf (839-858), who gave land to his thegn Duda "in the royal vill called Hamtun" in 840 [S288]. Edgar (959-975), who gave Abingdon his royal dues at *Suðhamtun* (962, doubted) [S701] and gave the nun Wulfhild a church at Hamtun in amends for trying to seduce her [M; W10]. Cnut, chosen king here in 1016 [FW]. Edward the Confessor, who fought near here in 1036 [W8].
- **Ealdormen.** Wulfheard, victor over 33 ship-crews at Hamtun in 840, dead that year [ASC 837]. Osric (860) and Wulfred (d. by 896) of Hampshire [ASC 860, 897].
- **Northmen.** Olaf Tryggvason and Swein Forkbeard, wintering 994-995; Bishop Ælfheah and Ealdorman Æthelweard came to lead Olaf to the king at Andover [ASC 994; FW].
- **Bishops of Winchester** [W5]: Cyneberht (to 801/3), Ealhmund, Wigthegn (d. 836), Helmstan, Swithun (852/3-862/5), Denewulf (878/9-908), Frithestan, Æthelwold (963-984), Ælfheah (984-1006), Ælfwine (1032-1047), Stigand.
- **Moneyers** of the West Saxon mint, here or at Winchester: Ecghard and Peochthun for Beorhtric; Eanwald, Beornheard, Bosa, Ifa, Tideman, Tilred for Ecgberht [D].
- **Not Hamwic's.** Beaduheard, the king's reeve at Dorchester, killed at Portland about 789 by three shiploads of Northmen he took for traders and ordered to the king's vill [W3; M]. It shows a reeve's work.
- **Ordinary people:** smiths, pin-casters, comb-makers, weavers, potters, leather-workers, butchers, well-diggers; shore fishers of eel, flounder, bass and mullet; oyster and winkle gatherers [M; BC; AH2]. Frankish traders [HO], perhaps Frisians [AH2]. Ship-masters taking fares; pilgrims [HW]. One possible name, in runes: Cat(t) or Catte [AH2].

## 4. What was traded

- **In.** Pottery from northern France, chiefly the Seine valley near Rouen: black and grey table wares, 18% of all pottery by weight, 80% in one pit near the hythe; under ten Rhenish pots in 270 [M; HO]. Tating ware, probably by Dorestad [AH2]. Eifel lava querns, bits in nearly every pit [M; AH2]. Glass vessels, over 200 finds [AH2]. Wine, fish or salt in casks; whetstones from Brittany and Purbeck; iron bloom, antler [M].
- **Out.** Little shows [M]. Wool and cloth are likely: sheep were kept old for the fleece [M; BC]. Lead and tin are the project's inference [RTS]. Passengers paid fares [HW]. Slaves are often claimed; nothing here shows it [AH2].
- **Where.** Rouen: a day and a night at 4 knots, wind on the beam [M]. Up the Seine lay the October fair of Saint-Denis, whose charters of 709 and 753 name "Saxon" traders [M]. Quentovic only at second hand, and Dorestad is half as far again and upwind: Boniface went to London to find a ship for each [M]. Yet 25 sceattas of the Low Countries lay in the pits [M].
- **Coin.** Its own sceattas (types 39, 49) from the 720s, perhaps to Cynewulf's day (757-786) [M; AH1]. Over 150 stray coins, six in seven sceattas; then pennies of Offa, Coenwulf and the West Saxon kings, two deniers of Charlemagne, one of Charles the Bald from Toulouse [M; AH1]. In 793 the coin in hand is Offa's [W4]; Beorhtric's own, three known, perhaps come after 796 [D].
- **Toll and law.** No toll, fair or market charter survives. Æthelstan (about 928) makes Hamtun a port with two moneyers and forbids buying above 20 pence outside a port [CHR; M].
- **Seasons.** Willibald sailed at summer's end [HW]; wool in June is the project's guess [RTS].
- **Ships.** Clinker-built, sailed, rowed in shoal water, beached [M; HW]; clench nails in the town [AH2]. Oak felled in 769 near Hamwic went into an English ship whose planks ended at Domburg [WAL].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- **Before 793.** Founded about 700-710, probably under Ine: oak felled soon after 704 [M]. *Hamtunscir* by 757 [ASC 755]. "Homwic" is listed among towns burnt in 764; no burnt layer has been found [M].
- **802** Beorhtric dies; Ecgberht comes home from Francia [CHR].
- **825, 26 December** Ecgberht at *Omtune* [S273].
- **840** Wulfheard beats 33 ship-crews "at Hamtun" [ASC 837]; Æthelwulf's charter in the royal vill [S288]. The West Saxon mint stops about now [D].
- **842** Northmen sack Quentovic, cross the sea and plunder "Hamwig and Nordhunnwig" [NI]. The Chronicle is silent; Morton thinks Nithard muddled it with 840 [M].
- **c. 850** The town is largely empty; hoards are buried about 850 and 870 [M]. Hodges ends it about 840 [M; HO], Addyman and Hill about 940-970 [AH1].
- **860** A fleet storms Winchester; Osric and the men of Hampshire help beat it [ASC].
- **c. 880-920** The Burghal Hidage rates Hamtun at 150 hides [W6; M].
- **c. 900-950** A new ditched town rises to the south-west, on the Test shore [M].
- **c. 928** Two moneyers allowed at Hamtun [CHR].
- **c. 973-1015** Pennies signed both HAMTUN and HAMWIC [R; AH1].
- **980** "Southampton plundered by a pirate-army, and most of the population slain or imprisoned" [ASC, Ingram].
- **994-995** Olaf and Swein, come to London with 94 ships, winter here, fed by Wessex; 16,000 pounds are paid [ASC; FW].
- **1016** The bishops, abbots and ealdormen come to Cnut at Southampton, forswear Æthelred's line and swear to him; London chooses Edmund [FW].
- **c. 1020-1025** The mint closes for good [AH1].
- **1045** King Edward's charters name the minster at Wic, the wic-hythe and a haw in Hamtun [S1012; S1008; AH1].
- **1066** King Edward had 76 (or 79) men here; by 1086, 65 Frenchmen and 31 Englishmen had been settled [DB; VCH].

## 6. Faith and custom

- All graves found are Christian: heads west, pegged coffins; yet into the 700s a glass cup, a coin, a knife or a spear went in [M; AH2].
- About 750 burial moves from small plots outside the houses to churchyards inside; old plots are built over, the bones thrown out with rubbish [M].
- Two infants lay in rubbish pits: unbaptised, or worse [M].
- Churches: St Mary's and the shore church; Leland (about 1540) saw a small chapel of St Nicholas "where the old parish church of Old Hampton stood" [M].
- Minsters near: Nursling, 6 km off, once Boniface's (said destroyed 878 [W9] (?)); Bishop's Waltham, Willibald's; Abbot Cyneberht's, who in 686 baptised two princes of Wight before Cædwalla killed them [B; M].
- Swithun's feast 2 July, translation 15 July 971 (?). Fish and fowl "perhaps for fast days" [EA].
- No heathen shrine, amulet or grave. Bede calls this shore Jutish [B].
- Law: Ine's code [M]; a ceorl's wergild is 200 shillings [ENG].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- A cow's toe bone cut with four runes, *catæ*: "cat", or someone called Cat(t) or Catte; one rune looks Frisian [AH2].
- A green turtle's bone from African waters; the handle of a classical cup; a pot gritted with north Irish rock; a Stone Age flint axe kept as a curio [M; AH2; EA].
- A rabbit's shoulder blade in a well, when England bred no rabbits [M].
- A bronze fork and spoon in one [M]. A comb scratched like a fish [AH2]. Eel bones crushed by human teeth [EA].
- Eleven sceattas "in a little box", as new [AH1]; forty-three in a pit at Kingsland [HO].
- Willibald's crossing: fares paid to the master, oars creaking, a north-west wind, tents by the Seine at Rouen [HW; M].
- Cnut and the tide: Henry of Huntingdon names no place; Southampton's claim is modern [W7].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- No townsman, reeve, priest or merchant of Hamwic is named in 793.
- Whether a mint, toll, fair or fixed market existed in 793; where the king's hall stood; whether Offa's ban of about 790 reached here [SP].
- Whether the fire of 764 or the raid of 842 happened [M]. "Nordhunnwig" is Northam, or a garbled "north of Hamwic" [R; M]; [CHR] suggests Norwich.
- When it ended. The port row runs to 1066 unbroken; the evidence wants a near-empty shore from about 850 and a smaller Hamtun on the Test from about 900-950. Its note says "trading with Quentovic and the Seine": the Seine is sure, Quentovic indirect [M].
- No walls, quay, jetty, stone church or tower. No "Southampton" before about 980; never "Hamwih" [R].
- No Northmen living here: no Norwegian hones, hog-backed combs or long-tanged knives [AH2].
- Not as fact: Birinus founding St Mary's in 634 [W14]; a mint "786-858" [W1]; slaves shipped to Rouen [W1]; Cnut "crowned" here; more than 3,000 people.
- Quote only Ingram 1823 or Forester 1854; Talbot's Willibald (1954) is modern.
