# Doccinga (Dokkum): dossier

Port `doccinga`: Doccinga, now Dokkum, 53.326 N 5.999 E; realm franks, county austrachia (Oostergo), region frisia, kind village, landing beach, size 1, no end year [P]. Researched 4 Oct 2026. Source keys are spelled out in section 9. (?) marks what I could not check.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What it is.** A wooden church on a raised mound in the salt marsh, with a clergy house and a graveyard, kept as the place where Boniface was killed in 754. South of it, touching it, a dwelling mound lies on the north bank of a sea arm [BP]. It is a mission church of Utrecht and a pilgrims' place, not a market [BP]. Whether anyone lived here before the church is unknown: Halbertsma thought not, and no dig has found a settlement [BP].
- **Who rules.** Frankish since Charles Martel's victory on the Boorne in 734 [enwp Boarn]. A count holds the district (Ostrache, Hostraga: Oostergo) for the king; Abba did under Pippin, before 768 [W ch. 9]. The count of 793 is not named. The church is the bishop of Utrecht's: Hamacar, about 790-806 [BP; nlwp Bisschoppen].
- **Who lives there.** Clergy from Utrecht, and Frisian stock farmers on the mounds around [BP]. No numbers exist.
- **Coming in.** Unembanked salt marsh, cut by winding creeks and flooded by the sea; dikes come only in the 11th century [BP]. Several creeks (Paesens, Zuider Ee, Dokkumer Ee, Hantumervaart) meet here in a narrow sea arm, the later Grootdiep, which runs east to the Lauwers mouth, a bay still forming about 800 [BP; nlwp Grootdiep; nlwp Humsterland]. The Ee led only to the sea; the cut to Leeuwarden is of about 1240-1250 [DB; nlwp Ee]. From the south the way is down the Rhine and over the Almere, as Boniface came and as his body went back under sail [W ch. 8]. No bar, pilot, sea-mark or quay is recorded. Landmarks are mounds: the church mound, 3-4 m of sods, and Aalsum just north, 5.3 m above sea level, about 4 ha [BP; nlwp Aalsum]. Willibald gives the spring and neap tides as the reason for the mound, and says fresh water is short in almost all Frisia [W ch. 9]. The arm's depth, width and tide are guesses [WL].

## 2. What stood there

- **The martyr's mound** (today's Markt). Raised after 754 by the church and "a great part of the Frisian folk", Count Abba directing by Pippin's order [W ch. 9]. Built in one go, 3-4 m of salt-marsh sods; ordinary mounds grew in stages [BP]. A protected monument: it holds the remains of wooden, tuff and brick memorial churches with their burials [RM].
- **The wooden church.** Built after the mound [W ch. 9]; dedicated to St Paul and St Boniface, as Alcuin's verses for Liudger's church say [W ch. 9 note, citing MGH Poetae I 304]. Timber traces were found in soil research, 1950-1978 [DB; BP]. Burned in 784 and rebuilt (?): no source I read names it.
- **The clergy's house** on the mound [W ch. 9]; later a bishop's community from Utrecht, the seat of church rule and church law for the district [BP].
- **The graveyard** on the mound [BP; RM].
- **The spring** (section 7). A 9th-century Utrecht Life puts it beside the church, on the mound; perhaps a pond fed by the fresh water that gathers under a mound [BP]. Today's well south of the town is the old brewers' well, wrongly identified in the 1920s [enwp Dokkum].
- **The village mound.** Long, its high street square to the water, as at Leeuwarden. Age unknown; digs on its west flank found nothing before the late 12th century [BP].
- **The landing** on the arm below it: nothing found [BP].
- **Aalsum,** the next mound north. Finds: a 7th-century brooch, an oak weaving sword, late Merovingian or Carolingian caps, a bronze cloak pin with inlaid stones, a gold ring with a red stone. "Atlesheim" in Fulda's list of 944/45 [nlwp Aalsum].
- **11th century.** A tuff church of St Boniface and St Paul for canons and pilgrims; south of it a small tuff parish church of St Martin, its footings found by Halbertsma under today's Grote Kerk (one account has a first church there in the 10th century) [BP; DB; nlwp Grote Kerk]. Mint, market and toll [BP; fywp].
- **Not there in 793.** Stone building, wall, dike, mint, market, toll, abbey of monks (the abbey dates from 1170) [BP].

## 3. Who could be met

Remembered in 793:

- Boniface, killed 5 June 754 with Bishop Eoba, the priests Wintrung, Walthere and Ethelhere, the deacons Hamund, Scirbald and Bosa, the monks Wacchar, Gundaecer, Illehere and Hathovulf, and others: fifty in Fulda's martyrology, 52 by tradition [W ch. 8 and note; nlwp Bonifatius].
- Abba, Pippin's count, builder of the mound [W ch. 9].
- Willehad, a Northumbrian priest: began his mission here between 765 and 774, then crossed the Lauwers; bishop at Bremen 787, died 8 November 789 [CE-W; ADB-W].

Alive between 793 and 1066:

- Liudger, a Frisian: in charge of Oostergo from Dokkum 777-784, teaching at Utrecht each autumn; from 787 east of the Lauwers; bishop of Münster 805; died 26 March 809 [CE-L; NDB-L].
- A cleric of Utrecht who, in the first half of the 9th century, asked in this country about Boniface, and a very old woman who swore she had seen the killing [W note, from Levison p. 73; BP]. His Life is credited to Radbod, bishop 899-917, who reworked it [BP; nlwp Bisschoppen].
- Albdag, count in Oostergo for Louis the German, 873; Rodulf, a baptised Dane of royal kin, killed in Oostergo in June 873; an unnamed Christian Northman, long settled among the Frisians, who led them [enwp Rodulf, quoting the Annals of Fulda; nlwp Albdag].
- The Brunswick counts: Liudolf (from 1006 or 1024; died 1038), Bruno (1038 to 26 June 1057), Egbert I (1057-1068). Nothing puts them here in person [nlwp Midden-Friesland; nlwp Liudolf; nlwp Bruno; nlwp Egbert].

Ordinary people in the evidence: priests and clerks; pilgrims; stock farmers; women weaving cloth (the Aalsum weaving sword; the law's "woman who makes fresum"); a harper and a goldsmith, protected by the same law; in the 11th century a toll-taker and a moneyer [nlwp Aalsum; LF; BP]. The coins name only count and place [NUM]. No priest, reeve, merchant or moneyer of Dokkum is named.

## 4. What was traded

- No market is known before the 11th century [BP]: in 793 a skipper finds a church and farms.
- The mound coast sells Frisian cloth, cattle, hides, butter, cheese and wool, and wants timber, grain, wine, querns, pots and iron [TS 2.2, a project summary]. The mission of 754 brought its own food, wine and book chests by ship [W ch. 8].
- Law between Vlie and Lauwers: wergild of a noble 80 solidi, a freeman 53 and a third, a half-free man 27 less a penny. Who sells a slave to heathen peoples pays his own wergild to the king. Sunday work costs 4 solidi here, 12 beyond the Lauwers [LF I, XVII, XVIII]. The law counts in old and new money; Charlemagne's heavier penny came in 793/794 [LF intro].
- Between 1038 and 1057 (the catalogue dates the type 1050-1057): Count Bruno's silver pennies, about 0.7 g and 17 mm, reading DOCC / BRVN / VGGA, with a crowned head and HENRICVS RE on the other face (Dannenberg 499, 501; Ilisch 21.19, 21.22) [NUM]. Dokkum pennies are found in Scandinavia, the Baltic lands and Russia; Iceland and the Urals are also claimed (?) [BP; fywp].
- No toll before the 11th century, no fair, no ship find. Pilgrims on 5 June are likely, not recorded (?).

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- Before 793: 754, 5 June (755 by the Mainz and Fulda count), Boniface killed; by 768 the mound, then the church; 765-774 Willehad; 777-784 Liudger; 784, in Widukind's rising, the Frisians drive out the missionaries, burn the churches and return to the old gods [W ch. 8-9; enwp Boniface; CE-W; CE-L].
- 785-793, or 802/803: the Frisian law written down; the date is disputed [LF intro; enwp Lex].
- 792-793: Saxons and Frisians east of the Lauwers, the next river east, rise again; churches burned, priests driven out [CHR 793-autumn-saxon-revolt].
- 809, 26 March: Liudger dies [CE-L].
- 810, summer: 200 Danish ships ravage the Frisian islands; 100 pounds of silver paid [CHR 810-summer-frisia-raid-810].
- 838, 26 December: storm flood over Frisia; 2,437 counted drowned [CHR 838-12-26-frisia-flood].
- About 840: Altfrid's Life of Liudger, the first sure text to name the place [BP].
- 873, June: Rodulf demands tribute of Oostergo. The Frisians answer that they owe it only to King Louis and his sons. He falls with 800 men (Fulda) or 500 (St Bertin, Xanten); the rest, shut in a building, buy their lives with their treasure and an oath never to return [enwp Rodulf]. Halbertsma argued the building was Dokkum's church; no annal says so [nlwp Albdag].
- 944/45: Aalsum in Fulda's list [nlwp Aalsum].
- About 1000: the name written Doccinga [fywp].
- 1006 or 1024: the Brunswick counts take Oostergo [nlwp Midden-Friesland; nlwp Liudolf].
- 11th century: tuff churches; first sea dikes along the arm; mint, market, toll [BP].
- 1038-1057: Bruno's pennies. 1057: Bruno killed; Egbert I follows [NUM; nlwp Bruno].
- After the game: 1088, the county given to the bishop of Utrecht; 1170, the abbey; 1298, a town [nlwp Midden-Friesland; BP; fywp].

## 6. Faith and custom

- The church is St Paul's and St Boniface's; the later parish church St Martin's, Utrecht's patron [W note; BP]. Feasts: Boniface 5 June, Willehad 8 November, Liudger 26 March [enwp Boniface; CE-W; CE-L].
- Burial is in the churchyard on the mound [BP; RM].
- The old gods are a living memory. Boniface broke shrines across Frisia in 753-754 [W ch. 8]; in 784 the country went back to them [CE-L]. The law keeps a heathen rule: who breaks into a shrine and takes holy things is led to the sea, and on the sand the tide covers his ears are slit, he is gelded and given to the gods he robbed [LF Additio XI].
- In this district guilt for a killing in a crowd is found by lots: two cut twigs, one marked with a cross, wrapped in clean wool and laid on the altar or on relics; a priest, or an innocent boy, draws [LF XIV]. A man clears himself with oath-helpers; the one ordeal is boiling water [enwp Lex]. A blow to the hand of a harper, a goldsmith or a woman who weaves fresum costs a quarter more [LF, judgements of Wlemar].
- In Humsterland, over the Lauwers, heathens cast lots, as custom required, on whether Willehad should die; he left alive [ADB-W].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **The spring.** With the mound finished, people worried about fresh water. Abba rode round it; a servant's horse sank to its forelegs, and where it was pulled free a spring broke out, clear and sweet. Pippin had it from eyewitnesses, Lul from Pippin, Willibald from Lul [W ch. 9].
- **The book chests.** The killers carried the locked chests to the ships, found the mission's wine, drank, quarrelled over gold they had not seen, and fought each other. The survivors found only books, strewed them over the meadow and threw some into the reeds, where they were found long after, unharmed. Three days later Christians crossed the boundary, killed many and carried off wives, children and slaves [W ch. 8].
- **The old woman.** She swore that when the sword fell he covered his head with a gospel book. A slashed book is kept at Fulda; it holds tracts against heresy, not gospels [W note; nlwp Bonifatius].
- **A dog's life.** The Xanten annalist on Rodulf: though baptised, he ended a dog's life with the death it deserved [enwp Rodulf].
- **The name.** A 9th-century text, the Life of Willehad (?), writes Dockynchirica, "Dokkum church" [nlwp Dokkum].
- **Far-travelled pennies.** One struck here is shown among Finland's Viking-age finds (?) [FIN].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- That Boniface died at Dokkum. Willibald names only the river Bordne (the Boorne), on the border of Oostergo and Westergo, far to the south-west; Dokkum is first named in 9th-century Lives [W ch. 8; BP; fywp]. In 793 people here believe it and a church marks it: write it as their belief. Ignore the theory that moves it to Dunkirk.
- Who the killers were: heathens after plunder (Willibald), or an open fight at dawn (some historians) [nlwp Bonifatius]. And 754 or 755.
- A settlement before 754; the age of the village mound; the form, depth and tide of the landing.
- That this church burned in 784 or 793: likely for 784, stated nowhere by name (?).
- Any raid on Dokkum: none is recorded. The place of the 873 fight is not given.
- Relics of Boniface here: the body lies at Fulda [W ch. 8]; I found nothing on relics at Dokkum in these years.
- Bernlef at Dokkum: Altfrid's "Helewyret" is now read as Helwerd in Groningen; an older book said Holwerd near Dokkum [enwp Bernlef].
- The present well as the miracle spring; a named priest or count in 793; anything in section 2's "not there" line.
- I did not read Altfrid, the Life of Willehad or the annals in Latin (?). Robinson's Willibald is of 1916 but his death year is unchecked: paraphrase.
- Keep "terp" out of game text [wording_793.json].
