# Ganda (Ghent): dossier

Port row [ports_793.json]: Ganda, Ghent, 51.054 N 3.724 E, franks, flanders, monastery, riverbank, size 2. [n] is a source in section 9; (?) means not checked. Latin is Englished by me unless a translator is named.

## 1. The place in 793

- Not a town: two royal abbeys and a riverside village where the Leie (Lys) meets the Scheldt, in the pagus of Ghent under a Frankish count [7,9,11]. Einhard, 828: "the monastery of Saint Bavon, which is situated near the Scheldt in the place called Ghent, where that stream is joined with the river Lys" [6].
- Ganda is the abbey at the confluence (later St Bavo's); the name is taken to be Celtic for a confluence [11]. Blandinium (St Peter's) stands upstream on a sand hill about 29 m high, 20 m above the flats: the landmark [16].
- Both are the king's houses, under an abbot he names, with canons, not monks [11]. The abbot of 793 is unknown; a 12th-century list says St Peter's "had come almost to nothing" before Einhard [5].
- People: clergy, abbey serfs and tenants, farmers on the dry sand ridges [13]. No numbers survive.
- Water: the Leie runs in several arms; the meadows flood part of the year and suit sheep, not corn [7,13]. Boats lie on the Scheldt's left bank between the abbey and a lay village 500 m upstream; Verhulst pictures "bank settlements, shipyards" there in the 9th century [7].
- Coming in: up the Scheldt past Walcheren and Antwerp; about 800 its main mouth was the Eastern Scheldt [19]. The tide reaches Gentbrugge today; its reach then is a guess [19]. In 830 Einhard came by boat from Valenciennes and reckoned fifteen days by boat from here ("Sanctum N.", read by his editor as St Bavo's) to Seligenstadt on the Main [5].

## 2. What stood there

- Ganda abbey: a church of St Peter founded by Amand before 639, an abbey before 700, holding Bavo's grave by the 8th century [7]. The Life of Bavo (early 9th century) calls the place "Gandavum castrum"; a fort is unproven [7]. First stone church probably Einhard's [11]. Burned 851 [2]; the Northmen's winter seat 879-80 [3]; empty until about 920-37 [8,21]; rebuilt from 946; Romanesque church begun under Abbot Odwin (981-98), dedicated 10 May 1067 [15,17] (1058 in [21]).
- A church beside it, probably the parish church of the Holy Christ: dug at the old Beestenmarkt, rebuilt in phases, the first perhaps timber; Carolingian layers with sherds of Rhenish Badorf ware, a sign of trade [7].
- Blandinium: on royal land given by Dagobert [7,8]. Einhard set 24 clerics there [5]. Plundered in the winter of 879-80; reformed 941; the counts' burial church from 918; a vineyard on the slope to the Scheldt, date unknown [12].
- The lay village, later "portus": on the Scheldt's left bank by the present cathedral, from the early 9th century [7]. Usuard of Paris (858/65): "in portu Ganda sancti Bavonis confessoris", the word's first use here [7,8]. Town plots ("mansiones") between Scheldt and Leie are named in 941 [7].
- The half-round ditch round it: 75 m found in 1987 at the Borreputsteeg, 9th-10th century, filled in during the 10th; about 6 ha inside [7,13].
- St John's church (now the cathedral) inside the ditch: consecrated 942 by Bishop Transmar of Tournai [17] (947 in [20]); first charter mention 964; nothing older than the 12th century found under it [7].
- The counts' "new castle" on the Leie's left bank: named in the Miracles of Bavo (later 10th century); first timbers tree-ring dated no earlier than about 950 [7]. A timber-braced platform with a hall and grain stores [14]; leather-workers beside it (disputed) [7,14]; a chapel of St Pharaildis [7]. The stone hall is Robert the Frisian's (1071-93), after the game's end [14].
- Mint: deniers of Charles the Bald reading GANDAVVM [7]; struck 864-75, sixteen known (?) [22].

## 3. Who could be met

- Charlemagne, November 811 [1].
- Einhard, abbot of Blandinium by 2 June 815 and of St Bavo's by 13 April 819, until he died on 14 March 840. He ruled from Aachen, but was at St Bavo's in 827 and, sick, in spring 830; a grant of his is dated at Blandinium, 21 January 830 [5,6,9].
- His men: Erembert the steward (vicedominus), Liuthard the priest, Willibald the priest who collects the dues; Norbert (830) and Engelhard (839), holders of abbey benefices [5].
- Pilgrims healed at St Bavo's in 828: the blind girl Hartlinda of Furnes; Eberald and Gundrada, serfs of St Bavo; Liodold of Alphen on two crutches; Germar, blind, from "the sea-coast of the Frisians near the mouth of the river Scheldt" [6].
- Godfrid, "king of the Danes", named with the army of 879-80 at Thimeon, not at Ghent [3].
- Counts: Baldwin II (879-918) and Ælfthryth, King Alfred's daughter (died 7 June 929), both buried at St Peter's [17,18]; Arnulf I (918-964/5) [18]; Baldwin IV (988-1035), buried there [17].
- Gerard of Brogne, abbot of St Peter's for three years from 941 [12]. Dunstan, exiled there about 956-57 [10,18]. Womar: "The same year died Womare, Abbot of Ghent" (981) [4].
- The count's castle-keepers: Wichman (949-65), Dirk II of Holland (965-88), his son Arnulf, born here in 951; dates not firm [17,18].
- St Bavo's abbots Odwin (981-98), Erembold (1011) and Othelbold (letter to Countess Otgiva, 1019-30) [7,15,17]. Adelard, monk of St Peter's, wrote lessons on Dunstan for Canterbury, 1006-11 [18].
- Macarius, an eastern pilgrim, 1011-12 [15]. Emperor Henry II, who took the castle in 1007 [17].
- Ordinary people in the evidence: canons, abbey serfs, a steward, shipwrights (811), leather-workers, holders of town plots, a man of the portus carried off to Rouen by Northmen [1,7]. No moneyer, comb-maker or reeve is named.

## 4. What was traded

- In 793 no market, toll or fair is recorded. The abbey gathered its estates' yield and passed it on; that was the trade [7].
- Dues: Einhard orders the census of both abbeys taken "in full and in good silver" [5].
- Wax: he wants one pack-load sent with his vassals who return "after the Mass of Saint Bavo", because "the yield of honey has been small these two years" [5]. The addressee is unnamed; the feast points to Ghent.
- Ships: built here for the emperor's fleet, 810-11; the Northmen refitted theirs here in 881 [1,3].
- Coin: the king's deniers, struck here only under Charles the Bald [7,22].
- From about 950: wool from the abbeys' sheep marshes on the Flemish and Zeeland coasts, and cloth for sale abroad; a wool and cloth town by the 11th century [12,13]. The abbeys owned every plot in the portus for a yearly rent; rich families bought it off, 1038-1120 [12,13].
- England: St Peter's held Lewisham, Greenwich and Woolwich on the Thames [10]; London's tolls of about 1000 name the men of Flanders [19].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 811, November: "Inde ad Scaldim fluvium veniens in loco, qui Gand vocatur, naves ad eandem classem aedificatas aspexit" [1].
- 815, 2 June; 819, 13 April: Louis the Pious takes Blandinium, then St Bavo's, under his protection at Einhard's asking [5,9].
- 828, 3 July: relics of Marcellinus and Peter reach St Bavo's; cures until 30 September [6].
- 851: Danes rage "usque ad monasterium sancti Bavonis quod Gant dicunt" and burn it; the community flees to Laon with Bavo's relics [2,8].
- 879, November: the army from Fulham winters "in Gandao monasterio" [3]; it went "to Ghent in Frankland, and sat there a year" [4].
- 880-81: it wastes Tournai and the Scheldt monasteries, beats Abbot Gauzlin, moves to Courtrai in November 880, and after Saucourt comes back to refit its ships before going to the Meuse [3].
- 918: Baldwin II dies on 10 September; Ælfthryth's grant of Lewisham is dated the 11th [10,17].
- About 920-37: St Bavo's community returns; Count Arnulf holds its lands [8,11,21].
- 941 and 946: Gerard of Brogne turns St Peter's, then St Bavo's, from canons into monks under Benedict's Rule [11,12]. 942: St John's consecrated [17].
- 964: King Edgar confirms Lewisham [10]; Arnulf I dies 27 March (or 965) [18].
- 974: Otto II takes St Bavo's under his protection (diploma of 21 January (?)); the Scheldt is his empire's border [8,11].
- 980: the bones of Landoald come from Wintershoven to St Bavo's [17].
- 1007: Henry II takes the castle; "Livinus" is brought from Houtem [17].
- 1012, 10 April: Macarius dies in a plague [15].
- 1044: Edward the Confessor's charter for St Peter's [10].

## 6. Faith and custom

- Feasts: Bavo, 1 October ("Bamis") [17]; Amand, 6 February; Pharaildis (Veerle), 4 January, her relics here since about 754 [18]; Marcellinus and Peter from 828 [6]; later Livinus, 12 November, and Macarius, 10 April [15,17].
- The 819 charter: the count has no power on abbey land; the abbey fines and punishes there, and owes the emperor prayers, gifts and army service [9].
- The old gods: the people threw Amand in the river more than once; he bought and baptised captives, and won them by reviving a hanged man [11,18]. That was the 630s; in 793 the land is Christian.
- Burial: Baldwin II was moved from Saint-Bertin to St Peter's so that his widow could lie by him; Saint-Bertin took no women [17].
- From about 950 the two abbeys fought over which was older, forged charters and invented saints [8].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- Bavo was Allowin, a lord who sold his own people; he had a man he had sold lead him in chains to prison, and lived in a hollow tree [17,18].
- Einhard's letters survive in one 9th-century book, thought to have gone to Laon with the fleeing canons; it was found there in 1636 [5].
- The 819 charter survives whole, with Louis's head in white wax; the scribe signs as Faramund [9].
- Macarius knocked first at St Peter's and was sent away; St Bavo's took him in and got a saint [15].
- Livinus is thought invented, copied from Lebuin of Deventer; in 1984 Landoald's shrine held a forged 11th-century bishop's seal [17].
- Pharaildis brought a cooked goose to life from skin and bones; Amalberga crossed the Scheldt on a sturgeon [18].

## 8. What we do not know

- The count and every layman of 793; the first names are Einhard's (815-40).
- No mint and no "portus" before the 850s-60s.
- Where the ships of 811 were built and how many; where the camp of 879 lay and who led it. No dig has found it.
- The castle: writers give Baldwin I, Baldwin II, Arnulf I or "about 1000" [13,14,18,22]; the timbers say about 950. Do not call it the Gravensteen (1180) or show a stone keep.
- Whether the community fled in 851 or 879, and when it returned; the ditch's date within 850-950.
- The charters of 918, 1016 (Edward's vow at Ghent) and 1044 are abbey texts, possibly forged (?); do not show Edward at Ghent as fact [10].
- Otto I taking the castle in 949 is old tradition [22].
- Do not claim guilds, a belfry or "the largest town" before 1066, cloth export in 793, or that Einhard lived here.
