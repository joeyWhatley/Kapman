# Aldeigjuborg (Staraya Ladoga, on the Volkhov): dossier

Keys in brackets: see Sources. (?) = not checked on a page opened on 6 Oct 2026 (from memory of the literature; check before it ships). **late** = told only in the kings' sagas of about 1220-1230. Web search was shut today; only pages that could be named were read.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What.** A small craft and trading place forty years old, the only harbour for sea ships that cannot go up the Volkhov [Wde; WL]. Oldest timber 753 [Wen; Wru].
- **Name.** Northmen say Aldeigja (so the poem of about 1010); Aldeigjuborg, "Aldeigja fort", is the sagas' form [Wen] and fits only once there is a fort (870s on) [Wru]. The name is from a Finnic river name, *Alode-jogi, "lower river"; Slavs turned it into Ladoga [Wru; Wen]. German Wikipedia gives another root, "wavy" [Wde].
- **Rule.** No ruler is named by any writer before 839 [rus]. The name Rus is not written before 839: nobody is called that in 793 [audit].
- **Who lives there.** The first settlers of the 750s were Northmen, perhaps Gotlanders (Ryabinin), in three "big houses" with a hearth in the middle [Wru]. In the 760s that colony ends and people from the south, building log cabins, take the place; Slavs are the larger part from about 760-770 [Wru; Wen]. Finnic people lived on this river before either [Lyu]. Northmen may at first have come only for the season [Wen]. So in 793: Slav and Finnic householders, Northmen coming and going.
- **How big.** 2 to 4 hectares; "several dozen to 100" people from the later 700s to the early 800s [Wru]. It grows to as much as 12 hectares later [Wde].
- **Coming in.** Across Lake Ladoga: a freshwater sea, its south shore all shoals and reefs, waves to 6 m [WL]. Then 12 km up the Volkhov [Wru] (15 km by [Wde]), brown water [WL], to the left bank where the small river Ladozhka runs in. The mouth and channels of the Ladozhka are the harbour [Wru]. The first houses stand on the Ladozhka's right bank [Wru]. On the right bank of the Volkhov, 2 km below the town, a stone-and-earth fort stands at the mouth of the Lyubsha: a skipper passes it first [Lyu]. Great mounds stand along the river [Wen]. Above the town are rapids, hard at low water, drowned by the dam of 1926 [WL; Vol].
- **Ice.** The Volkhov freezes in late November and opens in early April [Vol]; the lake shore freezes in December and clears in April or May [WL].
- **Map.** Wikipedia gives 59°59′30″N 32°17′48″E [Wsv]: the table's 60.0, 32.3 is right.

## 2. What stood there

- **The Earthen Fort ground** (Zemlyanoe gorodishche): the dug heart of the town, about 2,500 square metres opened, layers from the mid 700s to the 900s [Wru]. Dug by Ravdonikas (1938-1959), Kirpichnikov (from 1972), Ryabinin; tree-ring scheme by Kuzmin [Wru].
- **The workshop of the 750s**: a Scandinavian "production complex" in the oldest layer [Wru], with a smith's tool hoard of some 26 pieces (?). From it, a small bronze finial of a man with a horned head-dress, called "the singing Odin", in the Vendel style [Wru].
- **The bead-maker's shop**, 780s to the end of the 830s: glass "eye" beads made by an Arab low-heat method [Wru; Wde]. The beads bought furs [Wru].
- **A burned barn**, 700s: wheat, 80 per cent emmer, 20 per cent soft wheat [Wru].
- **Casting moulds** [Wru]. Comb-making in antler and amber-working are reported by the diggers (?).
- **The Lyubsha fort**, 2 km north, right bank: built about 700 by Slavs on an older Finnic palisaded site; over 1,000 finds, with ship rivets, moulds, and both Norse and local arrowheads; given up in the later 800s as the lake fell and the Lyubsha silted. Dug by Ryabinin from 1997 [Lyu].
- **Varangian Street** (Varyazhskaya), left bank of the Ladozhka: settled by the Northmen who took the town about the 830s-840s [Wen; Wru]. In its early 900s layers: lustre pottery from Mesopotamia; a birch-bark roll with a ship drawn on it [Wru].
- **The "big house" of 881**: read as a merchants' inn; a rune stick lay with a large Norse-style house [Wru]. About 894 a very large building, perhaps of two floors [Wru; Wen].
- **The wooden fort**, 870s, on the point between Ladozhka and Volkhov (Kirpichnikov) [Wru]. The fort page credits Rurik in 862, "with some conditionality" [Fort].
- **The stone fort**, about 900: flagstone laid without mortar, walls about 8 m, a fighting walk 2 m wide, and a loading hatch 7 m up the river wall [Fort; Wru]. Broken in 997; rebuilt in wood in the early 1000s behind a ditch from Ladozhka to Volkhov [Fort]. A stone fort again only in 1114 or 1116, after our time [Fort].
- **Plakun**, the grave field on the right bank opposite: thirteen mounds counted in 1940; in use about 850 to 950 (Mikhailov: only from the 900s); the early graves are cremations [Plak; Wru]. Mound 11: a chamber grave of a man of 60 to 70, with a horse, perhaps a Christian [Plak]. Boat rivets in some mounds (?). Strontium shows at least five incomers, matching the Mälar country [Wru].
- **The sopki**: the tall mounds along the Volkhov. One, about 10 m high, is called Oleg's Grave. A third of it was dug in 1820: burnt bone, a two-barbed spearhead of the 700s or early 800s, iron scraps [Oleg].
- **Churches.** None is known before 1066. St Clement's stone church is of 1153 [Wru]. Unmounded Christian graves at Plakun are of the 1000s-1100s (65 skeletons dug 1938-1939) [Wru].
- **Mint.** None. Silver is weighed [rus].

## 3. Who could be met

- **Nobody named, 793-838** [rus].
- **Rurik**, by the chronicle's 862 (?): most copies of the Primary Chronicle make Ladoga his first seat; the Laurentian copy says Novgorod [Wru]. Partly legend [rus].
- **Oleg** (died 912 by Kiev's chronicle, 922 by Novgorod's): the Novgorod chronicle says his mound is at Ladoga [Wen; Oleg].
- **Jarl Eirik Hakonarson**, as an enemy, 997 [Wen; Eric].
- **Ingigerd** (born 1001, died 10 February 1050), daughter of Olof of the Svear, wife of Yaroslav from 1019; the town is her bride-gift (**late**) [Ing; Wen].
- **Jarl Ragnvald Ulfsson**, her father's cousin, jarl here from the summer of 1019 (**late**) [Rag]. **Eilif**, his son, jarl after him [Wru]. Another son is Uleb [Rag].
- **Passing through** (**late**): Olaf Haraldsson 1029-1030, his son Magnus to about 1035, Harald Sigurdsson about 1031 and 1045 [rus].
- **Ordinary people the ground shows:** a smith and bronze-caster; a glass bead-maker; a comb-maker (?); farmers with emmer; boat-menders (rivets); an innkeeper; someone who cut runes; women buried with Mälar-country dress (?) [Wru; Lyu; Plak]. River pilots for the rapids are a fair inference, not a find [WL].

## 4. What was traded

- **Out:** furs above all, bought with beads [Wru]; wax, honey and captives [rus].
- **In:** silver; beads; blades; amber; later, lustre ware from Iraq [Wru; rus].
- **Coins.** The oldest dirham hoard in the town ends in 786; single coins were struck in 738-739 [Wru]. They are the oldest dirhams of European Russia [Wen]. Weighed, cut, nicked; no stamp counts [rus].
- **Beads as money.** The Russian page cites a 10th-century Arab traveller: one glass eye-bead could buy a slave [Wru]. Ibn Fadlan says a dirham a bead on the Volga [rus] (?).
- **Tolls and law.** No toll is known here [audit]. From 1016 Yaroslav's law at Novgorod [rus].
- **Ships.** Sea ships stop here; goods go on by river boat [Wde; WL]. Adam of Bremen: five days by sea from Birka to the Rus [rus] (?).
- **Seasons.** Furs come in over the snow; boats leave at ice-break; silver comes back before the frost [rus]. One of the chief ports of eastern Europe until about 950 [Wen].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- **753**: oldest timber [Wen; Wru].
- **760s**: the first Norse colony ends; log-cabin builders take the site [Wru].
- **780s**: bead-making begins [Wru]. **786**: the first hoard [Wru].
- **About 840**: an enemy burns the town; much of it lies empty some 25 years [Wru]. Northmen hold it from about now [Wen].
- **852** (?): Danes sent from Birka by lot sack an unnamed town of the Slavs; some think Ladoga [rus: Rimbert 19].
- **862** (?): Rurik, by the chronicle [Wen; Wru].
- **Between 863 and 871**, about 865: burned to the ground [Wru; Wen]. Rebuilt with more Norse things, Thor's hammers among them [Wru].
- **870s**: the wooden fort [Wru]. **881**: the big house [Wru]. **About 894**: new large building [Wen; Wru].
- **About 900**: the stone fort [Wru; Fort].
- **922** (?): Oleg's death and mound, by the Novgorod chronicle [Oleg].
- **About 950**: its best years end as Novgorod grows [Wen; rus].
- **997**: Jarl Eirik takes and burns the fort after a long siege; the burning was found in the 1980s [Fort; Eric; Wru]. The year is the sagas' [rus].
- **Early 1000s**: a wooden fort again [Fort].
- **1019**: Ingigerd's bride-gift; Ragnvald jarl (**late**) [Wen; Rag].
- **1029-1045**: the Norwegian kings pass (**late**) [rus].
- **1050**: Ingigerd dies [Ing].

## 6. Faith and custom

- **Heathen throughout the 800s and 900s**: cremation at Plakun, Thor's hammers after 865, the horned figure [Wru; Plak].
- **Mixed burial**: chamber graves with horses laid north to south and Baltic horse gear, which is not ordinary Norse custom [Plak].
- **The mounds** are older than the princes they are named for [Oleg].
- **Christians**: perhaps the old man of Mound 11 [Plak]; no church until after 988, and none proven here before 1066 [Wru; rus]. From 988 the Greek rite [rus].
- Ingigerd is later honoured as St Anna of Novgorod, 10 February and 4 October [Ing]: long after 1066 (?).

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **Oleg's Grave is not Oleg's.** The spearhead is a century too old, and the name may be of the 20th century (Petrenko) [Oleg]. A second grave of his is shown at Kiev [Oleg].
- **The horse and the snake**: a seer said Oleg's horse would kill him; a snake from its skull did [rus].
- **The singing Odin**: a finger-long bronze man with horns on his head, from the oldest workshop [Wru]. (The horns are a head-dress on a figure; nobody wore such a helmet.)
- **A bead for a slave** [Wru].
- **Two rune finds**, 1950 and 1975: a stick, and an amulet no later than the 900s [Wde; Wru]. The stick is said to carry verse (?).
- **The ship on birch bark** [Wru].
- **The river that runs backwards**: the Volkhov's upper course can reverse [Vol; WL].
- **The poem**: Eirik's skald Eyjolf, perhaps a heathen himself, praised the burning about 1010 [Eyj].

## 8. What we do not know, and must not claim

- Who ruled before 839, and who burned the town about 840 and about 865.
- That Rurik lived, or sat here: the copies differ.
- A toll, a mint, a thing-place, a temple, a church or a market square: none is recorded.
- "Rus", "Russia", "Aldeigjuborg" as words in 793: say Aldeigja; no fort stands until the 870s.
- Stone walls before about 900.
- The jarls and the kings' visits as fact: they are saga.
- That Ingria is named for Ingigerd [Ing]: an old guess (?).
- The tool-hoard count, the comb and amber shops, boat graves at Plakun and the rune stick's text: all (?) here.
- That Frisians came this far in 793: no find read today shows it.
