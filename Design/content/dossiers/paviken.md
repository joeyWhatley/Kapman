# Paviken (Västergarn, west Gotland): dossier

Keys in brackets: see Sources. (?) = not checked on a page I opened (from memory of the literature, or a guess marked as one). **late** = written 150 years or more after the event. Web search was shut for this session, so this rests on the pages named below and on the repo.

## 1. The place in 793

- **Name.** "Paviken" is a modern name: older maps call the water Västergarnsviken, and the name is said to come from "pa viken", "on the bay" [Wsv, after Lundström]. What the Gotlanders called the harbour in 793 is not recorded. The game's name is a convenience.
- **What.** A beach harbour, workshop ground and trading place on the east shore of a lagoon, at the mouth of the stream Idån (older name Sudertingsån). Founded in the 700s, used for about 300 years [Wsv; Lst]. In 793 it is one or two generations old.
- **Rule.** Nobody. Gotland has no king and no town; the island's farmers are its merchants [RSG; PSG]. No lord, reeve or toll-man is known for Paviken from any text. About 890 Wulfstan says Gotland "belongs to the Svear" [RSG]; the Guta saga (**late**) says the islanders agreed this of their own will [Wgs].
- **People.** Craftsmen at hearths (beads, bronze, silver, iron), men mending boats, and the farmers of the country behind. Whether anyone lived there all year is not settled: the dig found hearths and postholes but no certain dwelling [GFS]. No count of heads exists.
- **Size.** Finds and graves are spread over about 400 by 1,000 m of the east shore; the dense part ("Paviken I", the northern area) is far smaller, and only about 1,000 square metres of it has been dug [GFS].
- **Water.** Then a sheltered, lagoon-like bay joined to the sea by one narrow strait [Wsv; Lst]; now a lake of 0.27 square km at sea level, cut off by land rise and silt [Wen; Wsv]. The outlet is the stream Västergarnsån [Wsv]. The modern lake freezes in winter [Wen].
- **Coming in.** The west coast of Gotland, about 25 km south of where Visby will stand [Wvg]. An islet, Västergarns utholme, lies about 2 km off the coast today [Wve]; its shape in 793 is not known (?). The skipper finds the narrow strait, passes into still water, and crosses to the east shore where the stream runs in.
- **Where boats lie.** Drawn up on the beach: a hollow worn by dragging boats was found at the waterfront [GFS]. Jetties are reported from the 8th century [Wen], but the timber jetties actually dug and dated are at the outlet and are of 1008 [GFS].

## 2. What stood there

- **The trading shore (Area 1).** The western, waterfront part of Paviken I, where the stream meets the lagoon: the most silver and the most weights. Read as the place of exchange [GFS].
- **The fine-craft yard (Area 2).** Inside a stone wall, opening toward Idån: raw glass, raw garnet, amber, ingots. A large hearth (2 by 1 m) and a clay platform perhaps used in bead-making, found 2013 [GFS].
- **The rough-work ground (Area 3).** South of it: stone paving laid as a working floor, hearths, rubbish pits, postholes, bronze and iron scrap, much burnt bone [GFS].
- **Boat mending.** Boat rivets and nails all over the site, from the first test pits of 1961 on [GFS]. Usually called a shipyard (?); the pages I opened show rivets and a slipway hollow, not a dug yard.
- **The grave in the middle.** A round stone setting at the centre of Paviken I: one man buried unburnt with a ring brooch, and cremations beside him [GFS].
- **The waterfront burial.** A second body laid in the boat-dragging hollow, with no grave goods and no marker; poorly recorded [GFS].
- **North grave field**, running east to west along the stream: a grave of the Vendel or early Viking Age with burnt bones, nails, rivets and a ring brooch [GFS].
- **South grave field (Paviken I).** Stone settings, cremations and burials; one grave held a dog and a horse [GFS].
- **Paviken II grave field**, further south: graves without stones, ploughed out, loose finds since the 1800s; perhaps hundreds of graves once [GFS]. Metal-detecting found Vendel dress ornaments, Viking Age coins and cubo-octahedral weights, more than at Paviken I: perhaps a second trading spot near the outlet [GFS].
- **The stone-walled house** between Paviken I and II (dug 1995), Viking Age: no hearth; rivets, scale fragments, lead weights, glass, two pieces of Arabic silver coin, and a sandstone mould for crosses or Thor's hammers. A workshop or store, not a dwelling [GFS].
- **The beach house** (dug 2014): a thin gravel floor, one posthole dated to the 8th century; a store or shelter [GFS].
- **Paviken II.** Thin layers: whetstones, beads, knives, rivets, silver bits, a mould for silver bars, a large hearth (1 by 1.5 m) [GFS].
- **The outlet works.** Timber quays and jetties and a stone facing at Västergarnsån, tree-ring dated to 1008, wrecked by a spring flood [GFS; Wvv], and a row of piles that closed the entrance, early 1000s [Wsv; Lst].
- **After the game's harbour: Västergarn.** A half-ring wall about 1 km long, 3 m high and 3 m wide at the foot, open to the sea, "probably about 1000", though its date is not settled [Wvv; Lvv]. Houses inside are of the 1100s and later [Wvv]. The church ruin is of about 1150, the round tower of the 1100s, the present church of about 1200 to 1250: all after 1066 [Wvk; Wvv].
- **No church, mint, well or hall** is known at Paviken.

## 3. Who could be met

- **No historical person is tied to Paviken.** Not one name survives.
- **Island figures a writer may mention, not place here:**
  - Avair Strabain of Alva, who made the Gotlanders' treaty with the king of the Svear; undated, "before the end of the 9th century" by modern guess [Wgs; Wgu] (**late**).
  - Olaf Haraldsson of Norway: takes tribute of Gotland about 1007, lies at Akergarn (north-east Gotland) in 1029 [RSG] (**late**).
  - Jarl Eirik of Lade, robbing ships off Gotland 996 to 997 [RSG] (**late**).
  - Ormika of Hejnum, Botair of Akebäck, Likkair Snielli: the saga's first Christians (?) [GS] (**late**; 11th century).
- **Ordinary people the finds show** [GFS]: bead-makers (glass, garnet, amber); bronze-casters; silversmiths casting bars; iron smiths; comb and dice makers in bone and antler; boat-menders; fishers (stone net sinkers); men with folding scales and lead weights; cattle-keepers; women buried with dress ornaments (?).
- **Names** attested on Gotland's rune stones (mostly later) are in the repo: Rodvisl, Hegbjörn, Ravn, Ottar, Liknat; Rodälv, Ailikn [PSG].

## 4. What was traded

- **In, as raw stuff.** Opaque glass cubes (mosaic tesserae, read as Italian); garnet, only raw (source not given); amber; bronze and silver ingots; Arabic silver coins, whole and cut [GFS]. Dirhams reach the Baltic from about 800 [RSG].
- **Made here.** Glass beads at every stage; amber beads and amber Thor's hammers; bronze and silver ornaments; silver bars; combs; cast crosses or hammers from the stone mould [GFS].
- **Out.** Not shown directly. The repo makes Gotland a source of bar iron and silver arm rings [SMA; SC].
- **Food.** 65 per cent of the bone studied is cattle, unlike other harbours, but only 3 per cent of the bone was studied [GFS].
- **Silver and weights.** No coin is struck; silver is weighed. Scale fragments, lead weights, cubo-octahedral weights, a small gold bar [GFS]. The diggers think some weights served the craftsmen, not trade [GFS]. Gotland has over 700 silver hoards [PSG]; coin import falls sharply after the 1050s [Wvv].
- **Tolls and law.** None recorded for Paviken. The treaty (**late**): 60 marks of silver a year, 40 to the king and 20 to the jarl, and Gotlanders go toll-free in the Svear's lands; Svear may come to Gotland with no ban on corn [Wgu]. A stranger's life is worth 10 marks of silver against a Gotlander's 24 in the later law [PSG].
- **Seasons.** Not recorded for this harbour. The eastern silver comes home between midsummer and the first frost [SC].
- **Ships.** Clinker boats with iron rivets [GFS]; the island's picture stones show square, chequered sails [Wps].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- **700s.** The harbour begins [Wsv; Lst]; one posthole is of the 8th century [GFS].
- **793.** Working: beads, bronze, boat-mending.
- **From about 800.** Arabic silver reaches Gotland [RSG]; cut pieces lie in Paviken's workshop [GFS].
- **After 870.** The Spillings silver, 67 kg, is buried in north-east Gotland [SC].
- **About 890.** Wulfstan: Gotland belongs to the Svear [RSG].
- **Before 900 (?).** Avair Strabain's treaty [Wgu] (**late**).
- **900s to 1000s.** A silver arm ring, a Thor's hammer and a bronze pendant of this date from Paviken I [GFS].
- **996 to 997.** Jarl Eirik robs ships off Gotland [RSG] (**late**).
- **About 1000.** The Västergarn wall, if the usual date holds [Wvv].
- **About 1007.** Olaf Haraldsson takes tribute of the island [RSG] (**late**).
- **1008.** Timber quays built at the outlet; later a spring flood wrecks them [GFS].
- **Early 1000s.** Piles driven to close the entrance [Wsv].
- **1029.** Olaf at Akergarn [RSG] (**late**).
- **1030s.** An English penny of this decade, worn as a pendant, lost at Västergarn [Wvv].
- **By about 1050 (?).** The lagoon is too shallow; trade moves to Västergarn [Wen; Wsv].
- **After the game:** Västergarn fades behind Visby, finally after the war of 1288 [Wvv].

## 6. Faith and custom

- **Heathen throughout the harbour's main life.** Thor's hammers in amber and metal; a mould that could cast hammers or crosses [GFS].
- **Burial.** Burning and unburnt burial side by side, under stone settings or in plain pits; a dog and a horse in one grave; a man with a ring brooch [GFS].
- **Sacrifice** (**late**): shared sacrificial meals, the sharers called "boiling-fellows" [PSG].
- **Conversion** (**late**): merchants bring priests home in the 11th century; the first church was burnt [PSG]. No church at Paviken.
- **Picture stones.** Over 400 on Gotland; those of 700 to 1100 are tall, with ships under chequered sails and a rider met by a woman with a horn [Wps]. None is recorded from Paviken itself on the pages I opened; stones stand in neighbouring parishes (?).

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **The island that sank by day**: Gotland rose only at night until Thieluar brought fire to it [Wgs] (**late**).
- **Hwitastierna's dream** of three snakes in her lap: three sons, who divide the island in three [Wgs] (**late**).
- **The harbour was sealed twice**: by piles in the early 1000s, and by archaeologists, who laid black plastic over the unexcavated floor in the 1970s. In 2013 the plastic was found whole, and a small gold bar turned up in the old spoil [GFS].
- **One mould, two faiths**: the sandstone mould for crosses or hammers [GFS].
- **The dead man in the slipway** [GFS].
- **Italian church mosaic, melted for beads** on a Baltic beach [GFS].
- Today the lake is a bird reserve (240 species) known for giant crucian carp [Wen; Wsv].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- The harbour's old name; any ruler, toll, law, fair or season of its own; its population; whether it was lived in all winter.
- Do not call it a town, or give it a wall, church, mint, hall or thing-place. The wall and churches are Västergarn's and later.
- Do not write "shipyard where ships were built" as fact: say boats were mended, and built perhaps (?).
- Do not place Avair, Olaf or any saga figure at Paviken.
- Do not call Gotlanders Swedes or Svear, or give Gotland a king.
- The main dig (Lundström and Lamm, six years to 1973; over 10,000 find records) was never fully published [GFS]. Details beyond this dossier are not safe.
- Whether trade passed straight to Västergarn about 1000 is disputed: Västergarn's finds are thin for the 1000s [Wvv].
- **Table errors.** ports_793.json puts Paviken at 57.42 N, 18.18 E; the lake is at 57.455 N, 18.141 E [Wen]. It has no end year: about 1050 is fair (?).
