# Áhús (Åhus, Skåne): dossier

Port `ahus`: Áhús, modern Åhus, 55.92 N 14.30 E; realm danes, region skane, kind market, landing beach, size 2 [ports_793.json]. Researched 6 Oct 2026. Source keys are in section 9. (?) marks what I could not check.

**Read this first.** No text names this place before 1149. What is known of it in the game's first sixty years comes from one dig (Johan Callmer, from 1978); I read one full paper of his [C], rich on crafts and the town's plan, silent on coins, graves, gods and rulers. After about 850 the craft town is gone and the record is nearly empty until an 11th-century castle. Web searching was shut off this session, so some well-known claims stand with (?).

## 1. The place in 793

- **What it is.** A permanent trading and craft town on the north bank of the lower Helge river (the "Holy River"), on sand: the archaeologists' Åhus II, or Transval. About 750 to 850 and a little later [C].
- **How big.** Over 12 hectares; about a hundred plots of about 25 m by 20 m, five to ten people each: 500 to 1,000 people, "several hundred" of them craftsmen. Far more than the district could use: they worked for the whole coast [C]. The game's size 2 is low for 793.
- **Who rules.** Not known. Callmer says only that such a place lived under "the protection of local holders of power", that its people were mostly free, and that it could fight [C]. About 880-890 Wulfstan called "Sconey" subject to Denmark [W]; another account puts Skåne under the Danish king only from Harald Bluetooth, mid 10th century [enwp Scania]. "Danes" in 793 is the game's choice.
- **Who lives there.** Families on plots, often two families working together, women and men both at craft work. Many craftsmen went away in the sailing season, April to the end of September, and wintered at home; craftsmen from other markets came as guests. Traders came and went; some "may have stayed on board their ships anchored or beached on the riverbank below the site" [C].
- **Coming in.** The river reached the sea at Åhus itself until a new mouth broke through further south (1785 [svwp Helge å]; 1775 [dewp]). A brown river, about 46 cubic metres a second today, a mouth about 0.1 km wide, a brown plume in the bay, surf on the sand in an easterly [repo water]. A bar is not recorded (?). The town lay a short way upriver of the later town [enwp Åhus]. Open country with little woodland; today's dunes south of the river are early modern, not there in 793 [C]. Boats lie on the bank under the town [C].
- **Seen from the water.** Rows of plots along the river in strips 25 or 50 m deep, open lanes of 10 to 20 m between them, the last plots about 300 m inland; one open space of at least 50 m by 30 m in the middle. The oldest and richest strip is on the bank [C].

## 2. What stood there

- **The old market on the south bank (Åhus I)**, about 700 to 750, a few hundred metres upstream: three or four hectares, used for short spells, flimsy shelters only. Bead-making on a great scale (over 70,000 pieces of glass waste), bronze casting, comb-making, smithing; the finds match the oldest layers of Ribe. In 793 it is grazing land of Yngsjö village, 5 km upriver [C].
- **The clay cliff** in the river bank there: kaolin was dug for crucibles [C].
- **The town (Åhus II).** About 5,200 features dug. 149 sunken-floored huts (workshops and stores, not dwellings, in Callmer's view). Dwelling houses on the ground, no more than 5 m by 10 m or so, usually one a plot, as at Hedeby and Ribe [C].
- **Workshops, on nearly every plot** [C]:
  - Combs: 28,136 pieces of antler waste, mostly red deer, 84 per cent shed antler.
  - Bronze and silver casting: 3,596 mould pieces, 532 crucible pieces, scrap and bars; silver about 6 per cent. Brooches (the oval type Petersen 37, 9th century, at four places), arm rings, mounts, keys.
  - Amber: 3,015 pieces; beads and small axe-shaped pendants.
  - Smithing: about 100 kg of slag; half-made knives, chest locks, riveted sheet iron from cauldrons; a piece of mail, few weapons.
  - Cloth: 107 spindle whorls (more than any other known site), many for very fine thread; light loom weights; 36 metal sewing needles; 33 bone needles, perhaps for sailcloth; two antler ribbon-weaving combs, matched only at Birka.
  - Glass: 124 pieces of bead waste, by the river, from the town's first years only.
- **The bank.** Over 2,400 boat rivets: ship repair was "a major activity", and old boat timber was burnt [C]. The waterfront is the least-dug part [C].
- **Not reported in what I read:** grave field, hall, shrine, wall, well, jetty, mint.
- **Late.** A stone castle, about 20 m by 10 m, dated by radiocarbon and tree rings to the mid 11th century, perhaps Scandinavia's oldest [svwp borg]. Saint Mary's church is 12th century: after the game [svwp Maria].
- **Near by.** Fjälkinge: two Viking Age grave fields, longhouses, a well, a silver hoard [svwp Fjälkinge]. Vä, inland: the name means a holy place [svwp Vä]. A runestone at Älleköpinge [dewp], text not checked (?).

## 3. Who could be met

No person of Áhús is named before 1149. Tied to the river, all late:

- **Cnut**, king of England and Denmark, at the Holy River in 1025 or 1026 [ASC; enwp Helgeå].
- **Olaf Haraldsson** of Norway and **Anund Jacob** of the Svear, his enemies there [enwp Helgeå].
- **Ulf** and **Eglaf**, who by the Chronicle came against Cnut [ASC]; another account has Ulf lead Cnut's ships [enwp Helgeå]. The sources disagree.
- **Ottar the Black**, skald, praised Cnut for beating the Svear at the Holy River [svwp Helgeå].
- After the game: **Archbishop Eskil** of Lund (1137-1177) was given Åhus village by King Sven Grathe in 1149 [svwp borg].

Ordinary people the dig shows [C]: comb-makers; brooch casters; amber workers; smiths of knives, locks and cauldrons; women spinning fine thread, weaving, sewing, stringing beads; ship repairers; traders living aboard; guest craftsmen. Most were generalists: one plot cast brooches, cut combs and worked amber. Bead-makers belong before about 770, not to 793.

## 4. What was traded

- **Out:** in Callmer's reading, whole sets of fine dress for women and men (cloth, ribbons, brooches, strung beads, ornamented combs); knives and tools, iron cauldrons, locked chests; probably wooden bowls, belts and shoes (no proof) [C].
- **In:** antler; raw amber (gathered on Skåne's shores [U6]); scrap bronze, bars and silver; from the later 8th century beads from western Europe and the Middle East "in vast numbers", unstrung (over a thousand found) [C]. Rock crystal and garnet were worked [U6].
- **Where to:** a coast road of markets from the Danish isles and Hedeby to the Mälar country [C].
- **Coins and weights.** None in the paper I read. Sceattas and dirhams are reported from Åhus elsewhere (?). Silver by weight, as the game has it.
- **Tolls and law.** Nothing for Áhús. Of such markets in general: rulers took tolls, "most probably a tithe", and market peace protected only briefly [C].
- **Seasons.** Sailing April to end of September; the town is fullest in winter [C]. No fair date is known.
- **Ships.** Clinker boats, by the rivets [C].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- about 700: the south-bank market begins, with Ribe the earliest in the north [C].
- about 750: the north-bank town begins along the river; within a generation it doubles inland; bead-making stops as foreign beads flood in [C].
- 793: the town at or near full size [C].
- 9th century: Petersen 37 brooches cast here [C].
- second half of the 9th century: abandoned, "probably relocated nearby"; the ground is ploughed from then on [C]. Elleköpinge is the usual guess (?).
- about 880-890: Wulfstan sails past; Skåne "subject to Denmark" [W].
- mid 10th century: Skåne under Harald Bluetooth [enwp Scania].
- about 990: Lund founded by Sweyn Forkbeard [enwp Scania].
- 1025 (Chronicle) or 1026: the fight at the Holy River; this river or one in Uppland is disputed [ASC; svwp Helgeå].
- mid 11th century: the stone castle [svwp borg].
- 1060: the diocese of Lund [enwp Lund].
- After: 1149 Eskil; 1286 or 1296 the name first written, as Aos [svwp Åhus; enwp Åhus].

## 6. Faith and custom

- Heathen through the town's life; no church in Skåne before about 990 [enwp Scania].
- The river's name means the holy, or luck-bringing, river [svwp Helge å]. Why is not known.
- The old religion sent fine clothes, ornaments, tools and weapons into the grave or to offerings; that steady loss kept the craftsmen in work. Fire crafts may have been forbidden to women: Callmer calls it likely, not proven [C].
- Do not call the amber axe pendants Thor's hammers: their meaning is not given [C].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- A sandstone slab with round cup-shaped hollows, perhaps for shaping gaming pieces; three amber gaming pieces [C].
- The brooch pin changed here from a coiled spring to a sprung plate, for no reason anyone can see [C].
- A century of comb-making left waste for only 160 to 350 combs: the rest rotted or was gnawed [C].
- Snorri: Olaf dammed the river with turf and timber and loosed it on Cnut's fleet. Saxo: a bridge broke under the Danes [enwp Helgeå; svwp Helgeå]. The Chronicle (Ingram 1823): "There were very many men lost on the side of King Knute, both of Danish and English; and the Swedes had possession of the field of battle."
- Aos means river mouth, not "river house" [svwp Åhus].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- The name in 793. "Áhús" is a back-formation from Aos (1286 or 1296).
- Any ruler, toll, law, thing, shrine, grave field, mint or named person before 1025; raids, fires or floods.
- That the Danish king held it in 793.
- Bead-makers at work in 793, or a summer-only fair: this is a year-round town.
- Dunes, canal, mill, castle, church: all later.
- A market here between about 850 and 1050. The game keeps the port open; write those years as a landing and farms unless Elleköpinge is checked (?).
- That the battle of 1025/1026 was certainly here.
