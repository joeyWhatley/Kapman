# Quentovic (Visemarest, La Calotterie): dossier

Keys in brackets are listed under Sources. (?) marks what I could not check on a page I opened. English renderings of the Latin are mine. No web search was available: everything comes from pages and scans I could name and open.

## 1. The place in 793

- **Name.** *Quentawich* [GAF]; plain *Wicus* in the charter of 779 [WRD], *Uuicos* in Alcuin [ALC]; *Quantovicus* [AB]; *Contwig* [NI]; *Cwantawic* in English [ASC]. The wic on the Canche (*Quantia*) [WCA].
- **What.** The Franks' Channel emporium: with Dorestad one of the realm's two great northern ports, and the usual landing for the English bound for Francia and Rome [WE]. The texts say *emporium* and *portus* [AB; MW].
- **Rule.** The king's. His officer is Gervold, abbot of Saint-Wandrille on the Seine, "procurator over the kingdom's trade for many years, exacting tributes and tolls in divers ports and cities, chiefly in Quentawich" [GAF]. It lies between Ponthieu and the land of the Morini [WF]. No count is named.
- **People.** Franks, with English ("Saxon") and Frisian merchants [WE]; craftsmen; abbey agents; pilgrims waiting for a ship or a horse [ALC].
- **Size.** More than 35 ha of occupied ground [WF]. The number of people is unknown.
- **Land and water.** The site is at Visemarest, a hamlet of La Calotterie on the Canche, 3 km north-west of Montreuil and about 12 km from today's coast [WLC; WLF]. Anchors and ship timbers came out of the peat there about 1789 [WLF]. The river now reaches the sea between Étaples (north) and Le Touquet (south); the south spit grows and the north point wears away [WBC], so the mouth, bar and channel of 793 are unknown. Marks: the Roman lighthouse at Boulogne, about 29 km north [WD], an eight-sided stepped tower [WTO], dark in 793 and relit in 811 [CHR]; the hill of Saint-Josse south of the mouth [LF]. The tide's range is large (?): no figure was opened.
- **Where boats lie.** Not known. The game's riverbank landing is an assumption.

## 2. What stood there

- **The settlement at Visemarest.** Found by David Hill's Manchester team, 1984-1991 [WLC; HILL]. A dig on the Chemin de Visemarais in 2009 opened 1.5 ha: over a thousand early medieval features; 30,000 animal bones, mostly cattle and sheep or goat, with no sea fishing on any scale; waste from pottery-making, cloth-working, glass, metal, bone and antler [WD]. Pottery runs into the tenth century [WE].
- **The mint.** Coins from the sixth century; deniers of Pippin (754-768); few of Charlemagne or Louis; many of Charles the Bald after 864 [WE; WD].
- **The toll**, taken by the procurator's men [GAF].
- **St Peter's church "near the emporium of Quentovic"**, Saint-Wandrille's property. The bodies of St Wandrille and St Ansbert lay on its altar for five days from 11 May 858, and came again in 866 [MW].
- **The cell of Saint-Josse** (*cella sancti Iudoci*), a small monastery and hospice: "at Wicus, at Saint-Josse" [ALC 25]; downstream on the same bank, a league from the port [WF].
- **Abbey houses or stores**: Saint-Vaast, Saint-Riquier, Saint-Bertin, Saint-Germain-des-Prés, Saint-Wandrille, Ferrières [WE; WF].
- **Étaples** (*villa Stapulas*), on the north shore: first named in 849 (or 852 (?)) as the landing for English lead [LF 14]. The name means a store [WFE].
- No wall, grave field, well or quay is recorded in what I opened.

## 3. Who could be met

- **Gervold**: chaplain to Queen Bertrada, bishop of Evreux, abbot of Saint-Wandrille from 787 to his death on 14 June 806; procurator, and often the king's envoy to Offa, whose letters calling him his dearest friend were kept at the abbey. "Not much learned in other letters", but a fine singer; he founded a school and roofed two churches with lead [GAF]. His seat is the abbey, not the port.
- **Alcuin** (died 804), to whom Charles "once entrusted the cell of Saint-Josse, to give alms to pilgrims" [LF 11], about 792 by an editor's reckoning [LF]. A letter reached him "at Wicus" on 10 July 799 [ALC 176].
- **Martin**, Alcuin's priest, left sick at Saint-Josse, autumn 794 [ALC 25].
- **Æthelheard, archbishop of Canterbury**, bound for Rome in spring 801, met at Saint-Josse by Alcuin's man [ALC 232].
- **Warembald**, holder of the cell after Alcuin; **Hruoding**, given it by Lothar about 841; **Count Odulf**, who kept it through the 840s; **Lupus, "abbot of Ferrières and of Saint-Josse"**, who won it back [LF].
- **Grippo, "prefect of the emporium of Quentovic"**, an *illuster vir*, Charles the Bald's envoy "to the kings of the English", in 858 or soon after [MW].
- **Helgaud, count** (dead by 926), **Herluin** his son (killed 945) and **Roger**, Herluin's son, lords of Montreuil; **Arnulf, count of Flanders**, their enemy [FL; WHM].

Ordinary people: toll-takers; moneyers (no Carolingian coin names one); the craftsmen of section 2; herdsmen [WD]; church wardens (*custodes*) and "countless clergy and lay folk" [MW]; a blind and lame boy and his fevered mother, healed in 866 [MW]; householders who could buy off raiders [AB]; monks caring for strangers "from over the sea" [LF 71]. No ordinary name survives.

## 4. What was traded

- **Goods.** Wine, quernstones and cloth to the English; pottery bottles and glass [WE]; English wool, tin, Baltic amber [WF]; slaves [WE]; English lead [LF 13]. The cell sent Ferrières wax, clothing, fish, cheese and pulses [LF 43, 71].
- **Coin.** The king's denier, about 1.7 g after 793/4 [TR]. Ship deniers of Quentovic and Dorestad, 812-814 [SH]. In 864 Quentovic is one of ten mints allowed, and "the mint of Rouen belongs to Quentovic by ancient custom" [EP; WFP]. The Fécamp hoard (about 980-985) held 472 deniers and 27 half-deniers of Quentovic in Charles the Bald's name [WFH].
- **Toll.** The king's tenth (*decima*), written down in 828: his merchants go free everywhere but Quentovic, Dorestad and the Alpine passes [TRF]. Saint-Germain-des-Prés was freed of toll here in 779 [TRF]. The rate in 793 is assumed.
- **Law.** From 796 true pilgrims pass free; traders among them pay [CHR]. Only old and just tolls (805) [ECO]. From 864, death for selling arms or horses to Northmen [CHR].
- **Fairs.** None recorded. Men of the wic went to the Saint-Denis fair, from 9 October [MA; RTS] (?).
- **Ships and season.** Boniface came in "a small swift ship" with "huge sails" to "the mouth of the river called Cuent" [VB]. Grippo sailed for home in December with a fair wind and was half-way over as the night ended [MW]. Æthelheard crossed in spring [ALC 232].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- **c. 790** Charles's son asks for Offa's daughter; Offa wants Charles's daughter Bertha for his son; the king, "somewhat angered", orders that no Englishman land in Gaul to trade. The abbey's history says Gervold's plea stopped it [GAF]; Alcuin in 790 says sailing "is forbidden to merchants on both sides" [ALC 7].
- **794-801** Alcuin's people pass through the cell (section 3).
- **796** Charles's letter to Offa [CHR].
- **800, spring** Charles inspects the coast, sets a fleet and guards against pirates; Easter at Saint-Riquier [CHR]. In **811** he is at Boulogne [CHR].
- **806, 14 June** Gervold dies [GAF].
- **842** "A fleet of Northmen came on the emporium called Quantovicus suddenly at first light, and so raged with plunder, captivity and the killing of both sexes that they left nothing in it but the buildings ransomed for a price" [AB]. They did the same at Hamwic and "Nordhunnwig" [NI]. The English Chronicle (under 839): "great slaughter in London, in Cwantawic and in Rochester" [ASC].
- **c. 849** Lupus regains Saint-Josse and asks for lead at Étaples [LF].
- **858, 11 May** The saints of Saint-Wandrille, carried from the Seine for fear of the Danes, reach St Peter's [MW]. That December or soon after, Grippo's storm [MW].
- **864, 25 June** Edict of Pîtres [EP; CHR].
- **866** The bodies at St Peter's again; **885** they reach Chartres, still fleeing [MW].
- **898** Montreuil first named [WFM]. Raids in 864, 881, 890 and 894, and Danes holding the port about 898 [WF] (?).
- **903** The monks of Saint-Josse flee to England; the relics go to Winchester [WJ].
- **929** Counts Heribert and Hugh besiege Montreuil, "castle of Herluin, son of Count Hilgaud" [FL].
- **936** Louis, sent by his uncle Athelstan, lands on the sands at Boulogne [FL].
- **939** Arnulf takes Montreuil through a traitor and sends Herluin's wife and sons over the sea to Athelstan; Herluin retakes it with Northmen. An English fleet sent to help Louis plunders the Morini coast and goes home with nothing done [FL]. Arnulf takes it again in 948 [FL].
- **980** Hugh Capet takes Montreuil, by 988 called his only sea port [WFM]. About now, the last known coin of Quentovic [WE; WF].
- **early 11th century** The site is given up, slowly [WE].
- **1064** Harold Godwinson, wrecked on the coast of Ponthieu, is held by Count Guy at Beaurain on the Canche [WG].

## 6. Faith and custom

- **St Josse (Judoc)**, a Breton king's son who died a hermit near here about 668; feast 13 December. His body was said not to decay, so his keepers went on cutting his hair [WJ]. The cell's duty: "alms to pilgrims", care "of those from over the sea and other poor" [LF 11, 71].
- **Meeting relics** (858): on the eve of the Ascension the people came out with gospel books, crosses and candles, threw themselves down weeping, and walked ahead singing. Where the bodies had rested by the Authie bridge a wooden cross was set up [MW].
- **Vows at sea.** Grippo promised gifts in the storm and paid them at the saints' church on Christmas Eve [MW].
- **Not found:** burial, the bishop (Amiens (?)), any heathen cult.

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **The archbishop's horse.** Alcuin sends Æthelheard "the saddle I am used to ride on, fitted out as the pastors of churches have it in this country", a horse and a servant [ALC 230].
- **Grippo and the lighthouse.** A storm rose near dawn in mid-Channel. At sunrise he saw "the lighthouse on the shore, built of old by the skill of the ancients for the courses of seafarers" (Boulogne's, by inference) and the church where St Wandrille then lay; he prayed, the sea calmed, sail was hoisted, and the boat ran ashore unbroken among rocks [MW].
- **The leaking painted church.** Lupus asks King Æthelwulf for lead, and the king's secretary Felix to see it shipped to Étaples [LF 13, 14].
- **Theodore of Tarsus** fell sick here on his way to Canterbury (668-669) and sailed when he mended [BE].
- **A lost town.** An 1886 editor put it at "Wicquinghem" [GAF]; a 1936 translation of the edict reads "St. Josse" [EP]; old translations of the Chronicle turn Cwantawic into Canterbury [ASCT]. The museum at Étaples (1967) bore its name before the site was found [WMQ; WLC].
- Coins of Quentovic lay in the Cuerdale hoard in Lancashire (about 903-910) [WCU].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Where ships lay, where toll and mint stood, the streets, any wall, the graves, the number of people.
- Any moneyer, toll-man, priest or merchant by name: only Gervold and Grippo.
- Whether the ban on English merchants ever bit: the sources disagree [GAF; ALC 7].
- Gervold and Alcuin did not live here. No text has Gervold buying English lead: that is Lupus, about 849.
- The place was not destroyed in 842: a prefect, a church, a mint and a busy coinage follow.
- No light at Boulogne before 811. No castle or count at Montreuil before about 900. Étaples is a *villa*.
- Why it died is argued: silting, sea level, bigger ships, fortified Montreuil [WE; WD].
- The goods list is Wikipedia's; the studies behind it were not opened.
- The port table has no end year; the evidence says fading after 900, gone early in the eleventh century.
