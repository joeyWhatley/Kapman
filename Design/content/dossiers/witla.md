# Witla (lost, the Maas mouth): dossier

Port `witla`: Witla, modern "lost, the Maas mouth (?)", 51.89 N 4.22 E; realm franks, county masaland, region rhine_delta, kind emporium, landing beach, size 2, last year 836 [ports_793.json]. Researched 4 Oct 2026. Source keys are spelled out in section 9. (?) marks what I could not check.

**Read this first.** Witla as a market is known from one sentence, written of its burning. Nothing of it has been dug. No building, person, cargo or toll of Witla itself is on record; the rest here is the river mouth round it.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What it is.** An emporium "near the mouth of the river Maas" [AF 836]: the whole record of it as a trading place [D 324]. It is probably the same as Witle, a small place (locellus) "in the Frisian district", where one Rathadus gave St Peter's abbey at Ghent a farm with grazing for five cows, and four serfs, in the late 7th century [D 175, 324; nlwp Witla]. Gysseling dates that note to the early 9th century, in a copy of 941 [G].
- **What it was for.** Dijkstra's reading, not a record: a safe harbour on the coast, and most likely the place where toll was taken from ships on the Maas and the Waal, the two river roads that did not pass Dorestad [D 324]. Traffic through the mouth came down the Rhine and Maas and mostly went on south: to Domburg (`walichrum`), Flanders, southern England, the north French coast [D 54].
- **Who rules.** Charlemagne. The district is the pagus Marsum (about 700), later Masaland (985) [D 16]. The people are Frisians: their law of 802-803 counts the coast from the Sincfal (the Zwin) to the Vlie as its western part [D 359]. No count of 793 is named.
- **How big.** Unknown. Size 2 is the game's guess.
- **Where.** Unknown. Hoek (1979) put it at the mouth of the Widele, a great tidal creek on Voorne later called the Bernisse, because the names are alike [D 324]; the game's coordinates sit there. Others say the Hoeksche Waard [nlwp Witla]. Dijkstra calls it "later washed away by the sea" [D 383].
- **Coming in.** A wide estuary, the Romans' Helinium, between the dune spit of Monster and Naaldwijk on the north and the head of Voorne on the south [nlwp Helinium; D 38-39]. Behind the dunes lies salt marsh silted above the storm-flood line, cut by tidal creeks, with peat fen behind; no dykes yet [D 38-39]. The settled ground is the north bank [D 16-17]. The river is Mosa in 726 and 836, Merwede in 1018 [D 282; AF 836; nlwp 1018]. The game's water study gives a bar, a strong ebb, about 1.7 m of tide and a double low water: modern figures carried back [repo water]. Where boats lay is not known; if Hoek is right, in the creek mouth on the south shore.

## 2. What stood there

Of Witla itself nothing is found, and nothing is expected: the storm floods of the 12th century drowned the old land south of the Maas and cut it with deep channels [D 39, 81]. These are real, and near.

- **The farm at Witle**: house, pasture for five cows, four serfs [D 175].
- **Dredged from the mouth** when the Europoort harbours were dug (west Rozenburg, off the north shore of Voorne, 1959-1976; all washed out of place): bronze pins, a Domburg-type brooch, two equal-armed brooches (one late Merovingian, one Carolingian), two enamelled Carolingian brooches, four spearheads (4th to 9th century), a piece of a glass tumbler, a bronze chest fitting, Carolingian sherds, human bone; three sceattas of the early 8th century and one of the "Wodan and monster" type; two gold tremisses, one of the Dorestad moneyer Madelinus; five deniers of Louis the Pious (822-840). Dijkstra lists them as "settlement and/or grave field", not as Witla [D 266, 451-452].
- **Botlek** (dredged 1962): a Badorf sherd, three sceattas, a denier of Pippin III (751-768) [D 453].
- **South-bank farms**: at Geervliet (dug 1966) two washed-out dwelling places on a creek, with Badorf ware and relief-band amphorae; at Spijkenisse-Hartel West (dug 1985-1987) standing timber and Badorf ware of the 9th-10th century [D 455].
- **Vlaardingen, north bank**: the church "in the district Marsum, where the Maas goes into the sea", given with a sheep pasture by the cleric Heribald to Willibrord, and by him to Echternach abbey in 726/727. The first church is not found; late Merovingian and Carolingian dwelling beside it is [D 282].
- **Harg** (Harago, later Kethel), in the fen behind Vlaardingen: the name means "heathen shrine" [D 288, 301].
- **Naaldwijk**: a Merovingian grave field on the dune; Hontsele, perhaps a lord's hall; the church of Holtsele, St Martin's of Utrecht's [D 281, 339].
- **Maasland**: the king's estate; its whole tithe was St Martin's [D 285]. **Masamuthon** ("Maas mouth", probably Monster): what one Elegsuind inherited there went to St Martin's [D 284, 482].
- **Maassluis**: a whole Badorf spouted pot left in a creek, with butchered cattle and sheep bone [D 451].
- **Rotterdam**, upriver: washed Carolingian sherds, a denier of Charlemagne (793-814) [D 453-454]; the farm village Rotta, 9th to 11th century, its church named in 1028 [nlwp Rotta].
- **Not there**: no church, mint, wall, well or grave field of Witla is known. Dijkstra expects no late 9th-century fort at it [D 302], though his summary leaves that open [D 383]. The first fort known on the Maas mouth is Vlaardingen's, named in 1018 [D 303].

## 3. Who could be met

No person is recorded at Witla between 793 and 836. Tied to the river mouth:

- Rathadus, Heribald and Willibrord (died 739 (memory)): all before the game [D 175, 282].
- Elegsuind, landholder at Masamuthon, before about 900 [D 482].
- Louis the Pious, emperor 814-840: in 837 he hurried to Nijmegen, held a public inquiry into the lords charged with the coast watch, sent "strenuous abbots and counts" and ordered ships built [AB 837].
- Eggihard, count of Walcheren, and Hemming son of Halfdan, killed on Walcheren on 17 June 837 [AF 837].
- Rorik the Dane, holding Dorestad from 850, last named 873; Godfrid, killed 885 [enwp Rorik; repo chronicle]. Whether either held the Maas mouth is not said.
- Gerulf, count on this coast from about 885; Dirk II (died 6 May 988), given Masaland between Lier and IJssel in 985 [enwp Gerulf; nlwp Dirk II; D 303].
- Dirk III, count 993-1039, who took toll at Vlaardingen; Godfrey, duke of Lower Lotharingia, beaten there in 1018; Dirk IV, 1039-1049; Floris I, 1049-1061 [nlwp 1018; D 128, 281].
- Ordinary people the evidence shows: stock farmers with cows and sheep, serfs, a priest, fishermen, farm wives at the loom, Frisian skippers passing [D 175, 282, 314-315]. A toll-taker is likely, not proven [D 324].

## 4. What was traded

No cargo of Witla is recorded. From the coast round it [D 308-315]:

- **In, down the Rhine and Maas**: Rhenish wine in casks (old casks line wells in every settlement); wheel-thrown pots from Badorf, Walberberg and Mayen; relief-band amphorae; drinking glasses from the Eifel and Ardennes; lava querns from Mayen; whetstones; iron; grain.
- **Out**: Frisian cloth (the coast's herds are rich in sheep), linen, hides; sea fish dried, smoked or salted (7 to 9 per cent of the fish eaten at Utrecht and Dorestad came from the sea). Salt was burned from salted peat in Zeeland; in South Holland only the islands in the Maas mouth were fit for it.
- **Coin**: sceattas (old by 793), deniers of Pippin, Charlemagne and Louis the Pious; no mint [D 452-454].
- **Toll**: none recorded. In 1018 Dirk III's toll at Vlaardingen, on the run between Tiel and England, was plain robbery to the Tiel merchants [nlwp 1018]. Vlaardingen, and in the 12th century Geervliet on the south bank, took over Witla's part [D 324].
- **Season and ships**: sea-going was a summer trade of a little over six months [D 54]. No ship is found.

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- Late 7th century (or early 9th [G]): Rathadus gives Witle to Ghent [D 175].
- 726/727: Willibrord gives the church at the Maas mouth to Echternach [D 282].
- 802-803: the Frisians' law is written at Aachen [D 359].
- 834: a Danish fleet wastes part of Frisia and comes by Utrecht to Dorestad [AB 834].
- 835: Dorestad again [AB 835; AF 835].
- 836: "The Northmen burned the town of Antwerp, and likewise Witla, the emporium near the mouth of the river Maas, and took tribute from the Frisians" [AF 836, my translation; Latin in section 9]. The Annals of St-Bertin say only that they wasted Dorestad and Frisia again [AB 836].
- 837, 17 June: Walcheren; then Dorestad; tribute again [AF 837].
- 838, 26 December: the sea covers nearly all Frisia; 2,437 counted dead [AB 839]. Thought to be Witla's death blow, though it may have been failing already, like other emporia [D 324].
- 841: Lothar gives Walcheren and the places near it to the Dane Harald [AB 841].
- About 885: Godfrid dead, Gerulf count; a ring fort is built at Rijnsburg for the Rhine mouth. None is known on the Maas [D 128, 302].
- 985, 25 August: Otto III's grant of Masaland to Dirk II [nlwp Dirk II].
- 1006: Northmen burn Tiel [repo chronicle]; if they came by the Merwede they passed this mouth (?).
- 1018, 29 July: Dirk III beats the emperor's army at Vlaardingen. The ships had stood off into deep water for the ebb, and men drowned trying to reach them [nlwp 1018].
- 1047: Henry III takes Rijnsburg and Vlaardingen [D 128].
- 1063: a charter says the counts seized Echternach's churches between 993 and 1061 [D 281].
- After the game: Gysseling has a form "Witla, 1167" from a source I could not open (?) [G].

## 6. Faith and custom

- **Church**: none known at Witla. The mother church is Vlaardingen's, Echternach's (Willibrord, feast 7 November (memory)); Holtsele and the Maasland tithe are St Martin's (11 November (memory)) [D 281-285].
- **Old gods**: Harg behind Vlaardingen; Dijkstra thinks Willibrord may have wanted the church set against it [D 288]. The Frisians' law ends with the temple-breaker led to the tide line and given to the gods he robbed (memory) (?).
- **Law**: in the western part the blood-price of noble, free and half-free runs 4:2:1, and so does the count of oath-helpers needed to deny a killing [D 359].
- **Burial and meeting**: grave fields served as meeting places; Naaldwijk's, or Harg, may have been Maasland's [D 301].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- A market known only by the sentence that burns it.
- At a very low tide in 1752 Jan Kluit of Brielle saw heavy pieces of stone, like lumps of foundations, just breaking the water off Oostvoorne: probably Roman, perhaps a fort [D 454].
- Alcuin sends a poem down the Rhine: greet the bishop of Utrecht, "rich in cattle"; prior Hadda will give honey, porridge and butter, since Frisia bears neither oil nor wine [D 279, Alcuin, Carmina IV; my paraphrase].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Where Witla lay. The Widele site is a guess from a name; name no modern village as fact.
- Its size, layout, church, saint, mint, market day, fair, toll, reeve, or anybody who lived there.
- That it was walled; that the flood of 838 ended it (an idea, not a record); that it was rebuilt.
- That the dredged brooches and coins are Witla's.
- That Rorik, Harald or Godfrid held it.
- That Witle and Witla are one place (probable only), or when the Witle note was written.
- That the Annals of St-Bertin name it. The sentence is in the Annals of Fulda; the game's chronicle entry `836-witla-antwerp-burned` cites "AB 836" for it.
- The table's last year, 836, is the last mention, not a recorded end.
