# Reric (Groß Strömkendorf, Wismar Bay): dossier

Keys in brackets are listed under Sources. (?) marks what I could not check on a page I opened on 6 Oct 2026. No web search was available (the session's budget was spent), so the digs are known here only through encyclopedia summaries of the excavators' books; everything marked "from memory" needs a look at the book before it ships. English renderings of the Latin are mine.

## 1. The place in 793

- **Name.** *Reric*: "the emporium which stood on the shore of the ocean and was called Reric in the Danes' tongue" [ARF 808]. That is the Danes' word for it; what the Wends called it is not recorded. The name is usually explained from reeds [WDE]. Adam of Bremen and Helmold call the Obodrites *Reregi*, probably the same word [WEN].
- **What.** A sea trading place of the Obodrites (Abodrites), a Wendish people, standing since about 735 and at its fullest in the 780s [WDE]. In 793 it is near its peak.
- **Rule.** The land is Obodrite: Witzan is their prince until 795, then Thrasco [ARF 789, 795; CHR]. But the market "brought great profit to [Godfred's] kingdom by the payment of dues" [ARF 808]: the Danish king takes its toll. Who was king of the Danes in 793 is Sigfred (?) (named 777 to 798 in the annals, from memory); Godfred is first named in 804 [CHR].
- **People.** Mixed: Slavs and Scandinavians [WEN]; Saxons and Frisians are likely from the graves and goods (?) (from memory of Jöns and Gerds). The common pottery is the Slavs' own Sukow ware; western pots are few [WEN].
- **Size.** About 500 m along the shore; with the grave field at least 20 ha [WEN; WDE]. The first settlement of 735 was about 3 ha [WDE]. The German Wikipedia gives "10,000 to 20,000" permanent inhabitants [WDE]: this is not believable for the century and must not be used. No sound head-count was found; hundreds, more in the trading season (?).
- **Land and water.** The east shore of Wismar Bay, the southernmost bight of the Baltic, at 53°57'20"N 11°28'53"E [WDE; WWB]. The island of Poel lies across the mouth of the bay to the north-east; between Poel and the mainland runs the Breitling, about a kilometre wide, with small islands in it [WBL; WWB]. A skipper comes in from the open bay with Poel to port (if from the west) and the low mainland shore ahead; the Lieps sandbank lies west of today's fairway and Walfisch island in the eastern bay [WWB] (modern marks: the shoals of 793 are not known). The Baltic has no tide to speak of (?). The shore is low, with reed beds, if the name means what it seems to [WDE].
- **Where boats lie.** "A deep water port with a planned layout" [WEN]. The harbour basin is thought to be a sheltered inlet since silted and partly lost to the sea, the sea then standing lower than now (?) (from memory of Jöns). No jetty has been reported in what I opened: the game's "beach" landing is fair.

## 2. What stood there

- **The first settlement, 735/736.** About 3 ha at the north end, with wells set close together; dated by tree rings [WDE; TUM].
- **The laid-out town, 760s to early 770s.** The settlement moved south and was set out afresh in a regular, chequered plan; the old northern ground became the grave field [WDE; WEN].
- **The 780s.** The most wells of any phase: fast growth [WDE].
- **The last phase, 806 to 811.** Wells of 806, 809 and 811, then nothing [WDE; WEN]. So the place was mended after 808 and given up soon after 811.
- **Sunken-floored huts** (pit houses), like those of West Stow in England [WEN]; about a hundred dug (?) (from memory of Tummuscheit). Dwellings and workshops in one.
- **Wells.** Timber-lined; 127 wood samples, 95 dated, 70 to the exact year [WDE]. They are the town's calendar.
- **Workshops.** Weaving, iron-smithing, bronze-casting, glass-working (beads), amber-working, antler and horn (combs); unfinished pieces show work done on the spot, at a modest scale [WEN].
- **Hoards of whetstones** of Norwegian schist [WEN].
- **The grave field**, north of the town, from about the middle of the 700s [WDE]. Boat graves are listed there [WSG]. From memory (?): some 250 graves, burnt and unburnt, about six boat graves, horses and dogs buried with or beside the dead; the mixture of rites is the best evidence for a mixed people (Gerds).
- **Open Slav settlements** within 10 km; the forts of Mecklenburg and Ilow inland, thought to begin in the early 700s [WEN]. But the Mecklenburg rampart's dated building is 929 to 945 [WBM], so do not put a prince's hall there in 793 without (?).
- **Not found, in what I opened:** a wall, a hall, a temple, a toll house, a mint, a church. There was no church: the Obodrites were heathen.
- **Dug** 1989 to 1992 by the Schwerin research office, and 1995 to 1999 by Kiel University with the state office; Frank Wietrzichowski, Hauke Jöns, Astrid Tummuscheit [WDE; WEN].

## 3. Who could be met

- **Witzan** (Witzin), prince of the Obodrites; with Charles against the Wilzi in 789; killed by Saxons crossing the Elbe at Hliuni (Lüne) in 795 [ARF 789, 795 (from memory); WDR].
- **Thrasco** (Drasco, Dražko), his son (?) [WDR], *dux* of the Obodrites from about 795; victor on the Sventana field at Bornhöved in 798, beside the king's envoy Eburis; given the Saxon land beyond the Elbe at Hollenstedt in 804; driven out in 808; "killed by guile in the emporium of Reric by Godfred's men", late 809 [ARF 798, 804, 808, 809; CHR]. The one great man certainly at Reric.
- **Godelaib**, "another duke" of the Obodrites, taken by a trick and hanged by Godfred, 808 [ARF 808; CHR].
- **Godfred**, king of the Danes, first named 804, killed by one of his own following in 810 [ARF; WGU]. At Reric with his fleet in 808.
- **Reginold**, Godfred's brother's son, killed with many Danish chief men storming a Slav stronghold, 808 [ARF 808; CHR]. Which stronghold is not said.
- **Thrasco's son**, given to Godfred as a hostage in 808 or 809 [ARF 809]; probably the **Ceadrag** who rules from 819 [WDR] (?).
- **Sclaomir** (Slavomir), Thrasco's successor, 810 to 819 [WDR; WAB].
- **Charles the Younger**, the emperor's son, on the Elbe in 808 [ARF 808]: never at Reric.
- **Ordinary people the evidence shows:** merchants worth a king's trouble to carry off [ARF 808]; merchants who carried Godfred's message to the emperor in 809 [ARF 809]; smiths, bronze-casters, bead-makers, amber-cutters, comb-makers, weavers [WEN]; well-diggers and carpenters [WDE]; boat-owners buried in their boats [WSG]; the Danish king's toll-taker, implied by the dues [ARF 808]. Not one ordinary name survives.

## 4. What was traded

- **In.** Whetstones from Norway [WEN]; a little western pottery, Tating ware among it [WEN]; Rhineland quernstones, Frankish glass, a few sceattas of the Ribe kind (?) (from memory of Jöns).
- **Out.** Not shown by the dig. The Slav coast's goods in general: horses and cattle, grain, honey, wax, salt, furs, slaves [WPO, citing Herrmann 1985]; amber worked on the spot [WEN].
- **Coins and weights.** No mint. Silver by weight. Dirhams only reach this coast about 800 and after (see `economy/silver_coin.md`): few or none here (?).
- **Tolls and laws.** Dues to the Danish king [ARF 808]; rate and manner unknown. By Frankish law no mail or swords may be sold to Slavs, and from 805 Franks trade with them only at the Elbe posts [CHR].
- **Seasons.** Not recorded. The sailing summer, by likeness with other Baltic markets (?).
- **Ships.** Clinker-built boats, known from the rivets of the boat graves (?); the repo's `ships.md` gives 9 to 15 m.
- **Why it mattered.** A Baltic door to the Franks' allies that passed by the Saxons and the Danes [WEN]; the eastern end of a chain Ribe, Reric, Hedeby (Müller-Wille) [WDE].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- **c. 735.** First settlement [WDE].
- **760s.** Moved south and laid out anew [WDE; WEN].
- **780s.** The busiest years [WDE].
- **789.** Witzan marches with Charles against the Wilzi [ARF; WDR].
- **795.** Witzan killed at the Elbe; Thrasco follows him [ARF; WDR].
- **798.** Thrasco beats the Nordalbingian Saxons at Bornhöved [ARF; CHR].
- **804.** Charles gives Thrasco the Saxons' land beyond the Elbe; Godfred comes to Sliesthorp [ARF; CHR].
- **806.** New wells dug [WDE].
- **808, summer.** Godfred crosses with fleet and army, the Wilzi with him; storms Slav strongholds at heavy cost; Thrasco flees, Godelaib hangs; two thirds of the Obodrites pay tribute. "Before he went home" he destroys Reric, carries off its merchants, sails to Sliesthorp and orders the wall with one gate [ARF 808].
- **809.** A well dug [WDE]: people are back. Godfred sends word to the emperor by merchants; talks at Badenfliot fail. Thrasco, with Saxon help, wastes the Wilzi and takes the Smeldingi's chief place; then is murdered at Reric [ARF 809]. (The Moissac chronicle puts the death under 810 [CHR].)
- **810.** Godfred killed by his own man; Sclaomir rules the Obodrites [ARF; WDR].
- **811.** The last dated timber. The place dies soon after [WDE; WEN].
- **817 to 819.** Sclaomir falls away from the Franks and is deposed; Ceadrag follows [WAB].
- **844.** King Goztomuizli killed by Louis the German [WAB; CHR].
- **929 to 945.** The Mecklenburg rampart built, some 12 km (?) inland; about 965 Ibrahim ibn Yaqub calls it Nakon's fort; 995 a charter names *Michelenburg* [WBM].
- **983, 1018, 1066.** The heathen risings; Gottschalk killed 7 June 1066 [WAB; CHR].
- After 811 the bay has no market the texts name. Wismar is a later town.

## 6. Faith and custom

- Heathen throughout. No church, priest or saint, and no feast days: say so.
- **Burial** is the evidence: burning and earth burial side by side; some dead in boats; horses and dogs (?) [WSG; from memory of Gerds]. The boat burial is a northern custom, so Scandinavians probably lived and died here.
- **The Wends' gods** are named only by writers of the 11th and 12th centuries, at other places (see `peoples/wendish.md`). No god is recorded for Reric.
- **Law.** Unknown. The one legal fact is the king's dues [ARF 808].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **A king steals a town.** Godfred took the merchants and left the houses: the plainest proof in the annals that traders, not buildings, were the wealth [ARF 808].
- **The lost town.** For centuries nobody knew where Reric had been; Oldenburg, Lübeck and the Mecklenburg fort were all proposed. In 1938 the village of Alt Gaarz, 17 km north-east, renamed itself Rerik on a guess. The spade found the real place in the 1990s [WDE; WEN]. (For the user, not for a person of 793.)
- **The wells tell the years.** 806, 809, 811: a town rebuilt after a king destroyed it, by people who had three more years [WDE].
- **Merchants as messengers**: in 809 a king's word to an emperor went in a trader's mouth [ARF 809].
- **"By guile."** The annals say only *per dolum*. How Thrasco died is not told [ARF 809].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- The Wends' own name for the place, or that "Reric" means falcon or reeds for certain.
- Any head-count; never the 10,000 to 20,000 of [WDE].
- That Thrasco or Witzan had a hall here. The annals put Thrasco here once, at his death.
- Who killed Thrasco, how, or on what day; that Godfred "burned" the town (the word is *distructo*, destroyed).
- Any named merchant, craftsman or toll-taker; any temple, idol, wall, mint, church or fair day.
- The rate of the toll, and why Danes took it in an Obodrite town.
- That every merchant went to Sliesthorp, or went willingly.
- The numbers for graves, boat graves, huts, the sceattas, the querns and the harbour's form: all (?) here, from memory.
- **A clash in the tables:** `ports_793.json` ends Reric in 808, but the murder is in 809 and the last well in 811. The port should stand, much reduced, to 811.
