# Grobin (Grobiņa, Rimbert's Seeburg): dossier

Keys in brackets are listed under Sources. (?) marks what I could not check on a page I opened on 6 Oct 2026. No web search was to be had (the session's budget was spent), so the digs are known here through encyclopedia summaries of the excavators' books, not the books. "From memory" means exactly that. Rimbert is quoted from Robinson 1921, which I read in full for chapter 30.

## 1. The place in 793

- **Name.** No name of 793 survives. *Seeburg* ("lake fort" or "sea fort") is the Saxon Rimbert's word, about 870, for a town of the Cori [R30]; Latvians render it Jūrpils or Ezerpils [WLP; WLG]. *Grobin* is first written in 1253 [WLG; WLP]. The Latvian page derives it from Curonian *gruobīns*, a hornbeam wood (Latvian *skābardis*; the fort hill is Skābarža kalns, "hornbeam hill") [WLG] (?) (the meaning of *skābardis* is from memory).
- **What.** A settlement of Scandinavians among the Curonians, from about 650: "comparable in many ways to Hedeby and Birka but probably predating them both" [WEN]. Traders, by the German page, "comparable to Staraya Ladoga" [WDE].
- **Who lives there.** Gotlanders and Svear from the Mälar valley, told apart by their graves: two grave fields are Gotlandic, the third is of Mälar kind [WDE]. Women in the flat graves wear Gotland's belt buckles and brooches; the mounds mostly hold men with Scandinavian weapons; one field is "military in character", the other two civilian [WEN]. Around them, the Curonians, a Baltic people [WCU].
- **Who rules.** Unknown. Rimbert says the Cori "had in former time been in subjection to the Swedes, but had a long while since rebelled", and that "their kingdom contained five towns" [R30]. He names no king or chief. Whether the Svear still held Grobin in 793 is the open question (see 8).
- **Size.** A fort plateau up to 80 m by 40 to 45 m, a settlement of about 3 ha beside it, and grave fields of some thousands of burials [WAE]. Rimbert's "seven thousand fighting men" [R30] is not a head-count to use.
- **Land and water.** The fort stands on a spit on the right bank of the Ālande, which guarded it on three sides [WAE; WEN; WAT]. Grobiņa lies about 11 km from the open coast [WAT]. The Ālande runs into Lake Liepāja: 37 km2, 16 km long, 2 m deep on average and 3 m at most, with 13 islands, behind a sand bar [WLL; WAT]. The lake's outlet to the sea kept silting shut until a canal was dug in 1697 to 1703 [WAT]. Outside is a harbourless surf coast [WAT].
- **As a skipper sees it.** From what is above, and no more: a low sand coast with no haven; a shifting outlet over a bar; a wide, shallow, reedy lake; then a small river to a fort hill. Where the outlet lay in 793, its depth, and how far a laden boat could go up the Ālande are not known (?). The lake "served as a harbour" [WDE]; no jetty has been reported in what I opened. Today's mill pond under the hill is a later dam [WLP].

## 2. What stood there

- **The fort, Skābarža kalns.** Plateau up to 80 by 40 to 45 m; at the east end a rampart about 30 m wide and 2 m high; culture layer up to 1.2 m, dated 5th to 13th century [WAE]. So it was a Curonian fort before, during and after the Scandinavians. Which people held it in 793 is not settled (?).
- **The settlement.** About 3 ha beside the fort, culture layer up to 0.6 m: Nerman's "black earth", rich in finds [WAE; WSV].
- **Priediens grave field.** About 2,000 low mounds, mostly 7 to 10 m across and 0.5 m high; more than 100 dug between 1929 and 1989; cremations [WAE]. The Swedish page counts about 450 mounds left of about 1,000 [WSV]: the counts disagree. This is probably the weapon field of Mälar kind (?) (from memory of Nerman).
- **Smukumi flat grave field.** More than 100 graves dug (1929, 1930, 1962, 1987): women with Scandinavian brooches, pendants, rings and glass beads; men with one-edged and two-edged swords, spearheads and shield bosses [WAE]. Gotlandic (?) (from memory).
- **Porāni (Pūrāni) mounds.** 30 mounds, 5.7 to 8.3 m across, 0.3 to 0.6 m high; six dug in 1929 to 1930; 7th to 9th century [WAE].
- **Atkalni flat grave field.** Seven cremation graves dug in 1988 to 1989 [WAE]. Curonian (?) (from memory).
- **The picture stone.** Found in 1987 in a grave at Priediens: 0.70 m high, 0.08 m thick, carved with two duck-like birds; second half of the 7th century. Such stones are otherwise found only on Gotland [WEN; WAE].
- **The three Scandinavian fields lie in a half circle** round the settlement [WSV]. All burials are cremations, with weapons and ornaments of Vendel-period Nordic style [WDE].
- **Not found, in what I opened:** a church, a mint, a toll place, a named hall, a wall of stone, a temple, a jetty, a well.
- **Later, not for the game's years:** the Livonian Order burned the Curonians' timber fort in 1263; the stone castle is later still [WLP; WDE].
- **Dug** in 1929 and 1930 by Birger Nerman with Karl-Alfred Gustawsson and Hans Hansson (about 100 flat graves and 30 mounds) [WSV; WEN]; again in 1962 and 1984 to 1989 [WAE], by Latvian and Russian archaeologists (Petrenko, Urtāns (?), from memory).

## 3. Who could be met

- **Olof (Olaf), king of the Svear**, at Birka in the early 850s. He "collected an immense army", came first to Seeburg, and "ravaged and despoiled and burnt" it, about 854 [R30]. The one named person certainly there, and only as its burner.
- **Anskar**, archbishop, was in Sweden when the Danes sailed against the Cori [R30]. Never at Grobin.
- **Erimbert**, the priest Anskar left at Birka; the chapter ends with his work prospering after the war [R30]. Never at Grobin.
- **No Curonian of these years is named** in any source I opened. Lamekins, a Curonian prince, appears only in 1230 [WLG].
- **Unnamed, from Rimbert:** Svea merchants who had heard Anskar and half followed Christ, sailing with the host; "the young men" who wanted plunder and captives instead of a treaty; the king's "chief men"; thirty Curonian hostages; the lot-casters [R30].
- **Unnamed, from the graves:** Gotland women in their own island's dress; armed men from the Mälar valley; a man who had a Gotland picture stone cut or carried for a grave [WEN; WDE; WAE]. No craft is attested in what I opened: do not place a named comb-maker or smith on evidence.
- **Late, colour only:** Egil Skallagrimsson and his brother Thorolf in Kurland, about 925 (?), in a saga written in the 13th century [WCO] (details in 7, from memory).

## 4. What was traded

- **Nothing is recorded directly.** No text lists Grobin's goods, and I opened no find list of trade goods beyond the grave goods.
- **In, from the graves:** Scandinavian swords, spearheads, shield bosses; Gotland brooches, buckles, pendants, rings; glass beads [WAE; WEN].
- **Out, by the region's pattern, not by Grobin's evidence:** amber gathered after autumn and winter storms and sold in spring; furs, wax, honey; captives. Wanted: salt, cloth, swords, silver, iron [TSH] (?).
- **Silver by weight.** At Apulia the Cori offer "half-a-pound of silver for each individual man now in this town", plus the gold and arms taken from the Danes [R30]. The project reckons that at 120 d a man. No coins and no mint: this coast strikes none in these years (?).
- **Tribute.** The Cori had paid the Svear tribute before, and promise it again in 854 [R30]. Its size is not said.
- **Captives.** The young men wish to "carry them off as captives" [R30]: slaving is taken for granted.
- **Seasons.** The Baltic ice lies from November to April [TSH] (?). No fair, feast or assembly is known for Grobin [SAI].
- **Ships.** The Danes come with "a large number of ships"; the Svear leave theirs at a port and march five days inland [R30]. Whether that port was Seeburg's own water is not said.

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- **About 650.** The Scandinavian settlement begins [WEN; WLG].
- **Second half of the 600s.** The picture stone [WEN].
- **793.** The settlement stands. Nothing is recorded for the year.
- **About 800.** By the Latvian pages, the Curonians take over the settlement [WLG; WLP]. The Scandinavian graves are dated to 800 by one page and to 850 by others [WEN; WDE; WSV]. Treat as "between 800 and 850" (?).
- **Before about 853.** The Cori have "a long while since" thrown off the Svear [R30].
- **About 853.** A Danish fleet comes to plunder and subdue the Cori. The five towns gather, kill half the Danes and take their ships, gold and silver [R30].
- **About 854.** Olof and the Svear burn Seeburg, march five days to Apulia (taken to be Apuolė [WCU]), fight eight days, cast lots, and on the ninth take a ransom, tribute and thirty hostages [R30]. The Swedish page gives 855 [WSV]; Rimbert gives no year.
- **After 854.** Seeburg loses standing to other markets such as Truso [WDE]; no later Scandinavian graves are reported. The Curonians keep the fort: its layer runs to the 13th century [WAE].
- **About 925 (?).** Egil's saga sets a truce market and a raid in Kurland; not at a named place [WCO].
- **About 1075.** Adam of Bremen describes the Curonians: rich in gold, with the best horses, houses full of soothsayers [WCU]. Outside the game's years by a decade.
- **1230, 1253, 1263.** The land is called Esestua; Grobin is first named; the timber fort is burned [WLG; WLP]. All after the game.

## 6. Faith and custom

- **Heathen throughout.** No church, priest or saint; the project's calendar gives Grobin none [SAI].
- **Burial.** Cremation for all: Scandinavian men under low mounds with their weapons, women in flat graves in Gotland dress [WEN; WDE; WAE].
- **Lots.** The Svear at Apulia cast lots to learn "whether their gods were willing to aid them", found none willing, cast again for Christ and found him willing [R30]. This is Svea custom on Curonian ground, not Curonian custom.
- **A vow.** Afterwards, on Christian merchants' advice, the Svear kept seven days at home, then seven without meat, and after forty days another forty without meat; many took to alms [R30].
- **Curonian soothsayers.** Houses "full of pagan soothsayers, diviners and necromancers", about 1075 [WCU]. Late for the game; use lightly.
- **Law.** Nothing is known. Peace is made by treaty, tribute and hostages [R30].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **The ducks.** A Gotland picture stone with two duck-like birds, far from the only island that makes such stones [WEN]. A good easter egg.
- **The gods who would not answer.** "Having cast lots they failed to discover any god who was willing to aid them" [R30]. Quotable: Robinson 1921.
- **The Cori's offer.** "We desire peace rather than fighting, and we wish to enter into an agreement with you" [R30].
- **Pride as a cause of war.** The Svear sailed "to win for themselves the reputation that they could do what the Danes had not done" [R30].
- **Danish gold changing hands twice:** taken from the Danes one year, paid to the Svear the next [R30].
- **Egil in Kurland (late, from memory (?)).** A truce of half a month for trading, then raiding; Egil is taken at a farm, breaks loose at night, carries off the farmer's silver, and goes back to say who robbed him, because leaving unseen would be a thief's act (Egil's saga, ch. 46).
- **Robinson's own note** put Seeburg at Selburg on the Düna and Apulia at Pilten [R30 notes]: the Grobin match came with Nerman's spade in 1929 [WSV].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- **That Seeburg is Grobin is a scholar's match**, "usually" accepted [WEN], not a fact in the text. Say "taken to be".
- **Who held the place in 793.** Scandinavians still, or Curonians already: the pages disagree (800 against 850). Write a mixed place, and name no ruler.
- **The table closes `grobin` in 850; the chronicle burns it in 854.** One of them must move (already noted in `realms/sweden_gotland.md`).
- **No names.** No king, chief, merchant, priest or craftsman of Grobin is known. Every person is invented and must be marked so.
- **No Curonian words, gods or rites** are recorded for these years in what I opened. Do not put Perkūnas or any named god in a mouth on this dossier's evidence.
- **No goods list, no toll, no weights, no fair, no coins.** Section 4's trade is regional pattern.
- **The water.** The outlet, the bar and the channel of 793 are unknown. Do not name a landmark.
- **Rimbert's numbers** (7,000 and 15,000 fighting men) are a hagiographer's. The grave counts differ by page (about 3,000 in all [WEN; WDE]; about 2,000 at Priediens alone [WAE]; about 1,000 [WSV]).
- **No Christians lived here.** The Christian merchants are Svear in the host.
- **Not "Vikings", not a "colony" in speech.** Northmen, Gotlanders, Svear.
- **Nothing from Egil's saga or Adam as fact for 793.**
