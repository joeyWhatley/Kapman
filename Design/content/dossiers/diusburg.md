# Diusburg (Duisburg): dossier

Port id `diusburg`: Diusburg, modern Duisburg, 51.435 N 6.762 E, realm franks, region rhineland, kind market, landing riverbank, size 2, no end year [ports_793.json]. Spellings: Diusburh (884), Dusburhc (893), Diuspurch (904), Tusburch (1065). Researched 4 Oct 2026. Keys in square brackets are listed in section 9. (?) marks what I could not check.

## 1. The place in 793

- No text names the place before the winter of 883/84 [R884]. 793 is inferred from digs: Frankish graves from about 450 into the 8th century, and 5th-century settlement traces at the edge of the hill [K-D].
- Wikipedia puts a royal court here from about 740, a base for Charles Martel's Saxon wars, on archaeological grounds [W-G][W-Pf]. Krause, the excavator, says only that one is very likely by the late 9th century [K-Pf]. So in 793: probably an estate of King Charles with its dependants, and a landing. No count, reeve or priest is named; size unknown [K-D].
- It lies on the right bank of the Rhine at the Ruhr's mouth, at the west end of the Hellweg, the road east [W-en][W-B]. Beyond it the Saxons are in revolt in 793 [chronicle_793].
- The lie: the hill (Burgberg, now Burgplatz) is a flood-free spur of the low terrace, about 8 m above the bank, between the mouths of the Dickelsbach and the Ruhr [W-B][K-Pf]. The Rhine ran in a bend right under it; that bed lies some 5 m under today's Alter Markt [K-AM].
- The water: no tide, no bar. Floods silted the foot of the hill until the early 10th century [K-Pf].
- Boats lie on the Rhine bank below the hill and probably on round to the Ruhr mouth; a harbour is proved there only from the last third of the 9th century [K-D][K-H].
- Landmarks: north, the Kaiserberg with two old ramparts [K-W1]; east, the Hellweg's Ruhr crossing at Broich [K-W1]; over the river, the ruins of Roman Asciburgium [W-G]; upstream, St Suitbert's island monastery at Kaiserswerth [W-Kw]; up the Ruhr, Liudger's Werden from 799 [W-Werden].

## 2. What stood there

- **The royal court on the hill.** Unfortified in the 9th century [K-W1]. After 883/84 rebuilt to a plan as a palace: show buildings, a hall church, and a closed ring of houses in tuff and rubble with cellars, entered by gates; finished in the first third of the 10th century [K-Pf]. Its ditch on the east side was over 5 m wide and 2.6 m deep, filled in the early 10th century [K-Pf]. Burned 1283 [W-B].
- **The church of the Saviour.** First named 893 [PU]. A wooden chapel at the court, proved by digging; a stone palace church in the 11th century [W-S].
- **The causeway** from the Rhine to the palace, with a side drain and a small rubble-stone bridge, early 10th century [K-Pf].
- **The market** at the foot of the hill (Alter Markt), by the harbour, in use from the last third of the 9th century [K-D]. Wet ground kept wood, leather, bone and plant remains [K-AM]. A timber well there is tree-ring dated 872 and was later filled with burnt rubble [K-W1].
- **The burn layer of 883/84**: burnt house walls across the whole settlement; hasty burials of both sexes, one a man of about 40 some 2 m tall, one with arms crossed as if bound; lance heads, a rider's spur; two ship rivets of northern kind, 7 cm long [K-W1][K-W2].
- **The Northmen's fort.** Built "in the usual way" [R884]; not found. Krause proposes the Kaiserberg ramparts, or the Ruhr mouth [K-W1][K-D]. Henry's camp lay at Broich; a castle followed there [K-W1].
- **Prüm's estate**, 893: the church, 19 dependent holdings and two more; perhaps also three empty ones and four given by one Meginard (the text may mean Emmerich, named just before) [PU].
- **The Frisians' settlement**, 893 [PU]. Not inside the later old town; where is unknown [K-D].
- **The Frankish grave field**, about 450 to the 8th century, at Düsseldorfer Straße and Friedrich-Wilhelm-Straße [K-D].
- **A potters' quarter**: Krause names its ware beside Hunneschans and Pingsdorf ware [K-Pf]; dates not seen (?).
- **The first town rampart and ditch**, 10th century [K-D]; a town wall from 1120 [W-G].
- **Mint and toll**, in the king's gift of 1065, with **the king's forest** between Rhine, Ruhr and Düssel, bounded by the bridge at Werden and the Cologne road [L205].

## 3. Who could be met

- Lords near the place in 793, none attested here: King Charles, at Lippeham on the Lippe's mouth in 799 and 810 [chronicle_793]; Hildebold of Cologne (bishop 787, archbishop 794/95, died 818), the king's archchaplain from 791 [W-Hild] (that Duisburg was in his diocese, not checked (?)); Liudger (about 742-809), buried at Werden [W-Werden].
- 883/84: Northmen "who had come from Denmark to Kennemerland", sailing with the consent of Godfrid (killed June 885); their leader is not named. Duke Henry (died 28 August 886) [R884][chronicle_793][W-Henry].
- Regino, abbot of Prüm, who wrote of the raid and is credited with the estate book [W-PU]. Meginard, a giver of land before 893 [PU]. Counts Otto and Eberhard, 904 [L83].
- Kings holding court here: Henry I (929); Otto I (945, 966); Henry II (1002, 1016) [CR][W-G][L109][Th][L149]. From a list I could not open, also Henry I 935; Otto II 973, 976, 979; Otto III 985, 986, 992, 993; Henry II 1005, 1009 (?) [snippet]. Krause counts 16 sure stays between 922 and 1016 [K-Pf].
- With them: Archbishop Ruotbert of Trier, Bishop Richar of Tongres, Duke Conrad (945) [CR]; chancellor Liutolf (966) [L109]; Bishops Notger of Liège (972-1008) and Erluin of Cambrai (about 995-1012), Archbishop Heribert of Cologne (1002) [Th]; chancellor Gunther (1016) [L149].
- Count Palatine Ezzo (about 955-1034), his wife Matilda, Otto II's daughter (died 1025), their son Otto (count palatine 1035-45) [W-E][W-Ezzo]. Count Palatine Hermann and Archbishop Adalbert of Hamburg-Bremen, 1065 [L205].
- Ordinary people in the evidence: Frisians who "stay" here and pay in silver; tenants owing two days' work a week; eight *haistaldi*, men without a holding; Prüm's boatmen from up-river [PU]; a priest [K-Pf]; caulkers [K-H]; potters [K-Pf]; foresters, toll takers, moneyers [L205]. None is named but Meginard.

## 4. What was traded

- For 793 nothing is proved. The project's river goods (Rhenish wine, Badorf ware, Mayen querns) pass on their way to Dorestad [economy]; I saw no report of them from Duisburg's layers (?).
- 893: the Frisians pay Prüm 8 ounces at Martinmas, and 5 ounces and 12 pence at Easter (my reading of Beyer's print; at 20 d to the ounce, 272 d a year) [PU]. They were the abbey's protected men and probably used its freedom from toll, which went back to King Pippin [Ludwig].
- 893, off the land: each holding pays an ounce at Christmas, a hen and ten eggs at Easter, 12 d host-tax in summer, a pound of flax, a 5 d pig every other year; Meginard's pay barley, rye, oats and wethers [PU].
- Prüm's tenants at Retersdorf (near Königswinter) pay in wine and owe a trip with a ship twice a year to St Goar or Duisburg [PU][Ludwig].
- The Koblenz toll: ships from Duisburg give a cake of wax and a pennyworth of wine; ships from Cologne 4 d and wine. A bought slave pays 4 d; sword sellers give every tenth sword [KZ]. The list stands in a diploma dated 1104; Krause dates the tariff mid 11th century [K-H]. A toll at Duisburg itself is first named in 1065 [L205].
- Coin: no mint in 793. Pennies "on the Cologne stamp" from the 10th century (?) [W-G]; pieces survive of Conrad II (1024-39), Henry III and Henry IV [W-G]. They are found widely in Scandinavia and the Baltic [K-H]; one lay in the Sandur hoard on the Faroes, buried about 1070-80 [W-Sandur].
- Ships: clamps that held moss caulking between planks, 9th to 13th century [K-H]. The project models river barges and expanded logboats [traffic_793].
- Seasons: the Frisians' terms are Martinmas and Easter [PU]; new wine comes down from Martinmas [seasons]. No fair before 1066 (two cloth fairs come in 1173) [W-G].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 793: Saxon revolt east of the Rhine; famine in Francia [chronicle_793].
- 799: Werden founded [W-Werden]; assembly at Lippeham [chronicle_793].
- 810: Charles musters at Lippeham; the elephant Abul Abaz dies there [chronicle_793].
- 863: Danes go up the Rhine [chronicle_793]; Xanten's church burned [K-W1].
- 872: the timber well [K-W1]. About 870-80: market and harbour begin [K-D].
- 881/82: Cologne and Bonn burned [chronicle_793].
- Winter 883/84: Northmen take the *oppidum Diusburh*, fortify it and stay all winter. Henry camps against them and lets them take no plunder. In spring they burn their camp and go back to the coast [R884].
- About 884: Charles III gives the royal estate to Herrieden abbey; 888: King Arnulf takes it back (Milz, by Krause) [K-Pf]. Wikipedia puts the grant early in the 9th century [W-S].
- 893: Prüm's estate book [PU].
- 3 August 904: a royal charter names the *pagus Diuspurch* and the Keldachgau, in the counties of Otto and Eberhard [L83].
- First half of the 10th century: the palace finished; the Rhine breaks through, leaving the town on a side arm (Krause) [K-Pf]. Wikipedia: about 1000 [W-G].
- 929: a synod here excommunicates the men who blinded Bishop Benno of Metz [CR].
- 12-14 May 945, Rogation days: Otto I's assembly of Lotharingians and Franks. Two bishops accused of disloyalty are cleared [CR].
- 1 March 966: Otto I, "at Diuspargum", gives an estate to the nuns of Essen [L109].
- 1002: Henry II waits here for the slow Lotharingians; the bishops of Liège and Cambrai come first, Cologne late [Th].
- 6 December 1016: Henry II gives two estates to Burtscheid abbey [L149]. After 1016 (or about 1011): he gives Duisburg and Kaiserswerth to Ezzo [W-E][W-Ezzo].
- 1045: Otto gives them back for the duchy of Swabia [W-E].
- 16 October 1065: Henry IV gives "our court called Tusburch" in the Ruhrgau, with mints, tolls and forest, to Adalbert [L205]. 1066: Adalbert falls [W-Ad]; the gift's fate not checked (?).
- No end: the side arm stayed a harbour until it silted up at the end of the 14th century [K-H].

## 6. Faith and custom

- The church is the Saviour's [W-B]. In 893 it owes Prüm 30 shillings a year [PU]; Prüm kept the patronage until the 13th century [W-S].
- Dues fall on Christmas, Easter and St Martin [PU].
- Neighbour saints: Suitbert, the English missionary (died 713), at Kaiserswerth [W-Kw]; Liudger (died 26 March 809) at Werden, whose church is also the Saviour's (charter of 820) [W-Werden][L39].
- Burial: the Frankish grave field [K-D]; one Carolingian grave lay east-west, head to the west [K-W1].
- The old gods: nothing from Duisburg. The Saxons to the east drove out priests in 793 [chronicle_793].
- Law: in 1065 the estate passes "with all right of command" (*omnique districtu*) [L205]. Whether this bank counted under the Lex Ribuaria I did not check (?).

## 7. Stories and oddities

- The king's clerk of 966 wrote "at Diuspargum, which we commonly call Diusburg" [L109], claiming Gregory of Tours's *Dispargum castrum*, seat of the Frankish king Chlodio [W-Chl].
- St Servatius went to law in person: in 945 his clergy carried his body from Maastricht to the king at Duisburg, to complain of Count Immo [CR].
- Ekkehard of Meissen, riding west to meet Duke Hermann and other great men at Duisburg, was told at Paderborn the meeting could not be held [Th] (1002, before his death (?)).
- Henry IV's Duisburg pennies show a church twice, once from the water above a wall [K-Pf].
- From the Rhine bed between Duisburg and Homberg (1968): a Roman anchor's lead stock, 1.36 m, about 150 kg, stamped L V for the Fifth Legion [K-Anker].
- The name: one theory takes it from a god Deuso of a holy spring [W-G]. A theory only.

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- That a market, a church, Frisians, a palace, a wall or a mint stood here in 793. First proofs: market about 870-80, church and Frisians 893, palace after 884, rampart 10th century, mint 11th century. The table's "market" fits 793 only as a landing under a royal estate; let it grow by eras.
- A market "since the 5th century" [W-G]: the dig does not show it [K-D].
- That Charlemagne came here. No visit is recorded.
- That Dispargum is Duisburg. Others place it in Brabant [W-Chl].
- That Godfrid led the raid: Regino says "with his consent" [R884]. Where the camp was, how many ships, who died: unknown. Tying the skeletons to that winter, and the plunder of the Frisians, are Krause's readings [K-W1].
- How many Frisians, their names, what they shipped.
- The year the Rhine left: first half of the 10th century, about 1000, or (older books) about 1200 [K-Pf][W-G][K-H].
- When Ezzo's house got the place: about 950 [W-G], after 1016 [W-E] or about 1011 [W-Ezzo].
- Vineyards: the word in 1065 is charter formula [L205].
- Any guild, fair, Hanse or town law before 1066.
