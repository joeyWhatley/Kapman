# Walichrum (Domburg, Walcheren): dossier

Keys in brackets: see Sources. (?) = not checked on a page I opened. Renderings of the Latin are mine.

## 1. The place in 793

- **Name.** *villa Walichrum* in Alcuin's Life of Willibrord, about 796 [Alc; W1]; *Walacria* (837), *Gualacras* (841) [AB]; *Walicrum* [AX].
- **What.** An open settlement of timber houses on Walcheren's north-west coast, about 1 km north-east of modern Domburg [TH]. Archaeologists rank it with Dorestad, Quentovic and Hamwic as an emporium, on its coins and metalwork, and know it least: the sea took it before anyone dug [TH]. It was "the outlet to the sea for Dorestad" [MO].
- **Rule.** The Frankish king's, Frankish before 700 [MO]. Zeeland's heritage service calls it a royal estate from the late sixth century [EZ]: a reading, not a charter. First named count: Eggihard, killed 837 [AF]. Bishop: Utrecht's (?).
- **People.** Settled, not seasonal: men, women and children lie in the graves [TH]. Sheep farmers on salt marsh, eating mostly from the land; fish bones are rare, mussels common [TH; DM]. Their teeth say only that they grew up on a southern North Sea coast [TH].
- **Size.** Frederiks, who drew the remains in 1866, judged the occupied strip about 1,300 m long [DM]. Houses mostly 5-10 by 14 m [DM]. Numbers of houses and people: unknown.
- **Land and water.** In 793 it lay *behind* the dunes, with creeks, mudflats and salt marsh inland [KZ1; KZ2]. Later the sea ate the dunes, sand buried the houses, and by the fifteenth century the site was open beach [KZ2; DM]. One account says a creek joined it to the Scheldt [FCT] (?); an undated ditch with a rounded bed and earth-faced banks was seen on the beach [DM]. About 800 the Scheldt's main mouth was the Eastern Scheldt, north of the island [NSC]. Banjaard shoals off the north-west corner; mean tide about 3.3 m (estimate) [NSC]. No tower or stone building to steer by [DM].
- **Where boats lie.** Not known; the game's beach landing is an assumption [NSC].

## 2. What stood there

- **Houses** (seen 1687-1866). Oak hewn with the axe, no iron or stone; squared corner trunks, upright plank walls, earth floors, a central room with side rooms [DM]. Tradition told of "a sort of street" running east-west; Frederiks saw irregular family groups [DM]. Between them: dung heaps with bones of cattle, sheep and pig (no horse), mussel and shrimp shells, acorns; fenced stock pens [DM].
- **Tanning pits** with leather strips and ox horns; **hearth pits** lined with stones and red clay [DM]. **Brooch-making** in copper alloy [TH].
- **Four grave fields along the beach** [TH; DM]. *Hoge Hil*: about 23 coffins, eighth or ninth century. *Duinvliet*: 41 or more, from about 700; first seen 1687. *Westhove*: about 50, seventh to ninth century; fenced with oak pales, 325 paces by 70, a gate of two great trunks on the west. *Berkenbosch*: two eighth-century men in coffins of reused boat planks.
- **Older graves under the later fort**: a woman and a child, seventh or eighth century [TH].
- **Roman shrine of Nehalennia**, west of Domburg: a small building with limestone columns, found January 1647; a paved room with six great altars and a headless Victory, found 1705-06 [DM]. Lost long before 793.
- **The ring fort** (not there in 793). Sod rampart and moat, 4.5 ha inside, the largest of five in Zeeland, two crossing roads [UF]. Inside: five timber byre-houses of 6-7 by 16-17 m, farmed about 875-950; planks of an English clinker ship, oak felled 769 near Hamwic; a Borre-style buckle [UF]. In the rampart: a woman of 30-40 in a coffin made from a Rhine barge, oak felled after 851 [TH]. Sister forts at Middelburg and Souburg [KZ1]; builders unknown [ZA1].
- No church, mint, toll-house or quay is recorded [DM].

## 3. Who could be met

- **Eggihard (Eccihard)**, count "of that place", killed here 17 June 837 [AF].
- **Hemming son of Halfdan**, a Dane in Frankish service, "a most Christian leader", killed with him [AF; W3].
- **Harald "the Younger"**, Danish exile, given Walcheren by Lothar I in 841; dead by 850 [AB; W2; W4]. His brother **Rorik** is linked with Zeeland in the 840s only [W4].
- **Frederick, bishop of Utrecht** (815/816 to 834/838), said to have preached here; murdered at Utrecht, 18 July 838 by the usual date [W5]. Before him **Ricfried** [TRA].
- **Baldwin IV of Flanders**, holding Walcheren in fief from 1012 [W6] (?).
- Off-stage: **Alcuin** and **Beornrad of Echternach**, for whom he wrote [Alc]; **Louis the Pious** at Nijmegen, 837 [AB].

Ordinary people the evidence shows: sheep farmers, tanners, a brooch-maker, mussel and shrimp gatherers [DM; TH]; salt-boilers [KZ1]; skippers of Rhine barges and of an English ship [TH; UF]; women with keys, beads and hairpins, a man with a sword [DM]; owners of combs, ear-scoops and tweezers [KZ2]; a disfigured woman nursed for years [TH]. No ordinary name survives.

## 4. What was traded

- **In:** Rhenish pottery and quernstones; Rhineland oak as barges and stave barrels [TH]; glass cups and beads [DM]; English goods, of which the delta bought more than it sold, paying in silver [MO].
- **Out:** wool, hides and cloth from marsh sheep; the abbeys of Ghent and Echternach held land round the Scheldt mouth, tied to the trade in Frisian cloth [TH]; leather [DM]; brooches [TH]; salt boiled from salt-soaked soil [KZ1].
- **Coins.** About a thousand sceattas from the beach; before 690, gold tremisses of Dorestad are commoner here than at Dorestad [MO]. A mint here for the first "porcupine" pennies (about 690-715) is a hypothesis only [MO]. Finds peak in the eighth century [TH]. By 793 sceattas are forty years out of use [W9]; the coin is the Frankish denier, and temple-type deniers of Louis the Pious turn up in coffins [DM].
- **Tolls, law, seasons.** No toll, fair or market right is recorded. From 841 the island is a Dane's benefice [AB]. Salt in autumn and wool in June are the project's guesses [RTS].
- **Ships.** A clinker-built English sea ship; flat-bottomed Rhine barges [TH; UF].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- **c. 796** Alcuin writes of *villa Walichrum* [Alc].
- **815/816-838** Frederick is bishop and preaches on the island [W5].
- **835-838** The fort is begun, on Ufkes's dating [UF]; others say after 851 [TH] or about 890 [KZ1].
- **837, 17 June** Northmen "fell on our people unprepared on the island called Walacria, killed many and plundered more", stayed, took what tribute they liked, then did the same at Dorestad [AB]. Eggihard and Hemming die [AF]. "The heathen laid waste Walicrum and led many women away captive"; a comet is seen [AX].
- **837, after** Louis gives up his journey to Rome and comes to Nijmegen. The inquiry blames "partly impossibility, partly the disobedience of some"; abbots and counts go to curb the Frisians; a fleet is ordered [AB].
- **838, 26 December** A flood covers nearly all Frisia, almost to the tops of the dunes; 2,437 drowned [AB 839].
- **841** Lothar gives Walcheren and the places round it to Harald; the annalist calls it detestable that Christians should serve worshippers of demons [AB; W2].
- **850** Rorik takes Dorestad; Harald is dead [W4].
- **c. 875** The traders have gone, on Ufkes's reading [UF].
- **879-892** Great fleets pass Domburg eight times [UF]; the army winters at Ghent, 879-880 [CHR].
- **10th century** The beach site is still used, more thinly [TH]; the fort's farms end about 950 [UF].
- **c. 1000** Sand drives the last people inland [KZ1].
- **1012** Baldwin IV receives Walcheren in fief [W6] (?).
- **1014, 28 September** A sea flood wrecks the island [W7].
- **early 11th century** Churches of St Willibrord at Westkapelle and Oostkapelle [W8].

## 6. Faith and custom

- Alcuin: at Walichrum an idol of the old belief still stood; Willibrord broke it; its keeper struck his head with a sword without wounding him, and died three days later [Alc]. Local tradition says about 690 [ZA2]. Willibrord's feast: 7 November (?); Frederick's: 18 July [W5].
- Churchyard burial on this coast begins only in the tenth century [TH].
- **Burial.** Oak coffins pegged with wood, no iron nails, heads to the west; many are hollowed trunks with a thin lid and a small board under the head [DM]. Some are old boat planks [TH]. Eighth- and ninth-century graves have few goods [TH], yet coins were still laid in, usually on the right shoulder [DM].
- Churchmen thought the islanders half-heathen: the Passion of Frederick accuses them of marrying close kin [FCT].
- **Law.** The Lex Frisionum (785 to 802/3) has a western district from the Zwin to the Vlie [W10], which includes Walcheren (?). Its clause on temple-breakers belongs east of the Lauwers (?), not here.
- **Nehalennia**: a second- and third-century goddess of seafarers, seated, a dog beside her, a basket of apples, sometimes a ship's prow [LIV]. Her cult ended with Rome [ZA3].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- The stone at Westkapelle said to bear Willibrord's blood (Thiofrid, about 1103), perhaps a Roman altar [FCT]. The well at Zoutelande he opened by drawing a cross with his staff [ZA2].
- The comet of 837 was Halley's [FCT]. The flood annal has the word "dunes" (*dunos*) [AB].
- Coffins set in a star round a man with a sword; a mother and child in one coffin; a small bronze bell; a footless green glass cup [DM].
- An Abbasid dirham struck in Armenia in 866, set in filigree as a pendant [DM].
- A seventeenth-century map marks the ruins "dwellings of the ancient Goths" [DM].
- In September 1817 crowds robbed the graves within two days [DM].
- One altar was set up by Marcus Secundinius Silvanus, a dealer in pottery with Britain, for goods kept safe [LIV]. Counts of the 1647 stones differ: 26, 28 or about 40 [ZA3; W11]. They stood in Domburg church until lightning burned it on 10 October 1848; five survived [LK]. Three sceattas lay among the Roman coins there [DM].
- Thiofrid on the islanders: stubborn, fickle as the waves, fearing no law, taught only by miracles [FCT].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Where ships lay; what the market looked like; how many lived there; any toll, mint, fair, priest or reeve in 793.
- Whether anything of the shrine showed in 793; it lay under the dune until 1647. No living cult of Nehalennia; nobody knows her name.
- Who led the raid of 837 (Harald and Rorik are a modern guess [FCT]); whether Hemming held the island in fief [W3]; who built the fort (Baldwin II of Flanders is a guess [FCT]).
- When the place ended: about 850 (the game table), about 875 [UF], the tenth century [TH], about 1000 [KZ1]. De Man saw no burnt houses: the timbers were taken down and the people drew inland [DM]. Only the annals say "laid waste".
- Danes living here: "no tangible evidence" [KZ1].
- No stone buildings. No coin names a mint here. The name Domburg ("dune fort") is later than the fort [KZ1].
- The port row says "on the dunes"; the evidence says behind them.
