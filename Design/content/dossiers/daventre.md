# Daventre (Deventer): dossier

Port id `daventre`: 52.252 N 6.158 E, realm franks, county salon, town, riverbank, size 2, no end year [ports_793.json]. Researched 4 Oct 2026. Bracket keys: see section 9. (?) = not checked.

## 1. The place in 793

- A small fishing and trading settlement with a wooden church on the right (east) bank of the IJssel, on the border of Frankish and Saxon land [RDMZ]. The king is Charles, at war with the Saxons [chronicle_793]. The church is a daughter of Utrecht: Gregory sent Lebuin here, and his heir Alberic sent Liudger to rebuild it [VL].
- No count, reeve or priest of 793 is named in anything I read. Size is unknown; the first house count is of 952 (section 2).
- The people: Christians of the IJssel bank, who built Lebuin's chapel and church [VL]; fishers and traders; one report calls the settlement "originally Saxon" [RDMZ].
- The land: a row of east-west sand ridges crosses the river here; the rest of the valley is peat. The high ground on either side comes closest at this point, so the land road to Westphalia and Münsterland crosses here, and the bogs guard the place [RDMZ]. The river was "easily fordable" here [Canon-IJssel].
- The water: no tide. About 800 the IJssel was a young, minor branch of the Rhine, perhaps half today's flow (a low-confidence estimate) [north_sea_core]. It leaves the Rhine at Westervoort; the level at Deventer now swings by up to six metres [W-IJssel]. Brooks join here, the Hunneper Aa (later Schipbeek) on the south side. At high water a second channel ran round the north-east, making the place an island in winter [RDMZ].
- Landmarks: the church on a river dune beside "a natural harbour" [W-Kerk]; south-east of it the Berg, a second dune, called Heest after its beech scrub, steep where river and brook cut it, with no sign of early building; the low open Worp on the far bank, never built on [RDMZ]; Lebuin's chapel at Wilp on the west bank [VL].
- Where boats lie: along the bank on the line of today's Polstraat, the quay until the 12th century. Three timber revetments were dug up there, the oldest 9th-century [RDMZ]. Whether one stood by 793 is not known.

## 2. What stood there

- **Lebuin's church.** Wooden, about 768 [Canon-Lebuinus]; burned by Saxons and rebuilt; burned again after his death; built a third time by Liudger about 775-777, the foundations moved so the grave lay inside [VL][W-Liudger]. This is the church of 793. Burned with the port in 882 [AF]. First stone church under Bishop Balderic, 10th century [W-Kerk]. Bernold's basilica, about 1040: three aisles, column arcades, transepts, a westwork [Madoc], a crypt for relics and the Holy Week rites; tuff walls and crypt survive [Canon-Kerk].
- **Graves in the church.** Lebuin [VL]; Bishop Radboud, north part, 917 [W-Radboud]; Marchelm's relics beside Lebuin's (date not stated) [W-Marcellinus].
- **The oldest houses.** Remains of a 7th- or 8th-century building, 4 m under today's ground, in the block between Engestraat, Broederenstraat and Lange Bisschopstraat, lying as the streets still lie. The ground was raised later against floods [RDMZ].
- **The king's court.** Charters of 952, 956 and 960 give the king's property here to Saint Maurice's at Magdeburg: a court, two farmsteads, some thirty dwellings; the place is urbs or civitas. A fortified court of Charlemagne's time on the same block is a guess ("perhaps") [RDMZ].
- **Saint-Bertin's land.** Seven manses "in Daventre portu", 877 [SB].
- **The quays.** Besides the Polstraat revetments, three quay works in the IJsselstraat (dug 1983) were faced with parts of old barges: Deventer 3 (felled after about 913), Deventer 1 (spring 990; flat-bottomed, oak, moss caulking, wooden pegs), Deventer 2 (about 1035; iron nails, burned out). The oak was probably from the Ardennes [MaSS].
- **The wall.** Raised after 882 over a burn layer 20 cm thick: earth and sods, probably a palisade, 1.9 km long, finished before 890, enlarged in phases to 7.5 m high and 23 m broad [Canon-Wal]. The state register dates the remains at the Stenen Wal, wall and ditch, to the 10th century [RCE].
- **The bishop's house.** A substitute court of the Utrecht bishops from about 857 to about 920 [W-Gesch]; where is not known. From the mid 11th century a palace against the north side of the church [RDMZ][Canon-Kerk], and outside the town the Hof van Colmschate, head farm of the bishop's lands [RDMZ].
- **The mint**, from about 990; first site unknown [Canon-Munt]. **The toll**, the king's by 896 [vdAa]. **The chapter**, mid 11th century [RDMZ]: twenty canons and a provost [W-Kerk], housed on Kleine Poot and Sandrasteeg [Canon-Kerk]. An early **market** inside the wall is only supposed [RDMZ].

## 3. Who could be met

- Liudger (about 742-809), rebuilder of the church; in 793 he fled a rising in Frisia; bishop of Münster 805 [W-Liudger]. Not attested here in 793.
- Bishops of Utrecht living here: Hunger (about 854-866; "finally in Deventer") [W-Hunger]; Odilbald, also Adalbold I (about 867-898/899) [W-Adalbold]; Radboud (899/900-917), of the heathen king Radbod's line through his mother, bred at Charles the Bald's court, who wrote on Lebuin and a poem on a swallow [W-Radboud]; Balderic (918-975), who took the see back to Utrecht about 920 [W-Balderik]. Later Bernold (1027-1054), the builder [W-Bernold].
- Counts of Hamaland: Wichman (about 855), Meginhard (before 881), Everhard Saxo (before 882-898), Meginhard (898-about 915) [W-enHamaland], whom Radboud took to the pope about 914/915 [W-enRadbod]; Wichman, founder of Elten (died 973) [W-Hamaland]; his daughter Adela (about 952-after 1021) [W-Adela].
- Named in charters, not known to have come: Abbot Hilduin of Saint-Bertin (died June 877) and Charles the Bald [SB]; Zwentibold, Henry I [RI]; Otto I [RDMZ]; Henry III [Canon-Munt].
- Ordinary people: fishers and traders [RDMZ]; tenants of an abbey's seven manses [SB]; the king's toll takers [vdAa]; bargemen, and men who broke up old barges for quay timber [MaSS]; wall builders [Canon-Wal]; moneyers from about 990, none named [Canon-Munt]; canons [W-Kerk]. I found no named layman of Deventer before 1066.

## 4. What was traded

- A "portus" in 877 and 882 [SB][AF]. After Dorestad fell, the trade of the north moved to Tiel, Utrecht, Deventer and Stavoren [RDMZ]; that Dorestad's merchants fled here is "probable" only [W-Gesch].
- Proven links: Rhineland pottery in the 9th-century layers [Canon-Wal]; Ardennes oak in the barges [MaSS]; the road to Westphalia [RDMZ]; coin going north and east.
- Coin: pennies of about 1 g in the names of Otto III and Henry II; about 1000 Tiel and Deventer were the chief mints of these lands. Such coins turn up mostly in northern and eastern Europe, where they paid for imports [Adela]. The earliest type reads ODDO REX, with DAVNER/A in three lines after Cologne, and may predate 983 [Künker]. A Deventer coin lay in the Sandur hoard on the Faroes [W-deDeventer]. From 1046 the bishop struck here [Canon-Munt].
- Toll: in 896 Zwentibold ruled on the king's toll at Deventer and Tiel in favour of the church of Utrecht (freedom from it for the church's people, or the church's tenth of it: the reports differ) [vdAa][RI]. In 1046 toll, mint and justice passed to the bishop [Canon-Kerk]. In 1104 Deventer is named, with Tiel and Utrecht, as owing a toll per ship at Koblenz (amount not seen) [CDN].
- Law: the Ewa ad Amorem, about 802-803, 48 chapters, for the Franks of "Amor", usually read as Hamaland: a Frankish man's wergild 600 solidi, a freeman's 200 [W-Ewa].
- Ships: flat-bottomed river barges [MaSS]. Fairs and seasons: no evidence before 1066.

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 793: Saxons and East Frisians rise; the road east is unsafe [chronicle_793].
- About 857: the Utrecht clergy flee the Northmen; a bishop's court here for some 65 years [W-Gesch].
- 20 June 877: Charles the Bald confirms Saint-Bertin's seven manses in the port [SB].
- Late 882: the Northmen burn the port "called Taventeri in the Frisian tongue, where Saint Liobomus rests", killing many [AF].
- Before 890: the wall is finished [Canon-Wal].
- 896: Zwentibold's charter on the toll [RI].
- 29 November 917: Radboud dies at Ootmarsum; buried here [W-Radboud].
- 919-931: Henry I confirms the Utrecht rights [RI].
- About 920: the see returns to Utrecht [W-Balderik].
- 952, 956, 960: Otto I gives the king's court to Magdeburg [RDMZ].
- About 990: first coins [Canon-Munt]; Adela's pennies, about 990-1002 [Adela].
- After 1014: the mint strikes the Hand type [Adela].
- About 1040: Bernold's basilica [Canon-Kerk].
- 1046: Henry III gives Deventer and the county in Hamaland to the bishop of Utrecht [Canon-Munt][W-Bernold].

## 6. Faith and custom

- Lebuin (Liafwin), an English priest, came to Utrecht in 754 and died here about 773-775; feast 12 November [W-Lebuinus][W-deLebuin]. Marchelm's feast is 14 July [W-Marcellinus], Radboud's 29 November [W-Radboud], Bernold's 19 July [W-Bernold].
- The square by the church is still the Grote Kerkhof, the great churchyard [RDMZ].
- The old gods are the Saxons' across the border. The Life shows their yearly assembly at Marklo on the Weser, and their anger that Lebuin was "sending people out of their minds" [VL].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- At Marklo Lebuin stood up in vestments with cross and gospel book and warned that a king nearby would take their land. Men tore stakes from a fence to kill him; he was gone. Buto then said God's messenger deserved the hearing other peoples' envoys got [VL]. A later legend hides him in a tree trunk [W-deLebuin].
- The Saxons searched three days for his body and failed. Liudger found it after a dream, under the south wall he had just built [VL].
- Town tradition: he died "of the cold" in the Assenstraat [Canon-Lebuinus].
- The "Lebuin chalice" is an ivory cup of the early 9th century from the Aachen court school, a generation after his death; with it a Carolingian gospel book [Canon-Lebuinus].
- Countess Adela's penny: Æthelred II's Hand on one side, Deventer's cross on the other, a legend to be read DAVANTRIA OTTO. The king's own mint later copied her [Adela].
- Hucbald of Saint-Amand, who wrote Lebuin's Life (918-930) [W-Lebuinus], also wrote 146 lines in praise of bald men, every word beginning with C [W-Hucbald].

## 8. What we do not know, and must not claim

- No layman, moneyer, toll man, reeve or priest of the place is named; give none as historical.
- The first burning has no year in the Life (770 and 772 are both printed); tying it to Charlemagne's war of 772 is modern [W-enDeventer]. Scholars doubt the Marklo story [W-deLebuin].
- "City rights in 956" misreads the Magdeburg charters; town rights come about 1190 [RDMZ].
- The wall's sizes are its last phase. [AF] names no leader in 882. I read of no raid here in 834-837.
- Whether Hunger himself lived here, and from when, is unclear [W-Hunger]. Van der Aa dates Zwentibold's charter 890; the regest says 896.
- The county: the table says salon; the 1046 grant speaks of Hamaland; the border is unclear [W-Hamaland].
- The IJssel's age as a Rhine branch is disputed [W-IJssel].
- Later things, not to be used: stockfish from Bergen, Scania herring, the Hanse, the Brink fairs, the Bergkerk (about 1200), the Proosdij (about 1130), dikes, a bridge [RDMZ][W-Gesch].
- Do not quote [VL]: Talbot's translation is of 1954.
