# Lindisfarena (Lindisfarne): dossier

Port `lindisfarena`: Lindisfarena, modern Lindisfarne (Holy Island), 55.669 N 1.801 W; realm northumbria, region northumbria, kind monastery, landing beach, size 2 [ports_793.json]. Researched 4 Oct 2026. Source keys are in section 9. (?) marks what I could not check; (memory) is known to me but not opened.

**Read this first.** The game opens thirteen days after the raid. Alcuin's letters are the only account written that year; Symeon of Durham's, the fullest, is three hundred years late. The Anglo-Saxon churches have not been found.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What it is.** A bishop's monastery on a tidal island, founded about 634 by Aidan of Iona on King Oswald's gift; since 687 the shrine of Saint Cuthbert [enwp L; HE III.3]. The name is the Chronicle's: "Lindisfarenaee" [ASC 793]. The Britons called it Medcaut [enwp L].
- **Who rules.** The island is the bishop's: Higbald, who took over the church in 780 and died 25 May 803 [LDE xix-xx; enwp Hig]. The king is Æthelred, killed 18 April 796 [repo chronicle]; Symeon calls him "that most wicked king" [LDE xx]. Bamburgh, the kings' rock, is in full view, 6 km south-east [DV back; repo water].
- **Who lives there.** Monks and clerks, and boys in their keeping: the raiders took "youths" [Alc]. Lay people too: the graveyard holds men, women and children [DV bur], and a farm stood in the north dunes in the 9th century [enwp L].
- **How big.** Not known. The island is 3 miles by 1.5, about 1,000 acres at high water [enwp L].
- **The water.** Bede: the place "is twice a day enclosed by the waves of the sea like an island; and again, twice, when the beach is left dry, becomes contiguous with the land" [HE III.3]. The mainland is 0.8 mile off [enwp L]. No causeway: people cross the sand on foot or by cart at low water [repo water], today from about three hours after high water to two before the next [enwp L]. Neap range about 2.7 to 3 m; springs about 4.2 m, an estimate [repo water].
- **Coming in.** From the south: the Farne islands, Bamburgh's rock, then the low island with the Heugh, a ridge along its south edge, and the bare crag of Beblowe (no castle before 1550) [enwp L; repo water]. Reefs lie 1 to 3 km east and north-east [repo water, unchecked there] (?). No beacon or daymark before 1810 [enwp L].
- **Where boats lie.** Not recorded. The harbour pool inside the sands, the Ouse, is calm in westerlies [repo water]; the table's "beach" is fair. In 687 Cuthbert's body came from Farne by ship [VC 40].

## 2. What stood there

- **Saint Peter's church.** Bishop Finan (651-661) built it "not of stone, but entirely of hewn oak, and covered it with reeds"; Bishop Eadberht (688-698) took off the thatch and covered roof and walls with plates of lead [HE III.25]. It probably lies under the Norman priory church [EH desc].
- **Cuthbert's shrine.** Buried in 687 in a stone coffin right of the altar; in 698 found whole and laid in a new coffin above the sanctuary floor, Eadberht's body in the grave beneath [VC 40, 42-43; HE IV.30]. That carved oak coffin survives at Durham, with a gold and garnet cross, an ivory comb and a small altar [enwp coffin].
- **A second church**, perhaps where the parish church of Saint Mary stands, end to end with the first [EH desc].
- **The graveyard.** King Oswald's head lay in it [HE III.12]. About 100 burials of the 7th to 11th centuries have been lifted: shrouded, feet to the sea, white quartz pebbles from the shore laid on the graves; some stone-lined, three on charcoal, one in a hinged chest (800-1000), one beside boat timbers with clench nails [DV bur; DV 15].
- **Name stones**: small carved slabs, once painted, 7th to 9th century. Names read: Osgyth (in runes and in capitals), Beanna, Pausil, Ythfrith, Cuthwulf [EH coll; DV names; DV 10].
- **Bishop Æthelwold's stone cross** (721-740), his name cut on it [LDE xii]. A large cross socket lies between priory and parish church [EH desc].
- **The healing pit**, south of the church: square, lined with wood, filled with small stones, where the water that washed Cuthbert's body was poured [VC 41].
- **The watch height.** In 687 a brother waited on a height for two candles shown from Farne [VC 40]. Digs on the Heugh in 2016-17 found footings of a small stone church and a tower-like building (memory) (?).
- **Saint Cuthbert's Isle**: a rock of black dolerite just offshore, reached at low water, where Cuthbert and Eadberht kept Lent alone; wall footings show in the turf [HE IV.30; EH desc; enwp L].
- **A workshop**: an oven for copper or glass; a smithy with slag and copper droplets, and by it six coins of about 840 [enwp L; DV 2020].
- **The boundary ditch**, traced in 2025 [DV time].
- **Green Shiel**, on the north shore: five stone-footed buildings of the 9th century; its cattle bones may mean calfskin for the writing room [enwp L; EH res].
- **Not found**: the writing room that made the Gospels about 715-720 [enwp LG]; any quay, guest house, market or mint.

## 3. Who could be met

- **Higbald**, bishop to 25 May 803 [LDE xx].
- **Osbald**, king for 27 days in 796: fled here, then went by ship "with some of his brethren" to the king of the Picts; died an abbot in 799 [HR 796; repo chronicle].
- **Bishops after**: Ecgberht (803-821), consecrated at Bywell on 11 June 803; the poem De abbatibus is addressed to him. Heathwred (821-830). Ecgred (830-845), "of noble birth, and energetic". Eanberht (845-854). Eardulf (854-899), who left with the body [LDE xx-xxi; enwp bish; enwp Ear; enwp abb].
- **Eadred**, called Lulisc, abbot of Carlisle, with Eardulf in 875 [LDE xxi].
- **The bearers of 875**: Hunred, Stitheard, Edmund and Franco, the four named of seven [LDE xxvii].
- **Æthelwine**, bishop of Durham (1056-1071): here with the body in the winter of 1069-70 [LDE l; enwp bish].
- **Kings near by**: Æthelred (to 796), Eardwulf (796-806, back 808), Eanred, Æthelred II, Osberht and Ælle (both killed 21 March 867) [repo chronicle; LDE xx-xxi]; Guthred of York (about 883-895) [enwp Guth].
- **Cudrad**, a priest who lived through the raid and had a letter from Alcuin (memory) (?).
- **Ordinary people the evidence shows**: a smith; a scribe; a herdsman at Green Shiel; the owner of a bone comb scratched "-EDE"; a woman of rank, buried in the 9th century with gold thread at her head and children round her [DV 10; DV 15; DV bur].

## 4. What was traded

No market, toll, fair or cargo is recorded. This is a house that receives.

- **In.** Wine: since King Ceolwulf entered in 737 the monks might drink "wine or ale", having had only milk or water before [HR 854]. Lead for the roof [HE III.25]. Calfskin: the Gospels took some 150 skins [enwp LG]. Wax, oil and incense, as any church [repo seasons].
- **Out.** Nothing recorded. The habit was undyed wool, "that appearance which the natural wool of the sheep presents" [VC 16].
- **Estates.** By 854 the church held Norham, Carham, Jedburgh, Melrose, Tyninghame, Coldingham, Tillmouth and Warkworth, and every church from Tweed to South Tyne [HR 854].
- **Coin.** In Bede's day the monks "had also no money, but only cattle" [HE III.26]. Found: a sceat of King Eadberht (from 737); six coins of about 840, some of Æthelred II; coins of Wessex; a penny of Edward the Confessor, 1046-48 [DV 15; DV 2020; enwp L]. Northumbria's coin from the 790s is the styca, naming king and moneyer [enwp Styca]. None was struck here.
- **Far things found**: a blue glass king for the board game tafl, of 700-900, like pieces from Dorestad and Dublin; a chip of green porphyry; a bear's claw (973-1234), likely from a pelt; great auk bones [DV king; DV 15].
- **Ships.** The heathen fleet "from the north" in 793 [LDE xx]; Osbald's in 796 [HR 796]. No merchant ship is recorded.

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 793, 23 April: the body of Sicga, who killed King Ælfwald and died by his own hand, is carried to the island [HR 793].
- 793: lights, whirlwinds and dragons in the sky; a great famine [ASC 793].
- 793, 7 or 8 June: the raid. "Some of the brethren they slew, some they carried off with them in chains, the greater number they stripped naked, insulted, and cast out of doors, and some they drowned in the sea." The see stays [LDE xx].
- 793, later: Alcuin promises Higbald to speak to King Charles about the captive youths [Alc].
- 794: raiders plunder Jarrow; their leader is killed and a storm breaks their ships [LDE xx].
- 796, May: Osbald flees here and sails north [HR 796].
- 800, 24 December: a gale; the sea floods the land [repo chronicle].
- 803: Higbald dies; Ecgberht follows [LDE xx].
- 830-845: Ecgred builds a church at Norham and moves King Ceolwulf's body there [LDE xx]; the Historia de Sancto Cuthberto says Cuthbert's body went too [enwp HSC; EH hist].
- About 840: the Durham Liber Vitae is begun, probably here [enwp DLV].
- 866-867: the army takes York and comes no further north than the Tyne [LDE xxi; repo chronicle].
- 875: Halfdan winters on the Tyne. Eardulf and Eadred leave with Cuthbert's body, Oswald's head, some of Aidan's bones and three bishops' bones, 241 years after the founding [LDE xxi].
- 875-882: the wandering. A ship for Ireland is driven back to the Derwent's mouth; a gospel book lost overboard is found on the sands near Whithorn [LDE xxvi-xxvii].
- 883: the see settles at Chester-le-Street [HR 883].
- 883-895: Scots plunder "the monastery of Lindisfarne" [LDE xxviii].
- 941: "the men of York ravaged the island of Lindisfarne, and slew many" [HR 941].
- 995: Bishop Aldhun takes the body to Durham [HR 995; LDE xxxvi].
- 1061: Malcolm, king of Scots, "violated the peace of St. Cuthbert in the island of Lindisfarne" [HR 1061].
- 1069, 11 December: Æthelwine and his people flee King William with the body, by Jarrow, Bedlington and Tughall, and reach the crossing on the fourth evening; back at Durham in Lent 1070 [HR 1069; LDE l].

## 6. Faith and custom

- **Saints and days**: Cuthbert, 20 March [enwp Cuth]; Aidan, 31 August [HE III.17]; Ceolwulf the king-monk, 15 January [enwp Ceol].
- **The rule**: plain wool, plain food; Lent kept alone on the islet [VC 16; HE IV.30]. Eadberht gave a tenth of beasts, corn, fruit and clothes to the poor each year [HE IV.29].
- **Women**: Symeon says Cuthbert shut women out of his church and built them the "Grene Cyrice", the green church, on a green plain of the island [LDE xxii]. Written about 1104-07 [enwp Sym].
- **Sanctuary**: about 750 Offa, son of Aldfrith, fled to the shrine and was dragged away starving; the king shut Bishop Cynewulf up at Bamburgh [HR 750; LDE xvii]. From 883 the law gives 37 days' peace at the body, by then at Chester-le-Street, and a fine of 96 pounds [HR 883].
- **Burial**: section 2. At least three lay face down, and not at the graveyard's edge [DV bur].
- **The old gods**: nothing here. Alcuin told the monks to shun fine dress and drunkenness [Alc].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- The cross's head, broken by the heathen in 793, was run together with lead, carried with the body, and stood at Durham in Symeon's day [LDE xii].
- The gospel book lay three days in the sea and came out with gold and gems bright. The bearer Hunred dreamed of a bridle on a tree and a bay horse for the cart, and found both [LDE xxvii].
- In 1069 it was full tide at evening, a little before Christmas; the sea drew back at that spot only, and followed at the carriers' heels [LDE l].
- Symeon derives the name from the Lindis, a stream "two feet broad" seen only at low water [HR 793; LDE xx].
- Symeon counts "Danes and Frisians" in the great army [LDE xxi].
- The king-slayer's body was carried here six weeks before the raid [HR 793].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Who the raiders were, how many ships, how many died, what was taken by name, whether any youth came home.
- That everyone was killed. Symeon says so once and the opposite once [LDE xx, xxi]; the see stayed 82 years more.
- That the Lindisfarne Gospels or their cover were harmed in 793. Wikipedia has the jewelled cover lost to raiders [enwp LG]; no early source read here says so. That the book in the sea was this one is Stevenson's note, not Symeon's word [LDE xxvi note].
- That the island lay empty after 875: carved stones run to the late 10th century [EH hist], and 883-895, 941 and 1061 say otherwise. Where the see sat between 830 and 995 is argued: perhaps Norham [enwp Nor].
- The carved stone of armed men (9th century) as a picture of the raid: "perhaps" [EH coll; enwp L].
- The name "Holy Island" before the Durham monks (memory) (?). The castle, causeway, beacons and lime kilns are modern; Lindisfarne mead is a modern maker's; Cuthbert's ducks have "no evidence" [enwp L; enwp Cuth].
- The otters (Coldingham), the crows, the goose and the five onions (Farne) happened elsewhere [VC 10, 20, 36-37].
- Quotable: Stevenson 1855, Giles. Sellar 1907: her death date is unchecked (?). Whitelock's Alcuin is not free.
