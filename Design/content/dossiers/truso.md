# Truso (Janów Pomorski, Lake Drużno): dossier

Researcher's file for the writer of `places/truso.json`. Written 6 Oct 2026. Every fact carries a source key (section 9).
`(?)` marks what I could not check tonight. Keys: [Oros] the Old English Orosius; [WP-en], [WP-pl], [WP-de], [WP-sv] the
Wikipedia pages on Truso; [WP-Wulf], [WP-Elb], [WP-Druz], [WP-Pruss], [WP-Adal]; [Med] Medievalists.net, Sept 2026;
[Repo] this project's own notes; [K] my own knowledge, not checked, always with (?).

**Read this first.** Truso has one text (Wulfstan, about 880 to 890) and one dig (Janów Pomorski, from 1981). There is
no charter, no king's name, no church, no coin struck here, and not one named inhabitant. Nearly everything below is
either Wulfstan's word about the country or what came out of the ground.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What it is.** A craft and market settlement on the east shore of a bay that is now Lake Drużno, south of modern
  Elbląg, at the east edge of the Vistula delta [WP-en][WP-pl]. The ground covered by finds is about 20 hectares
  [WP-en][WP-de].
- **Was it there in 793?** Yes, but how grown is disputed. English Wikipedia and the Elbląg museum's archaeologist put
  the beginning at the end of the 7th century [WP-en][Med]; Polish Wikipedia says late 8th century [WP-pl]; Swedish
  Wikipedia says a seasonal market from about 650 that became a year-round town only about 850 [WP-sv] (?). The safe
  picture for 793: a market already working, busiest in the sailing months, not yet at its largest.
- **Who rules it.** Nobody known. Wulfstan says of the Este that their land is very large, with many strongholds, "and
  in each stronghold there is a king" [Oros]. He names no king and does not say Truso had one. The country east of the
  Vistula he calls Witland, "which belongs to the Este" [Oros]. Later sources call the peoples here Pomesanians and
  Pogesanians [WP-Elb][WP-Pruss]; those names are not attested for 793.
- **Who lives there.** A mixed place. Scandinavians are plain in the finds (jewellery, ship rivets), and the museum
  credits them with founding it [WP-en][WP-pl][Med]. Goods of Prussians, Scandinavians, Slavs and Frisians were
  exchanged [WP-de]. Small log and frame houses of Slavic kind stood on the northern edge [WP-pl]. Swedish Wikipedia
  says the traders were Gotlanders who brought their families about 850 [WP-sv] (?).
- **Coming in, as Wulfstan tells it.** From Hedeby, seven days and nights under sail the whole way. Wendland to
  starboard all the way to the mouth of the Vistula; to port Langeland, Lolland, Falster and Skåne (the Danes'), then
  Bornholm (its own king), then Blekinge, Möre, Öland and Gotland (the Svear's) [Oros]. The Vistula runs out of
  Wendland into the Estmere (the Vistula Lagoon), which is "at least fifteen miles broad". The Ilfing (the Elbląg
  river) comes from the east into the Estmere "out of the lake on whose shore Truso stands". The joined water runs out
  of the lagoon "west and north" to the sea, and that outlet is called Vistula-mouth [Oros].
- **So the skipper's way is:** find the gap in the sand spit, cross a wide, shallow, nearly fresh lagoon (2.7 m mean
  depth today, about 3 parts salt in a thousand) [Repo], find the mouth of the Ilfing on the south-east shore, and go
  up it into the bay. Where the sea gap lay in 800 is not known; it was not at modern Baltiysk [Repo] (?).
- **The bay.** Lake Drużno today averages 1.2 m deep, 3 m at most, ringed by reed and alder marsh, and was "much
  larger" [WP-Druz]. Expect reeds, low shores and no landmark but the higher ground behind the town (the modern
  village stands on a rise looking over the lake) [WP-pl Janów].
- **Where boats lie.** On the shore by the houses. The dig found wrecked boat remains and Scandinavian ship rivets in
  1985 and 1986 [WP-pl]. The project's ship notes record riveted boats of 9 to 11 m by 2.5 to 3 m, tar barrels and
  unused rivets here [Repo ships.md] (?). A dug harbour basin and a boundary ditch are reported by the excavator [K] (?).

## 2. What stood there

- **Long houses.** Three-room post-built houses of 50 to 120 square metres [WP-pl]; about 6 by 21 m [WP-en][WP-de].
- **Small houses.** About 5 by 10 m, found with burnt posts [WP-en][WP-de]; and log or frame houses of 20 to 30 square
  metres on the northern edge [WP-pl].
- **Plots.** The houses stood in regularly marked-out plots [WP-pl].
- **Workshops.** Glass-working, antler-working (combs above all), amber-working, smithing and goldsmithing [WP-pl].
  Amber and antler work is dated late 8th to early 10th century [WP-Druz].
- **The boat shore.** Boat wreckage and rivets, as above [WP-pl]; the tar and unused rivets suggest repair [Repo] (?).
- **The market.** No market place has been identified as such. What proves the trade is scales and weights
  [WP-en][WP-sv] and silver coin.
- **A buried purse or hoard (dug 1986).** Eleven whole coins and five pieces: ten Abbasid dirhams and one Sasanian
  drachm of Khosrow II, struck in Baghdad, Basra, Damascus, Iran and Afghanistan; the youngest is of 814/815 [Med].
- **Graves.** "Merchant graves along the river" [WP-en]; a large cemetery was found in the 1920s digs and its finds
  went to the Elbing museum [WP-de]. A grave field with Scandinavian women's jewellery on the edge of Elbląg itself
  (Neustädterfeld) is the usual candidate for the settlers' burial ground [K] (?).
- **Defences.** Swedish Wikipedia says works were raised about 950 [WP-sv] (?). None in 793.
- **Not there:** no church, no mint, no stone building, no wall in 793, no named well or hall.

## 3. Who could be met

- **No inhabitant of Truso is named in any source.** Every person in the game's Truso is invented, except:
- **Wulfstan**, a seaman or merchant, English or from Hedeby (not known which). He sailed Hedeby to Truso about 880
  [WP-Wulf] or about 890 [WP-en], and his account was written into the Old English Orosius at King Alfred's court
  [Oros][WP-Wulf]. The only historical visitor.
- **Adalbert (Vojtěch) of Prague**, bishop, came by Gdańsk in April 997, entered Prussian land on 17 April and was
  killed after 22 April, "likely near present-day Elbląg" [WP-Adal]. With him: his half-brother **Radim Gaudentius**
  and the priest **Benedict-Bogusza**. A heathen priest named **Sicco** is said to have struck first [WP-Adal]. Duke
  **Bolesław** of the Poles bought the body for its weight in gold [WP-Adal]. None of the Lives names Truso; the tie
  is nearness only.
- **Kinds of ordinary people the dig shows:** comb-makers working antler, amber-cutters, glass-workers, smiths, a
  goldsmith [WP-pl]; men who weighed silver on folding scales [WP-en]; boat-menders [Repo] (?); players of board games
  (pieces and boards found) [WP-sv] (?); armed men (swords, weapon parts) [WP-en]; women wearing Scandinavian brooches
  [WP-en].
- **Kinds Wulfstan shows:** a king in each stronghold, "the richest men", "the poor and the slaves", fishermen,
  horse-owners who keep fast horses for the funeral races, and one kin-group who know how to make cold [Oros].

## 4. What was traded

- **Out:** amber, furs and slaves [WP-Elb]; honey ("there is very much honey, and fishing", Wulfstan) [Oros]; worked
  amber and antler combs made on the spot [WP-pl]. Wax is likely beside honey but not attested [Repo] (?).
- **In:** Arab silver; Scandinavian jewellery and dress fittings; swords; Carolingian things [WP-en][WP-sv]. Salt,
  cloth and iron are the project's own inference for this coast, not finds [Repo] (?).
- **Coins and weights.** No coin of its own. Silver by weight: scales and weights, dirhams whole and cut
  [WP-en][WP-sv][Med]. The excavator's counts run to many hundreds of coins and of weights [K] (?). In autumn 2026 six
  more dirhams and two fragments of the first half of the 9th century were reported from woodland near Młynary, north
  of Elbląg, studied by Dr Jakub Jagodziński [Med].
- **Roads.** North to Birka by Gotland; west along the coast to Hedeby [WP-en][WP-de]; inland by the Vistula, and on
  toward the Rus, Byzantium and the Caliphate [Med].
- **Tolls and laws.** Nothing is known. Do not invent a toll-man with an office.
- **Seasons.** The Baltic is shut by ice from about November to April; amber is gathered after the autumn and winter
  storms and sold in spring [Repo] (?).
- **Ships.** Clinker-built Scandinavian boats, by the rivets [WP-pl]. Wulfstan's ship sailed day and night [Oros].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- **End of the 7th century, or late 8th:** the market begins [WP-en][WP-pl].
- **793:** a working market; no event is recorded.
- **First half of the 9th century:** dirhams reach the district (the Młynary coins) [Med].
- **814/815 or soon after:** the silver deposit found in 1986 is put in the ground [Med].
- **About 850:** by one account, traders settle year-round with their families and build long houses [WP-sv] (?).
- **About 880 to 890:** Wulfstan comes from Hedeby in seven days and nights [Oros][WP-Wulf].
- **Early 10th century:** the latest dated amber and antler work [WP-Druz].
- **About 950:** defences raised, by one account [WP-sv] (?).
- **Second half of the 10th century:** silt in the Nogat begins to cut the town off from the lagoon and the sea
  [WP-en]; Gdańsk is founded and takes its trade [WP-pl][WP-de].
- **10th century, or about 1000:** fire. A thick layer of ash, burnt timbers and many arrowheads; pirates, raiders, or
  the wars of the first Piasts [WP-en][WP-de][WP-Elb]. The people scatter into the country round [WP-Elb].
- **April 997:** Adalbert killed in this country [WP-Adal]. **999:** he is made a saint [WP-Adal].
- **Beginning of the 11th century (or the middle of it):** the last activity on the site [Med][WP-pl].
- **1066:** nothing stands. The Elbląg of the Teutonic Knights dates from 1237 [WP-Elb].
- **The ports table closes Truso in 850. That is wrong:** Wulfstan was there a generation later. End it about 1000.

## 6. Faith and custom

- **Heathen throughout.** No church and no mission before 997.
- **Drink.** "The king and the richest men drink mare's milk, and the poor and the slaves drink mead. There is no ale
  brewed among the Este, but there is mead enough" [Oros, my paraphrase]. So the brief's "the ale" needs care: the Este
  brew none; the ale in the story is only in the cold-making trick below.
- **Strife.** "There is very much fighting among them" [Oros].
- **The dead.** A dead man lies unburnt in his house among kin and friends for a month, sometimes two; kings and great
  men longer, sometimes half a year. All that time there is drinking and games in the house, paid for from his goods.
  On the day of the burning, what is left is laid out in five or six heaps, sometimes more, the largest about a mile
  from the town and the smallest nearest. Men with the fastest horses gather five or six miles off and ride for them;
  the first takes the largest heap, and so down. Then the dead man is carried out and burnt with his weapons and
  clothes. Fast horses are very dear there because of this [Oros].
- **The law of the pyre.** Every man, of whatever people, must be burnt; if one bone is found unburnt, a heavy
  atonement is owed [Oros].
- **Sacred woods and waters.** Later witnesses say the Prussians held certain woods, fields and waters so holy that
  none cut, tilled or fished there, and barred Christians from the groves (Adam of Bremen, 1070s) [WP-Pruss][WP-Adal].
  The high priest "Kriwe" and the grove "Romove" come from a chronicle of 1326 [WP-Pruss]: too late; do not use.
- **Horse.** Horse milk, blood and meat among the Sambians (Adam) [WP-Pruss]; horse gear in later warrior graves
  [WP-Pruss].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **The cold-makers.** One kin-group among the Este "can make cold", which is why the dead lie so long and do not
  rot. Set down two vessels of ale or water and they will freeze one over, summer or winter [Oros]. Nobody knows how
  (an ice cellar is the modern guess [K] (?)).
- **A dead man can ruin his heirs.** The estate is drunk and ridden away by strangers; Wulfstan notes it as the reason
  nothing is left [Oros].
- **A Persian king's coin.** A drachm of Khosrow II (died 628), some two hundred years old when buried here [Med].
- **Amber's long fame.** The Aesti of this shore sent amber to King Theodoric, whose letter of thanks (about 537)
  explains to them what it is [WP-Pruss]. A good dry joke: a king far away telling the gatherers what amber is.
- **An old name.** Truso is called the earliest place-name recorded on the Baltic shore (Gimbutas) [WP-en]. It may
  mean "run off, hasten", or come from a Prussian word for reeds; unsettled [WP-de].
- **Sun compasses** of elk antler and whalebone are claimed [WP-sv] (?): doubtful, and the brief forbids compasses.
- **Found twice.** Placed at Hansdorf from finds in 1897 and digs in the 1920s, then found for certain by Marek F.
  Jagodziński from 1981 [WP-en][WP-de][Med].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- No name of any king, chief, trader or craftsman of Truso; no toll, law, thing or fair; no temple or idol on the
  site; no count of people.
- Whether the town had a Prussian lord over it, or stood apart as the traders' own place.
- Whether Wulfstan was English; the year of his voyage; whether he saw a funeral himself or was told.
- Where the lagoon opened to the sea, and the depth of the Ilfing and the bay.
- Who burnt it and in which year. Do not name the Poles, the Danes or anyone as the burners.
- Do not say Adalbert died at Truso, or that "Cholinun" is Truso.
- Do not use "Prussia", Elbing, the Knights, Romove, Perkunas or a high priest. Call the people the Este (Wulfstan)
  or Bruzi (a 9th-century Frankish list [WP-Pruss]); the land Witland or Estland. These Este are not the Estonians.
- Do not give the Este ale of their own, nor wine, nor a mint. Mare's milk is the rich man's drink.
- My paraphrases of Wulfstan's Estland passage are from the Old English (Sweet 1883). The game's chest text
  `wulfstan` holds only the sailing part. For quoting the customs, take Bosworth 1859 or Thorpe 1853 from the page.
