# Medemelacha (Medemblik): dossier

Port `medemelacha`: now Medemblik, 52.771 N 5.106 E; realm franks, county westflinge, kind market, landing jetty, size 2, no end year [ports_793.json]. Researched 4 Oct 2026. Source keys are in section 9. (?) marks what I could not check.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What.** A Frisian-Frankish trading settlement on the south bank of a peat river, at the east end of a dry sandy ridge (a silted prehistoric tidal creek) in wide, wet, half-open peat land [W61 1.1, 6.3]. It began about 675, probably as a seasonal bank market for skippers between the Rhine and the terps, a run perhaps too long for one day; people soon stayed [W61 6.2; A13].
- **Name.** River and place share it: "middle leek", a leek being a peat stream. The oldest written form is Medemolaca (about 900); Medemelacha is of 985, Medenblec of 1118 [W61 2.2.1].
- **Who rules.** Frankish since about 719 [A13]. It is king's property: the king takes toll from every trader sailing past, a right few places had, probably from the 8th century [A13; W61 6.3]. A tenth of his income here (the regalis decima) belongs to St Martin's in Utrecht, most likely by Pippin III's grant of 753, which does not name the place [W61 2.2.2.1; the charter itself not seen (?)]. A king's man oversaw the toll; whether he lived here or came from a larger place such as Dorestad is unknown [W61 2.2.2.1]. No count is recorded, and the place may have stood outside any gouw [W61 2.2.2.3].
- **People and size.** "Frisians" in the broad sense, seamen and traders, perhaps with Frankish agents among them [W61 2.3.2]. About twenty houses and 120 to 200 people in the 9th century: small beside Dorestad's 1,000 m of harbour, large for North Holland [W61 6.3].
- **Coming in.** From Dorestad by the Kromme Rijn, the Vecht and the Almere (fresh to weakly brackish) to the Vlie, a narrow water between Friesland and Wieringen; Texel and Wieringen were not yet islands [W61 1.1; A13; nlwp Almere]. West of the Vlie a bay, the Lake of Wervershoof, ran from Medemblik south round Wervershoof, and the Medemolaca drained into it [W61 1.1]. The river was about 30 m wide [W61 6.3]. No bar, pilot or sea-mark is recorded. One row of houses stands about 10 m back from the south bank, on plots square to the water, proven over 100 m and reckoned at 300 to 450 m [W61 6.3]. Boats lie against the soft bank, and each plot-holder keeps up his own revetment [W61 3.1.6, 6.2]. No jetty has been found; how the harbour looked is unknown [A13]. Trees grow on the ridge; beyond is open moss [A13].

## 2. What stood there

- **The house row** (south of today's Oude Haven, Nieuwstraat to Ridderstraat). Timber houses at least 10 by 5 m: posts, wattle and daub, thatch, a central hearth; some 10 m apart with working ground between. Two or three of 800-900 are dug (Muntstraat 1982, St Josephschool 1990) [W61 6.3; A13]. Those of 675-800 stood on peat and vanished with it [A13]. From the 10th century: thin turf walls, a three-aisled end (a byre?) and a one-aisled living end [W61 6.4].
- **Wells.** A square timber frame over a hooped stave barrel at least 1.2 m high and 75 cm wide; two of the 9th-10th century, one to a plot [W61 4.6.5.2].
- **A drain ditch** square to the river: bottom fill 675-725, later fill 850-925; bone, leather, sherds, glass [W61 4.6.5.3].
- **Bank revetments** along the stream, about 800-1350 (dig finished January 2026) [AWF26].
- **The church.** Probable, not found. The patron St Martin (named 1118) points to a Carolingian founding; a small wooden church near the present Bonifaciuskerk is thought possible by 750-800 and likely by 850 [W61 2.3.2, 6.3; A13]. The first building known is a tuff church of about 1100, over 20 by 45 m, with an 11th-12th-century graveyard (dug 1992-93) [A13; W61 6.4].
- **The king's house.** Not found. The open ground called Koningshof is suggested for the toll, and a defended spot near the river mouth. Both are guesses [W61 6.3].
- **The hamlet at Schuitevoerderslaan**, over 1 km south on the lake edge (dug 1967-69): ditches, wells, rubbish pits, burnt patches, perhaps two field ovens and a house of 6 by 20 m; 7,000 sherds (Badorf ware, relief-band amphorae), quern pieces, glass; late 8th and early 9th century, given up before 900 [W61 3.1.4].
- **Not there.** No mint: no early medieval coin has ever been found, and the oldest coin struck here is of 1280 [W61 2.3.3, 6.3]. No grave before 1100 [W61 6.4].

## 3. Who could be met

No person is named here before 1118 [W61 2.2]. These are tied to the place by lordship or charter only.

- Charlemagne, king 768-814, whose toll it is [A13]; Hamacar, bishop of Utrecht about 790-806, then Ricfried, 806-815/816, whose church holds the tenth [nlwp Bisschoppen; the list calls these dates uncertain (?)].
- Rorik the Dane: granted Dorestad "and other counties" in 850, last named 873 [enwp Rorik]. That he held Medemblik is likely, not stated [W61 2.3.3].
- Godfrid the Dane: given Rorik's lands 882, killed 885 [W61 2.1.5].
- Gerulf, count: granted lands by King Arnulf 4 August 889, died 895/896 [enwp Gerolf]; the place probably stood under him and his heirs [W61 6.4].
- Dirk II, count about 939-988, and Ansfried, count of Huy: the charters of 985 (section 5). Ansfried was bishop 995-1010, blind from about 1005, died 3 May 1010 [nlwp Ansfried].
- Arnulf, count 988-993: killed 18 September 993 at Winkel, some 14 km west (my measure), fighting West Frisians [nlwp Arnulf].

Ordinary people the evidence shows: the king's toll-gatherer (the office sure, the man unknown) [W61 2.2.2.1]; perhaps skippers carrying other men's goods for hire [W61 6.3]; households of six to ten, with a grandparent or a few slaves or servants [W61 6.3]; farmers of the lake hamlets bringing live cattle to be slaughtered and salted for ships [W61 5.5]; perhaps a priest [W61 2.3.2]; perhaps a finisher of rough quern stones (a half-made quern, found 1982) [W61 6.3]; in the 10th century perhaps a potter (a misfired sherd) [W61 6.4]. Danish visitors were "nothing new" [W61 2.1.5].

## 4. What was traded

- **In, from the Rhineland:** pots of Badorf, Mayen and Walberberg ware and relief-band amphorae, later Pingsdorf and Paffrath, carrying wine and oil [A13; W61 4.7]; glass (a small bowl of 675-725, a green funnel beaker) [W61 4.8]; costly Tating ware [W61 3.1.6]; lava querns [W61 6.3]; timber and iron, which the coast lacked [W61 5.4.1].
- **Out (perishable, inferred):** wool, honey, salt, fish, hides [A13]; perhaps Frisian cloth, since the sheep grow more numerous and older in Carolingian layers, though loom weights are few [W61 6.3].
- **Through:** the north-south road of Rhineland, Dorestad, Utrecht, Medemblik, the terps and Scandinavia; much Frankish ware found in the northern markets must have passed here. Yet no find at Medemblik proves contact with Scandinavia, and regular trade with England is unlikely [W61 5.4.2, 5.4.3].
- **Toll.** A tenth of the goods or their proceeds; whether paid in kind or in coin is unknown [W61 2.2.2.1, 6.3].
- **Law of money.** Between Vlie and Sincfal a noble's wergild is 100 solidi, a free man's 50, a litus's 25 [Lex I].
- **Salt.** Burnt patches at Schuitevoerderslaan were read as salt-making from peat, then doubted by the excavator, since rye grew there [W61 3.1.4].
- **Seasons, fairs, ships.** Nothing recorded [W61 2.2.2].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 810: first recorded raids on the Dutch coast [W61 2.1.5].
- 26 December 838: a storm flood over Frisia, 2,437 drowned [chronicle_793.json, from the Annals of St Bertin]. Its effect here is unknown.
- 841-885: Danish lords over western Frisia [W61 2.3.3].
- about 850 and about 880: silver hoards (1,662 g; 457 g and 129 g) buried in Badorf pots at Westerklief on Wieringen, nearly 20 km north [W61 5.2.1].
- about 863: Dorestad ends [BRIEF.md]; Medemblik lives on, perhaps because of its toll [W61 6.4].
- about 900: first written mention, "In Medemolaca regalis decima", in the goods register of St Martin's, drawn together about 918 under Bishop Balderik (918-975) [W61 2.2.2.1].
- 10th century: more houses; the clearing of the peat hinterland gets going [W61 6.4].
- 26 June 985: Otto III, aged five, gives Count Ansfried the king's share of toll, mint and cens at "villa Medemelacha" [W61 2.2.2.2].
- 25 August 985, Nijmegen: Otto III gives Dirk II in ownership his fief "between the two rivers called Medemelacha and Chinnelosa gemarchi" [W61 2.2.2.3, which heads it 24 August; nlwp Dirk II; the charter itself not seen (?)].
- 993: Count Arnulf killed at Winkel [nlwp Arnulf].
- 995: Ansfried becomes bishop; Utrecht now holds all the king's income here [W61 2.2.2.2].
- 1064: Henry IV gives Bishop William I of Utrecht (1054-1076) the count's rights in Westflinge, its first mention [nlwp Westflinge].

## 6. Faith and custom

- The church was St Martin's, like its mother church in Utrecht; Boniface's name came to the building only after the Reformation [A13].
- The country round was still largely unreclaimed and heathen in Carolingian times; the church may have been a base for the mission to the terps [W61 2.3.2, 6.3].
- Burial is unknown: no grave before 1100 [W61 6.4].
- Frisian law (written down about 802 (?) [enwp Lex Frisionum]): Sunday work costs 4 solidi here [Lex XVIII]; a shrine-breaker, and a newborn killed by its mother, may be killed without a fine [Lex V]; among the western Frisians a wound is paid by the inch [Lex, Additio III]. The clause that sacrifices a shrine-breaker on the tide sand is for beyond the Lauwers, not here [Lex, Additio XI].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- A chronicle of 1517 makes Medemblik King Radbod's seat (he died 719); one of 1767 has his son Diederik found it in 334. The castle called Radboud is of the 1280s. Nothing shows Radbod was ever here [A13; W61 2.1.1, 2.3.1; nlwp Kasteel Radboud].
- An elk's toe bone of about 700, and others: elk were hunted nearby [W61 4.10.2.8].
- An antler comb of about 960-1070 bears signs that are neither letters nor runes, perhaps a maker copying writing he could not read [W61 4.10.4.2].
- Two pipes of a pan flute of Pingsdorf ware, 1050-1150, without parallel [A13].
- A runner cut from a horse's foot bone, about 1050-1135, for a skate or sledge, and cattle-bone tips for pricker poles, made in haste at the frost and thrown away at the thaw [W61 4.10.4].
- At Dorestad the church's tenth of the harbour was staked out with posts [W61 2.2.2.1].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- No raid, fire or Danish settlement here is recorded or dug [W61 2.3.3]. `raiders_793.json` lists it as a target: a raid is the game's, not history's.
- No jetty (the table's landing), mint, moneyer, wall, fair, market day, shipyard or graveyard is evidenced for 793.
- The church in 793 is probable, not proven. Do not call it St Boniface's. A visit by Boniface is only possible [W61 2.3.2].
- No named resident. The count, the gouw and the toll-gatherer's seat are unknown. A Frisian shrine under the church is a guess [W61 2.2.2.4].
- The toll's form, the coast line, the lake and all head-counts are reconstructions. There is no Zuiderzee before the flood of 1170 [W61 2.3.4].
- `hoards_793.json` gives Westerklief one burial in 860; W61 has two, about 850 and about 880.
