# Helgö (Helgö, Ekerö, in Mälaren): dossier

Keys in brackets: see Sources. (?) = not checked on a page I opened (written from memory of the literature; check before it ships). A landing, so kept short. The web gave little this time: only the Wikipedia pages opened, so much below carries (?).

## 1. The place in 793

- **What.** An old craft and trading place that is, by 793, a farm with a long memory. Settled from about 200; its great days were 400 to 800; the fine bronze-casting stopped in the 600s and after that "Helgö took on the character of an ordinary farm" [Wsv; Wda]. Birka, from about 750, has the trade [Wsv; Wbi]. Traces of people run on into the 800s [Wsv].
- **Name.** Helgö is read as "hallowed island" [Wsv]. An older name of the island is Lillön, "little island" [Wsv]. What it was called in 793 is not recorded.
- **Rule.** No ruler is named. The Swedish page supposes its merchants served the great families of Vendel and Uppsala [Wsv]; that is a guess of scholars, not a record. The realm is the Svear's [ports_793].
- **How big.** Seven groups of houses, not all of one date, on the north-east part of the island [Wsv]. In 793 think of one or two farms: a few dozen people at most (?).
- **Water.** Mälaren is in 793 a bay of the Baltic, not a lake, with water about 5 m higher than now, fresh to faintly brackish, brown-green, no tide, frozen every winter [WL]. The island was therefore smaller and its shore higher up the slope [WL]. A ship comes in from the sea by the sounds at Stockholm or Södertälje [WL] and passes Helgö on the way west to Birka, about 10 km further on (measured from the coordinates in [Wen] and [ports_793]).
- **Landmarks and where boats lie.** A rocky, wooded island; the houses stood on terraces cut into a slope above the water (?) [Hol]. An old hillfort stands on the height, out of use since about 500 [Wsv]. Boats are drawn up on the beach below the houses; no jetty is reported [ports_793: landing "beach"].
- **A note on the map.** The ports table puts Helgö at 59.32 N, 17.70 E; Wikipedia gives 59.279 N, 17.680 E [Wen], about 4.5 km further south.

## 2. What stood there

- **Seven house groups**, dug 1954 to 1981 after a summer-cottage owner found the first traces in 1950 [Wsv]. The digs fill 18 volumes, 1961 (?) to 2011 [Wen].
- **The hall** (House Group 2): a feasting hall where the imported things lay; German Wikipedia calls it important evidence for early ritual practice in the north [Wde; WB]. Gold-foil figures (guldgubbar: stamped gold leaf 1 to 2 cm across, often a man and woman face to face, 550 to 800) were found on Helgö [Wgg; Wde]; 26 of them, in this hall (?). Sherds of many Frankish glass beakers (?) [PG names Helgö for funnel beakers].
- **The workshops.** Goldsmiths and bronze-casters from the 400s [Wsv; Wno]. Tens of thousands of clay mould pieces for brooches, buttons and pins, the largest such find in the north, mostly of the 400s and 500s (?) [Hol]. Cold by 793 [Wsv].
- **The hillfort**, used 200 to 500 [Wsv]. A ruin in 793.
- **Five grave fields** [Wsv]. Mostly cremations under low mounds and stone settings (?); some graves are of the Viking Age (?). Loaves of bread were laid in some graves (?).
- **A holy place.** The Swedish page speaks of heathen worship and "an early temple building" [Wsv]. Offerings (rings, arrowheads, food) at a rock by the houses, lasting into the Viking Age, are argued by Zachrisson (?) [Zac].
- **Not there:** no church, no mint, no wall, no market booths in 793.

## 3. Who could be met

- **No person is named at Helgö in any text**, in 793 or at any time before 1066 (?). Rimbert, who writes of Birka, does not name the island (?) [Rim]. Every person must be invented.
- **What the ground shows:** a farmer's household; the heirs of smiths and casters; a keeper of the hall and its old treasures (inferred from the hall, not recorded); Svear skippers passing to Birka [Wsv; Wde].
- **Elsewhere, for ties:** King Björn and the prefect Herigar at Birka in 829 [Rim 11; realms/sweden_gotland].

## 4. What was traded

- **In its great days:** silk, glass and spices for the lords, says the Swedish page (a supposition) [Wsv]; coins of Ravenna, Rome, Byzantium and Arabia [Wsv; Wno]; Roman gold coins, a rare Frisian sceat, jewellery from the east Baltic [WB]. A hoard of some 47 gold solidi (?).
- **Out, then:** cast bronze dress fittings and gold work [Wsv; Wno]. Iron in bars is likely (?).
- **In 793:** farm goods and little else [Wsv]. A trader goes on to Birka. No coin is struck among the Svear; silver goes by weight [BRIEF rule 2; realms/sweden_gotland].
- **Tolls, laws, fairs:** nothing is recorded.
- **Season.** Open water from spring to autumn; in winter the ice carries sledges [WL].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- **About 750.** Birka begins on Björkö and takes the trade of the bay [Wsv; Wbi].
- **793.** Helgö is an ordinary farm with old workshops gone cold [Wsv].
- **The 800s.** The Irish crozier head is dated here by the Swedish page [Wsv]; the Norwegian page says it lay in a house of the Viking Age [Wno]. So someone still lived on the island and still brought treasures home.
- **829.** Anskar reaches Birka, 10 km off; Helgö is not mentioned [Rim 10 to 11] (?).
- **After the 800s.** The settlement leaves no more traces [Wsv].
- **About 975.** Birka ends; in the 900s Sigtuna takes its place [Wsv; ports_793].
- **1287.** First written mention of the estate later called Kaggeholm, on the same island: outside the game's years [Wen].

## 6. Faith and custom

- Heathen throughout its life; no Christian burial or church is known [Wsv; saints_calendar]. The gold-foil couples are read by some as the god Freyr and the giantess Gerd, by others as offerings or forebears [Wgg].
- The name, if old, says the island itself was holy [Wsv].
- Burial by burning (?).

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **The Buddha.** A bronze seated Buddha 8.4 cm high, on a lotus throne, a gold dot on the brow, made in the 500s in north India (Kashmir or Swat are guessed). Found in House Group 2 in July 1954. A leather strap lay round its neck and arm, so someone wore or hung it as a charm. It was already battered [WB]. When it came north is unknown: [WB] says "likely during the Viking Age" and also sets it among Migration-period finds.
- **The crozier.** The crook of an Irish bishop's staff: dated to the 800s by [Wsv], to the 500s by [Wen]; mostly given as 8th century (?). Bronze with enamel and glass, a beast's jaws gripping a man's head (?). Irish book mounts lay there too [WB]. Loot or gift: nobody knows.
- **The ladle.** A bronze ladle from Coptic Egypt, 500s, called a baptismal scoop on one page and a wine bucket on another [Wen; Wsv; WB].
- All three are in the Swedish History Museum, Stockholm [Wen].
- So one farm on a northern bay held holy things of three faiths, none of them its own.

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Do not make Helgö a town or a busy market in 793; the table itself ends it at 800.
- Do not name a chief, a smith or a priest as historical. None is known.
- Do not say who owned the Buddha, how it came, or that anyone knew what it was. Do not put a Buddhist or an Irish monk on the island.
- If the crozier is of the 800s it may not be there yet in 793. Show it only from the 800s, or as something seen elsewhere first.
- Do not say Helgö founded Birka, or that its people moved there: it is often supposed, never shown (?).
- "Temple" is one page's word [Wsv]. Say hall, and a holy rock.
- Whether the name Helgö was used in 793 is unknown.
- All numbers marked (?) (26 gold foils, 47 solidi, the mould count) are from memory.
