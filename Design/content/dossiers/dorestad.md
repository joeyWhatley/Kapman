# Dorestad (Dorestat, Wijk bij Duurstede): dossier

Codes in brackets: section 9. (?) = unchecked.

## 1. The place in 793

The Franks' northern toll port and mint, on the left (west) bank of the Kromme Rijn just below the fork where the Lek leaves the Rhine [N]; to Liudger in the 790s a "vicus famosus" [S]. Lord: King Charles; no Carolingian king is known to have come [T7]. His agents ("procuratores rei publicae") take the dues [OSU56]; a prefect is inferred, not named [T5]. St Martin's of Utrecht, 21 km downstream [Nt], owns the Upkirika, a tenth of the royal income and its own bank plots [OSU48,OSU56]. Frisian shippers and merchants, Frankish officials, craftsmen, farmers: an estimated 2,000-3,000 souls [N,T9]; in the graves two men to each woman [T6]. 793 is a famine year [C].

A skipper sees an open timber ribbon 3 km long, no wall or rampart [N,T7]. Upstream means towing: Alcuin has his letter's prow drawn by a long tow-rope [Alc]. At the fork stands the Roman fort with St Martin's church; below it, in a concave bend, the harbour: a comb of earth-and-wattle dams (not pile jetties) 6-7 m wide, up to 200 m long, storehouses on them, 4 m ditches between. Boats of 2-3 m beam lie between dams; larger ships moor at the dam heads or lie on the sand beyond. In summer much of the comb stands dry; winter and spring floods drown the low ground. Behind: thatched houses, farms, graveyards, alder marsh [N].

## 2. What stood there

- Harbour dams (Hoogstraat, dug 1967-77): 18 m parcels, paired dams, 10,000 posts to 100 m of bank; large warehouses on the newest ground, c. 750-830; in use c. 675-875 [N,T8].
- Beach at the dam heads: ship nails and a dismantled clinker hull (repair, breaking) [N].
- The vicus behind a 2 m bank road: narrow houses 5-6 by 12-16 m; workshops for combs, amber beads, querns, iron, cloth (2,889 loomweights), glass beads [N,T9].
- Some 650 wells lined with Rhenish wine casks (felled 685-835); many were cesspits [N,W].
- About 30 rear farms: houses 8-11 by 20-30 m, granaries [N].
- Upkirika, St Martin's church "above Dorestad": given to Utrecht on 8 June 777 with 100 perticae of land, the bank due on the Lek and the island eastward between Rhine and Lek; probably lost to the river later [OSU48,T6].
- The castrum (Roman Levefanum), where Pippin beat Radbod in the 690s; washed away [S,N].
- De Heul graveyard, late 7th to early 9th century: fenced plots; a timber building 14 by 8 m (chapel?) with three limestone sarcophagi; the one stone well, of Roman tuff [T10].
- Graveyards at Frankenhof (wooden church: c. 800 per the excavators, 10th century per Van der Tuuk), De Engk, De Geer II [N,T10].
- De Geer, 500 m north-west: ditched enclosure 85 by 380 m, hall 10 by 22 m; a lord's seat, early 8th to mid 9th century [N,T5].
- Toll station and mint: certain; sites unknown [T5]. A lodging (hospitium) where Catla stayed [R20]. Wicker fish cars [T11].
- Nearby: Rijswijk with a church, Leut (Lote) with three [OSU49].

## 3. Who could be met

- Kings, never guests: Charlemagne (768-814), Louis the Pious (814-840), Lothar I (840-855), Lothar II (855-869) [T7].
- Bishops of Utrecht, holders of the tenth: Hamacarus (790-806); Hunger (854/56-866) fled the Northmen; Odilbald (896), Balderic (938, 948), William (1057) had it confirmed [B,S].
- Thuitbert "bishop of Dorostat": 8th-century owner's note in a 5th-century Livy (Vienna, Cod. Lat. 15); Bishop Theodard (784-790) or the Utrecht teacher Thiatbraht [S,T6].
- Hrotberct, "black", the "greedy merchant" who gives no lodging, c. 780 [Alc].
- Ansgar and the Dane Harald (Klak), passing in 826 in a two-cabin ship lent by Bishop Hadebald of Cologne [R7].
- Harald the Younger and his brother Rorik, Danes holding the vicus in benefice in Louis's time; Harald dead by 850 [AF].
- Rorik, lord from 850 ("qui praeerat Dorestado", 857), last seen 873, dead by 882; Godfrid Haraldsson with him in 855 [AB,AF,AX].
- Counts Sigir and Liuthar, fighting Northmen in 847; Gerward, provost at Gendt (Betuwe) from 837, probable Xanten annalist [AX].
- Catla of Birka with her dead mother Frideburg's silver, c. 845-852; the "devout women" who guided her; "priests who were of repute" [R20]. Swedes and Danes baptised here; Dorestad merchants at Sliaswich [R24,R27].
- Hagabard, giver of a manse at Hasehem (unplaced) [OSU49].
- From the digs: weavers, comb-makers, bead makers, quern cutters, smiths (after 850 perhaps a Scandinavian), bronze casters, wood turners, fishermen, farmers; a man buried with a silver-gilt-hilted sword, c. 750-800 [N,T9,T10].
- Moneyers Madelinus and Rimoaldus are of c. 630-650; Carolingian pennies name none [M].

## 4. What was traded

In: wine from Rheinhessen and Alsace; Badorf pots (three-quarters of the wheel-made ware), amphorae, Tating jugs; Mayen lava querns as roughouts; Rhenish glass; Veluwe iron; Rhineland oak; Baltic amber; Scandinavian whetstones and, after 850, soapstone; three cowries from the Red Sea or Indian Ocean [N,T5,T9,W]. Out or through: Frisian cloth, combs, beads, finished querns, knives; from the Frisian coast salt, fish, hides, furs, honey, dyes; slaves likely, unattested [N,T5].

Coin: with Melle the kingdom's busiest mint in the later 8th century [T6]. Until 793/94 a light penny of 1.3 g, CARO/LVS and DOR/STAT with an axe; then the 1.7 g monogram penny, compulsory from 794. 812-818 a ship with DORESTADO or DORESTATVS; 818-822/23 DOR/ESTA/TVS; then Christiana religio, here from some 450 dies; from c. 850 poor pennies in Lothar's name, probably Rorik's. Foreign coin must be reminted, at the king's profit; the Frisian coast kept the light weight [M,T7].

Dues: the king's tenth (decima), taken at Quentovic, Dorestad and the Clusae even from the emperor's own merchants (828) and Strasbourg's men (831); an abbey freed of toll here, 779 [S]; the bank due (ripaticum) [OSU48]; "giscot", read as shippers' payment for lodging and protection [OSU56,T6]. St Martin's men on the banks owed none; no one might lodge in their houses or seize their goods at death [OSU56].

Seasons and ships: no dated fair is recorded; crews waited for cargo, season or convoy [T5]. Four pennies bought wine for a party of women [R20]. About 852 Swedes called the way here "beset" by pirates [R27].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 793/94: the heavier penny [M].
- 815, 18 March: Louis confirms Utrecht's tenth and its banks "in Dorestado" [OSU56].
- 834: Danes come by Utrecht, plunder, kill, take captives, burn part; 835 and 836, again [AB,AF,AX].
- 837: after Walcheren (17 June) they take tribute here; Louis hurries to Nijmegen, blames disobedience, orders a fleet [AB,AF].
- 846 or 847: burned with two other villages while Lothar looks on from Nijmegen [AX,AF]. 847: Danes hold Dorestad and the Betuwe [AB].
- 850: Rorik comes by the Rhine mouths; Lothar grants "Dorestad and other counties" on terms: pay the king's dues, keep pirates off [AB,AF].
- 855: Rorik and Godfrid fail in Denmark, keep Dorestad [AB]. 857: Rorik sails to the Eider; other Danes storm the town [AF,AB].
- 863, January, a winter of floods: sacked; Frisian merchants killed in a "villa non modica" where they had fled; the Danes leave on Rorik's advice [AB,AX].
- 867: the "Cokingi" drive Rorik out. 870: Charles the Bald treats with him at Nijmegen [AB]. 873: he swears to Louis the German [AX]. 882: another Godfrid, baptised, receives what he held [AB].
- After 870: flood clay over De Horden; harbour given up c. 875 [T2,T8].
- 896, 24 June: Zwentibold gives Utrecht's men at Deventer and Tiel the freedom they had "in Dorestadio" [OSU88].
- 948, 1 April: Otto I: "villa quondam Dorsteti, nunc autem Wik nominata" [OSU111].
- 1016-1019: Wijk's church and estate pass from Adela of Hamaland to Archbishop Heribert and Deutz abbey [S,T12].
- 1057: Henry IV confirms the banks of Dorestad [OSU218].

## 6. Faith and custom

St Martin's Upkirika is the one church named in Dorestad [OSU48]. Burials lie east-west, head west, in coffins, shrouds or sarcophagi, almost without grave goods after c. 750 [T10]. No patron saint, relic or holy well, hardly a Christian object, no text on religious life [T6,T9]. Rimbert's "many churches, priests, and clergy, and a multitude of poor people" (Robinson 1921) may count the district as far as Utrecht [R20,T6]. Heathen traders came; some took baptism [R24,R27]. Seventh-century north-south and crouched graves show older custom [T9,T10]. The Lex Frisionum (c. 802) is not attested in use here [LF]. In 825 Martinmas was the last day for old pennies [M].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- Catla's purse: half the alms given, four pennies spent on wine, the bag full again, less the four [R20].
- Alcuin: at Utrecht honey, porridge and butter, since Frisia pours neither oil nor wine; then hoist sail and flee Dorestad [Alc].
- The gold brooch (8.4 cm; garnets, glass, pearls; c. 775-800 or Merovingian), found 1969 in a well on De Heul; it had been nailed down as a mount [T9,W].
- Odbert (11th century) gives Dorestad 55 churches; perhaps IV misread as LV [S,T6].
- Vita Dagoberti (10th century): Boniface strikes the prison door with the king's staff and the chains fall [S].
- A swan-bone flute; amber lyre bridges and gaming pieces; dogs' tooth marks on kitchen bones [T9,N].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- No count, reeve, tollman, moneyer or priest of 793 is named: "the count" is invention; the attested office is the king's procurator. Hrotberct may be dead by 793.
- Sites of toll, mint and market are unknown; no market square: trade was on dams and ships [T6].
- No wall, stone church, named fair, guild or heathen shrine.
- Bernlef belongs to the northern terps, not here [T9].
- No burnt layer was found; after 834 the annals may mean the fort [T7].
- The 863 storm dam is conjecture; Dutch Wikipedia's "toll registers" are unverified (?) [W].
- The "Rorik" fasting from meat and mead (AX 845) is King Horik.
- Rorik's baptism rests on Hincmar's letters, unread (?) [Rw].
- Robinson's 1904 Xanten translation wrongly says "Dordrecht".
