# Eoforwic (York): dossier

Codes in brackets: section 9. (?) = from memory, not checked tonight.

## 1. The place in 793

The chief city of the Northumbrians: the archbishop's seat, a king's seat, a school and a trading shore [HY,VCH]. King: Æthelred I, restored 790, killed by his own people 18 April 796 [K,ASC]. Archbishop: Eanbald I, c. 780-796 [AB]. Three parts. (a) The old Roman legionary fortress on the north-east bank of the Ouse, about 50 acres (21.5 ha), its stone wall standing for much of its circuit: inside it the minster, the school and (?) the king's hall [W,HY]. (b) Across the river, the ruined Roman town on the Bishophill slope [SMB](?). (c) Downstream, where the Foss meets the Ouse, the wic at Fishergate: "the city's principal manufacturing and trading centre" [F]. "By the 8th century York was an active commercial centre" trading with the rest of England, northern France, the Low Countries and the Rhineland [HY]. No head-count exists for 793.

Coming in: the Humber mouth past Spurn (?), up the estuary to where Trent and Ouse meet (Trent Falls), then the Ouse past the mouths of Don, Aire, Derwent and Wharfe [O]. The river is 84 km long in all, flat-banked, and floods; the tide runs today to Naburn weir just below York, and before the weir reached the city (?); the bore is called the Aegir [O]. Landmarks at the end: the grey fortress wall with its ten-sided corner tower (Multangular Tower, 9 m high) above the left bank [W]. Boats take the ground on the bank at the Foss mouth (?); no quay is proven [F].

## 2. What stood there

- Fortress wall and towers, Roman. Asser says the city had no strong walls in 866 (?). The Danes after 867 "demolished all the towers save the Multangular Tower and restored the walls" [W].
- The Anglian Tower: a small square stone tower with tunnel vault, filling a breach in the wall, over 3 m high; found 1839; dated 7th or 9th century, or late Roman [AT].
- St Peter's minster: wooden church 627 for Edwin's baptism; stone church finished 637; Wilfrid's lead roof and window glass c. 670; fire April 741 (and another fire 764); burnt 1069. The altar of St Paul stood in it (796). Digging in 1967-73 found no building older than 1080: its exact place is unknown [M,VCH]. A graveyard with carved 10th-11th century slabs lay under the later south transept (?).
- Alma Sophia (Holy Wisdom): thirty altars, lofty, "many columns and arches", glazed windows; built for Archbishop Æthelbert by Eanbald and Alcuin; consecrated ten days before his death on 8 November 780. Never found [AE].
- The school and library, "probably the largest contemporary collection of books ... outside of Rome" [AE].
- Fishergate wic (dug 1985-86): 8th-9th century timber buildings; metal, bone and leather waste [F]. Founded c. 700, given up in the mid 9th century; Rhenish and north French pots, lava querns (?).
- Coppergate (dug 1976-81): outside the fortress, empty after the Romans, taken up again in the 9th century; timber houses, workshops, fences, pens, privies, pits, wells; over 40,000 objects; wood, leather and cloth preserved from c. 900 [J,C]. Four long plots laid out c. 900-935; wattle houses, then cellared plank houses c. 970 (?).
- A wood-lined well at Coppergate, 1.4 m square, with the 8th-century helmet in it (section 7) [H].
- The mint: base-silver, then copper stycas from the 790s to 867; pennies again from c. 895 [ST,SY]. Two iron coin dies found at Coppergate (?).
- "Konungsgurtha", the kings' court, just outside the fortress's east gate (King's Square) [SY].
- A Danish stronghold (castrum), levelled by Athelstan in 927 [VCH].
- Earlsburh, the earls' residence, at Galmanho outside the west wall; beside it St Olave's church, built by Earl Siward, who was buried there in 1055 [VCH,SI].
- St Mary Bishophill Junior: 10th-century tower of reused Roman stone, heightened in the 11th [SMB].
- The Shambles (butchers) and St Crux, both in Domesday [VCH]. All Saints Pavement, Holy Trinity (Christ Church) (?).
- Streets with Danish names in -gate; a bridge over the Ouse by the 10th century (?).
- Two Norman castles, 1068-69, with one of the city's seven shires wasted for them [HN,VCH](?).

## 3. Who could be met

- **Kings of the Northumbrians**: Æthelred I (790-796); Osbald (27 days, 796); Eardwulf (796-806/8, 808-810), consecrated at York 26 May 796; Ælfwald II; Eanred (810-841); Æthelred II (c. 841-849, with a break); Osberht (to 862/3) and Ælla, both killed 21 March 867 [K,ASC,T].
- **Kings at York after 867**: Halfdan (c. 875-877); Guthred (c. 883-895, buried in the minster); Siefred (c. 895-900); Cnut (c. 900-905); Ragnall (919-920/1); Sihtric (921-927); Olaf Guthfrithson (939-941); Olaf Sihtricson; Eric "son of Harold" (947/8-948, 952-954) [K,SY,EB,T,VCH].
- **English kings who came**: Athelstan 927; Edmund 944; Eadred 948; Harold II, September 1066 [T,EB,BF].
- **Earls**: Oswulf (from 954); Oslac, Thored, Ælfhelm, Uhtred (killed 1016), Eric of Hlathir (?); Siward (1041-1055); Tostig (1055-1065); Morcar (1065-) [EB,SI,TG].
- **Archbishops**: Eanbald I (c. 780-796); Eanbald II (796-c. 808), Alcuin's pupil "Simeon"; Wulfsige (c. 808-c. 834); Wigmund (837-854); Wulfhere (854-c. 896; fled 872, back 873); Æthelbald (900-c. 916); Hrotheweard (c. 916-931); Wulfstan I (931-956), tried and held by Eadred in 952; Oscytel (c. 958-971); Oswald (971-992); Ealdwulf (995-1002); Wulfstan II "the Wolf" (1002-1023); Ælfric Puttoc (1023-1051); Cynesige (1051-1060); Ealdred (1061-1069) [AB,EB,E2].
- **Scholars**: Alcuin (born York c. 735; master from 767; in Northumbria 790-793; died Tours 19 May 804) [AL]. Liudger the Frisian, his pupil for three and a half years, home c. 773 with "a store of books" [VCH,L,SP].
- **Moneyers**: Leofdegn (Leofthegn), under Æthelred II, known for fancy dies [ST]; Ingalger and Radulf, for Olaf and Eric [EB]. Others on stycas: Eanred, Monne, Fordred, Wendelberht (?).
- **Others**: Oshere, named on the helmet [H]. Egil Skallagrimsson and his friend Arinbjorn at Eric's hall c. 948, by a 13th-century saga only [EB,VCH]. St Cathroe, guest of "king Erichius" [EB]. Four "judices" (lawmen), once twelve, hereditary [VCH].
- **Ordinary people the digs show**: workers in iron, antler, bone and leather, weavers, wood-turners, amber and jet cutters [J,F](?); butchers [VCH]; Frisian merchants (to 773) and Danish ones (c. 1000) [SP,VCH].

## 4. What was traded

- In 793: the Rhine trade by Frisian hulls: wine, querns, pots, glass (?) [HY,RT]. Alcuin sent wine and tin from Francia for the minster's bell-house [VCH,E2]. Out: wool, hides, cloth, jet, slaves (?) [RT].
- After 866: silk (a silk cap), Baltic amber, a cowrie from the Red Sea or Persian Gulf, coins from Samarkand [SY]. Soapstone, Norwegian hones, walrus ivory (?). Grain from the East Riding; "white fish from the Hebrides" [VCH].
- c. 1000 the city is "filled beyond telling, and enriched with the treasures of merchants", who come above all from the Danish people [VCH: Life of St Oswald].
- Coin: stycas, small and base, struck for kings and archbishops alike; useless abroad (?) [ST]. From c. 895 silver pennies; the "St Peter" pennies c. 905-927, some with a sword and a hammer (?); Sihtric's coin has "both Thor's hammer and the cross" [VCH]; Eric's has a sword [EB]. Hacksilver and ingots by weight beside coin [VH]. One of 44 mints for Harold II [VCH].
- Tolls and law: Domesday records the city's customs, tolls and lawmen [VCH]. No toll rate for 793 is known.
- Ships: Frisian traders to 773 [SP]; Danish and Norse traders, and from Dublin, after 866 [SY]; Egil's companions "sold their wares" in peace under Arinbjorn's protection [VCH].
- Seasons: no fair is recorded.

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 793: in Lent, Alcuin writes, a rain of blood fell from a clear sky on the north side of St Peter's roof (?) [SP]. 8 June: Lindisfarne.
- 796: 18 April Æthelred killed; Osbald 27 days; 26 May Eardwulf consecrated at St Paul's altar; 10 August Eanbald I dies, buried at York; 14 August Eanbald II consecrated; pallium 797 [ASC,K,VCH,E2].
- 798: Eanbald II's synod at Finchale [E2].
- c. 806-808: Eardwulf driven out and restored [K].
- c. 837-855: the coinage turns to copper [ST].
- 866, 1 November: the great army takes York [VCH,SP,T].
- 867, 21 March: the Northumbrians break in; "both the kings were slain on the spot. The survivors made peace with the army" [ASC,T].
- 869: "the army went back to York, and sat there a year" [ASC].
- 872-873: Wulfhere flees and returns [AB].
- 876: "Healfden divided the land of the Northumbrians; so that they became afterwards their harrowers and plowers" [ASC].
- 895, 24 August: Guthred dies; buried in the minster [VCH].
- 919: Ragnall takes York [T].
- 927: Athelstan takes York, levels the Danish stronghold [T,VCH]. About then a hoard of 617 coins is buried near Harrogate [VH].
- 939: the men of York choose Olaf of Dublin [K]. 944: Edmund takes York [T].
- 947-954: Eric twice king; Eadred burns Ripon minster 948; Eric killed on Stainmore 954; earls rule after [EB].
- 1014: Wulfstan II's Sermon of the Wolf [WU].
- 1055: Siward dies at York; Tostig earl [SI,TG].
- 1065, 3 October: the thegns rise at York, kill Tostig's men, choose Morcar [TG].
- 1066: 20 September Fulford; York yields; 25 September Stamford Bridge; Tostig buried at York [BF,TG].
- After: 1068 William's castle; 11 September 1069 Ealdred dies; the city and minster burn; William keeps Christmas 1069 at York [HN,VCH].

## 6. Faith and custom

St Peter's church: feast 29 June (?). York's own saints: Paulinus, first bishop (consecrated 625), King Edwin (baptised Easter, 12 April 627), Wilfrid, John of Beverley (?) [T,VCH]. The archbishop strikes his own coin [ST,E2]. Eanbald II shelters the king's enemies and rebukes his adultery [E2]. After 867 the church stays: an archbishop lives beside heathen kings, and coins carry Peter's name with Thor's hammer [VCH]. Danes are buried in churchyards under carved slabs within two generations (?). Wulfstan II wrote laws for Æthelred and Cnut [WU]; the Northumbrian Priests' Law, of his time, fines heathen worship at wells, stones and trees (?).

## 7. Stories and oddities

- The helmet: 8th century, hidden in a well; on its crest in Latin, "In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ and of the Spirit of God, let us offer up Oshere to All Saints. Amen." Found 12 May 1982 [H].
- The Frisians' flight, c. 773: a Frisian merchant killed a count's son in a brawl and all the Frisians left [SP: Altfrid I.11-12].
- Egil's Head-Ransom: a praise poem made in one night to save his neck from Eric; he calls York "the dank demesne". Saga, not record [EB,VCH].
- Siward, dying of dysentery, asked for his armour so as not to die "like a cow" (Henry of Huntingdon, 12th century) [SI].
- A man's stool from Pavement, 20 cm long, 9th century: meat and bread, and "several hundred" worm eggs [LB].
- 30,000 grown people c. 1000, says the Life of St Oswald; Domesday counts 1,418 dwellings in the king's shires and 189 in the archbishop's in 1066 [VCH].

## 8. What we do not know; what the writer must not claim

- Where the minster, Alma Sophia, the school or the king's hall stood in 793. Do not place them on the present minster.
- The size, people and toll of the wic in 793; whether any Frisian came back after 773 (the game's feud is invention [SP]).
- "Jorvik", Danish street names, Coppergate's workshops, St Olave's, Earlsburh, lawmen: nothing before 866; most after 900.
- Coppergate's name: coopers, first recorded 1120-35 [C], or cup-makers (?). Do not lean on either.
- The Anglian Tower's date [AT]. Ragnar Lothbrok and the snake pit, the blood eagle on Ælla: late legend, not for a dated fact.
- Whether Eric of York is Eric Bloodaxe of Norway is disputed [EB].
- The Chronicle's dates for 794-797 run two years early in some copies [ASC]; use 796.
- No named reeve, no fair, no flood year before 1066 was found.
