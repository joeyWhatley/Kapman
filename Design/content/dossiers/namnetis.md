# Dossier: Namnetis (Nantes)

Tags: section 9. (?) = unchecked. Latin and old French quotes are my own translations and may be reused; Munro's Columban is public domain; figures from modern pages are evidence only. CN and MN are cited by chapter.

## 1. The place in 793

- An old Roman bishop's city of the Franks on the edge of the Breton march: north bank of the Loire where the Erdre runs in, the Sèvre opposite [EN; HN]. Named urbs Namnetica, Namnetis, Namnetum [CN; AB 843, 851].
- Rule: the king's count, who also guards the march. Wido "presided in the march of Brittany" in 799 [ARF 799]; who held Nantes in 793 is unknown [GdN]. Bishop: Odilard, c. 776 to c. 800 [LE]. The bishops later claimed the whole city, five leagues round it and the Loire islands' fishing [CN 31]: overstated [Mer].
- Size: a wall built after 276, 1,665 m round, holding 16 ha (18 by another count) [EGR; HN]; "a great city" its thinned citizens could not man, c. 900 [CN 26]. No headcount.
- People: Gallo-Roman and Frankish townsmen, Bretons to the west [Repo; ChN]; on St John's day, pilgrims "from far cities" [CN 6].
- Coming in: about 50 km of estuary; at the city the river splits round a dozen islands and sand ridges [EN]. It was hard to ascend: later ages used outports downstream [EL]. The tide runs past the city (6 m today, after dredging) [EN; EL].
- In 843 a fleet came up on a west wind "by the swift flight of sails and the beat of oars" and moored under the walls [CN 6]. Marks: Batz at the mouth [CN 5]; the island monastery of Indre, three leagues below, in fish-rich water [CN 6; HI; Mer]; the isle of Bièce "near the walls" [CN 28].
- Boats lie at the city's "port", which takes toll [CN 14]. The Roman port ran from Richebourg to Port-Maillard [PN]; Bishop Felix (c. 549-582) cut a channel to serve it [FN]. Opposite: Rezé (Ratiatum), the old south-bank port [Rz; PN].
- No bridge is known in 793; one is claimed under Alan I (876-907), a timber footway swept off by floods and ice (?) [PP].

## 2. What stood there

- **The wall**: 7.5 to 8.5 m high, about 4 m thick, round corner towers, along the Erdre and the Loire [EGR]. In 843 raiders climbed it with ladders and broke through "an entrance long since blocked" [CN 6]. Thrown down by Nominoe and Lambert, 850 [L2; Mer]; "broken in parts, as is shown to this day", c. 1050 [CN 26].
- **Gates** named c. 940: the bishop's postern and, next south, the Porta Cararia [CN 31].
- **The cathedral of SS Peter and Paul**, by the east wall [EGR]: Felix's basilica, dedicated 30 September between 567 and 573 [Mer]; three naves, a tin roof [CC]; marble altars and columns, mosaic walls, gold crowns, a crucifix held by a silver chain from the beam [CN 1]. Gunhard died at St Ferreolus's altar in the left aisle [Mer]. Wrecked 843; rebuilt and lengthened by Bishop Fulcher, 897 to c. 910 [CN 24]; burned 919 [CN 28]; roofless 937 [CN 30]; east end rebuilt by Guerech, 981-988 [CN 42]. An 11th-century crypt survives [CC].
- **St John the Baptist's church**, the pilgrims' goal on his feast [MN 4; Mer]. **Notre-Dame**, rebuilt by Alan Barbetorte; his tomb, 952 [CN 36].
- **The castle by the cathedral**: Fulcher's walled refuge, c. 900 [CN 26]; fell 919 [CN 28]; Alan's "great earthwork" on its line, c. 940, with a tower where he lived [CN 30].
- **The Bouffay**: Conan's castle of 990 in the western corner by the Loire [CN 44], at first of wood, it seems [CB].
- **The bishop's palace** by the cathedral, razed by Count Budic while Bishop Walter was at Jerusalem, c. 1033 [CN 47].
- **Outside the wall**: the basilica of SS Donatian and Rogatian (by tradition c. 490; digs of 1873 found four successive churches, Merovingian stone coffins, a pagan cemetery; rebuilt c. 980) and St Stephen's chapel, early 6th century [SD]; St Similian's, 5th century, 20 by 9 m, with a well [SS]; the little abbey of St Andrew on the Erdre [CN 25]; St Cyr's church and graveyard by the wall [MN 3].
- **The mint**: pennies of Louis the Pious (814-840) reading NAMNETVM, 1.78 g, and of Charles the Bald, NAMNETIS CIVITAS, 1.62 g [Prou].
- **Markets, ale-houses and workshops** that paid toll [CN 14].
- **St Aignan's meadow** outside the town, St Mary's spring on the hill above [CN 29].
- **Indre**: Hermeland's monastery, founded c. 673; burned 29 June 843, never rebuilt [HI; CN 6; Mer].

## 3. Who could be met

- Counts: Wido (Guy), attested 799, dead by 818 [ARF 799; GdN]; Alcuin, abbot at Tours 796-804, wrote him a book on virtues and vices [Alc]. Lambert I, 818-831. Ricuin, 831-841. Renaud of Herbauges, 841-843. Lambert II, 843-846 and 850-851, died 1 May 852. Amaury, 846-850 [LC; L2; CN 4, 10].
- Breton rulers: Erispoe (851-857) and Queen Marmohec; Salomon (857-874); Alan the Great (to 907) [CN 13-27; AB 851].
- Northmen: Sidroc, c. 855 [Mer]; Ragenold, 919 to c. 930; Incon, to 937 [RN; LC].
- Alan Barbetorte, 937-952. Fulk the Good of Anjou, guardian, 952-960. Hoel, 960-981. Guerech, count and bishop, 981-988. Conan of Rennes, 990-992. Judicael, 992-1004. Budic, 1004-1038. Mathias, 1038-1051. Then Judith and Hoel of Cornouaille [LC; CN 29-48]. Viscount Haimo, c. 990 [CN 44].
- Bishops: Odilard; Alan, c. 800; Atto, c. 820-833; Drutcar, 834-835 [LE]. Gunhard (Gohard), 835-843 [LE; Mer]. Actard of Tours, 843-871, twice driven out [CN 9-19; Act]. Gislard, Nominoe's man, 850-851 [CN 12]. Landran, died 5 February 897; Fulcher, 897 to c. 910; Adalard, fled 919 [CN 21-28]. Hesdren, c. 940-960; Walter I, to c. 981; Hervé, 992 to c. 1004; Walter II, 1005-1041; Budic, deposed 1049; Airard; Quiriac, 1052-1079 [CN 31-48; LE]. Susan of Vannes, who cleansed the cathedral in 843 [CN 7].
- Named lesser folk: four canons who came home c. 940, Letard the archdeacon, Oger, Hugh, and Durand called Pabion [CN 31]; Nominoe, a knight of Count Judicael's household, c. 1000 [MN 4].
- Kinds of people: the bishop's toll-gatherers, ale-house keepers, carters, market sellers [CN 14]; fishermen [Mer]; monks of Indre [CN 6]; clerks of St John's [MN 4]; pilgrims; captives held for ransom or sale [CN 6]; "poor Bretons tilling the soil" under the Northmen [CN 27]. No moneyer, reeve or merchant is named.

## 4. What was traded

- **The toll.** In 857 Erispoe gave the bishop back "half the toll of all goods that come to the port of the city, by ship or any other way": markets, cart-toll, wheel-toll, all ale-houses, workshops; gathered by the bishop's own men. The church had once held "tolls and fairs" [CN 14]. Alan Barbetorte split it in three: himself, the bishop, the viscounts [CN 31].
- **Its worth.** About 955 three sacks of pennies came to Fulk of Anjou from Nantes (and four barges of great fish, in the French version); he said no man in the kingdom was so rich as he who held it [CN 37].
- **Salt**: 54 salt-works at Guérande and Batz in the Redon charters, 9th-10th centuries [MG]; Noirmoutier's lies south [Repo]. **Wine**: the Loire vintage [Repo]. **Fish** from the river [Mer]. Tin went out in Roman times; nothing shows it in 793 [HN; PN].
- **Irish ships**: about 610 a ship lay here that "had brought Scottish wares" (Irish); bishop and count put Columban aboard [VC 47].
- **Captives**: in 843 the Bretons kept prisoners "for trade"; the Northmen took townsfolk "to hold or to sell", and survivors paid much to buy them back [CN 6].
- **A Northmen's market**: in 873 they asked to stay on a Loire island until February "and hold a market" [AB 873].
- **Seasons**: the St John's gathering, 24 June [CN 6; MN 4]; salt shipped August to October, wine after the vintage [Repo].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 799: Wido tours Brittany and brings Charles the chiefs' weapons, each marked with its owner's name [ARF 799].
- 811: an army sent against the Bretons [ARF 811].
- 841, 25 June: Count Ricuin dies at Fontenoy; the king gives Nantes to Renaud, not Lambert [CN 4; L2].
- 843, 24 May: Lambert and the Bretons kill Renaud at Blain [CN 4; AB 843].
- 843, 24 June: Northmen storm the city on St John's day, kill Bishop Gunhard and the crowd in the cathedral, and leave at dusk with captives [CN 6; AB 843]. 29 June: they burn Indre. 30 September: the cathedral is cleansed [CN 6-7].
- 850: Lambert and Nominoe take Nantes and throw down its walls [L2; Mer].
- 851: Charles grants Erispoe Rennes, Nantes and Retz [AB 851].
- 853, July: Danes from the Seine plunder Nantes [AB 853] and hold the Loire islands until 856 [Mer].
- 857: the toll charter [CN 14]. 867: the king writes that Nantes, "once most flourishing", has been "a desert for ten years" [Mer].
- 871: Franks attack the Northmen's island fort in the Loire and barely escape [AB 871].
- 886: Northmen again; Bishop Landran flees to Angers until c. 889 [CN 21].
- 897 to c. 910: Fulcher rebuilds the cathedral and walls it round [CN 24-26].
- 919: Northmen take Nantes; the people hold the castle one day and flee by night; the cathedral burns [CN 28].
- 921: Robert besieges them five months, then yields them Nantes and Brittany. 927: a five-week siege fails [RN].
- 937: Alan Barbetorte beats them in St Aignan's meadow and enters a city "deserted for many years" [CN 29-30].
- 952: Alan dies [CN 36].
- 960: Northmen seize Bishop Walter; after eight days the townsmen drive them off; ransom is paid at Guérande [CN 38; MN 1].
- 981: Hoel murdered; Guerech fights Conan at Conquereuil [CN 40-41].
- 990: Conan takes Nantes and builds the Bouffay. 992, 27 June: he dies at Conquereuil against Fulk Nerra [CN 44].
- c. 1033: Count Budic razes the bishop's palace; war, then peace [CN 47].
- 1049: Bishop Budic deposed for simony at Reims [CN 48].
- 1066: Hoel, count of Nantes, becomes duke of Brittany [HN].

## 6. Faith and custom

- Province of Tours. Nominoe drove out Actard and set up his own bishop, 850-851; Nantes held to Tours [CN 11-16].
- Feasts: St John's Nativity, 24 June, with a night vigil at St John's [MN 4]; Donatian and Rogatian, 24 May [DR]; the cathedral's dedication, 30 September [CN 1]; Gohard, 24 or 25 June (?), a saint only from 1096 [Go]; Hermeland, 25 March [HI].
- Burial outside the walls: St Donatian's, St Cyr's, St Andrew's [CN 23; MN 3].
- A church stained with blood is cleansed by a bishop before Mass is said again [CN 7].
- Bishops could be married: Walter II made his son bishop after him [CN 48].
- Law: the chronicle, in its editor's summary, has King Louis confirm at Alan's asking the end of serfdom in Brittany, 946 [CN 34].
- Old gods: Roman dedications to Mars Mullo, Vulcan and Minerva [HN]. Heathen Northmen held the town 919-937; nothing is known of their rites.

## 7. Stories and oddities

- A carbuncle from Alexandria on a marble column lit Felix's church by night [CN 1]; behind it, Fortunatus's line: "the world has night, the hall keeps the day" [Mer].
- Gunhard was cut down saying "Sursum corda" [CN 5]. Later legend: he took up his head, walked to the Loire, and a boat bore him to Angers [Go], where two lead plates lay with his bones: "the humble Gunhard", "father and martyr" [Mer].
- On Noirmoutier the raiders fought over the spoil "like dogs at meat"; the captives fled over the sands at low tide, one with the church's great Bible on his neck, still kept c. 1050 [CN 7].
- Erispoe made his gift by a gold-trimmed key of St Peter's relics [CN 14].
- Alan, thirsty on the hill before the fight, prayed and St Mary opened a spring [CN 29]; he then cut his way to the cathedral door through brambles with his sword [CN 30].
- His corpse would not stay buried at St Donatian's; a host of riders was heard by night outside the walls until he lay in Notre-Dame [CN 36].
- An oak opened to hide a man from the Northmen; raiders in St Donatian's were struck blind [MN 1-2].
- A man blessing the dead in St Cyr's graveyard heard them answer "amen, amen" [MN 3].
- On St John's night a knight and a woman were stuck together "like a dog" until the next sunset, then beaten by the clerks [MN 4].
- Fulk was playing at tables when the sacks of pennies came [CN 37]. In 960 his wife, knowing his sloth, told him the great stake fixed in the Loire's mouth, which scared the Northmen, had fallen [CN 38].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- The count in 793; the population; any named merchant, moneyer, shipwright or reeve.
- That Lambert piloted the raiders: the Angers account of c. 860 calls him their scout, the chronicle of c. 1050 their guide; the Annals of St Bertin are silent, and Merlet thought it a legend [CN 5-7; AB 843; Mer].
- "67 ships" and "Westfaldingi" are not in the Annals of St Bertin; they come from annals I did not see (?) [PN; ViB; Repo]. The island where the raiders wintered is unnamed there; the chronicle says Herio [AB 843; CN 7].
- No mint under Charlemagne: the table's `has_mint: false` is right for 793, wrong from Louis the Pious [Prou]. The realm is Frankish to 851, then Breton, the Northmen's 919-937 [AB 851; RN].
- No castle before c. 900, no Bouffay before 990.
- "Namsborg" as a Norse name rests on a modern page only (?) [ViB].
- The chronicle is a canon's work of c. 1050, hostile to Bretons, surviving in extracts and a French translation [ChN; Mer]. Its miracles are stories.
- Bars, pilots and depths of the Loire: nothing is written.
