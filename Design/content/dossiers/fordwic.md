# Fordwic (Fordwich, Kent): dossier

Port `fordwic`: Fordwic, modern Fordwich, 51.295 N 1.13 E; realm kent, region england_south, kind market, landing riverbank, size 1, no end year [ports_793.json]. Researched 4 Oct 2026; two points in section 8 checked again 6 Oct. Source keys are in section 9. (?) marks what I could not check; (memory) what I know but did not open.

**Read this first.** Fordwich itself is known in these years from two toll charters, a reeve's gift, one writ, one Domesday entry and a few coins and sherds. What a merchant comes for (archbishop, mint, books, market) is in Canterbury, two miles up the track, and the game's tables hang the city on this port. Each fact says whose it is.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What it is.** The king's toll landing for Canterbury on the Great Stour. Old English *fordwic*, "trading settlement at the ford" [enwp Fordwich]. First named in 675: a marsh "called Stodmerch beside Fordeuuicum" [S7; a doubted text resting on a 7th-century record]. In 763/4 it is, with Sarre, a "public place" where the king of Kent's toll-gatherers (*thelonearii*) take toll on ships [S29].
- **Who rules.** Offa of Mercia, directly, since 785; Kent has no king [enwp Kent kings]. On 21 June 793 Archbishop Æthelheard, Offa's man, is chosen but not enthroned: that is done on 21 July by Hygberht, Offa's archbishop of Lichfield [enwp Æthelhard].
- **How big.** Not known. Domesday counts 96 house plots in 1066 [DB Fordwich]. The ground has given a few early to middle Saxon sherds and three sceattas of 680-720 [HER]; at Sturry, over the ford, sunken huts, hearths, slag and pottery of the 6th-7th century [HER Sturry].
- **The land.** The south bank, low and "close to the marshes" [H9]; the river is tidal here, and Fordwich is its tidal limit [TB]. A ford (later a bridge) crosses to Sturry; a track runs south-west to St Martin's church and the city [TB], two miles [H9] or three [TB]. The Thanet road runs through Sturry to the ferry at Sarre [enwp Sarre; enwp Sturry].
- **Coming in.** By the Wantsum, between Thanet and Kent: "about three furlongs over, and fordable only in two places, for both ends of it run into the sea" [Bede I.25, Jane 1903]. North mouth: Reculver, St Mary's minster (669) in the Roman fort. South mouth: the ruined fort of Richborough [enwp Wantsum; enwp Reculver]. The Stour joins the strait near its midpoint, at Stourmouth [enwp Wantsum]; Sarre is about 9 km east-north-east of Fordwich (table coordinates). West of Stourmouth the river is a tidal inlet through marsh, Chislet's to the north, Stodmarsh to the south [enwp Stour; S7]. Width, depth and tide in the game's water study are guesses [repo water]; dossiers/sarre.md has the strait.
- **Where boats lie.** Not known. Hasted, of the later port: ships "were moored" at the quay, "where all goods were laded and unladed" [H9]. A boundary or track "running to the river" was seen near the church in 2006 [HER ditches].

## 2. What stood there

**Fordwich**

- The toll place of 763/4 [S29]: not found.
- Ditches and pits at the village hall (TR 1815 5976), 2006: a post pit, recut ditches, three later Saxon sherds, burnt material; boundary, drain or defence. No archaeologist watched the digging [HER ditches].
- Test pits, 1998: a few sherds, early to middle Saxon by fabric [HER sherds].
- Coins, placed only to the kilometre: sceattas of Series A (680-700), Series E with the name Æthiliræd in runes (700-710) and Series N (715-720); a London penny of Edgar's reform (973-975) [HER coins].
- St Mary's church: probably first built in the late 11th century, its corner stones of Quarr stone from Wight [TB]; the county record calls that work "Saxon" [HER church].
- 96 plots in 1066 [DB Fordwich]; a reeve's two *hagas* (fenced plots) and a meadow [S1654].

**Sturry**: the Popes Lane huts, found 1997 [HER Sturry]; a penny of Offa (East Anglian mint, 770-792), the moneyer Wihtræd's name partly in runes [HER Sturry penny]. The oldest English charter surviving as first written (679) grants land here [S8].

**Canterbury**

- The Roman wall and gates. In 804 the nuns of Lyminge get "as a refuge in need" six acres by St Mary's church in the west of the city, between the Stour and "the city wall" [S160, my translation]. Later: chapels over the gates, a cattle market outside the south-east wall [enwp walls].
- Christ Church: the Saxon cathedral, found under the nave in 1993; rebuilt larger (49 by 23 m) in the 9th or 10th century; burnt 1067 [enwp Cathedral].
- St Augustine's abbey, outside the walls: two churches (613, about 624), library, writing room, the graves of Kent's kings; renamed for Augustine in 978 [enwp StA].
- The mint. None is known at Fordwich.
- "The cnihts' guild", in a charter of 858 x 866 [S1199]; a burgesses' guild and a clerks' guild in Domesday [DB Canterbury].

**Not before 1066**: the Fordwich Stone (about 1100), the town hall (1544), a bridge, Reculver's twin towers [HER church; enwp Fordwich; TB; enwp Reculver].

## 3. Who could be met

**Tied to Fordwich**

- Nobody is named there in 793. Earlier: King Eadberht II and Abbess Sigeburh of Minster-in-Thanet, 763/4 [S29]; Abbot Deneheah of Reculver [S1612].
- Bruman, port-reeve: granted Abbot Sigeric of St Augustine's two hagas and a meadow at Fordwich for life, then to the abbey; dated 991 by the abbey's late chroniclers [S1654]. Domesday's reeve Bruman, "in the time of King Edward", took the customs of foreign merchants on the two churches' land in Canterbury and later swore he had taken them unjustly [DB Canterbury].
- King Edward (1042-1066), who gave the abbey his two thirds of the borough; Earl Godwin (died 15 April 1053), who had the third [DB Fordwich; enwp Godwin]; Archbishop Stigand, Earl Harold and Osweard, greeted in the writ [S1092]; Abbots Wulfric II (1045-1061) and Æthelsige (1061-1067/70) [enwp StA].

**In Canterbury**

- Archbishops [enwp archbishops]: Æthelheard (793-805), Wulfred (805-832), Feologild (832), Ceolnoth (833-870), Æthelred (870-888), Plegmund (890-923), Athelm (to 926), Wulfhelm (to 941), Oda (941-958), Ælfsige and Byrhthelm (958-959), Dunstan (959-988), Æthelgar (988-990), Sigeric (990-994), Ælfric (995-1005), Ælfheah (1006-1012), Lyfing (1013-1020), Æthelnoth (1020-1038), Eadsige (1038-1050), Robert of Jumièges (1051-1052), Stigand (1052-1070).
- Kings in Kent [enwp Kent kings]: Offa (to 796), Eadberht Præn (796-798), Cuthred (798-807), Coenwulf (807-821), Ceolwulf (821-823), Baldred (from 823), then the West Saxons; Æthelstan, under-king 839-851 [enwp Æthelstan].
- Abbot Guthheard of St Augustine's (traditional dates 787-803) [enwp StA]; Abbess Selethryth of Lyminge, 804 [S160]; Ealdorman Ealhhere (named 839-853) [enwp Ealhhere]; Ealdorman Alfred and his wife Wærburh, later 9th century [enwp Codex].
- City reeves, Hasted's list from Somner: Aldhun (about 780), Hlothwig *portgerefa* (956), Ælfweard the king's reeve (1011), Bruman [H11 reeves].
- Taken in 1011: Ælfheah, Bishop Godwine of Rochester, Abbess Leofrun, Ælfweard. An Ælfmær whose life the archbishop had saved betrayed the city; Abbot Ælfmær was let go. The Chronicle does not say whether they are one man [ASC 1011].
- Moneyers on Canterbury coins: Eoba (for Offa's queen Cynethryth), Beornferth (805 to about 810), Swefherd (about 815-822), Eadwold (about 997-1003) [enwp Offa; enwp penny]. From memory, for the 790s: Babba, Æthelnoth, Osmod (?).
- Witnesses of 858 x 866: Æthelstan "and the inner townsmen", Æthelhelm "and the cnihts' guild", Herewine [S1199]. Eadui Basan, monk and scribe of Christ Church, early 11th century [enwp Vespasian].

**Ordinary people the evidence shows**: toll-gatherers; minster ships' crews and shipwrights [S29]; foreign merchants [DB Canterbury]; an iron-worker [HER Sturry]; scribes and a goldsmith [enwp Codex]; herdsmen on the marsh (Stodmarsh is "mare marsh") [enwp Stodmarsh].

## 4. What was traded

- **No cargo of Fordwich is listed.** The Thanet nuns' crew deal in "goods of various kinds" [S29].
- **Toll**, by the ship. Eadberht II freed two of Minster-in-Thanet's ships at Sarre, and a third, newly built at the minster, of "all tribute and toll" at "Fordwic and Seorre"; Mercian kings had done as much at Lundenwic. If that ship is wrecked, broken or worn out another may replace it, provided that whatever its people gain by trading "they must faithfully offer to us, together with the ship, at the place called Fordwik" [S29, my translation]. I read this as the king's first look at the cargo. Reculver had the toll of one ship at Fordwich [S1612]. No rate is given. After Edward's gift the toll was the abbot's [H9]; Canterbury's own was worth 68 shillings in 1086 [DB Canterbury].
- **Goods, from the region's record, not Fordwich's**: wine "from the south", 1012 [ASC 1012]; Quarr stone from Wight, 11th century [TB]; salt from the Chislet marshes, undated [enwp Chislet]; the Graveney boat, a Kentish coaster with hops and Rhenish querns [repo english (?)]; the Stour's big trout, famous by 1653 [enwp Fordwich]. For the black stones and cloaks of 796 see dossiers/lundenwic.md.
- **Coin**: struck in Canterbury for kings and archbishops. Offa's heavy penny dates from 792/3: Æthelheard has no light coins. About 822-824 pennies name only the moneyer and *Dorobernia civitas*, the moneyers unsure whose name to use [enwp penny]. Æthelstan's law gives Canterbury seven moneyers [repo chronicle]: four the king's, two the bishop's, one the abbot's (memory).
- **Seasons, fairs, ships**: nothing recorded beyond the minsters' own ships.

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 792: Offa frees Kent's churches of burdens, except service in Kent "against pagan seamen with roving fleets" [S134, my translation].
- 793, 21 July: Æthelheard enthroned [enwp Æthelhard].
- 796: Offa dies; Eadberht Præn, once a priest, takes Kent; the archbishop flees [ASC 794; enwp Eadberht].
- 798: Coenwulf wastes Kent and leads Eadberht bound into Mercia; Cuthred king [ASC 796; enwp Cuthred].
- 803, 12 October: Clofesho ends the archbishopric of Lichfield [repo chronicle].
- 804: the Lyminge nuns' refuge [S160].
- 825: Baldred driven north over the Thames; Kent goes to Wessex [ASC 823].
- 842: "great slaughter in London, Canterbury, and Rochester" [ASC 839, Ingram 1823; see section 8].
- 851: sea fight at Sandwich, nine ships taken; heathens winter on Thanet; 350 ships storm Canterbury and London [ASC 851].
- 865: the army on Thanet takes Kent's promise of money, then overruns east Kent by night [ASC 865].
- 969: King Edgar has all Thanet plundered [ASC 969].
- 980: Thanet overrun [ASC 980].
- "991": Bruman's grant [S1654].
- 1009, soon after Lammas: Thorkell's army lands at Sandwich and turns on Canterbury; east Kent buys peace for 3,000 pounds [ASC 1009].
- 1011, between the Nativity of St Mary and Michaelmas: Canterbury beset and taken by treachery [ASC 1011].
- 1012, 19 April: Ælfheah killed at Greenwich; his body comes home in June 1023 [ASC 1012, 1023].
- 1030, 18 May: St Mildred's body moved from Thanet to St Augustine's against "fierce local opposition" [enwp Mildrith].
- 1031: Cnut gives Christ Church the haven of Sandwich [ASC 1031].
- 1053 x 1066 (1055, says Hasted): Edward's writ gives St Augustine's "as much land within Fordwic" as he had [S1092, my translation; H9].
- 1066: a "small borough" of 96 plots paying 13 shillings, worth 100 shillings [DB Fordwich].
- After the game: the cathedral burns, 1067; Odo of Bayeux adds Godwin's third; 73 plots in 1086 [enwp Cathedral; DB Fordwich].

## 6. Faith and custom

- **Church.** None can be shown at Fordwich before the late 11th century [TB]. Mass is heard in the city: Christ Church, St Augustine's, or St Martin's, where the Fordwich track begins [TB].
- **Saints and days.** Mildred, 13 July; her translation, 18 May [enwp Mildrith]. Ælfheah, 19 April [ASC 1012]. Tatwine, 30 July [enwp Tatwine]. Æthelheard, 12 May, kept as a saint until Lanfranc ended it [enwp Æthelhard]. Augustine, 26 May; Dunstan, 19 May (memory).
- **Burial.** Kings of Kent at St Augustine's [enwp StA]; Ælfheah north of Christ's altar, 1023 [ASC 1023].
- **Old gods and law.** King Wihtred's law of 695 punishes gifts to idols, meat in a fast and slaves' work on Sunday; a stranger who leaves the track and does not blow his horn may be killed or taken. It is dated "the sixth day of Rugern", the rye harvest [enwp Wihtred].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- Tatwine, archbishop 731-734, wrote forty Latin riddles; one "Eusebius" added sixty [enwp riddles].
- A gold-lettered gospel book, probably made in Canterbury, was carried off by a heathen army in Kent. Ealdorman Alfred and Wærburh bought it back with pure gold and gave it to Christ Church; a goldsmith, Wulfhelm, is named in it [enwp Codex].
- The charter naming the cnihts' guild has a hole cut by rats [S1199, notes].
- 1011: "Then was a captive he who before was of England head and Christendom" [ASC 1011, Ingram 1823].
- 1012: "They were also much drunken; for there was wine brought them from the south." Then: "They overwhelmed him with bones and horns of oxen" [ASC 1012, Ingram 1823]. Thorkell offered all he had, save his ship, for the archbishop's life [enwp Siege].
- 1031, at Sandwich: Christ Church's rights reach as far inland as a man can throw a taper-axe from a ship afloat at the highest tide [ASC 1031].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Where the landing, toll place or quay of 793 lay; how many lived there; any Fordwich name before Bruman.
- A church or priest there before the 1070s, or its dedication then.
- A mint there: `has_mint` in npc_roles_793.json can only mean Canterbury's.
- A toll rate, market day, fair or season.
- That Northmen rowed up the Stour. In 851 the ships came into the Thames mouth; in 1009 the army landed at Sandwich [ASC]. Under 839 Ingram prints Canterbury, but the oldest copy has "Cwantawic", usually read as Quentovic (memory) (?). The raid "via the Wantsum in 839" [enwp Wantsum; repo water] has nothing better behind it.
- Bruman's date: 991 fits neither Abbot Sigeric (abbot from about 975 or 980; bishop of Ramsbury 985 or 986; archbishop 990-994 [enwp Sigeric]) nor Domesday's reeve of King Edward's day.
- "In 866 the abbot gained six houses, three acres and a meadow in Fordwich" [enwp Fordwich, from Woodruff 1895, not opened] (?).
- "1055" for Edward's gift is Hasted's; the writ allows 1053 to 1066.
- Which toll lay where: the Sawyer summary of S29 puts the two ships at Fordwich, the Latin at Sarre; `ship_classes_793.json` follows the summary.
- S1612's text: the Sawyer page prints another charter under it, so I have only the summary. Its year, 747, may be wrong: the notes on S29 tie it to charters redated to an earlier Eadberht (my reading) (?).
- The sea fight "at the mouth of the Stour" in 884 or 885, where Alfred's ships met 13 (the Chronicle says 16 (?)) Danish ships, was on the Essex and Suffolk Stour by Harwich, not this one [enwp Stour Suffolk].
- BRIEF.md bars guilds by name before about 1000; Canterbury's cnihts' guild of 858 x 866 is a real exception.
- Caen stone landed here: only after 1070 (memory).
