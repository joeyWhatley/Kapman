# Burdigala (Bordeaux): dossier

Port `burdigala`: Burdigala, modern Bordeaux, 44.841 N 0.571 W; realm franks, region aquitaine; town, quay landing, size 3, no end year [ports_793.json]. Keys in brackets: see section 9. (?) means not checked.

## 1. The place in 793

- An old Roman city on the left bank of the Garonne, at the mouths of two small streams, the Devèze and the Peugue, ringed by marsh [W:Burdigala].
- It lies in the kingdom of Aquitaine that Charles made for his son Louis [W:Histoire], who in spring 793 is away in Italy [Repo:chronicle]. In 778 Charles set a count named Seguin over Bordeaux [Astr 3]; whether he still holds it is not recorded. Across the river live the Gascons [EW:Seguin I].
- A metropolitan see, one of the 21 chief cities in Charles's will of 811. The bishop of 793 is unknown: the list is blank from the 7th century to Sicharius (814/816) [Repo:chronicle, W:Archevêques].
- Size: the Roman wall holds about 32 ha and the town stayed mostly inside it [W:Histoire].
- From the sea: the Gironde, 75 km long and up to 12 km wide, with shifting sandbanks, islands and muddy water; the tide runs far above the town, with a bore at spring tides [W:Gironde]. Blaye and St Romanus's monastery lie on the right bank [GC]. Past the Dordogne's mouth the town is some 25 km up the Garonne, about 100 km from open sea (my measure (?)).
- At the town the river bends like a crescent, then about 700 m wide [W:Port]. Landmarks: the square wall and its towers; just north, on the highest ground, the columns of a roofless Roman temple, 26 m high, "seen from afar" (24 when whole; 17 still stood in 1669) [W:Tutelle, W:Burdigala]; north-west, the ruined amphitheatre, 132 by 111 m, a stone quarry, "the Arenas" in the 10th century [W:Gallien]; south, Holy Cross on a rise in the marsh [W:Croix].
- Where boats lie: the Roman basin inside the wall (1.5 ha at high water) silted up from the 6th century, and work moved to the bank of the Garonne [W:Port]. No quay of these years is known; the table's "quay" is unproven (?).

## 2. What stood there

- **The wall** (about 278-290): 740 by 480 m, 9 to 10 m high, 5 m thick, towers about every 50 m. Four gates: north (to the Médoc), south, Porta Jovia (west, to St Seurin) and Porta Navigera, the water gate by St Peter's; two more cut about 950 and 980 (?) [W:Remparts, W:Burdigala]. Strengthened in the late 9th century, when armed men (milites) first appear [W:Histoire].
- **St Andrew's**, the bishop's church, by the west wall, with St Mary's church and St Helen's chapel beside it in Louis the Pious's immunity of 814. Said to be wrecked in 848 and 864; rebuilt in the 11th century [W:André].
- **St Seurin's**, outside the west gate: a burial church over the saint's tomb, first built about 450, in a grave field used from the 4th to the 13th century. Digs found 435 burials, 265 in stone coffins up to five deep, 70 in amphorae, mostly infants [W:Seurin, W:Nécropole]. Under the bishop from 814/816 [GC]; canons about 990 [GC-I 3]; rebuilt early in the 11th century [W:Seurin].
- **Holy Cross**, south of the wall by the Eau Bourde stream: a Merovingian house where Mommolin, abbot of Fleury, died about 679; "destroyed by the pagans"; rebuilt before 977 by Count William the Good for thirteen monks and Abbot Elis [W:Croix, GC-I 2, EW:William].
- **St Peter's**, by the old harbour mouth: the harbour church in Gregory of Tours, then unnamed until 1173 [W:St-Pierre].
- **The palace at Saint-Projet**, inside the wall: an enclosure with a hall ("la Salle"), a chapel, the mint, gates and a well, known only from 11th- to 13th-century texts and the street plan; never dug; date open [Boutoulle 2016].
- **The mint.** Prou lists no penny of Charles from Bordeaux. Louis the Pious: BVRDIGALA in two lines. Lothar I (817-843): BVRDICALA round a temple, 1.60 g. King Lothar (954-986): "Burdegal" [Prou]. Then the counts' poor silver: SANCHIVS with a worn royal monogram (0.76 to 1.30 g); one BERNARDV with a hand; GVILELMO with four small crosses [Poey].
- **Soulac**, at the estuary mouth: an oratory of St Mary with "hills" (dunes (?)), pine wood, marsh and a fishery, given to Holy Cross before 977 [GC-I 2, 4].

## 3. Who could be met

- Kings: Louis to 814; Pippin I 814-838; Pippin II from 838, handed to Charles the Bald in 852 [Repo:chronicle, EW:Sancho II, AB].
- Counts: Seguin (778), probably the "duke of the Gascons" deposed in 816 for "arrogance and wicked ways" [Astr 3, EW:Seguin I]. Seguin II, from about 840, killed 845 [EW:Seguin II]. William, "duke" of the city, taken 848 [CF]; perhaps Dhuoda's son [EW:William S]. Amalvinus, 887 and 906 [EW:Amalvinus]. William the Good, son of Raymond and Entregodis, husband of Aremburgis, before 977 [GC-I 2].
- Dukes of Gascony holding Bordeaux: William Sancho from 977/988 (died 996); Bernard William to 25 December 1009; Sancho William to 4 October 1032; Eudes of Poitou to 10 March 1039; Guy Geoffrey from about 1044, duke of Aquitaine from 1058 [EW:William, W:Sanche, W:Eudes, W:Comté].
- Bishops: Sicharius 814/816-after 825; Adalelm 829-after 848; Frotar by 860-876; Aldebert (10th century); Gombaud about 989-994, Duke William Sancho's brother; Seguin 1010 (or 1000); Arnold 1022; Islo 1024; Geoffrey II, "a Frank by birth, honest in his ways", 1027 to 10 July 1043, buried at St Seurin; Archambaud 1047-1059; Joscelin of Parthenay 1059-1086 [GC, W:Archevêques].
- Northmen: "Hoseri" (Asgeir), whose fleet sailed from Bordeaux in 851 and came back to it in 852 [CF]. Shipmen of King Alfonso III of Asturias, May 906 [EW:Epistola].
- Ordinary people in the charters: Trencard, a young noble "learned in letters" [GC-I 2]; Gombaud, abbot of Holy Cross, William the provost and the knights Eduard and Guitbert (1027) [GC-I 4]; the matron Peironeria, who gave the canons of St Seurin half a church [GC-I 3]; Arnold, "free and freeborn", son of Erland and Jarsinde, who sold land about 1030; Adalsius, who gave St Seurin a mill "by the Broken Tower" (1027-1043) [GC]; serfs, men and women, at Soulac [GC-I 2]; Jews, named once, in 848 [AB]. No moneyer, tollman, shipman or merchant is named.

## 4. What was traded

- Wine: the fame is Roman ("famed for thy wine", "vine-clad hills") [Aus]. After Rome "documents are lacking" for the vineyard [W:Vignoble]; William the Good gives "lands with vines" [GC-I 2]. I found no text of 793-1066 that names wine shipped from Bordeaux; the repo's run to the Irish rests on McCormick, not read [Repo:goods]. Vintage September to October [Repo:seasons].
- Salt and fish: the charter dated 1027 gives Saint-Macaire's road toll (pedagium) and a custom of three measures of salt; Soulac has its fishery [GC-I 2, 4].
- By sea: ties with Spain and Armorica in the 6th and 7th centuries [W:Port]; Asturian ships in May 906 [EW:Epistola]. By river: a fleet reached Toulouse in 844 [AB]. By road: north by Saintes to Poitiers [W:Guillaume VIII].
- Coin: section 2. Melle, the silver mint to the north, was sacked by the Northmen in 848 [W:Siège].
- Tolls, market days, fairs, weights: nothing found for Bordeaux itself.

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 816: Seguin deposed; the Gascons rise [EW:Seguin I].
- 844: Northmen go up the Garonne to Toulouse, plundering unopposed [AB].
- 845, autumn: Northmen break out "between Bordeaux and Saintes"; the Christians fight on foot and are cut down; Seguin is taken and killed; Saintes burns [Lupus, CA, EW:Seguin II].
- 847: Danes besiege Bordeaux "for a long time" [AB].
- 848, Lent: Charles the Bald takes nine Danish ships on the Dordogne. Then the Northmen take the city by night, with William; "the Jews betraying it", they waste and burn it. The Aquitanians, sick of Pippin's sloth, choose Charles at Orléans [CF, AB, CA].
- 851, 13 October: Asgeir's fleet, come from "most strongly fortified" Bordeaux, enters the Seine; next June it goes back to Bordeaux laden [CF].
- 855: Northmen at Bordeaux again, "roaming as they please" [AB]. They winter in the country in 848, 855 and 864 [Boutoulle 2008].
- 876: Frotar pleads at Ponthion that he "could not stay in his city for the pagans". The pope gives him Bourges "as long as the pagans hold the church of Bordeaux" [AB, GC].
- 887: Count Amalvinus at the council of Bourges. 906: Alfonso's ships [EW:Amalvinus].
- Before 977: Holy Cross rebuilt. By 988 the county has passed to William Sancho, who about 982 beats Northmen at Taller, far south [EW:William, W:Taller].
- 989, 1 June: Gombaud presides at Charroux, the first Peace of God [W:Charroux].
- 1027: Geoffrey II chosen and anointed at Blaye, before Duke Sancho's assembly [GC].
- 1039: Eudes killed; Bernard of Armagnac contests the county until Guy Geoffrey wins it (1044, or 1052 in Poey) [W:Eudes, W:Comté, Poey].

## 6. Faith and custom

- Saints: Seurin, a bishop from the East, met by Bishop Amand after a dream (Gregory of Tours); Amand, 18 June; Mommolin at Holy Cross [W:Seurin, W:Amand, W:Croix]. Seurin's day: 23 October in the Roman calendar; Bordeaux's own use not checked (?) [W:Séverin]. The canons, about 990: he came "warned by an angel" and chose to rest here [GC-I 3].
- Burial: beside St Seurin. A charter's curse: the breaker "shall never be buried in the cemetery" but "laid in the burial of asses" [GC-I 3]. Another: the lot of Judas, and 100 pounds of gold and 1,000 of silver [GC-I 2].
- Charroux, 989: a curse on any who break into churches, strike unarmed clergy, or take a poor man's sheep, ox, ass, cow, goat or pig [W:Charroux].
- Old gods: only ruins and a gate's name (Jove's). The heathens here are the Northmen.

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **Trencard's counsel.** Count William asks his household where to build a monastery, "inside the city or out". Young Trencard answers that old men remember monks at the oratory of the Holy Cross, on his family's land, and gives it up [GC-I 2].
- **News on oath.** Lupus of Ferrières writes that men come from Aquitaine "affirmed even on oath" that Seguin was taken and killed, and adds: every kingdom divided against itself shall be laid waste [Lupus].
- **The king's crown.** Alfonso agrees to buy a gold and jewelled crown from the clergy of Tours; it is to wait at Bordeaux with "our friend" Amalvinus for his ships. Some scholars doubt the letter [EW:Epistola].
- **Roland's horn.** The Song of Roland, of about 1100 (?), lays it on St Seurin's altar, "where pilgrims see it" [W:Seurin]. Not to be shown in 793.
- **Words to quote** (Evelyn-White 1919): "Her goodly walls four-square raise lofty towers so high that their tops pierce the soaring clouds"; the tide brings "a whole sea gliding onward with its fleets" [Aus].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Unknown: the bishop and count of 793; the number of people; quays, tolls, markets, fairs; any named merchant; wine exports in these years.
- Do not state the Jews' betrayal as fact: it is one line by a distant bishop, and some read it as Pippin's party [EW:Pepin II].
- Whether Northmen ruled the town from 848 into the 880s is disputed. No dig has found them, and the local tales of ruined churches were written from the 11th century on [Boutoulle 2008, EW:Viking].
- Gallia Christiana doubts that Archbishop Gombaud was the duke's brother; the 1027 charter's Latin was suspect by the 13th century [GC, GC-I 4].
- Not in 793: a mint; the Peace of God; Gascon dukes in the city; the Ombrière palace (tower first named 1080); "Palais Gallien" (a name of 1367); St Fort (a 13th-century cult) [W:Ombrière, W:Gallien, W:Fort].
- Never in this game: English Bordeaux and its wine fleets (after 1152), the second wall (1227), the Cordouan tower [W:Gironde, W:Remparts].
