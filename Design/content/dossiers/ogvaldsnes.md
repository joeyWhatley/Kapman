# Ögvaldsnes (Avaldsnes, Karmøy): dossier

Port `ogvaldsnes`: Ögvaldsnes, modern Avaldsnes on Karmøy, 59.353 N 5.276 E; realm norse_west, region norway_west, kind royal seat, landing beach, size 2, open through the whole game [ports_793.json]. Researched 6 Oct 2026. Source keys are spelled out in section 9. (?) marks what I could not check. **late** marks what rests only on sagas written about 1230.

**Read this first.** For 793 the evidence is the graves: two ship burials, tree-ring dated, a short walk from the farm. Almost everything with a name and a year after that is Snorri's Heimskringla, three to four hundred years late. Of the Viking Age farm itself the digs found post holes and a workshop area, no more.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What it is.** A great farm on a headland on the north-east side of the island Karmøy (Kormt), at the narrow part of the Karmsund, where the sailing route "is forced into a narrow passage that runs along Avaldsnes" [enwp Av]. The Karmsund is 30 km long and is the southernmost sheltered stretch of the inner lead, the North Way, which probably gave Norway its name [enwp Ks; repo water]. Whoever sits here sees every ship on the coast road [av-hist].
- **The name.** Old Norse Ǫgvaldsnes, a man's name and "ness" [snl]. Snorri says a king Ogvald gave his name "both to the ness and to the house" [Hkr OT 71, **late**].
- **Who rules.** A sea-king whose name is lost; the land is Rogaland. His forerunner was laid in a ship in the mound Storhaug in the summer of 779 [av-sth; repo norway]. A second ship grave, Grønhaug, was made between 790 and 795 [nowp Gr]: in June 793 it is new, or the man in it is still alive.
- **Who lives there.** The lord's household, his armed men, farm hands and thralls, smiths and craftsmen [av-forsk]. No count of heads is possible. It is a farm and a hall, not a town: no market, plots or streets are known. Size 2 is the game's judgement.
- **Coming in.** The island is "very long, but not broad"; its ocean side is largely untilled, and the usual ships' channel runs inside it, through the sound [Hkr SO 124, **late**]. The tide hardly matters: spring range 0.46 m, neap 0.18 m; the shore lay perhaps a metre higher about 800 (estimate) [repo water]. The landmarks are mounds: Bronze Age mounds in a row on the heights, the great mound Flaghaug by the farm, the two ship mounds to the north, and the tall standing stone at the hall [enwp Av; av-sth]. The farm is "not far from the sea"; ships lie below it in the sound, crews under ship-tents ashore, and the lord's man comes down in the morning to ask who commands and what the errand is [Hkr SO 123, **late**, of 1022]. A sheltered bay south of the church is now called the Kings' Harbour, but harbour use is only traced there from the 1100s [av-forsk].

## 2. What stood there

- **The king's farm and hall.** South of the present church: the wall trench of a hall of the late Roman or Migration period, and post holes of Viking Age buildings (found 2006; dug 2011-2012 by the Royal Manor Project, University of Oslo, under Dagfinn Skre) [av-forsk; khm]. In 1023 the saga gives it a feasting hall with an ante-room and a high seat, a separate cook-house, and a church with a path between church and hall [Hkr SO 124, 127, **late**]. The stone royal hall found in 2017 was built 1240-1319: not in the game's years [av-forsk].
- **The workshop area.** Forging, handicraft, grain drying and salt making, in use about 200 to 1000 [av-forsk].
- **Storhaug** (the Great Mound), at Gunnarshaug north of the farm: 40-50 m across, 5-6 m high. An oak ship with a keel of about 22 m (some 27 m in all), planks 6 cm thick; burial in summer 779 by tree rings. In it: a smaller boat, a horse, a sledge, two Frankish swords (one single-edged), a gold arm ring, a spear and lance, an iron quiver, iron tools, two sets of gaming pieces (amber and coloured glass), a wax cake marked with a cross, a feather sealed in tin. Dug in 1886, after farmers had burned some of the timber [av-sth; av-king].
- **Grønhaug** (the Green Mound), at Bø: about 30 m across and 4 m high. A boat about 15 m long, built about 780; a man's burial of 790-795 (tree rings, published 2009), robbed long ago. Left in it: a piece of a very fine glass beaker called English, hooped wooden vessels, turned bowls, textiles, tapestry and bedding. Dug by Haakon Shetelig in 1902; reopened in August 2026 [nowp Gr]. (English Wikipedia's "10th century" for it is out of date [enwp Av].)
- **Flaghaug**, by the hall: a mound 43 m across and 5 m high until it was levelled in 1835. It held Norway's richest grave of the Roman period (the 200s): a neck ring of 600 g of gold, weapons, Roman vessels of silver and bronze [enwp Av; av-kings]. In 793 it stands whole and nobody knows what is in it.
- **Kjellerhaug (Kuhaugen, "the cow mound")**, a second big mound by the farm; its date was a question for the 2011-2012 dig [khm]. Result not seen (?).
- **The standing stone**, now called the Virgin Mary's Sewing Needle: 7.2 m high (6 m by another count), leaning to within 9.2 cm of the church wall; the second tallest of its kind in Norway [enwp Av; snl]. It is older than the church; its age is not given in what I read (?). The Mary name is Christian and later.
- **The Bronze Age mounds on the heights** (Rehaugane): the only row of Bronze Age mounds in Norway; six of an old count of 98 mounds in the district survive [enwp Av; snl].
- **An older ship grave**, of about 680-750, called the oldest in the North [enwp Av]. Which mound is meant is not said (?).
- **A church.** One stands by the hall at Easter 1023 [Hkr SO 127, **late**]. A wooden church is assumed before the stone one of about 1250, and is credited to Olaf Tryggvason without proof [enwp Ch]. No trace has been reported (?).
- **Skrattasker**, the skerry "under water in flood tide" where sorcerers were left bound in 998 [Hkr OT 70, **late**]. Which rock it is, is local tradition (?).
- **Not there:** no mint, wall, market place, toll house or town.

## 3. Who could be met

- **The lord of the 790s**: nameless. The local museum offers the saga kings Hjorleif "the woman-lover" and his son Half for the 779 grave and says itself "we are not sure" [av-king]. Those names come from a legendary saga: do not use them as fact.
- **Harald Fairhair** (died about 931-933): in old age "generally dwelt on some of his great farms", Ogvaldsnes among them; buried under a mound at Haugar on the Karmsund [Hkr HH 40, 45, **late**]. His own poet names Utstein, not Ögvaldsnes [nowp Kg].
- **Eric Bloodaxe** (king about 932-934) [repo norway; av-kings].
- **Hakon the Good** (king about 934-961) and **Guthorm Ericsson**, who fell to him here; the poet **Guthorm Sindri** made the verse on it [Hkr HG 20, **late**].
- **Olaf Tryggvason** (king 995-1000), here at Easter 998 with 360 men and a bishop; **Eyvind Kelda**, sorcerer, said to be a great-grandson of Harald, drowned here [Hkr OT 69-71, **late**].
- **Erling Skjalgsson of Sola** (killed 1028), the great man of the coast, who came with nearly 1,500 men [Hkr SO 127, **late**; repo norway].
- **Olaf Haraldsson**, later Saint Olaf (king 1015-1028, died 1030), at his Easter feast here in 1023; **Bishop Sigurd**; **Thorarin Nefjolfsson**; **Skjalg Erlingsson** [Hkr SO 124-127, **late**].
- **Thorir Sel**, the king's bailiff at the farm until Easter 1023: "of low birth", "polite in speech, showy in clothes" [Hkr SO 123, **late**]. **Asbjorn Sigurdsson** of Halogaland, called Selsbane, who killed him and was named bailiff in his place but never came back to serve [Hkr SO 124-128, **late**].
- **Ordinary people the evidence shows:** smiths, salt makers, grain dryers [av-forsk]; cooks and a cellar-master, watchmen, the bailiff's sixty men raised in a night, slaves with corn of their own to sell (Erling's, at Sola) [Hkr OT 70-71, SO 123, **late**]; shipwrights and weavers, by what lay in the mounds [av-sth; nowp Gr].

## 4. What was traded

- **No market is recorded.** Goods changed hands with the lord or his bailiff, by gift and by leave.
- **In, by the graves:** Frankish swords, glass gaming pieces, a fine glass beaker, amber, gold [av-sth; nowp Gr].
- **Made on the spot:** iron work, salt, dried grain [av-forsk].
- **Out, by the one story:** corn and malt. In 1022 the farm has "great corn stacks" and the north is starving [Hkr SO 123, **late**]. The region's wider goods (soapstone, whetstones, iron, hides, dried fish) are in the repo's notes, not proven for this farm [repo trade].
- **Law and toll:** in 1022 the king forbade corn, malt and meal to go from the south to the north. The bailiff seized a whole cargo for it and swapped the ship's good striped sail for his old one [Hkr SO 123, **late**]. No toll at the strait is recorded in what I read (?).
- **Coins and weights:** none reported from the farm (?). Silver goes by weight in Norway until the king's penny [repo norway].
- **Ships:** a 27 m sea-going oared ship and a 15 m boat in the mounds [av-sth; nowp Gr]; in 1022 a cargo ship with twenty men, in 1023 a warship of twenty benches with nearly ninety men [Hkr SO 123-124, **late**].
- **Season:** ships go in the water after Easter and come out in October [repo trade].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 779, summer: the Storhaug burial [av-sth].
- 790-795: the Grønhaug burial [nowp Gr].
- About 870 (local claim), 872 by tradition, the 880s by most scholars now: Harald wins at Hafrsfjord and takes the farm [av-hist; enwp Hf].
- About 931-933: Harald dies and is buried at Haugar on the sound [Hkr HH 45, **late**; av-har].
- 953 (Laing's margin says 950): Eric's sons land; battle on the heights among the old mounds, later called Blodeheia; Guthorm falls [Hkr HG 20, **late**; av-hak; enwp Av].
- 998, Easter: Olaf Tryggvason's feast; Eyvind Kelda and his crew drowned on Skrattasker [Hkr OT 70, **late**].
- About 998: the one-eyed guest [Hkr OT 71, **late**].
- 1022, late summer: Asbjorn's corn seized [Hkr SO 123, **late**].
- 1023, Thursday of Easter week (Laing: 18 April): Thorir Sel beheaded at the king's table; the Sunday after, Erling frees Asbjorn [Hkr SO 124-127, **late**].
- 1028: Erling killed off Bokn, south of the sound [repo norway].
- 1031: Olaf held a saint; his feast is 29 July [repo saints].
- After 1066: stone church about 1250; royal hall burned by Hanse men in 1368 [enwp Av; nowp Kg]. Out of the game's years.

## 6. Faith and custom

- **Heathen in 793.** The great are buried in ships under mounds beside the farm, with horse, weapons and gaming boards [av-sth]. The wax cake with a cross in the 779 grave is unexplained: not proof of a Christian [av-king].
- **Thor** wades the rivers Kormt and Ormt each day on his way to judgement at the ash; the first name is the island's [Grímnismál 29, through enwp Ks].
- **Christian by force from the 990s.** A bishop travels with the king; Easter is kept with a feast in 998 and 1023 [Hkr OT 70, SO 124, **late**]. To break the Easter peace, and to kill in the king's lodging, are named as death matters; a man who kills the king's servant must take up his service if the king wills [Hkr SO 125, 127, **late**].
- **Church law** for the west was set at Moster in 1024: no trade on Sundays and feasts, no meat on Fridays [repo norway].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **King Ogvald's cow.** He "made great sacrifices to a cow which he had with him wherever he went, and considered it good for his health to drink her milk"; he fell fighting a king Varin and lies under a mound by the house, "and there stands his stone over him, and close to it his cow also is laid" [Hkr OT 71, Laing 1844, **late**]. One mound is still called the cow mound [khm]. A later telling has Olaf open two mounds and find a man's bones in one and a cow's in the other (?) [av-olk says only that he opened mounds].
- **The guest with one eye**, in a broad hat, who "had something to tell of every land", kept the king up, and gave the cooks two fat pieces of beef. The king had the meat thrown out: "Odin shall not deceive us" [Hkr OT 71].
- **The blinded sorcerers** who walked "round and round in a circle upon the island" in their own mist [Hkr OT 70].
- **"When we took the sail from him he wept"**: the boast that cost Thorir Sel his head [Hkr SO 124].
- **The needle and doomsday**: the world ends when the stone touches the church wall. Priests are said to have chipped the top (?) [av-ch for the saying]. A legend of after the game.

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- The name of any lord here before Harald, and whether Harald's house was kin to the men in the mounds.
- That Harald "made it his chief seat about 870": a modern claim built on Snorri. His own poet does not name it.
- That any market, toll or mint was here. Do not write a toll at the strait as fact.
- What the hall of 793 looked like: only post holes.
- Whether there was a church before 1066. If used, wooden, after 995, and marked as saga.
- Do not let anyone know what is in Flaghaug, or call the stone Mary's, before the place is Christian.
- The Storhaug and Grønhaug men are not named kings in any text. "Sea-king" is Skre's term.
- I could not open Skre's volume (2018); its findings reach this page only through the museum's summary.
