# Traiectum (Utrecht): dossier

Port row [ports_793.json]: Traiectum, Utrecht; franks, nifterlake; town, riverbank, size 3. [n]: see section 9. (?): not checked.

## 1. The place in 793

- A church stronghold, not a market: a Roman stone fort, 126 x 152 m, tuff walls of about AD 210 patched in the 7th century, on a low levee on the Rhine's south bank [1,2]. Charles Martel gave it with its land to the Utrecht church in 723 [2,36].
- Bede: Pippin gave Willibrord "his famous castle, which in the ancient language of those people is called Wiltaburg, that is, the town of the Wilts" [13]. Traiectum means a crossing [5].
- The Frankish king rules; the fort is the bishop's, in the county of Nifterlake, which runs down the Vecht [29]. The see answers to Cologne [41]. The bishop in 793 is Hamacar (c. 790-806) [4].
- People: the bishop's household, clergy of two churches, a school, church servants, a few boatmen, fishers and craftsmen, levee farmers; 150-400 souls is our notes' guess (?) [1].
- Coming in from Dorestad: down the Kromme Rijn, past the burnt Roman ruin of Vechten, robbed for the churches' stone [1,36]. The river bends round the fort's north side; just beyond, the Vecht leaves north for the Almere at Muiden and the Oude Rijn runs west to the sea [1,30]. No bar, no jetty: boats are drawn up a sloping bank (a "stathe") west of the fort [1,3,11]. The landmark is a pale stone wall with two small church roofs, the land's only standing stone; a peat bog darkens the north-east [1]. Alcuin has a boat hauled up from the Rhine mouth "prelongo remulco", on a long tow-rope [12].

## 2. What stood there

- **The burcht.** Gates north, east and south proven by digs; early 5th-century graves west and east [2]. Balderik is said to have repaired it about 920 with gates, bastions and a west bridge; enlarged from the mid-11th century to about 175 x 200-225 m [2].
- **St Salvator's.** Willibrord's, founded 695-724: a tuff hall of 12 x 18 m with an east annex of 9 x 12 m, where limestone sarcophagi were dug up. Bishops Ricfried, Frederik and Odilbald lie here [7,18,21]. Rebuilt by Ansfried with a westwork and aisles; twenty canons in the 11th century [7].
- **St Martin's.** Dagobert's chapel of about 630, razed by Frisians, rebuilt by Willibrord; the cathedral from about 750 [1,6]. Destroyed 857; rebuilt after about 920; burned 1017; Adelbold's cathedral consecrated 1023 before Henry II and twelve bishops [6].
- **Holy Cross chapel.** Under 20 m: hall, narrow choir, two arms; dug in the 1930s and 1993; outlined in the Domplein paving [8].
- **Monastery and school** inside the walls, of timber (?) [1,15].
- **The strand (later Stathe).** Carolingian pottery of 750-1000; over it a yellow flood clay and no occupation from about 850 to after 900 [3]. The merchants' Buurkerk is 10th or early 11th century [3,27].
- **Vecht-side port** (Waterstraat): 420 m of revetments, timber houses and ships, 10th-12th century, dug 1974-81 [1].
- **Lofen**, the emperor's palace: tuff, a row of sandstone columns, 35.7 x 16.75 m; built about 1020 or in 1040 [2,9,10].
- **Bernold's churches:** St Peter's (1039-1048; columns each one block of red sandstone; his sarcophagus found in the crypt in 1952), St John's (after 1040), St Paul's abbey (1050) [27].
- **Abstede**: a second landing, date unknown [1].

## 3. Who could be met

Bishops [4]: Hamacar c. 790-806; Ricfried 806-815/816; Frederik 815/816-835/837; Alberik II 838-844; Liudger c. 848-854; Hunger c. 854-866; Odilbald 867/879-898 and Radboud 899-917, both living at Deventer [21,22]; Balderik 918-975; Folcmar 976-990; Boudewijn 990-995; Ansfried 995-1010; Adelbold II 1010-1026; Bernold 1027-1054; Willem 1054-1076.

- **Hadda**, prior about 780 [12]; alive in 793 (?).
- **Liudger** the missionary: born c. 742 at Zuilen on the Vecht, schooled here 756-767, then at York under Alcuin until Angles and Frisians fell out in 772; wrote Gregory's Life; bishop of Münster 805, died 809 [32].
- Remembered in 793: **Gregory**, abbot from c. 750, never bishop, died c. 775 [15]; his nephew **Alberic I** (777-784), Alcuin's friend [16]; **Theodard** (c. 784-790) [17].
- **Odulf**, canon, sent to Stavoren by Frederik; died here after 854 [20].
- **Rorik** the Dane, lord of Dorestad from 850 [19].
- **Count Ricfried** of the Betuwe, Balderik's father, said to have driven out the Northmen [23]; **Waldger and Radbod**, counts of Nifterlake; Radbod rebelled in 939 [29].
- **Bruno**, Henry I's son, schooled by Balderik from the age of four; later archbishop of Cologne [23].
- Ordinary people: clerks, schoolboys, canons, boatmen; the merchants below the burcht in 1007 [2,3]. No moneyer, reeve or craftsman is named.

## 4. What was traded

- In 793: no market, toll or mint. The passing trade is Dorestad's: north by the Vecht to the Almere and Frisia, west by the Oude Rijn [1].
- The church buys wax, wine, oil, incense, books and Lenten fish (guess) [40]. Alcuin: Hadda serves honey, porridge and butter, "utpute non oleum nec vinum Fresia fundit" (Frisia yields no oil or wine) [12].
- Royal gifts: 723 the fort and Vechten; 777 Leusden, four woods in Eemland, the Upkirika at Dorestad [28,31]; a tenth of the king's income here, confirmed 753 (?).
- Otto I gave the mint and Muiden with its toll in 953 [23,30] (mint 936 by [11]); in 975 the toll could move to Utrecht (?) [30]. Goods then had to be sold here [11].
- Coins: bishops' pennies from Bernold on (?) [39]; TRIECTO pennies are Maastricht's (?).
- About 1000-1122: grain, wine, building stone, amber, glass, pottery in; herring out [11].
- Utrecht 1: a hollowed oak built up with planks, 17.8 x 3.8 m, 13 tons at 0.7 m draught, 997-1027 [33] (our ship table: after 1019).

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 834 A Danish fleet comes by Utrecht to plunder Dorestad (Annals of St Bertin) [31].
- 835-838 Frederik stabbed by two men after Mass; later writers blame Empress Judith, or men of Walcheren [18].
- 850 Rorik takes Dorestad; Utrecht too by one account (?) [19].
- 857 Danes sack Utrecht while Rorik is away; churches destroyed; Hunger flees [6,19,42].
- 858 Lothar II gives the clergy St Peter's at Sint-Odiliënberg; later they sit at Deventer [42].
- 863 Northmen take Utrecht and Nijmegen and winter there [34].
- c. 920 Balderik brings the see home; rebuilds churches, school, walls [2,23].
- 953 Muiden, its toll, the mint [23,30].
- 1007 Northmen come, a year after burning Tiel. The merchants burn their own quarter ("portum omnem ipsi incenderunt", Alpertus) and go into the fort; no attack follows [2,3]. Our chronicle: 1006.
- 1017 Fire takes cathedral and bishop's house [2,6].
- 1018 Adelbold beaten at Vlaardingen [25].
- 1023 New cathedral consecrated [6].
- 1024, 1026 Adelbold count of Drenthe, Teisterbant [25,28].
- 1039 Conrad II dies here at Pentecost, 4 June; his entrails ("exta" on the choir stone) stay in the cathedral [26].
- 1040 Henry III gives Groningen to St Martin's [28].
- 1054 Bernold dies, 19 July; buried in St Peter's [35].
- 1064 Willem count of West Frisia [28].

## 6. Faith and custom

- St Martin, 11 November: goose, new wine, then a forty-day fast to Christmas [37].
- House saints: Willibrord 7 November [43]; Gregory 25 August [15]; Alberic 14 November [16]; Frederik 18 July [18]; Odulf 12 June [20]; Radboud 29 November [22]; Bernold 19 July [35].
- Relics: Balderik brought Agnes and Pontian to St Martin's, Odulf to St Salvator's [23].
- The old gods: a baptismal vow of 776-800, perhaps from the Utrecht mission, forswears "Thunaer ende Uoden ende Saxnote ende allum them unholdum" [44] (in texts_793.json). Alberic sent Liudger to pull down Frisian shrines; Charlemagne took two thirds of the treasure found [38].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- Alcuin (by 782) greets Alberic as "vaccipotens praesul", bishop mighty in cows; after Hadda's porridge, "fugiens Dorstada relinque": flee Dorestad, where "niger Hrotberct", a "mercator avarus", dislikes poems [12].
- 754: Boniface's body came by boat over the Aelmere. The count read King Pippin's ban on moving it; the church bell rang untouched, and it went to Mainz [14].
- Liudger's mother Liafburg: her heathen grandmother had a slave drown the baby; a neighbour put honey in her mouth, and a child that had eaten could not be killed (Altfrid) (?).
- A 6th-century Livy in Vienna has an 8th-century note: "Theutbert, bishop of Dorestad" [17].
- Ansfried, once the emperor's sword-bearer, went blind about 1005. When he died (3 May 1010) Utrecht men carried off his body while the Heiligenberg folk fought a fire "not by chance" [24].
- 1034: raiders stole Odulf's relics from Stavoren; Bishop Ælfweard bought them in London for Evesham [20].
- Radboud wrote a poem on the swallow [22]; Adelbold, a letter to Gerbert on the volume of a sphere [25].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Hamacar is only a name. Our c800 notes make Ricfried bishop in 793: wrong.
- Whether the Holy Cross chapel is Willibrord's St Martin's or 10th-century [8]; where the Vecht left the Rhine [1]; whether Rorik held Utrecht; whether Hunger fled in 850 or 857 [19,42].
- Sources disagree: mint 936 or 953; Lofen 1020 or 1040; cathedral consecrated 26 July [6] or 26 June [27].
- Do not give Utrecht in 793 a market, mint, toll, jetty, fair or named layman.
- Not here: Willibrord's grave (Echternach) [43]; the Utrecht Psalter (came 1716) [45]; the Dom tower; Bernlef (Helwerd) [32]; St Martin's hammer (13th-century mount) [46]; Liudger himself in 793 (?).
- No written source ties the cross of churches to Conrad's death [27].
- Not checked: the Lex Frisionum at Utrecht; the 753 tithe; the 975 charter; the coins; Liafburg in Altfrid's words.
- Quote [12], [14] only in Latin or paraphrase; check [13]'s translator.
