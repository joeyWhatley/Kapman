# Herio (Noirmoutier): dossier

Keys in brackets: see Sources. (?) = not checked on a page I opened. Latin renderings are mine. V, E, F: Poupardin's chapter numbers (E I.81 = Miracles, book I, c. 81).

## 1. The place in 793

- **Name.** *Herus*, *Hero*, *Herio insula* [V 21; E]. "Herio monasterium" became Hermoutier, then Noirmoutier; "black monastery" is a later folk reading [P; WI].
- **What.** An island monastery founded about 675 by Philibert, exiled abbot of Jumièges, and endowed on 1 July 677 by Ansoald, bishop of Poitiers [V 21; P]. It keeps the founder's body in a marble coffin [E I.2; P].
- **Rule.** The Frankish king's; Louis, Charlemagne's son, is king of Aquitaine from 781 [ROL]. The island goes with the county of Herbauge in Poitou [D1; AE; WC]; bishop: Poitiers (?). The abbot of 793 is not known.
- **People.** Monks; the abbey's dependants (*familia*); islanders (*insulani*) with a lord of their own, so the monks did not own the whole island [E I.1; D3; Ca3]. Romance speakers [ROL]. No numbers survive [Ca1; Ca3].
- **Land and water.** Not today's island: two rocky islets joined by a sand bar and lagoon. The northern block, over 6 km wide, carried the monastery, probably on its south side where the town stands [Ca3; WI]. Most salt marsh and polder is post-1600 [WI]. Low land (highest point 23 m); rock shore on the north; the wooded head of La Chaize on the east, about 15 m high; flats and reefs west and south that dry at low water; the islet of Le Pilier off the north-west tip [WI]. East lies the Bay of Bourgneuf, about 27 km long, Bouin an island in it; the strait of Fromentine closes the south [WB; WI].
- **Tides.** The king's council of 836: because of the ebbs (*ledones*) "our men" cannot always reach the island; the Northmen can whenever the sea is calm [E I.1]. The monastery's channel (*alveus*) dries at the ebb [V 28].
- **Where boats lie.** "Our island's port called *Conca*", where ships anchor and wait for a wind: La Conche, on the north side [E I.81, II.9; P]. The monastery's west gate leads to it [E II.11]. The monks' mainland port is *Furca* (not located; near Ampan, by Beauvoir), a fast run before a north-west wind [E I.2; P; Ca3].

## 2. What stood there

- **The monastery.** Dedicated in 677 to the apostles Peter, Andrew and Paul and the martyrs Lawrence and Florent [Ca3]. The Life shows a cloister, a kitchen, a cellarer's store, the abbot's table, guests [V 26-27].
- **Two churches.** The basilica with the tomb, three lamps burning day and night at its head; and "the other church", near enough to fetch a light from [E II.8].
- **The tomb.** Dug out on 7 June 836 and shipped with its marble coffin, still shown at Saint-Philbert-de-Grand-Lieu [E I.2; P]. A crypt under the present church marks the place: "Merovingian" to some, eleventh-century to others; I found no published dig [WA; WI; WE; POP].
- **The fort (not there in 793).** Abbot Hilbod's *castrum*, built between 824 and 830 "around the monastery", guarded by the abbey's own men; a strong citadel with a rampart, says Falco [D3; E I.1; F 18]. Monastery and fort shared a west gate [E II.11]. The later castle is held to stand on it; its keep is late eleventh century (state record) or twelfth [WC; WI].
- **Le Vieil** (north shore). A large Gallo-Roman site reused as a Merovingian burial ground, dug from the late nineteenth century to the 1970s, with a chapel of St Hilary over the graves (undated) [Ca3]. Merovingian stone coffins are shown in the castle keep [WI].
- **Older things.** A dolmen on the shore at L'Herbaudière [WI]; St Philibert's cave in the wood of La Chaize, tradition only [PS].
- **Salt pans.** On the island only supposed. The attested pans are the abbey's on the mainland: at Ampan from 677, at Scobrit (given 839) [Ca3; P].

## 3. Who could be met

- **Atto**, bishop of Saintes (from 799 (?)): given the monastery by Charlemagne, restored the rule before 814; probably abbot [D3; P; Ca2].
- **Arnulf**, abbot by 819, died 824; close to Louis the Pious and Benedict of Aniane; built Déas [D1; F 18; Ca2].
- **Hilbod**, abbot from 824/825, still in February 856; built the fort, moved the saint [AE; P].
- **Ermentarius**, monk, eyewitness of 836, author of the Miracles (book I about 838-840, book II after 862); later abbot, off the island [P].
- **Adalard of Corbie**, Charlemagne's cousin, exiled here 814-821 [Ca2; P] (English Wikipedia: 817-824 [EA]).
- **Wala**, his brother, held here for a time after 830 (year (?)) [P; EW].
- **Rainald (Renaud)**, count of Herbauge by 835, of Nantes 841-843; fought the Northmen on the island, 20 August 835; killed by Lambert and the Bretons, 24 May 843 [AE; E II.11; AB; WR].
- Off-stage: **Louis the Pious**; **Pippin I** of Aquitaine (died 838), whose assembly agreed the move; **Hilduin of Saint-Denis**, to whom book I was sent [E I.1; P].

Ordinary people in the texts: monks, a church-keeper, a cellarer [V 27; E II.8]; the abbey's armed men; horsemen in 835 [D3; E II.11]; islanders and their lord [E I.1]; Breton shipmen with a steersman (*gubernator*) and a bow lookout (*proreta*) [E I.81]. On the mainland: a wine-seller, a Loire ferryman who wants his fare, the saint's servants Dominicus and Aigrada [E I.3-4, 71, 77]. No islander's name survives.

## 4. What was traded

- **Salt out.** About 838-840 Breton ships (yards, sails, anchors) came to Furca "to trade": they had brought grain (*annona*) and carried away "only salt" [E I.81]. The one clear text for the salt trade here.
- **Lead.** The monastery's lead, taken off the island for fear of the Northmen, lay at Furca. A Breton crew took some, "partly by theft, partly by buying on the quiet"; it came back as sheets and was recast in lumps [E I.81].
- **In, in Philibert's day.** A ship from Bordeaux with forty measures (*modii*) of lamp oil; an Irish ship "full of various wares" that supplied shoes and clothing (Cartron: an echo of the Life of Columbanus); windbound Breton ships given two beasts, flour and bread [V 27, 29; Ca3].
- **From the sea.** A stranded whale-like fish (*musculus*) gave thirty measures of lamp fat; 237 porpoises fed the house through a famine year [V 27-28].
- **The abbey's boats.** Six, toll-free on the Loire, Allier, Cher and Dordogne: Louis the Pious's grant (lost), confirmed by Pippin I, 18 May 826 [D2].
- **Dues.** From 2 August 830 the abbey has the emperor's protection, elects its abbot, and owes the royal chamber six pounds of silver a year, nothing more [D3; Ca2].
- **Market, coin, price.** At Déas a market (*nundinae*) was held outside the monastery's outer gate. Pennies and halfpennies passed there; about 838 a *sextarius* of wine cost one penny, "unless I am mistaken" [E I.71].
- **Seasons.** Raiders come in summer, "when the weather smiles on sailing"; from early spring to the end of autumn the monks are at Déas, their mainland refuge [E I.1; D3]. Salt cheapest in autumn is the project's table, not a text [GDS; RTS].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- **799** Alcuin: pagan ships did much harm among the islands off Aquitaine; about 105 raiders killed on the shore. No island named [CHR].
- **before 814** Atto restores the monastery [D3].
- **814-821** Adalard in exile here [Ca2].
- **819, 16 March** Charter for Déas, built as a refuge: the island house is "often laid waste by barbarians" [D1].
- **820** Thirteen ships sack Bouin, across the bay [CHR; P].
- **824/825** Arnulf dies; Hilbod abbot [F 18; AE].
- **830** June: the island "left by the general body of monks" for the summer. 2 August: the fort diploma [AE; D3].
- **835, 20 August** (the saint's feast). Nine ships; fighting from the ninth hour to evening; 484 Northmen said killed, one defender, many horses [E II.11; AE].
- **836, 7-11 June** The body goes by ship to Furca, then on men's shoulders by Ampan, Varenne and Paulx to Déas [E I.2; P].
- **c. 838-840** Breton salt ships call; islanders still live here [E I.81].
- **843** After sacking Nantes (24 June) the Northmen come to Herio to share the loot and kill each other over it; captives slip away "when the sea drew back", one with Nantes's great Bible on his neck (an eleventh-century telling) [CN]. That winter they settle on "a certain island", bringing houses from the mainland [AB].
- **846, July** Herio burned by Northmen [AE].
- **847-875** Déas burned (30 March 847); the saint goes on to Cunault (858), Messay (862), Tournus (14 May 875) [AE; P].
- **after 846** Falco: the island was never wholly without a few monks [F 22].
- **c. 1000** A priory of Tournus [WA; PS].
- **late 11th century** Church rebuilt over the tomb with the lords of La Garnache; the keep [PS; WC].

## 6. Faith and custom

- The Rule of St Benedict under Hilbod [E I.1].
- **Feasts.** St Philibert, 20 August, kept through the night before (so at Corbie, 835) [E II.11]; the moving, 7 June [E I.2].
- **At the tomb.** Three lamps always lit [E II.8]. The sick touch the coffin, its carrying frame (*scala*) or its cloth, and drink wine in which the saint's staff was washed [E I.2; P].
- **Women.** At Déas none had entered since monks lived there; in 836 they were admitted for one year, feast to feast; afterwards a cross outside the gate marked how near they might come [E I.29, 71]. The island: the same (?).
- **The fear.** That Northmen would dig up the tomb and scatter the bones or throw them in the sea [E I.1].
- **Charity.** Food for windbound strangers; Philibert "ransomer of captives" [V 29-30].
- Old gods: no evidence.

## 7. Stories and oddities

- Breton sailors stole a bullock; the sea rose until they found hide and meat aboard and took it back [V 28].
- The lead thieves, driven to Batz, saw a horseman with three spears charge them over the sea; a cripple there, cured in a dream, was sent to tell them why they had no wind [E I.81].
- A Breton fleet landed at Conca to plunder and killed each other; one man lived to tell the islanders [E II.9].
- A Saracen ship "like a wall", coming from Yeu, turned back: the shore was so thick with birds that they took them for an army [E II.10].
- A monk at Corbie dreamed the fight of 835 the night before: the saint in white with a golden sword, going out by the west gate [E II.11].
- The tavern story: given a full measure and his change by mistake, a man came back to spend the halfpenny, swore by St Philibert it was his own, and threw up the wine [E I.71].
- The filbert nut is named for the saint; it ripens about his feast [EP].
- A tenth-century Arabic notice, read by its editor as Noirmoutier: an island twenty miles by three, sweet wells, no snakes, fine saffron [WI] (?).

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- That the raid of 799 struck Herio: Alcuin names no island [CHR; Ca1]. (The port row says "raided in 799".)
- Salt pans on the island in 793, or that Philibert taught salt-making and built dykes: supposed, not attested [Ca3].
- That Saracens destroyed the abbey in 732: only the undated ship story stands behind it [P].
- A plundering in 835: the texts give a fight the defenders won; Cartron doubts even its date. The Annals of St-Bertin, cited for it in the project chronicle, do not mention it [E II.11; Ca1; AB].
- That the winter island of 843 was Herio, or that Northmen kept a lasting base here (English Wikipedia: from 824; Hastein, 862-882): "possible", says Cartron; the abbey's papers are silent [AB; Ca1; EN].
- Walking across at low water: Merlet read the captives' escape as the Gois ford; geographers say the sandbank became passable on foot only in the eighteenth century [CN; WG].
- The abbot in 793; the look of any building; the fort's plan; the crypt's date.
- Any mint, fair, toll-house or market on the island: none is recorded. The dates 801 and 804 for the rebuilding are unsourced [WA; WI].
- Ermentarius's well-known lament on the growing number of ships is about the 840s to 860s [E II, prologue].
