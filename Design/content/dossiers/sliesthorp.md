# Sliesthorp (Hedeby, Haithabu): dossier

Keys in brackets are listed under Sources. (?) marks what I could not check on a page I opened. Renderings of the Latin and German are mine. No web search was available.

## 1. The place in 793

- **Name.** *Sliesthorp*, "the village on the Schlei", in the Frankish annals (804, 808) [ARF; WDE]; *Sliaswic*, *Sliaswich* in Rimbert [RIM 24, 31]; *æt Hæthum*, "at the heaths", in English about 890 [OHT]; Norse *Heiðabýr*; Adam of Bremen uses both *Sliaswich* and *Heidiba* [WDE]. All mean the place on the Haddeby Noor, not the later town of Schleswig across the water [WDE].
- **What.** In 793 a young trading place "on a green field, without a town's fittings" [WDE], begun about 770 [WDE]; the oldest houses lie just south of the later town and are of the 700s [WDA]. It is not yet in any text. It grows fast only after 808 [ARF; WDE].
- **Rule.** The king of the Danes. Godfred is the first king named here (804) [ARF]. Sigfred is the Danes' king in the Frankish annals for 777, 782 and 798 (?); nothing ties him to this place. That Frisian merchants founded it is "probable" only [WDE].
- **Size.** Unknown for 793. The later walled town is 24 to 26 ha [WDA; WDE]; at its height in the 900s it has at least 1,500 people [WDE], "up to 2,000" [HD].
- **Land and water.** It stands at the narrowest neck of Jutland, where the north-south army road (Ox Road) passes the head of the Schlei [WDE; WDA]. A ship enters the Schlei from the Baltic and sails about 40 km inland [WDE]. The Schlei today is 42 km long, on average 1.3 km wide and 3 m deep, brackish at the head; near the head come the Great Broad (to 4.2 km wide), the 280 m narrows at Stexwig, then the Little Broad [WSCH]. Across the narrows off Reesholm stands a timber sea barrier of log caissons built about 737-740; how ships were let through is not known [WSP]. Two km north of the barrier lies Füsing, a garrison and fleet station of about 700 to 1000 [WFU]. The Haddeby Noor opens off the south shore of the inner Schlei; the town lies on its west shore, where the Hedeby brook runs out [WNO; WDA]. The Noor is now about 1 km², at most 5.2 m deep, and was open to the Schlei until a road dam of about 1882 [WNO].
- **Landmarks.** The Danevirke: the Main Wall, the North Wall (felled 737) and the East Wall are standing in 793 [WDW]. **Not** the great half-ring rampart: that comes after 930 [WDE].
- **Where boats lie.** In the Noor before the brook mouth. The harbour basin is 11 ha [WDE]; later it has a pile barrier and jetties up to 60 m long [WDA]. Dates of the first jetties were not opened (?): the game's jetty in 793 is an assumption.

## 2. What stood there

- **The southern settlement** (700s), south of the later rampart; dug from 1959 [WDA; WDE]. A **northern settlement** and a **middle one on the brook** follow in the 800s; north and south are given up late in that century and the middle goes on [WDE].
- **Houses**: timber and wattle, reed or straw roofs, 3.5 x 17 m to 7 x 17.5 m, short-lived on wet ground and often rebuilt [WDE]. Plank-paved streets run parallel and square to the brook [WDA], the east-west ones down to the jetties [WEN]. Pit-houses in the north-west were workshops [WDA]. A well is dated 1020 by its timber [WEN].
- **Harbour**: palisade, jetties, store buildings [WDE; WDA]. Wrecks: **Hedeby 1**, a royal-standard longship built about 985, 30.9 m by 2.7 m, the narrowest known, burnt to the waterline as a fire ship (between 990 and 1010 [WH1E]; "1050" [WH1D]: the pages disagree); **wreck 2**, fragments; **wreck 3**, a large cargo ship [WH1D].- **Workshops**: over 340,000 pieces of comb-makers' waste; leather tools; iron-working; goldsmithing, with mercury from fire-gilding; a glass furnace of 850-900; a domed oven on a burnt pit-house, perhaps for beads; 7,700 beads of carnelian, rock crystal, amber, jet, silver and glass; spindle whorls, loom weights and bone needles, for cloth from sailcloth to fine worsted [WEN; WDE].
- **The mint**: the first coins of the North, from about 825 [WEN], copying Dorestad's Frankish pennies; steady striking through the 900s [WDM].
- **Ansgar's church**, about 849-850, with a house for its priest; never found in the ground [RIM 24; WDE]. A fragment of an early bell; a whole bronze bell of about 950, found in the harbour in 1978, the oldest whole ringing bell in northern Europe [WGL].
- **The half-ring rampart**, after 930: about 1,300 m, a turf wall with a ditch, at first 2 m high, at last about 7 m (9 to 11 m today); gates north and south; it ran on into the water as a palisade [WDW; WDE; WDA]. **The Connection Wall** to the Danevirke, 3.5 km, about 968 [WDE].
- **Graves**: nearly 1,350 burials dug between 1812 and 1970 [ZG]: Danish cremation pits, chamber graves, Saxon and Slav urns, Christian earth graves [WDE]; ten chamber graves together [WEN]. **The boat chamber grave** south of the rampart (found 1908, about 900): a chamber 3.7 x 2.4 m with three men, swords, shields, spurs, a bronze bowl, a glass beaker, three horses in a pit beside it, and a boat some 16 m long set keel-down over the top. No other grave like it is known [WBK].
- **Four runestones** (section 3) and the mound pair *Twebargen* on the old road [WRS].
- **Godfred's wall of 808**, with "one gate only, through which wagons and horsemen could be sent out and let in" [ARF 808].
- **Hollingstedt on the Treene**, the North Sea landing: 16 to 18 km west by ox-cart along the north side of the walls; the tide reached it until 1570 [WDE; WHO].
- No hall, thing-place or toll-house has been found in what I opened.

## 3. Who could be met

- **Godfred**, king. In 804 he lay here "with his fleet and all the horsemen of his kingdom"; in 808 he came by ship with Reric's merchants and stayed some days; killed by one of his own followers in 810 [ARF].
- **Hemming**, his brother's son, king 810-812; his brothers **Hancwin and Angandeo** and the nobles **Osfrid Turdimulo, Warstein, Suomi, Urm, Osfrid son of Heiligen, Osfrid of Sconaowe, Hebbi, Aowin** swore the peace on the Eider in 811 [ARF 811].
- **Gluomi**, "warden of the Northmen's border", 817 [ARF 817].
- **Harald (Klak)**, king off and on from 812, driven out for good in 827 [ARF]; baptised among the Franks, he brought Ansgar north in 826 [RIM 7].
- **Horik the elder**, Godfred's son, sole king from about 828, killed 854 [WHK; RIM 31]. **Horik the younger**, a boy king after him [RIM 31-32].
- **Ansgar**, archbishop of Hamburg-Bremen, and his unnamed priest here [RIM 24].
- **Hovi**, count (*comes*) of the *vicus* of Sliaswich, who had the church shut and was then driven out [RIM 31-32]. **Burghard**, kinsman and counsellor of both Horiks [RIM 32].
- **Ragenbert**, a priest robbed and killed by Danish robbers on the road to the port, on the Assumption; **Rimbert**, a priest of Danish blood, who sailed from it to the Svear [RIM 33].
- **Sigfred and Halfdan**, kings, 873 [WSH].
- **Ohthere** of Hålogaland and **Wulfstan**, seamen, about 890 [OHT].
- **Olaf** from the Svear's land and his sons **Chnob (Gnupa) and Gyrd**, kings in the early 900s (Adam of Bremen, at second hand) [WRS; WGG]. **Asfrid, Odinkar's daughter**, Gnupa's queen; **King Sigtrygg**, their son; **Gorm**, who cut the runes [WRS].
- **Hored**, first bishop of "Sliaswig", 948 to 972; whether he ever came is unknown. Then Folkbert, Marco, Poppo, Ezico, Rudolf, Ratolf [WHR; WBL].
- **Ibrahim ibn Yaqub of Tortosa**, traveller, 960s [WEN; WIB].
- **King Svein**; **Skarthi**, his retainer; **Erik**, a steersman; **Thorolf**, Svein's retainer and Erik's partner, about 1000 [WRS].
- **Harald Hardrada** of Norway, about 1050 [WEN].

Ordinary people: comb-makers, bead-makers, glass-workers, smiths, bronze-casters, goldsmiths, leather-workers, weavers, carpenters, potters, boat-builders [WEN; WDA; HD]; ox-carters on the Hollingstedt road [WDE]; slaves in iron fetters [WDE]; baptised merchants "among the chief people of the place"; many who took the sign of the cross to enter the church and put off baptism until their deathbed [RIM 24]. No moneyer or toll-man is named.

## 4. What was traded

- **The crossing.** Baltic goods come up the Schlei, go by cart to Hollingstedt, and by the Treene and Eider to the North Sea, sparing the voyage round Jutland [WDE; WHO].
- **Goods.** From the North mostly raw stuffs; from far off, costly things: goods of Norway, Sweden, Ireland, the east Baltic, Constantinople, Baghdad and Francia; Arab silver and silk [WDE; WEN]. Cloth, pottery, salt, spiced wine [WDA]; Rhenish tuff landed at Hollingstedt, later [WHO]. Slaves [WDE]. Country people sold grain into the town [WDE]. Made here: combs, beads, jewellery, cloth [WEN].
- **Who came.** Reric's merchants, moved by force in 808; Reric had "brought the kingdom great profit in tolls" [ARF 808]. Traders of Dorestad and Hamburg, freely once the church stood [RIM 24]. Men of Saxony under the peace of 873 [WSH]. Adam of Bremen calls it a sea-port sending ships to Sweden and Greece [WSCH].
- **Sailings.** Sciringes heal to here in five days; here to Truso in seven days and nights, under sail all the way [OHT].
- **Coin.** None in 793. Own pennies from about 825 [WEN; WDM]. Weights and cut silver: not opened (?).
- **Tolls, laws, fairs.** No rate, law or fair is recorded in what I opened.

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 804: Godfred, with fleet and horse, at Sliesthorp "on the border of his kingdom and Saxony"; he will not meet Charles [ARF].
- 808: Reric destroyed, its merchants brought here; the wall ordered [ARF].
- 809: Thrasco of the Obodrites killed at Reric by Godfred's men [ARF].
- 810: 200 ships harry Frisia; Godfred murdered [ARF].
- 811: peace sworn on the Eider, twelve men a side [ARF].
- 815: a Saxon and Obodrite army crosses the Eider for Harald [ARF].
- about 825: first coins [WEN].
- 826: Harald baptised; Ansgar comes north [RIM 7].
- about 849-850: Horik allows the church [RIM 24; WDE].
- 854: Horik killed; Hovi shuts the church; soon it reopens, with a bell [RIM 31-32].
- 873: peace with Louis the German; merchants may cross the border [WSH].
- about 890: Ohthere's and Wulfstan's voyages told to Alfred [OHT].
- about 900: Olaf's house rules [WDE; WRS].
- 934: Henry I beats Chnuba, makes him pay tribute and take baptism (Widukind) [WRS].
- after 930: the rampart [WDE].
- 948: the bishopric [WHR].
- 960s: Ibrahim's visit ("965" [WEN]; he travelled 961-962 [WIB]).
- about 968: the Connection Wall [WDE].
- 974: Otto II takes the border; 983: the Danes take it back [WDE; WDA].
- about 985: Hedeby 1 built [WH1E].
- about 1000: a siege; Skarthi and Erik fall [WRS].
- about 1050: Harald Hardrada burns the town with fire ships [WEN].
- 1066: West Slavs sack and burn it; the people move over the Schlei to Schleswig [WDE].

## 6. Faith and custom

- Heathen in 793. Ibrahim: the people worship "Sirius", but for a few Christians with a church; a man who sacrifices sets poles at his yard gate and hangs the beast on them, ox, ram, goat or pig, so the neighbours know [WEN].
- The heathen held a church bell unlawful [RIM 32]. Hovi's party said the gods were angry at "another and an unknown god" [RIM 31].
- Burial is mixed (section 2). Horses, weapons and a boat for great men about 900 [WBK]. Svein's time still raises mounds [WRS].
- The church's dedication is not in what I opened: name no saint.
- Ibrahim: women have the right to divorce; infants are cast into the sea "for economy"; both sexes paint their eyes [WEN].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- Ibrahim: fish is the main food; and "never did I hear singing fouler than that of these people: a rumbling from their throats, like a dog's but more bestial" [WEN].
- Ohthere: these were the lands where "the English lived before they came hither" [OHT].
- The big Sigtrygg stone is cut in Swedish runes on a rock that already bore older marks; it lay broken at the ford between the two Noors [WRS].
- "King Svein set this stone for Skarthi, his retainer, who had fared west and now fell at Hedeby." "Thorolf, Svein's retainer, raised this stone for his partner Erik, who met death when warriors sat round Hedeby; he was a steersman, a well-born warrior" [WRS].
- Harald's poet: "All Hedeby was burned from end to end in anger... I stood on the stronghold's arm before dawn; high flame burst from the houses" [WEN; WDE].
- 810: while Charles waited on Godfred's threats, his elephant, the caliph's gift, died [ARF].
- Once the town was gone the people called the empty ring *Oldenburg* [WDE].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- 793 is before every text. No named person, king's man, toll or law can be placed here that year.
- No rampart, church, bell, coin, bishop or jetty (?) in 793. No town wall before about 930.
- Do not put Sigfred at Sliesthorp; do not say Godfred founded it.
- "Swedish" kings rest on Adam and the stones; who drove them out, and when, is unknown [WRS]. The 934 king is Chnuba in Widukind; the German page's "Knut I" and "Gorm retook it in 945" are unsupported there.
- The siege on the stones has no year. The Hedeby 1 fire is dated two ways.
- Do not call it Schleswig before 1066, or use "Viking" as the people's word.
- Not opened: Adam of Bremen, Widukind and the Annals of Fulda themselves; Kalmring on the harbour; the coin types; weights; the hillfort north of the town; the Viking Age mouth of the Schlei.
- The game's own `research_trade_seasons_and_hints.md` gives Hedeby 3 as 16 m and about 60 t: I could not confirm either figure.
