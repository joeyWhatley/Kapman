# Gipeswic (Ipswich): dossier

Codes in brackets: section 9. (?) = unchecked.

## 1. The place in 793

The East Angles' one trading town, at the head of the Orwell estuary where the Gipping meets the tide [SH,WO]. King: Æthelberht, under Offa of Mercia, who has him beheaded in 794 and takes the kingdom [K,C]. The town served "East Anglia (and Mercia during the time of Mercian supremacy)" and traded "mainly with Dorestad" [SH]. It began in the 7th century as 15 ha (elsewhere: 20-30 ha) north of the river crossing; about 720 it was laid out anew on a grid of streets over the old heath graveyard and across the river into Stoke: about 50 ha, no wall [SH,A]. Craftsmen; farmers on the east edge, in fenced yards; probably foreign traders in residence, to judge by their pots [SH]. No head-count exists.

Coming in: Orwell and Stour share one mouth at Shotley Point [WO]. North of it, on a cliff 30 m above the sea, stands the Roman shore fort of Walton, flint and brick with round corner towers (since lost to the sea) [Wc]. Then some 18 km (?) of estuary on the flood: wooded sides, salt marsh, mud flats that dry at low water; springs range about 3.6 m [N]. The town lies on the north bank at a bend, by the ford just east of today's Stoke Bridge [WP]. No quay: a bank faced with posts and wattle hurdles, "little more than a bank protection", where shallow boats take the ground [SH].

## 2. What stood there

- Waterfront (Bridge Street, dug 1981): post-and-wattle revetments from the 7th century on, each further out over the mud. Leather survives only here [SH,A].
- The ford; by 970 "the bridge", and a hythe on the Stoke bank [S781,WP].
- The grid of c. 720 (St Stephen's Lane, dug 1987-88): two metalled roads; a row of timber houses hard on the street; in the yards weaving, bone, antler and metal work, antler brooch moulds, carbonised bread, basketry [SH,A].
- Potters' quarter, north-east corner: its sherds along 160 m of Carr Street; kilns at Cox Lane (dug 1958); single kilns at the Buttermarket (c. 800-850) and Stoke Quay. From c. 850 Thetford-ware kilns: Carr Street, St Helen's Street, Turret Lane [SH,A,Al,Iw].
- Wells: 87 known, shallow, many lined with old wine casks; one cask's oak matches the Mainz area [SH,A].
- Old graveyards, built over c. 720. Buttermarket: 71 graves, c. 610/635-680/690; coffins, two like small boats, small barrows, swords, glass cups, three men with north French or Belgian belt sets. Stoke Quay: 20 graves, seven under barrows [SH,A].
- Churches: none proven by digging before the 11th century. St Peter's by the ford "has been suggested as the Minster"; St Mildred's on the Cornhill "may have been a royal chapel". Christian graveyards of the 8th-9th century: Elm Street, Philip Road (Stoke) [SH].
- The Cornhill, perhaps an unsurfaced market; Thingstead, the green by the north gate, a meeting place by its name [Al].
- Bank and ditch, first raised c. 900-917: at Shire Hall Yard at least 5 m wide, 2 m deep; streets closed; gates west, north and east [SH,A].
- 10th-11th century houses: sunken-floored, growing to two storeys over a cellar; several burnt with their contents after 1066 [SH,A].
- Churches by 1086: Holy Trinity, two St Mary's, St Augustine (Stoke; dug, 11th-century), St Michael, St Lawrence, St Peter, St Stephen, St George, St Julian; Ely's church and mill at Stoke [D,SM].
- Stoke's bounds, 970: "hageneforda brygge" (Handford Bridge (?)), a marsh mill, a holy well, "pottaforda" [S781].

## 3. Who could be met

Rulers of the kingdom (none recorded at Gipeswic):

- Æthelberht (d. 794); Offa (d. 796); Eadwald, c. 796-798, known only from coins; Coenwulf of Mercia, coining in East Anglia by c. 805 [K].
- Æthelstan (c. 827-840s), Æthelweard (d. 854), Edmund (855-869): known mostly from coins [K].
- Guthrum, baptised Æthelstan, settles 879-880, d. 890; Eohric, killed 902 [K,SH].
- Ealdorman Æthelwine (962-992); Ulfcytel "snilling" (the bold), leader 1004-1016. Earls Thorkell the Tall (1017-1021), Harold Godwinson (by 1045), Ælfgar (1051-52, 1053-57), Gyrth (c. 1057-66), who had the borough's third penny [Er,D].

Tied to the town itself:

- Theodred, bishop of London, will of 942 x c. 951: "min hage þat ic binnin Gypeswich bouhte", a town plot he had bought, left to his sister's son Osgot [S1526].
- 970: King Edgar gives ten hides at Stoke to Ely, won by Bishop Æthelwold, asked for by Queen Ælfthryth; witnesses include Dunstan, Æthelwine and Byrhtnoth [S781].
- Queen Edith (1045-66): two-thirds of borough and half-hundred, and a grange [D].
- Before 1066: Leofflæd, "a freewoman", held St Lawrence's; Wihtgar held St Peter's. In 1086: Culling, burgess, with a St Mary's; priests Alnulf, Leofstan, Wulfwine; Ælfric son of Rolf, burgess [D].
- Moneyers signing GIPES (read from a rough scan (?)): Lyfing (Edgar); Leofric (Edward the Martyr); Brantinc, Godric, Leofsige, Lytelman, Waltferth (Æthelred II); Æthelberht, Gotsalin (Cnut); Widfara (Harold I); Brunic, Wulfsige (Edward) [H].
- East Anglian moneyers, mint place not certain: Lul (Æthelberht, Offa, Coenwulf); Wilred (Beonna, and Offa "possibly at Ipswich"); Dudda, Eadmund, Twicga (Æthelweard, Edmund) [K,B].

## 4. What was traded

Out: Ipswich ware, c. 720-850: hard grey sandy jars, cooking pots, some bottles and decorated pitchers, coil-built, finished on a turntable, kiln-fired, on Frisian models; found across East Anglia and as far as Kent, London, Yorkshire and the West Country [Iw,SH]. At Lundenwic from c. 730/50 [L]. Wool and cloth [SH]. In: wine in casks; Rhineland lava querns; Norwegian hones; pots from Belgium, northern France and the Rhine, over 6,000 sherds of 900 vessels, the fine ones probably bound for lords' tables [SH,WP,E]. Herring August to October [T].

Coin: sceattas on many sites, continental "porcupines" among them; a Coenwulf penny lost before c. 840; a coin of Alfred with the first Thetford ware [A,WP]. Offa's heavier penny from c. 792/3 [C]. Some of Æthelstan's pennies show a ship; after 890 the St Edmund pennies, by over 70 moneyers, many with continental names, mints unknown [K]. From c. 973 pennies signed GIPES; the mint's site is unknown [WP,SH]. Fragments of bronze balances, Foundation Street [A].

Dues: nothing before 1086. Before 1066 the borough paid £15, six sesters of honey, 4s. honey-custom and 8s. to prebendaries; the moneyers £4 a year; the earl took a third [D]. In the 10th century trade turned inland to the east Midlands; overseas trade "picked up again" in the 11th [SH].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 794: Æthelberht beheaded [C]. 796-798: Eadwald; then Mercia [K].
- c. 825: the East Angles kill Beornwulf of Mercia; 827 Ludeca; Æthelstan king [K].
- 865: the great army winters in East Anglia and takes horses [C].
- 869, 20 November: Edmund killed; the army "destroyed all the monasteries to which they came" [C,ASC].
- 879-880: Guthrum's army settles. 880-920: sunken houses return, iron smelting, Scandinavian antler combs [SH,A].
- 885: Alfred's fleet takes sixteen ships at "Stourmouth", then is beaten the same day by a large fleet [ASC].
- c. 900-917: bank and ditch. 917 ([SH] says 920): the East Anglian army swears to Edward [Er].
- 970: Stoke to Ely [S781]. c. 973: the mint [C].
- 991: Olaf's 93 ships; "This year was Ipswich plundered"; then Maldon; first tribute, 10,000 pounds [ASC].
- 1010, after Easter: Thorkell's army "went up at Ipswich"; Ringmere, 5 May, where Sigvat's verse puts Olaf Haraldsson; three months' burning [ASC,Rn].
- 1014, Michaelmas eve: the great sea-flood [C].
- 1016: Cnut's ships go "into the Orwell; where they went up and proceeded into Mercia" [ASC].
- 1066: 538 burgesses, read as over 3,000 people. By 1086: 110 paying, 100 poor at a penny a head, 328 dwellings waste; laid to the earl's revolt of 1075 [D,Al,SM].

## 6. Faith and custom

Christian since Felix came to Dommoc c. 630 [Dm]. By 793 the dead lie without goods in graveyards at the town's edge; the barrows and boat-coffins of the 600s are under the streets [SH]. Dedications: Peter; Mildred of Thanet (d. after 732; 13 July (?)); Botolph (17 June), whose minster at Iken on the Alde was begun in 654 [Mi,Bo]. Æthelberht became a saint (20 May), with a chapel at Albrightestone by Boss Hall; Edmund (20 November) is on the Danes' own pennies within a generation [K,P]. A small later-9th-century graveyard lies among houses at St Peter's Street [A].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- An inquest of 1340 said the town was named for a heathen king "Ypus". Scholars offer Gip's wic, or gip, "corner of the mouth" [Al].
- The 970 bounds start at the hythe "æt Æscmannes yre" (an æscman is a shipman or pirate (?)) and pass "healdenes ho" [S781].
- Æthelberht's penny, a she-wolf with twins and REX: one turned up near Rome in 1908, one in Sussex in 2014 [K].
- Sutton Hoo is 14 km off; Rendlesham, the kings' place in Bede, was lived in until the 8th century [WP,Rd].
- The moneyers' names come from pennies found in Swedish soil [H].
- A penny type of Beonna (c. 750s) was found at Dorestad [B].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- No reeve, priest, merchant, toll, fair or ship of 793 is named. A wic-reeve is inference from London.
- No church, minster or hall has been found; St Peter's and St Mildred's are suggestions.
- Where East Anglian pennies were struck before 973: Ipswich is only "possible".
- Whether the first potters were Frisians or copied Frisian pots [WP]; the start is put at c. 720 or the late 7th century.
- Ipswich is not named in 865-869; the first named raid is 991. "Stourmouth" is probably this harbour, not surely [Sh].
- Birch put the Stoke of 970 at Stoke-by-Nayland; Blake and Hart at Ipswich [S781].
- The grid's core and the Carr Street potteries are barely dug; the waterfront digs are unpublished [A].
