# Emuthon (Emden): dossier

Port `emuthon`: now Emden, 53.367 N 7.205 E; realm franks, county emisga, kind market, landing beach, size 2 [ports_793.json]. Researched 4 Oct 2026. Source keys are in section 9; (?) marks what I could not check. The web-search ration was already spent, so everything comes from pages and PDFs opened by name.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What.** A Frisian trading settlement on a raised mound (Wurt, terp) on the north bank of the Ems, where a tidal creek, the Ehe or Aa, met a loop of the river [dwGE; FC14]. One of a wave of trading places founded on this coast under Frankish rule, "about 800" [dwGE]; its excavator Haarnagel said not before the mid 8th century, most likely near 800 [G10 p.19]. The oldest dated feature is a ditch round the first mound, "around AD 800" [G10 p.18]. **In June 793 it is new at best**, perhaps only a seasonal landing of Frisian merchants, with a village of traders and farmers soon after (Rasink) [G10 p.19].
- **Name.** "Mouth of the A" (Ee plus mutha) [dwEhe; HOO]. The first sure form is AMVTHON, on pennies of 1020-1051; an "Emedun" of the 8th-9th century is only "possibly" Emden [HOO]. "Emuthon" is a later medieval spelling, undated (?) [dwEm].
- **Who rules.** Frankish since 785, when Charlemagne took Frisia to the Weser [dwGO]. The district is the pagus Emisga: its county went to a Westphalian family, the Cobbonen (Ekbertiner), its church to Liudger's mission, later the see of Münster [dwGE; SH]. Emden was the king's own place, unlike Hatzum, which had a local chief; hence its later toll and mint [G10 p.17]. No officer here is named before the 11th century. In 792-793 the East Frisians are in revolt: churches burned, priests driven out [CHR; nlLiu].
- **People and size.** Frisians. Haarnagel: founded by Christian traders, not grown from a farm village (small houses as at Hedeby and Dorestad, few byres, early rich imports) [G10 p.19]. Yet beasts were kept, born and slaughtered on the mound in every period [G10 c.7]. The first mound was about 40 m across, and "population density was low" until the 11th century [G10 pp.12, 18]. No head-count exists.
- **Coming in.** The Ems meets the sea about 27 km to the north-west [dwEms]. Off the mouth lay Bant, a marsh island that may have reached Borkum; the eastern channel formed only later, so the way in was the western one [dwBant]. Inside: drying flats, shifting channels, salt marsh; tide about 2 to 2.5 m, stream to 2.5 knots (project estimates) [NSC]. **There is no Dollart**: south of the river is peat and the Reiderland marsh [NSC; dwDol]. The river bends close under the settlement (until 1509), the Nesse spit opposite [dwDol; dwNess]. Flat land, no dikes before about 1000 [dwGO]. No bar, sea-mark or pilot is recorded.
- **Where boats lie.** On mud in the creek mouth (later the Ratsdelft), not on an open beach; the shore flooded regularly [FC14; G10 p.14]. Empty ground lay between mound and river until the 12th century [G10 p.18]. No quay has been found.
- **Neighbours.** Older trading mounds within 11 km: Groothusen (500 by 130 m, on the Dorestad-Hedeby run) and Hatzum over the Ems (houses either side of a street on the crown). Emden outgrew both in the 10th century [dwGr; dwHa; G10 p.17].

## 2. What stood there

Dug by Haarnagel 1951-53, Reinhardt 1959 and the Ostfriesische Landschaft 1990-93 and 2001-03 [G10 pp.11-16].

- **The first mound** (between Schulstraße and Pelzerstraße): about 40 m wide, ringed by a ditch 0.8 m wide around 800; posts and planks show people lived on it. Enlarged northward with clay sods in the mid 9th to early 10th century; a timber palisade marked its edge until the 10th [G10 p.18].
- **The street**, forerunner of the Schulstraße, laid in that enlargement [G10 pp.13, 18].
- **Street houses**: 2.6 to 3.3 m wide, ridge-purlin roofs, too small for a byre: dwellings and workshops; three fronted the street. Walls of wattle and daub or upright planks [G10 pp.12-13, 19].
- **A second mound** to the north, 9th century. Its first house, about 900, was three-aisled with thick dung layers: a house with a byre [G10 pp.11-12, 18].
- **The church mound** to the west, apart, with a graveyard. Two timber churches lay under the stone one: tree-rings date the second to 966 and the first "most likely" after 941 [G10 p.14; FC01]. Other accounts put a small timber church and graves here from the 9th century [dwGK; KGL]. Graves lay east-west [KGL].
- **Plot ditches** of the later 10th century, one with a fence of birch stakes; many horse bones; a nearly whole shell-tempered pot [FC01; G10 p.16].
- **A fire**: burnt posts and charcoal in the 11th-century levels; year unknown [G10 pp.12-13, 19].
- **The mint**, about 1020-1051, known only from its pennies [HOO; Gie08].
- **A count's castle with a chapel of St Walburga**, 11th century: "probably" [dwH3].
- **Not there before 1066**: the brick church (about 1200), walls, the castle of about 1300. The mounds joined into one of 250 by 300 m, 8 m high, only later [G10 p.18; dwGE].

## 3. Who could be met

No person is recorded at Emden itself before 1066. These belong to the district.

- **Liudger** (about 742 to 26 March 809), a Frisian, pupil of Alcuin at York. From 787 head of the mission in five districts east of the Lauwers, Emisga among them, and the island Bant; in 791 he built the first chapel in East Frisia, of timber, at Leer up the Ems; driven out in 792-793; bishop of Münster from 30 March 805, keeping the five districts [dwLiu; enLud; SH; dwLe].
- **Unno and Eilrad**, leaders of the revolt (Altfrid); Unno is placed in Rüstringen (?) [nlLiu; enUn].
- **Bernlef** (died 809 (?)), the blind singer of the forefathers' deeds and the wars of kings, to the harp. He lived at "Helewyret": Holwierde, across the Ems mouth, or Helwerd further west. Liudger cured him, and in the revolt had him baptise dying infants [enBe; nlHo; nlLiu].
- **Bishops of Münster**: Gerfrid 809-839; Altfrid 839-849, author of Liudger's Life; Hildebold 941-969, in whose time the 966 church was built; Rudbert 1042-1063; ten others between [dwBi]. None is recorded here.
- **Counts.** Cobbonen: Ekbert (active 809-811), Cobbo the Elder (named 842, 845); "only sparse hints" tie them to Emden [dwGE; dwEk]. From about 900 the counts of Werl [dwGE]: Hermann III (died after 1055) held the Emsgau and struck coin at Emden [dwH3]; Bernhard II (about 1010-1070) was told in 1062-63 to hand it to Archbishop Adalbert and resisted [dwB2; dwWe].
- **Archbishop Adalbert of Hamburg-Bremen** (1043-1072), and **Gottschalk of Zutphen**, his man for the county, killed about 1063 [dwAd; dwB2; dwGo].
- **Archbishop Rimbert** (865-888), who led the Frisians at Norditi, about 30 km north, in 884 [dwNo].
- **Ordinary people the digs show**: traders and craftsmen in small street houses; a farmer with a byre; spinners and weavers (whorls, wool cloth scraps); leather-workers and shoemakers; workers in bone, antler and horn; shepherds of large ewe flocks; fowlers and fishers; skinners of cats [G10 c.7; FC01].

## 4. What was traded

- **Out.** Wool and woollen cloth: sheep were the main animal kept, in large ewe flocks run for wool, manure and milk. Cattle on the hoof, hides, leather; probably sheep's cheese; cat skins; horn, bone and antler work [G10 c.7]. Salt was boiled from sea-peat ash on Bant, from an unknown date (?) [dwBant].
- **In.** "Early and rich import finds" [G10 p.19]; which Rhenish wares came in the 9th century I could not open (?). By the 12th century Pingsdorf, Paffrath and Siegburg pottery [FC01]. Cured cod from far off [G10 c.7]. The terp coast lacks timber, grain, wine, querns and iron [TSH]. Local pots are shell-tempered [FC01].
- **Coin.** In 793 the king's deniers; the Frisian law counts a solidus as two new denarii between Lauwers and Weser [LF]. Emden's pennies, about 1020-1051, read HERIMAN (or HEREMON (?)) and AMVTHON (Dannenberg 772-773); in hoards from 1045; mean 0.75 g from 88 coins, the light Frisian standard, against over 1.00 g at Tiel and Deventer [Gie08; dwGE]. They reached Scandinavia and eastern Europe [G10 p.17].
- **Toll and law.** A royal place with toll and mint [G10 p.17]; the toll is first written of in 1244 [dwGE]. Loyal Frisians owed a coast-watch at the river mouths in place of war abroad [dwGO]. Blocking the public way on a river costs 12 solidi; pulling a man from the water earns 4; selling a slave to heathens costs your own wergild [LF].
- **Seasons.** New cloth in March-April, wool in June, butter and cheese in summer, cattle in October-November [TSH]. No fair is recorded.
- **Routes.** Coastwise between Dorestad and Hedeby; up the Ems into Westphalia [G10 p.17; G08]. No ship find. "The Vikings left Emden more or less alone" [G10 p.17].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 787: Liudger given Emisga and four other districts [dwLiu].
- 791: his timber chapel at Leer [dwLe].
- 792-793: revolt under Unno and Eilrad; Liudger flees [CHR; nlLiu].
- About 800: the ditch round the first mound [G10 p.18].
- 802: the Frisian law written down [CHR; enLF].
- 805: Liudger bishop of Münster; 26 March 809 he dies [dwLiu; CHR].
- 810: 200 Danish ships ravage the Frisian islands; 100 pounds of silver paid [CHR].
- 814: Louis the Pious restores rebel Frisians' right to inherit [dwGO].
- 826: Harald Klak given Rüstringen, further east [CHR].
- 838, 26 December: storm flood over Frisia; 2,437 counted drowned [CHR].
- Mid 9th to early 10th century: the mound enlarged; the first street [G10 p.18].
- 870: Meerssen puts the Ems mouth in the East Frankish kingdom; Emden "established as a regional market" [G10 p.17].
- 884, autumn: Rimbert's Frisians beat the Danes at Norditi [dwNo].
- About 900: the counts of Werl; the mound "generously enlarged" [dwGE].
- After 941: first timber church; 966: the second [G10 p.14].
- About 1020-1051: AMVTHON pennies [HOO; Gie08].
- 1047-1057: Adalbert gains Hunsingo and Fivelgo, west of the Ems, with markets, mint and toll at Winsum and Garrelsweer [Gie08].
- 11th century: a fire [G10 p.19].
- 1062-63: the Emsgau ordered to Adalbert; Gottschalk killed [dwWe; dwB2].
- 1066: Adalbert falls from power [dwAd].

## 6. Faith and custom

- **Diocese**: Münster; Liudger's day is 26 March [dwEg; dwBi].
- **The church** was later that of Saints Cosmas and Damian, perhaps the Cobbonen's choice; when it was dedicated is unknown [dwGE; dwGK]. Feast 27 September (?).
- **Burial**: a churchyard on its own mound, graves east-west [KGL].
- **The old gods.** In the Additions to the Frisian law, straight after the words "this beyond the Lauwers": whoever breaks into a shrine and takes holy things is led to the sea, and on the sand the tide covers his ears are slit, he is gelded and offered to the gods he robbed [LF Add. XI]. On Heligoland Liudger made the holy spring a font [enLud].
- **Law east of the Lauwers.** Wergild: noble 106 solidi 2 denarii, freeman 53 solidi 1 denarius, half-free 26 and a half solidi and half a tremissis; a woman's equals a man's. Proof by oath-helpers and boiling water. Servile work on Sunday costs 12 solidi here, 4 elsewhere. The hand of a harper, a goldsmith or a woman who makes fresum (Frisian cloth) is paid for a quarter above rank [LF; enLF].
- **Horse meat**: 46 horse bones, 17 % butchered; avoided by Christians, not wholly [G10 c.7].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- Bant, a whole district of Liudger's mission, is now a sandbank; the sea took the last of it in 1780 [dwBant].
- At Norditi Adam of Bremen counts 10,377 Danes dead, many drowned by the tide. In Norden they say Rimbert prayed on a boulder, and rain water in its hollows cures warts [dwNo].
- Bernlef is said to have had eighteen children, two of whom lived; much of his legend grew in the retelling [enBe].
- At Bentumersiel across the river Roman army gear lies in the marsh; no camp is proven [dwBen].
- Pliny wrote of this coast's people on mounds above the tide, burning dried mud (Natural History 16.1); from memory, not opened (?).
- From the Kirchstraße: a flute 11.5 cm long cut from a bone, and a bone toggle scored with three strokes crossed by an X, a house mark or a tally; "medieval", probably after 1066 [FC01].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- That anything permanent stood here in June 793.
- Any written mention before the coins of 1020-1051; the spelling Emuthon in 793.
- A church in 793: the tree-ring dates are after 941 and 966.
- Any named person at Emden before 1066. Liudger and Bernlef are not recorded here.
- Which Hermann struck the pennies: Hermann III of Werl [dwGE; dwH3] or a Hermann "of Kalvelage" [Gie08]. How Werl's hold ended: lost in 1063 [dwGE] or kept to about 1096 [dwB2].
- A mint before about 1020, a toll rate, a fair, a quay, a pilot, a raid on Emden. Norditi was not here.
- The Dollart, dikes, walls, a castle, the Hanse.
- A population figure. A mound width of 45 m reached me only through a search summary (?).
- Not opened: Haarnagel 1955, Brandt 1994, Stilke 1995, Altfrid, Adam of Bremen; cited through G10 and Wikipedia.
