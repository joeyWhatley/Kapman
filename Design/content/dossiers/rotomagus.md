# Rotomagus (Rouen): dossier

Port `rotomagus`: Rotomagus, modern Rouen, 49.44 N 1.098 E; realm franks, region neustria; town, quay, size 4 [ports_793.json]. Keys in brackets: section 9. (?) means not checked. Quoted words from Latin are our own rendering. Dudo [DU], about 1015, and William of Jumièges [WJ], about 1070, are late and flatter the dukes.

## 1. The place in 793

- A walled Roman city on the north bank of the Seine, an archbishop's seat. Written Rodomo (769, 779), Ratumagus (800-824), Rotuma (841), Rodomum (10th century) [ARF; W:Rouen; AB; FL]; to Northmen, Rúða [W:Rouen].
- King: Charles. Archbishop: Mainard, from 762 (or 771) to about 800 [W:Liste; W:Remi]. A Count Richard of Rouen is named in 787 [W:Wandrille]; whether he held it in 793 is not known.
- Size: the wall of 325-350 encloses 18 ha (23 by another count) [W:Rotomagus]. No number of people survives.
- The wall is a rectangle built in haste from torn-down buildings: south side on the river, north side against the hills, a stream down each flank, marsh about (the Malpalu, east of the cathedral) [W:Fort; W:Romain]. A visitor of the 960s still saw a town of reused stone [IY].
- Coming in: some 120 km of river from Honfleur, in great loops [W:Port], past Fontenelle (Saint-Wandrille) and Jumièges. No text I read names a bar or a pilot. Ships ride the flood: in spring 841 "a seething tide" carried merchant ships from the river mouth and left them by the city [NI]. The tide beats on the town's gates and walls, strongest by the moon [DU 45]. At Caudebec, below, the flood came as one wave, "la barre", until the 1960s [W:Mascaret].
- Seen from the river: the wall, the cathedral churches inside, St Peter's outside to the north-east, and north of the wall the broken amphitheatre, part standing until 1204 [W:Rotomagus].
- Boats lie under the south wall: Rollo moored at the gate joined to St Martin's church, in a picture drawn about 1015 [DU 10]. I found no dug quay of this age (?). In 800 the king crossed the Seine here; how is not said [ARF]. The repo gives the town a ferry [Repo:traffic].

## 2. What stood there

- **The cathedral group**, inside the wall: two churches side by side, St Mary's and St Stephen's, joined by galleries. One lay under today's nave, perhaps 30 by 15 m; Victricius's basilica of about 395 was found in the Albane court (digs: Lanfry 1954, Le Maho 1986). Canons from the 750s. The 9th century added a bishop's house, canons' lodgings and a west tower-block; the fire of 841 ruined the group and it was patched [W:Cath]. Richard I enlarged St Mary's [DU 58]. Archbishop Robert began a choir over a crypt about 1030; Maurilius finished the church (98 m) and dedicated it on 1 October 1063. The crypt survives [W:Cath].
- **St Peter's, later Saint-Ouen**, in the suburb: a basilica of 535, St Ouen's grave from 684, an abbey from about 750; ravaged 14 May 841; monks back from 918; Hildebert (about 960-1006) the first true abbot; a new church from 1062 [W:Liste; W:StOuen].
- **Saint-Godard**, north of the wall: graves of bishops Godard (525) and Romanus (644) [W:Godard].
- **Saint-Gervais**, north-west: a crypt older than 1000 in a great Roman and early Christian grave field with stone coffins [W:Gervais].
- **St Martin's at the river gate** [DU 10]. **The Beauvais gate**, north, with a bridge before it in 946 [DU 45; WJ iv].
- **The market place**, where Rollo had two of the king's knights killed [DU 13], and **the mint**, lawful in 864 [EP]: where either stood is not known.
- **The Tower**, south-east corner, where the Robec meets the Seine: Richard I's, mid 10th century, with the duke's hall; a prison about 1000 [W:Tour; WJ v].
- **Holy Trinity**, on the hill above the town: founded 1024 (1030 by my memory (?)) by Goscelin, vicomte of Rouen; first abbot Isembert [W:Trinité; WJ vii].
- **The Battle Meadow**, west wall to the Seine: William Longsword beat Riulf there about 934 [W:Fort].
- **Upstream**: the Northmen's island fort at Oissel, 858-861; the king's works at Pîtres, 862-866 [AB].

## 3. Who could be met

- **Archbishops** [W:Liste]: Mainard 762-c. 800; Gilbert 800-828; Ragnoard 828-836; Gombaud 836-849; Paul 849-855; Wenilo 855-869; Adalard 869-872; Riculf 872-876; John 876-889; Witto 889-c. 910; Franco 911-919; Gunhard 919-942; Hugh 942-989, a monk of Saint-Denis who gave church land to his brother [W:Hugues]; Robert 989-1037, Richard I's son, count of Evreux, married with sons [W:Robert]; Mauger 1037-1055, deposed [EW:Mauger]; Maurilius 1055-1067 [W:Maurille].
- **Lords after 911**: Rollo (dead by about 933); William Longsword (927-942); Richard I (942-996) and Gunnor; Richard II (996-1026); Robert I (1027-1035); William (from 1035) [W:Rollon; W:Richard1; W:Richard2]. Their own age called them count or prince [W:Rouen].
- **Passing through**: King Charles, 800; Louis the Pious, 818 and, with his wife, 824, when the Greek emperor's envoys and Patriarch Fortunatus of Grado came to him here; Bera, count of Barcelona, exiled here in 820 after losing a duel on horseback [ARF]. Charles the Bald, 841 [NI]. King Louis IV, 942-945; Herluin of Montreuil, given the city in 943; Turmod and the heathen king Setric, killed 943 [FL]. Ibrahim ibn Ya'qub of Tortosa, 960s [IY; EW:Ibrahim]. Swein of Denmark; Olaf Haraldsson; Æthelred, Emma and the boys Edward and Alfred, 1013-14 [WJ v]. Harold Godwinson, 1064 [W:Tour].
- **Clerks**: Dudo of Saint-Quentin, at the dukes' court (?); Warner, the archbishop's man, and Moriuht, the Irish poet he mocked, 996-1026 [EW:Warner]; abbots of Saint-Ouen: Hildebert, Henry (1006-1033), Herfast (1033-1042), Nicholas (1042-1092) [W:StOuen]. Downstream: Abbot Gervold of Saint-Wandrille, 787-806, the king's toll-taker [Dossier:quentovic].
- **Ordinary people in the evidence**: canons and Roman-trained singers [W:Remi]; merchants with ships [NI]; poor men and ruined merchants [DU 10]; an estate steward, a ploughman and his wife at Longpaon (Darnétal) [DU 13]; sworn moneyers, unnamed [EP]; men of Rouen selling wine in London [LT]; slaves [W:Normandie]. A moneyer Ranulf in 11th-century charters (?) [memory].

## 4. What was traded

- **Wine out.** The vine does not thrive at Rouen; the land bears wheat and rye [IY]. The wine is the Paris basin's, come down river [Repo:seasons]. About 1000 men of Rouen carry wine and blubber fish (whale) to London [LT; Repo:chron].
- **Fish**: salmon, and a small fish that smells of cucumber [IY].
- **Plunder and slaves in.** Swein and Richard II swore that what the Danes took from their enemies they would bring to the Normans to be bought, and that sick or wounded Danes would be nursed here [WJ v]. Early in the 11th century Rouen still takes slaves from the Northmen [W:Normandie]. Moriuht finds his wife a slave in Normandy and buys her back [EW:Warner]: for half a penny, the child for a quarter-penny and half a loaf (?) [memory].
- **Toll**: a toll place by 779, when Saint-Germain-des-Prés is freed of toll here, at Quentovic, Amiens, Maastricht and Dorestad [EW:Quentovic; Repo:traffic]. No rate survives. In London a great ship of Rouen pays six shillings and a twentieth of the big fish [LT].
- **Coin**: pennies. From 864 only ten mints may strike; Rouen is one, its right said to have been Quentovic's of old; the penny carries the king's name round his monogram, the city's round a cross [EP]. The Fécamp hoard (buried about 980-985; 8,584 coins) held 3,107 Rouen pennies of Richard I, 2,478 read as Archbishop Hugh's (I did not see the legends (?)), 532 nameless, and two of Edward the Martyr [W:Fécamp; FH].
- **Seasons**: the Saint-Denis fair upstream from 9 October [Repo:seasons]. The Seine froze for thirty days in 821/2 [Repo:chron].
- **Ships**: sea ships to the wall on the flood, barges above [Repo:traffic]. The Northmen's hundred great ships of 876 are "bargas" [AB].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- **800**, after Easter: King Charles crosses the Seine here on his way to Tours [ARF].
- **820**: Bera exiled here. **824**, 17 November: Louis the Pious arrives; the Greek envoys speak of images [ARF].
- **841**, spring: Charles the Bald fills 28 tide-borne merchant ships with armed men and crosses [NI]. 14 May: Oscherus's Northmen burn the city; monks and people are killed or taken [AB; W:Rouen]. Jumièges burns on the 24th; Fontenelle pays six pounds [W:Wandrille].
- **844-847**: King Charles takes Romanus's head and the bodies of Remigius and Godard to Soissons [W:Romain].
- **845**, March: 120 ships pass for Paris. **851**: Northmen land here and walk to Beauvais. **856**: they winter at Jeufosse. **858**: the king besieges them on Oissel from July to 23 September and fails [AB].
- **862-866**: the king bars the river at Pîtres [AB]. **864**, 25 June: his edict there [EP].
- **876**, 16 September: about 100 ships enter the Seine [AB]; Dudo puts Rollo's coming here [DU 10].
- **885**, 25 July: the Northmen enter Rouen "with their whole army" and cross in ships found on the river [AV].
- **911**: Rouen granted to Rollo [Repo:chron]. **912**: Franco baptises him Robert [DU 13]. **918**: Saint-Ouen's monks and saint return [W:StOuen; W:Ouen].
- **925**: "the Northmen of Rouen" break the peace; Franks burn their land this side of the Seine [FL].
- **About 934**: Riulf's rising beaten under the walls [W:Fort; DU 19].
- **942**: William receives King Louis "royally" here; famine and cattle plague; William murdered on 17 December (under 943 in Flodoard). **943**: Louis kills Turmod and Setric and gives Rouen to Herluin. **945**: Louis is seized here and held [FL; EW:William].
- **946**: Otto and Louis waste the Northmen's land [FL]. The Norman writers make it a siege, Otto's nephew killed at the Beauvais gate [DU 45; WJ iv].
- **About 960**: Richard weds Emma, Hugh the Great's daughter, inside the walls [WJ iv].
- **996**: Richard I dies; the peasants' assemblies are put down [WJ v].
- **About 1000**: the London tolls [LT]. **1013-14**: Æthelred's exile; Archbishop Robert baptises Olaf [WJ v].
- **1036**: Robert opens Romanus's shrine [W:Romain]. **1055**: Mauger deposed at Lisieux [W:Maurille].
- **1063**, 1 October: the cathedral dedicated before Duke William; Rollo's and Longsword's bodies moved into it [W:Cath].

## 6. Faith and custom

- St Mary's is the mother church. Days: Remigius 19 January; Godard 8 June; Maurilius 9 August; Ouen 24 August; Mellon 22 October; Romanus 23 October [W:Liste; W:Romain; EW:Audoin].
- Relics: Gervase, Protase and others sent by Ambrose about 393-396 [W:Victrice]. In the raids the saints leave: Ouen to Francia [DU 13], Victricius to Braine [W:Victrice]. Only Romanus's body stays, brought inside the walls [W:Romain].
- Chant: Remigius (755-771) brought Roman singers and set up the canons [W:Remi].
- Burial: by the saints outside the walls [W:Gervais; W:Godard]; the Norman lords in the cathedral [W:Cath].
- The old gods: in 943 Turmod "went back to idols and heathen rite" and pressed the boy Richard to follow [FL].
- Law: Rollo forbade theft and hanged for it; guilt was tried by the ordeal of fire [DU 13]. From 864, death for selling arms or horses to Northmen [Repo:chron].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **The beard.** Ibrahim saw a young man of Rouen whose beard reached his knees and, combed, four fingers past them; he swore that six years before he had none. In hard winters a white goose with red beak and feet comes, called "gansh" [IY].
- **The plough.** Rollo ordered ploughs left in the field. A wife at Longpaon hid her husband's irons to teach him care. Rollo gave him five shillings, tried the village by fire, found no thief, then asked whom he had told. Both hanged: she for the theft, he for knowing [DU 13].
- **The arm rings.** Rollo hung gold rings on an oak by a pool in the forest near Rouen; they hung three years untouched, and the wood is "Rollo's Mere" [WJ ii].
- **The rope.** Count William, the duke's brother, five years in the Tower, left by a high window on a very long rope [WJ v].
- **Words**: Robec is Norse "red brook" (Rodobech, about 1020); "la barre" is Norse bára, wave [W:Robec; W:Mascaret].
- **Romanus** tore the dedication from a temple of Venus in the amphitheatre and it fell, and he turned back floods. His dragon is first written in 1394 [EW:Romanus; W:Romain].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Not known: the count in 793; any reeve, tollman or moneyer by name; toll rates; whether there was a quay or a bridge (the stone bridge is 12th-century [W:GrandPont]).
- Not in 793: Northmen (first in 841), Rollo, Normandy, dukes; the Tower; Holy Trinity; the Robec's name. The suburb church is still St Peter's.
- Never in this game: the Gargouille and the prisoner's pardon; the Gothic cathedral; Joan of Arc.
- Dudo's year 876, his Bishop Franco in it (Franco sat 911-919) and his king's daughter Gisla have no other witness. The siege of 946 is not in Flodoard. "Hasting burned Rouen" is legend [WJ vii].
- Rollo's baptism: 912 in Dudo, 915 in one article [EW:Cath]. His death: between 925 and 933 [W:Rollon].
- An attack in 843 and the day 13 October 851 rest on a magazine cited in one article; the annals have no 843 [W:Rouen; AB].
- Which cathedral church was St Mary's and which the canons' is told two ways [W:Cath].
- "Slave market" is a modern phrase; the evidence is Warner's satire and the Swein oath.
- Jews: one article claims a quarter since Roman times and a killing in 1007, the last from a weblog; I could not check either (?) [W:Rouen].
- From memory, unchecked: the Fontenelle chronicle's 68 captives bought for 26 pounds on 28 May 841; Willibald's "market" at Rouen about 721 [Repo:traffic]; honey and madder in a forged Saint-Denis charter; a Rouen wharf at Dowgate in London held since King Edward's day; wool, cloth and tin back from England.
- The spine sets deported Saxon families near Rouen in 804-815; I found no source for them here (?).
