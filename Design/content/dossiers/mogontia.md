# Mogontia (Mainz): dossier

Port `mogontia`: Mogontia, now Mainz, 49.999 N 8.274 E; franks, rhineland, town, riverbank, size 4, no end year [ports]. Researched 4 Oct 2026. Source keys are spelled out in section 9; (?) marks what I could not check.

## 1. The place in 793

- **What it is.** The Roman city of Mogontiacum on the left (west) bank of the Rhine, facing the Main's mouth [dw:Mainz]; an archbishop's seat since 780/82, head of the largest church province north of the Alps [dw:Erzbistum]. The annals also write Mogontia [AF 882, 886].
- **Who rules.** King Charles: assemblies here in 800 and 803, a palace at Ingelheim just downstream [c:800-08-mainz-800; c:803-mainz-803; dw:Geschichte]. On the spot, Archbishop Riculf (787-813), a Frank of the Wetterau and Alcuin's pupil [dw:Richulf]. No count or reeve of 793 is known to me. By about 900 the archbishop is lord of the city [RG].
- **How big.** A Roman wall some 4,000 m round and 6 m high, about 118 ha inside, much of it unbuilt; people live in a long strip inside the wall along the Rhine [dw:Stadtmauer; dw:Mogontiacum; LMM:living]. In 965 it is still "a very large city, part inhabited and the rest sown" (my English of Jacob's German) [Jacob]. No head count exists.
- **The merchants' shore.** In Carolingian times long narrow plots run from the wall to the river, with jetties for ships; Frisian long-distance traders settle there [LMM:far]. In 886 it is "the best part of the city, where the Frisians lived" [AF 886].
- **Coming in.** Upstream all the way: the crew hauls the ship by a rope from the bank, "as the custom is", and stops at St Goar's cell in the gorge to pray [Wandalbert 839, in FCT]. At Mainz: the Roman wall along the west bank; in the stream the 21 stone piers of the dead Roman bridge, 600 m across [dw:Römerbrücke]; Kastel, the old bridgehead fort, opposite [dw:Kastel]; the Main coming in, with grain boats [Transl]; on the hill south of the town St Alban's, a new basilica rising, and the Drusus stone, a Roman tower-tomb 20 m high [dw:Alban; dw:Drususstein]. Boats lie on the shore below the wall [LMM:far]. No bar, no tide. The crossing is by boat: the bridge stands only from 803 to 813 [Einh 17, 32].

## 2. What stood there

- **The wall.** Late Roman. The quake of 1 January 858 split "the old walls" [AF 858]. In 882, the Northmen having burned as far as Koblenz, its repair began and a ditch was dug outside it [AF 882]. Under Hatto I (891-913) the river wall was rebuilt 30 to 50 m nearer the Rhine [dw:Stadtmauer]. An older timber front: a 70 m row of oak posts dated 651-730 (?) [HD].
- **St Martin's, the bishop's church** (today St Johannis). What stood in 793 is unknown. Rebuilt by Hatto I from about 900, consecrated 910; the coronation church of 1002 [dw:Johannis; dw:Dom].
- **Willigis's cathedral,** east of it: burned on or just before its consecration day, 29/30 August 1009; consecrated 10 November 1036. Bronze doors by Master Berenger, "the first metal doors since Charles" [dw:Dom].
- **St Alban's,** on the hill south of the wall: a graveyard since Roman times [LMM:dying]; Riculf's three-aisled basilica, 75 m long, consecrated 1 December 805 [dw:Alban; dw:Geschichte]; St Michael's oratory at its west end, crushed in 858 [AF 858]; councils (813, 847); graves of Fastrada (794), Riculf, Haistulf, Hrabanus, Sunderold, Liudolf of Swabia (957), William (968) [dw:Alban; dw:Haistulf; dw:Rabanus]. Destroyed 1552.
- **Other churches:** St Quintin, the oldest parish (774) [dw:Quintin]; Altmünster nunnery (8th century) [dw:Altmünster]; a chapel given to St Maximin of Trier in 893, taken to be St Christoph [dw:Christoph]; St Peter north of the wall (944) [dw:Peter]; St Stephan on the highest ground (990, first of wood; Willigis's grave) [dw:Stephan]; St Jakob on the hill (1050-55) [dw:Jakob].
- **The bridge,** 803-813: timber on the Roman piers, 500 paces long, ten years' work, burned in three hours in May 813 [Einh 17, 32; dw:Römerbrücke].
- **The mint.** A penny of Charles's early type (771-794), CARO/LVS, 1.32 g, struck at Mainz [SMB]. Merovingian moneyers Nantaharius and Martinus had signed Mainz coins [LMM:living]. Willigis is the first archbishop in the kingdom with his own head on a coin [LMM:Willigis].
- **The shore dig** (Hilton II, Löhrstrasse, early 1980s): stray finds including coins, coin-brooches and weights [Wamers]; sherds of Badorf and Mayen pots; brooches made on the spot [LMM:far].
- **Roman ruins in view:** the theatre (10,000 seats; graves in its vaults from the 6th century) and the aqueduct piers [dw:Mogontiacum].
- **The Jews' graveyard** ("Judensand") outside the wall; oldest stone 1049 [dw:Judensand].
- **Potters' workshops** (881) [AF 881]. The market's site is unknown.

## 3. Who could be met

- **Archbishops** [dw:Liste; own articles]: Riculf 787-813; Haistulf 813-825; Otgar 826-847; Hrabanus Maurus 847-856, born at Mainz about 780, before that abbot of Fulda; Charles of Aquitaine 856-863; Liutbert 863-889, archchaplain; Sunderold 889-891, killed by Northmen on the Geul, 26 June 891; Hatto I 891-913, regent for Louis the Child; Heriger 913-927; Hildebert 927-937; Frederick 937-954; William 954-968, Otto I's son; Hatto II 968-970; Rupert 970-975; Willigis 975-1011, of free but not noble birth, chancellor and regent; Erkanbald 1011-1021; Aribo 1021-1031; Bardo 1031-1051; Luitpold 1051-1059; Siegfried I 1060-1084.
- **Kings and guests:** Charles (800, 803); Harald Klak the Dane (826); Louis the Pious (Christmas 838); Louis the German (October 848, hearing envoys of the Northmen and the Slavs) [AF 838, 848]; Otto I, Liudolf and Conrad the Red (953); Henry II (1002); Conrad II (1024, 1036); Pope Leo IX and Henry III (1049).
- **Named lesser people:** Probus, a learned priest (died 25 June 859) [AF 859]; Thiota, a prophetess (847) [AF 847]; Gottschalk the monk, condemned here in 848 [AF 848]; Hruodrad, a priest waiting at Mainz for company to Rome (826-840) [Einh ep]; Aubrey, a blind Aquitanian carried by Mainz grain merchants (about 830) [Transl]; Liutefred, "a rich merchant of Maintz", Otto's envoy, who left Venice on 25 August 949 and reached Constantinople on 17 September [Liudp VI.4]; Ibrahim ibn Yaqub of Tortosa (965/6) [Jacob; LMM:hot]; Berenger the bronze-founder (about 1000) [dw:Dom]; Gershom ben Judah (about 960 to 1028/40), head of the Jewish school; Kalonymus ben Meshullam (about 1000); Yehuda ben Schne'or (died 1049) [dw:JG; ew:Gershom; ew:Kalonymos; LMM:jews].
- **Kinds of people:** Frisian merchants; Mainz grain merchants with their own boats; potters; moneyers; brooch-makers; mounted warriors with fine spurs; canons, monks, nuns; refugee clergy from Cologne and Bonn (881); from the mid-10th century Jewish long-distance traders from Italy and southern France [AF 881, 886; Transl; SMB; LMM:far; LMM:strategic; LMM:jews].

## 4. What was traded

- **Out:** wine (vineyards all round; "wine from south of Mainz" fills Dorestad's casks) [Jacob; econ]; grain, bought "in the upper parts of Germany" by Mainz merchants and boated down the Main [Transl, Wendell].
- **In, from downstream:** Badorf and Mayen pottery [LMM:far]; Frisian cloaks [Notker I.34]; silk in a Frisian skipper's cargo [FCT].
- **From the east,** by 965: dirhams struck at Samarkand in 301 and 302 of the Hijra (913-915); pepper, ginger, cloves, spikenard, costus, galangal [Jacob; c:965-ibrahim-hedeby]. Jewish merchants dealt in spices, silk, furs and metalwork [dw:JG].
- **Coin and price.** The silver penny; from 794 the new penny, and fixed top prices for grain and bread [c:794-06-frankfurt-new-denier; c:794-06-01-frankfurt-prices]. In the famine of 850 a modius of wheat cost ten "sicli" of silver at Mainz [AF 850].
- **Toll.** No Mainz tariff is known to me; only old and just tolls are lawful (805) [econ].
- **Seasons.** Vintage in September and October, new wine by Martinmas [seasons]. St Alban's feast, 21 June, draws pilgrims; on the Main, pilgrims ride in merchant boats [Transl; ew:Alban]. The Rhine froze for thirty days in 821/22 [econ]; from May to July 886 it flooded from source to sea [AF 886].
- **Ships.** River boats, hauled up and drifting down; ships passed under the bridge [Notker I.30].
- **Table ids that fit:** rhenish_wine, wheat, barley, rye, badorf_ware, quernstones, frisian_cloth, silk; later pepper, ginger, cloves.

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 793: famine; in autumn the king's canal from the Main's waters to the Danube's fails; at Christmas his boats come down the Main [c:793-great-famine; c:793-autumn-fossa-carolina; c:793-12-25-wurzburg-christmas].
- 794, 10 August: Queen Fastrada dies at Frankfurt; buried at St Alban's [AF 794].
- 800, 803: assemblies. 805, 1 December: St Alban's consecrated [dw:Alban].
- 810, 9-11 December: a fast against plague on men and beasts [c:810-12-09-mainz-fast].
- 813: May, the bridge burns; 9 June, reform council; 9 August, Riculf dies [AF 813; dw:Richulf].
- 826, 24 June: Otgar baptises Harald Klak, his wife and many Danes at St Alban's (the Xanten annals: Ingelheim, over 400 people) [AF 826; dw:Otgar; ew:Harald].
- 840: 20 June, Louis the Pious dies on a Rhine island near Ingelheim; in August two royal armies face each other outside Mainz [AF 840].
- 843: Mainz goes to Louis the German's eastern kingdom [c:843-08-verdun].
- 847: Hrabanus archbishop; council at St Alban's; Thiota flogged [AF 847].
- 850: famine; Hrabanus feeds over 300 poor a day at Winkel [AF 850].
- 855, 858, 859, 872, 881: earthquakes [AF].
- 873: famine, then locusts strip a hundred iugera near Mainz in an hour [AF 873].
- 881-882: Cologne and Bonn burn; their clergy flee to Mainz with treasures and saints' bodies; the wall is repaired [AF 881, 882].
- 883: Northmen come up the Rhine; Liutbert with a few men kills many and takes back the plunder [AF 883]. They never reached Mainz [HD].
- 886, March, after mid-Lent: the Frisians' quarter burns [AF 886].
- 953, July to September: Otto I besieges his son Liudolf and Conrad the Red in Mainz [dw:LA].
- 1002, 7 June: Willigis crowns Henry II [dw:Willigis].
- 1009: the new cathedral burns [dw:Dom]. 1012: Henry II drives the Jews out [dw:JG].
- 1024, 8 September: Aribo crowns Conrad II [dw:Aribo].
- 1049, October: Leo IX and Henry III hold a synod of 40 bishops against simony and priests' marriage [dw:Bardo; dw:Jakob].
- No end: the town outlasts the game.

## 6. Faith and custom

- Christian since Roman times. Patrons: St Martin (11 November) and St Alban (21 June), a priest beheaded by Arians who carried his head to his grave; the oldest account is in Hrabanus's martyrology, about 845 [ew:Alban]. Einhard: Alban "has in that city both a church and a very celebrated monastery" [Transl, Wendell].
- Relics: St Caesarius, brought to St Alban's in 810 [dw:Richulf]. The great are buried at St Alban's [dw:Alban].
- Church law made here: councils of 813, 847, 848, 852, 857 and 1049 [AF; c:813-summer-reform-councils]. The Romano-German pontifical, the rite book later used across Europe, was compiled at St Alban's under William [ew:PRG].
- No heathen cult is recorded; heathens come as guests, like Harald's Danes.
- Jews: Gershom's rulings of about 1000 forbid a second wife, divorce without the wife's consent, and reading another's letter [ew:Gershom].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **The game's first day, 21 June, is St Alban's feast** [ew:Alban].
- **The comb.** Riculf sent Alcuin an ivory comb; Alcuin answered with a riddle: a beast with two heads and sixty teeth, not as big as an elephant (about 796) [TTT].
- **The little napkins.** Notker's Charles on the Frisians' short cloaks, sold at the long price: "What is the good of those little napkins? I cannot cover myself with them in bed and when I am on horseback I cannot shield myself with them against wind and rain." [Notker I.34, Grant].
- **Who burned the bridge?** Einhard: an accident. Notker: "the knavery of a few rascals, who wanted to steal merchandise from the ships that passed underneath" [Notker I.30, Grant]. Modern retellings say ferrymen [dw:Rheinbrücke].
- **St Goar's due.** Two Frisian skippers skipped the prayer: one lost a hauler in the river (revived) and left a pound of silver; the other struck the rocks and left a silk cloth [FCT].
- **Thiota** foretold the world's end for 847; people brought gifts and priests followed her. Before the bishops she said a priest had coached her for gain [AF 847].
- **The potters' quake,** 30 December 881, before cockcrow: pots knocked together and broke, "as the potters said" [AF 881].
- **Portents:** a blood-red sky for several nights (870); locusts a thumb long, lying like snow (873) [AF].
- **Hatto I,** "well versed in every sort of guile" [Liudp, Wright p. 72]. The Mouse Tower tale is late and baseless [ew:Mouse].
- **Erkanbald** lay the wrong way round in his coffin, in blue silk with a gold border; 1.82 m tall, gouty (opened 2019) [dw:Erkanbald].
- **Under the shore** lie five Roman warships, found in 1981/82; nobody in 793 knows [dw:Römerschiffe].
- Ermold's poem has Harald's Danes come up the Rhine in a hundred white-sailed ships (?).

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Population, market site, toll, the cathedral's look in 793, any count or reeve by name.
- Where the Frisians lived, how many, since when: certain in 886, likely earlier [LMM:far], undated for 793 (the port table assumes it). The street name "Brand" as a memory of the 886 fire (?).
- No Jews are attested before the 10th century; the Kalonymos family's coming is put at 800, 876 or later [ew:Kalonymos]. No Jewish quarter in 793.
- No bridge before 803 or after 813; no Willigis cathedral before about 1000; St Peter from 944, St Stephan from 990, St Jakob from 1050.
- The Northmen never sacked Mainz. Do not call the bridge fire arson as fact.
- No Mainz wheel: it first shows on coins of 1230-1249; the wheelwright's-son tale of Willigis is legend [dw:Rad]. "Golden Mainz" is a byname of the 10th century and after [dw:Geschichte].
- Harald's baptism: Mainz or Ingelheim; say "at St Alban's, the annals say".
- The oak posts and the Hilton finds I saw only in summaries [HD; Wamers]; days on the river are the project's guesses [seasons].
