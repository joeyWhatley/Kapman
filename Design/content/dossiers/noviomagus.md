# Dossier: Noviomagus (Nijmegen)

Tags: section 9. (?) = unchecked. Latin quotes are my translations and may be reused; Einhard is Turner 1880 (public domain); modern-page quotes are evidence only.

## 1. The place in 793

- A king's house, not a town, never a bishop's seat [GN]. The texts say Niumaga [ARF 777, 827], Noviomagus [ARF; AB; AF], later Niumagun [Th]. "Valkhof" is a late medieval name [VV].
- It is Charles's own. Regino puts it "in Batua" [Reg 806]. Farms of the later Realm of Nijmegen and the forest toward Xanten fed the court [Canon; Ket].
- Einhard: Charles began a palace "at Nimeguen, on the Waal, the stream that washes the south side of the island of the Batavians" [Ein 17].
- Perhaps unfinished in 793: the annals put Charles here only at Easter 777 and in Lent 806 and 808 [ARF]; historians date completion about 804 or later [VP].
- People: merchants, boatmen, craftsmen and farmers in a small settlement on the bank below; a few hundred in the palace when the court comes [Canon]. No headcount survives.
- The land: a hill about 35 m high, a spur of an ice-pushed ridge, on the Waal's south bank in a bend [DV; VP]. From the top the flat Betuwe lies open to the Veluwe's edge [VP].
- What a skipper sees: the walls of the late Roman fort (about 320) on the hilltop, the palace inside them [VP; Kal]; a thick, high 4th-century wall of tuff along the bank [MG]; the landing at the hill's foot, a harbour since Roman times [DV].
- The water: upstream at Herispich (Spijk) the Rhine and Waal part "from one bed" and ring Batua [Reg 885]. An annalist calls Nijmegen "near Dorestad" [AB 837]. Charles came by ship down Moselle and Rhine in 806, Louis down the Maas in 821 [ARF].

## 2. What stood there

- **The palace.** A charter of 8 June 777 is dated "in Niumago palacio publico" [VP]. It stood inside the Roman fort, whose walls partly still stood [VP]. Regino: "a palace of vast size and wonderful work" [Reg 881]. Annalists call it a fort (castrum) in 837 and 846 [AB; AX].
- **Its remains.** Three Corinthian capitals of Carrara marble, about 775-800, two reused in the 12th-century St Martin's chapel [VP]. Foundations were quarried away in 1796-1799 [DV]; no ground plan is known [VP].
- **The Roman works.** The ditched fort; ditches found at Kelfkensbos and Lindenberg [VP]. The riverside wall of tuff; a stretch survives at the Waalkade [MG; GN].
- **The parish church** by the palace, near the later Voerweg: by tradition Nijmegen's first, of St Stephen or St Gertrude (sources disagree), 7th century, a modest hall of stone or wood. First mentioned in the 15th century; never found; pulled down 1247-1254 [GK; Kal].
- **The settlement** of the 9th-10th centuries: between today's Grotestraat, Priemstraat and Nonnenstraat, west of the hill, near the river [GN].
- **Old grave fields**, closed about 700: over 2,000 graves around Mariënburg, the largest early medieval grave field found in the Netherlands [GN]. Rich graves held iron axes, a great lance head, a cloth with gold thread [MG].
- **The Northmen's works, 880**: "a very strong rampart and walls" round Nijmegen [AF 880]. Burn traces with late Carolingian pottery at the eastern Waalkade are put, not certainly, to their fire [VP].
- **St Nicholas chapel**, only from about 1030-1040 (some say 996, some after 1047): eight-sided within, sixteen-sided without, 13.5 m across, after the Aachen chapel; tuff and reused Roman tiles stamped by the Tenth Legion [NK; DV].
- **The Ketil wood**, from Nijmegen to Xanten: the king's hunting [Ket; ARF 817, 825].

## 3. Who could be met

- Charles the Great: 777, 806, 808. Eardwulf, exiled king of Northumbria: 808 [ARF].
- Louis the Pious: 817, 821, 825, 827, 830, 837, 838. Lothar I: 825, 830, 846. Louis the German: 838 [ARF; AB; AX].
- Gerward, palace librarian, in charge of the palace works and buildings: here about 828 [Trans].
- Charles the Bald and Rorik the Northman, lord of Dorestad: 870 [AB].
- Louis the Younger, winter 880-881; Eberhard the Saxon, son of Count Meginhard, taken captive; his mother Evesa, who ransomed him "at a great price" [Reg 881].
- Charles the Simple: 898 [Reg]. Otto I: 949 [Kal]; perhaps July 956 [VP].
- Otto III: 985, 987, 991, 995, 996, 997; Count Balderik and Adela of Hamaland, heard over Elten abbey, 996-997 [VP].
- Empress Theophanu: died here 15 June 991 [Th].
- Henry II and Cunigunde: 1018; Bishop Adalbold of Utrecht and the merchants of Tiel [SV; Th; VP].
- Conrad II: 1031, 1033, 1036, 1039. Henry III: 1036, 1040, 1044; Gunhild, Cnut's daughter, 1036 [VP; Gun].
- Ordinary people: boatmen, merchants, craftsmen, farmers, court servants, soldiers [Canon]; fishermen [GN]; huntsmen [ARF 825]; the clerks of the capitularies [Cap806]; Saxon and East Frankish levies in 830 [AB]. No moneyer, reeve or priest is named.

## 4. What was traded

- No list of goods survives. The place consumes: a court of hundreds in Lent and at Easter [ARF; Canon].
- **Law made here, Lent 806** [Cap806]: famine price caps per modius: oats 2 d, barley 3 d, spelt 3 d, rye 4 d, cleaned wheat 6 d. Buying grain or wine at harvest for 2 d to sell at 4 or 6 is "shameful gain"; buying from need is "trade". Asking back more than was lent is usury. Old tolls stand; new ones go. Church treasure must be guarded, since Jewish and other merchants boast they can buy what they please.
- **Law made here, April 808** [Cap808]: best double cloak 20 solidi at most, single 10; marten or otter coat 30; fine 40 solidi, 20 to the informer. Coin is struck only at the court; palace pennies pass everywhere. Headings on guarding the march by land and sea, the ships ordered built, the palace market.
- **Coin.** Gold tremisses reading Niomago, in the Escharen hoard of 590-600, were very likely struck here [VvG]. No mint is known 793-1066 [Munt]; the table's `has_mint: false` is right.
- **The river.** After Dorestad, Tiel on the Waal led (925-1050), with Rhineland and English ties and its own pennies; Nijmegen and Zaltbommel followed [VvG].
- **Seasons.** Lent and Easter courts; spring and autumn hunts; assemblies in May, June and October [ARF; AB; AF].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 806: Charles arrives by ship; Lent, Easter, the capitulary [ARF; Cap806].
- 808: Lent and Easter after a mild, sickly winter; Eardwulf comes, then goes to Rome [ARF].
- 817: Louis hunts here twenty days after the wooden portico fell at Aachen [ARF].
- 821, May: the division of the realm is read again and sworn [ARF].
- 825: spring hunt; autumn hunt with Lothar [ARF].
- 827: an assembly waits for Horik, son of King Godfrid of the Danes, who promised to come and did not [ARF].
- 830, about 1 October: assembly held here so Saxons and East Franks could come; Louis recovers his power [AB].
- 837: Northmen take tribute on Walcheren and at Dorestad; Louis hurries here and they leave at once [AB].
- 838, May-June: coast defences shared out; a Danish fleet drowns in a storm; Louis the German is stripped of lands [AB; AF].
- 846: Lothar, in the fort, watches Dorestad burn and cannot avenge it [AX].
- 870: Charles the Bald binds Rorik by treaty in the palace [AB].
- 880-881: a Northmen's fleet enters the Waal, fortifies the palace and winters. Louis besieges in a hard winter, fails, and leaves on their promise to go; they burn palace and works and make for the Rhine mouths [AF 880; Reg 881].
- 898: Charles the Simple here [Reg].
- 949: Otto I's assembly [Kal].
- 980: Otto III born in the Ketil wood [Th].
- 991, 15 June: Theophanu dies; buried at St Pantaleon, Cologne [Th].
- 1018: 16 March, a great synod; 6 April, Easter court, where Dirk III's toll is complained of; the army sent against him is beaten at Vlaardingen, 29 July [Th; SV].
- 1036, Pentecost: Henry weds Gunhild, renamed Kunigunde; crowned 29 June [VP; Gun].
- 1047: the palace burned in Godfrey the Bearded's rising against Henry III [VP; DV].

## 6. Faith and custom

- The 1018 synod excommunicated Count Otto of Hammerstein and his wife Irmingard, too near of kin and deaf to summons; from an old book read aloud it ruled how host and chalice stand on the altar, the chalice to the right [Th].
- Monks of Saint-Omer carried St Audomar's relics here to move Otto I, perhaps July 956 [VP].
- The Nicholas cult came with Theophanu; the dedication is first written in 1470 [NK].
- Burial with weapons and pots ended about 700 [GN; MG].
- Law: each lord feeds his own poor [Cap806]; nobody is hanged without judgement; a perjurer loses his hand or buys it back [Cap808].
- Old gods: nothing for this period.

## 7. Stories and oddities

- The charter of 777 written here gave Leusden and four forests to St Martin's of Utrecht [VP]; the repo ties it to Dorestad's Upkirika [Repo].
- In 885 an Evarhard struck the first blow when Godfrid was killed at Herispich [Reg 885]: the captive of 881 (?).
- Gerward, lodging a night on the Aachen road, heard from his host of a healing, questioned the woman and told the emperor next day [Trans].
- An iron sword marked ADALFRIID, said to come from the Waal, later 9th century; the museum says "alleged Viking sword" [VP].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- What the palace looked like: every picture is invention [VP; GN]. Whether it was finished in 793, or whether the house of 777 was older [VP].
- No named count, steward, priest, merchant or craftsman of Nijmegen, 793-1066. No mint, market right, fair or toll.
- Charles is not attested here in 793. Visits in 772 [Kal] and 804 [VP] are not in the Royal Annals.
- The portico of 817 fell at Aachen [ARF], though a Dutch summary puts it here [VP].
- No leader of the Northmen of 880-881 is named [AF; Reg]; "Godfrid" is inference. An attack of 838 [GN], a wintering in 863 [VR] and a raid in 925 [GN] rest on late works (?).
- Who set the fire of 1047: one summary blames the Hollanders [H3].
- No St Nicholas chapel before about 1030; no St Martin's chapel or great tower before 1155 [DV].
- Nothing is recorded of bars, shoals or pilots. The diocese (Cologne?) is unchecked (?).
