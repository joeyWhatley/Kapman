# Colonia (Cologne): dossier

Port `colonia`: Colonia, modern Cologne, 50.938 N 6.962 E; realm franks, county ripuaria, region rhineland; town, riverbank landing, size 4 [ports_793.json]. Keys in brackets: see section 9. (?) means not checked.

## 1. The place in 793

- The old Roman city on the left bank of the Rhine, a bishop's seat. The bishop is Hildebold (since 787), also the king's archchaplain (since 791) and mostly at court; archbishop only from 794/795 [W:Hildebold]. The king is Charles.
- People: about 3,000 around 700 and 10,000 around 1000, by modern estimate; nothing for 793 [W:Geschichte].
- The Roman wall rings the town: 3.9 km, about 97 ha, 19 towers, 9 gates, 8 m high [W:CCAA].
- From the water: the wall's east side faces the river. Between them lies low ground, once the Roman harbour, a side arm behind an island 1,000 m long that silted up from the 2nd and 3rd centuries [W:Alter Markt, W:CCAA]. People lived and worked on this strip from the 5th century [Dig (?)]. Boats most likely lay along it; no quay is recorded (?).
- Opposite stands the Roman fort of Deutz, a stone square 142 m a side with 18 towers, a royal estate [W:Deutz]. Constantine's bridge (310; stone piers, timber deck, 400 m) once joined them; whether any of it still stood is unknown [W:Brücke].
- Landmarks: St Peter's in the north-east corner of the walls (?); St Mary's on the temple mound to the south [W:Kapitol]; outside the walls, St Kunibert's on the bank to the north and St Severin's on the Bonn road [W:Kunibert, W:Severin].
- Dorestad is about 300 km downriver: 3 to 5 days down, 10 to 15 up, rowed and towed, by the repo's guess [Repo:seasons]. No bar, no tide.

## 2. What stood there

- **St Peter's**, the bishop's church. A church of about 800 burned some ten years later; when the great one was begun is disputed. Dedicated 27 September 870 (or 873): a choir at each end, nave 97.5 m [W:Dom]. Its library listed 175 books in 833 [W:Bibliothek].
- **The Gero Cross**: oak, 2.88 m, made for Archbishop Gero (969-976), mid-nave over his grave [W:Gerokreuz].
- **St Gereon**, outside the wall to the north-west: a late Roman oval hall (about 350-365), "at the Golden Saints" in Gregory of Tours; Hildebold's grave [W:Gereon].
- **St Severin**, south: a 4th-century grave chapel in the Roman cemetery, enlarged before 866 [W:Severin].
- **The Holy Virgins (St Ursula)**, north: a 4th-century hall on a Roman cemetery, with the Clematius stone; nuns from Gerresheim, fleeing the Hungarians, from 922 [W:Ursula].
- **St Mary's on the Capitol**: Plectrude's church and grave (she died after 717) on the podium of the temple of Jupiter, Juno and Minerva; rebuilt 1049-1065, and its carved wooden doors survive [W:Kapitol].
- **St Pantaleon**, outside the wall: named 866; an abbey from 955/957; Bruno's grave (965) and Theophanu's (991) [W:Pantaleon].
- **Great St Martin**, on the old island over Roman warehouses: Bruno's chapter of about 960; nothing is proven earlier [W:Martin].
- Later: Holy Apostles by the west wall, Pilgrim's church and grave [W:Aposteln].
- **The markets**: "mercatus coloniae" 922 and "forum" 988 (the Alter Markt); the Heumarkt's first surface, dated by its timbers to 957 [W:Alter Markt, W:Heumarkt]. Beneath lay pit houses of the 5th to 7th centuries and a dark layer of dung, bones and waste of pottery, metal, glass and bone [Dig (?)]. The suburb was taken into the town about 950 [W:Geschichte].
- **The mint**: pennies of Charles, 771-794 [Search (?)]; the S / COLONIA / A type from Louis the Child (900-911) to Henry II, Otto I's weighing 1.0 to 1.7 g [Münster].
- **The Praetorium**, Roman governor's palace and Merovingian kings' hall: large parts slid towards the Rhine in the 8th or 9th century [W:Praetorium].
- **Deutz**: Heribert's abbey in the old fort, charter of 1 April 1003, and his grave [W:Deutz].

## 3. Who could be met

- Bishops and archbishops [W:Liste]: Hildebold 787-818; Hadebald 819-841; the see disputed 842-850; Gunthar 850-863 (deposed, died 873); Willibert 870-889; Hermann I 889/890-924; Wichfrid 924-953; Bruno 953-965; Folcmar 965-969; Gero 969-976; Warin 976-985; Everger 985-999; Heribert 999-1021; Pilgrim 1021-1036; Hermann II 1036-1056; Anno II 1056-1075.
- Passing through: Harald, king of the Danes, and the monk Anskar, 826 [VA 7]; Louis the German, 852 [AF 852]; the pope's legate Arsenius, 865 [AF 865]; Charles the Bald, 876 [AF 876]; Godfrid of the Northmen, 881/882 [W:Wikinger]; Empress Theophanu, Christmas 986 and 988 [W:Pantaleon]; Pope Leo IX, 1049 [W:Kapitol]; the boy king Henry IV, 1062 [W:Kaiserswerth].
- Of the churches: Hartwig, Willibert's priest, 876 [AF 876]; Ruotger, who wrote Bruno's Life about 968 [W:Brun]; the Irish abbots of St Martin's, Minnborinus (died 986), Kilian (1003), Helias (1040), Aaron (1052) and Molanus (1061) [EW:Martin]; the chronicler Marianus Scotus, there about 1056 [W:Martin].
- Ordinary people in the evidence: canons and nuns [AF 881]; scribes [W:Bibliothek]; workers in metal, glass and bone by the river [Dig (?)]; potters in the Vorgebirge villages [W:Badorf]; hired rowers (1062) and merchants with their own ships (1074) [W:Anno]; Frisians, from a street "inter Frisones" about 1100 [Friesen (?)]. No moneyer is named: the pennies name king and city [Münster].

## 4. What was traded

- Out: Rhenish wine (vintage September to October, new wine by Martinmas) [Repo:seasons]; Badorf ware from Walberberg, Eckdorf and Badorf (8th to 10th century), its relief-band jars up to 70 cm tall used to ship wine and oil, with Cologne its market and loading place, then Pingsdorf ware [W:Badorf]; Mayen lava querns, glass, swords [Repo:econ, Repo:seasons].
- In London about 1000 "the men of the Emperor who came in their own ships" paid toll and, at Christmas and Easter, two grey cloths and a brown one, ten pounds of pepper, five men's gloves and two casks of vinegar. Cologne men are usually counted among them (?); the code may be later than it claims [IV Atr].
- From 953 the archbishop held court, market, mint and toll [W:Geschichte, W:Brun]. I found no toll rate and no fair datable before 1066 (?).
- Law: Ripuarian; its payments could be made in kind at fixed values [Repo:econ].
- Ships: river craft. The boat the bishop gave in 826 had two cabins [VA 7].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 794/795: an archbishopric over Bremen, Utrecht, Liège, Minden, Münster and Osnabrück [W:Hildebold].
- 814: Hildebold gives the dying emperor the last rites. 818, 3 September: he dies [W:Hildebold].
- 821/822: the Rhine frozen for thirty days [Repo:seasons (?)].
- 826: Harald and Anskar take boat here for Dorestad [VA 7].
- 843: Cologne falls to Lothar's middle kingdom; 855 to Lothar II; 870 to the eastern kingdom; 911 to the western; 925 to Henry I [EW:Lotharingia].
- 852: Louis the German arrives by boat [AF 852].
- 857, 15 September: lightning in St Peter's [AF 857].
- 863, October: the pope deposes Gunthar over King Lothar's divorce; he acts as archbishop regardless [W:Gunthar].
- 870: Willibert ordained on 7 January at King Louis's order, against King Charles; synod on 26 September and the dedication [AF 870].
- 876: Charles the Bald sits here with his army. Willibert sends the priest Hartwig to warn Louis the Younger, who beats Charles at Andernach on 8 October [AF 876].
- 881/882: the Northmen burn Cologne and Bonn "with churches and buildings"; the clergy flee to Mainz with the treasures and the saints' bodies [AF 881]. A ransom paid in January did not save the town [W:Wikinger]. Digs have not found the burning [W:Geschichte].
- 883: rebuilt "without churches and monasteries", the walls restored "with gates, bars and locks"; the Northmen pass it by [AF 883, W:Wikinger].
- 891: Hermann I asks the pope for relics to replace those burned [W:Geschichte].
- 953: Bruno, Otto I's brother, archbishop and duke of Lotharingia. 965, 11 October: he dies at Reims [W:Brun].
- 999, Christmas: Heribert enters the town barefoot. 1021, 16 March: he dies [W:Heribert].
- 1049: Pope Leo IX consecrates an altar at St Mary's [W:Kapitol].
- 1062, April: at Kaiserswerth Anno seizes the boy king, who jumps from the ship into the Rhine and is pulled out by Count Ekbert, and brings him here [W:Kaiserswerth].
- 1074, after the game ends: Anno's men take a merchant's ship; the town rises; 600 merchants leave [W:Anno].

## 6. Faith and custom

- Patron: St Peter. Bruno brought his staff and chain [Search (?)]; Warin gave part of the staff to Trier [W:Liste].
- Saints: Gereon and the soldiers of the Theban Legion [W:Gereon]; the Holy Virgins [W:Ursula]; bishops Severin and Kunibert [W:Severin, W:Kunibert].
- Feasts: the Virgins 21 October [EW:Ursula]; Heribert 16 March [W:Heribert]. From memory (?): Peter and Paul 29 June, Pantaleon 27 July, St Peter's Chains 1 August, Gereon 10 October, Severin 23 October, Martin 11 November, Kunibert 12 November.
- Burial: by the saints outside the walls, in the old Roman cemeteries [W:Severin, W:Gereon, W:Ursula]. Willibert was the first archbishop buried in the cathedral [W:Liste].
- The old gods: the temples are ruins or churches [W:Kapitol]. I found no heathen worship in the town in these years.

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **The boat with two cabins.** In 826 Bishop Hadebald gave Harald and Anskar "a good boat". The king liked it so well that he moved in, a cabin each, and they became friends on the way to Dorestad [VA 7].
- **The fiery dragon.** In the storm of 857 the people ran into St Peter's and rang the bells. A bolt "like a fiery dragon" split the church and killed a priest at St Peter's altar, a deacon at St Denis's and a layman at St Mary's [AF 857].
- **The voices.** The night before the dedication of 870, evil spirits were heard grieving that they must leave seats they had held so long [AF 870].
- **The deer hide**, a late legend. The king, lost on a hunt, heard Mass in a chapel and offered gold. The priest, not knowing him, asked only for a scrap of deer hide to bind a prayer book. It was Hildebold [W:Hildebold].
- **The crack.** The head of Gero's cross split. He laid a piece of the Host and of the true cross in it, prayed, and it closed [W:Gerokreuz].
- **The Virgins.** "Thousands" in 848, 11,000 by the late 9th century; their leader is Pinnosa first, Ursula only in the 10th. So in 793: the Holy Virgins, unnumbered and unnamed [EW:Ursula].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Not known: the count; any named reeve, tollman, moneyer or merchant; the population in 793; where boats lay; whether the bridge stood; toll rates; fairs.
- Not yet in 793: an archbishop (794/795); the double-choir cathedral (870); the named markets (922, 957); St Pantaleon's abbey (955); Great St Martin (about 960); the S COLONIA A penny (900 on); the Gero Cross; Deutz Abbey (1003).
- Never in this game: the Three Kings' relics (1164), the Gothic cathedral (1248), the staple right (1259), the Hanse, a town council [memory, except 1248: W:Dom].
- Jews: the decree of 321 and the 11th-century quarter are sure; a ritual bath is dated by some to the 8th century; the time between is unproven [W:Juden]. No synagogue in 793.
- Frisians: a heritage site claims a Frisian settlement from Carolingian times; I did not see its evidence [Friesen (?)].
- The Irish came to St Martin's under Warin in one article, in 989 in the other [EW:Martin, W:Martin].
