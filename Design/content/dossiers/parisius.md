# Parisius (Paris, with Saint-Denis): dossier

Port `parisius`: Parisius, modern Paris (Saint-Denis fair), 48.853 N 2.349 E; realm franks, region neustria; town, riverbank landing, size 4, no end year [ports_793.json]. The port is two places: the island city, and the abbey of Saint-Denis 9 km north of it [Repo:chronicle, by its coordinates]. Keys in brackets: see section 9. (?) means not checked.

## 1. The place in 793

- An old Roman city on an island in the Seine: a bishop's seat and a count's, no longer a king's. Charles came through once, in summer 800 [W:Palais, ARF 800].
- Count: Stephen (779 to 811/815). Bishop: Erchanrad (775 to about 795). Abbot of Saint-Denis: Fardulf, a Lombard priest, given the abbey in 793 for uncovering the plot of Pippin the Hunchback [W:Comtes, W:Évêques, W:Fardulf, ARF 792].
- Size: no count of people exists before 1328 [EW:Paris]. The island wall encloses about 9 ha [W:Enceintes].
- The river: twice as wide as now, no embankments, no bar, no tide (today it stops at Poses, above Rouen); landing places all the way up from the sea [EW:Paris, W:Seine, AB 861]. Frozen in 821/822, in flood in 834 and 886 [Repo:chronicle, AB 834, Abbo].
- Coming up from Rouen a skipper reaches Saint-Denis first, for the river loops north below the city. An island lies in the river beside the abbey [W:Seine, AB 866].
- The island city: a 4th-century wall about 2 m thick and 2 m high, set 15 to 30 m back from the water, so boats can land under it at low water. Palace at the west end, the bishop's churches at the east, a market between. Two timber bridges, north and south [W:Enceintes, W:Cité, W:Siège 885].
- Where boats lay is not recorded: the charter of 753 says only "ports" [T 55]. The Grève strand on the right bank is a market only by 1141 [W:Grève].

## 2. What stood there

On the island:

- **Saint Stephen's**, the cathedral: 70 m by 36 m, five aisles on marble columns, mosaics; a church of Saint Mary behind it, the round baptistery of Saint John beside it. The council of 829 sat here [W:St-Étienne].
- **The palace**: the Roman citadel, about 1 ha, house of the counts; rebuilt by Robert II (996-1031) with a King's Hall and a chapel of Saint Nicholas [W:Palais].
- **Saint Martial's**, a house of nuns founded in 635, facing the palace [W:Palais].
- **The bridges of the siege**: Charles the Bald's new Great Bridge, its stone tower unfinished in November 885; the Little Bridge of timber, with a timber tower. The wall was repaired between 877 and 885 [W:Pont, W:Siège 885, W:Cité, Abbo].
- **The mint**: pennies of Pippin; of Charles before 793 (CARO/LVS, PRI/SVS, about 1.1 g); of Louis the Pious and Charles the Bald (PARISII) [Prou, Num]. One of ten mints allowed in 864 [Pîtres 12]. Its house is unknown.

On the banks:

- **The Holy Apostles** (later Sainte-Geneviève), on the left-bank hill: Clovis's church of about 508-520, with his grave, Clotilde's and Genevieve's. Burned in 857 [W:Ste-Gen, AB 857].
- **Saint Vincent's** (Saint-Germain-des-Prés), in the left-bank meadows: consecrated about 558; a roof of gilded copper, mosaic floor, marble columns; Germain's body behind the high altar since 25 July 754 (or 756); a large graveyard. Burned in 861; rebuilt by Abbot Morard (990-1014), whose tower stands [W:SGP, W:SG-Aux].
- **Saint-Gervais**, the oldest church of the right bank, and **Saint-Germain-le-Rond**, round and ringed by a ditch, the Northmen's camp in 885. A bank and ditch closed this suburb in the late 10th or 11th century [W:Histoire, W:SG-Aux, W:Enceinte, Abbo].
- **Saint Martin's and Saint Laurence's** on the road north: the fair was held between them before 710. Henry I refounded Saint Martin's in 1059-1060 [T 44, W:SMC]. **Saint Peter's on Montmartre** is first named in 850 [W:Montmartre].

At Saint-Denis:

- **Fulrad's church**, consecrated 24 February 775 before Charles: three aisles, a ring crypt, Pippin's grave before the west door. A text of 799 gives it 245 feet, 101 windows, and doors of gold, silver and ivory. Hilduin's chapel, 832, where eight monks sang Mass day and night [SD:basilica, SD:799, SD:abbey].
- **The monastery**: dormitory, refectory, warming room, kitchen, baths, bakery, storeroom, workshops, and a hospice at the gate. An aqueduct fed three stone tanks; in its trench lay a lead plate with trial strikes for a penny of Pippin, so the abbey had a mint [SD:abbey, SD:aqueduct, SD:coin]. Under Charles the Bald it struck SCI DIONVSII M [Prou].
- **A stone hall** north of the church, two storeys, at least 30 m by 14 m, perhaps for great guests. In one room five chests and a barrel had been buried in haste [SD:abbey, SD:chest].
- **The great graveyard** north of the church, over 8,000 square metres, its plaster coffins used again and again. Later the fair's stalls stood in it, at two pennies a pitch [W:St-Denis, SD:abbey, SD:fair].
- **The castellum** of 869, of wood and stone: about 8 ha, a double ditch fed by the Croult, a canal over 6 km long first named in 832. A shoemakers' shop inside is named in 898; its offcuts lay in the ditch. First mill, 894 [AB 869, SD:castellum, SD:shoemaker, SD:croult, SD:mills].
- Found in the digs: pennies of Dorestad and of Quentovic, and a sceat [SD:fair].

## 3. Who could be met

- Counts: Stephen 779-811/815; Begon 815-816; unsure from 816 to 882; Odo 882-888; Robert 888-922; Hugh the Great 922-956; Hugh Capet 956-987; Bouchard of Vendôme 987-1007, the last [W:Comtes, W:Bouchard].
- Bishops: Erchanrad I 775-c. 795; Inchad 811-831; Erchanrad II 832-857; Aeneas 858-870; Gozlin 884-886; Anscheric 886-911; the rest are in the list [W:Évêques].
- Abbots of Saint-Denis: Maginarius 789-793; Fardulf 793-806; Waldo 806-814; Hilduin 814-841; Louis, Charlemagne's grandson, to 867; King Charles himself 867-877; Gozlin 877-886; Ebles 886-892; then the Robertian counts [W:Abbés, AB 867].
- At Saint-Germain: Abbot Robert (779); Abbot Irminon (about 811-829), who had the book of estates made about 823-828; the monks Usuard (to Spain for relics in 858), Aimoin, and Abbo (about 850 to after 923), who saw the siege [T 81, W:Irminon, W:Usuard, W:Abbon].
- In the siege: Count Odo, his brother Robert, Bishop Gozlin, Abbot Ebles; the Northman Sigfred; the twelve of the tower, Ermenfred, Eriveus, Eriland, Odaucer, Ervic, Arnold, Solius, Gozbert, Wido, Ardrad, Eimard and Gozsuin [Abbo].
- Ordinary people in the evidence: the city's merchants with their own boats [AB 861]; Saint-Germain's own traders [T 81]; Saxon (English) and Frisian merchants [T 44, T 55]; the abbey's toll men and the count's agents [T 55, T 77]; the law-finders of the count's court [Capit 803]; Saint-Denis's provost, dean and treasurer [AB 867]; shoemakers [SD:shoemaker]; the boy monk Hunus, noble, dead at sixteen between 838 and 845 [SD:Hunus]; thousands of named tenants on Saint-Germain's estates [W:Irminon]. No moneyer is named: the pennies name king and city [Prou].

## 4. What was traded

- Out: wine above all. Merchants came "to buy wine in the ports and on the rivers" [T 55]. The abbey sold its grain and spare wine; also honey [SD:fair].
- In: wool and woollen cloth, brought by Saxons and Frisians [SD:fair].
- **The fair of Saint-Denis** opens on the saint's day, 9 October [T 77, SD:fair], and lasts a month [Power, Repo:fairs] or seven weeks, to Saint Andrew's [SD:fair]. A second fair falls on 24 February, the day the church was dedicated; when it began is not said [SD:markets]. The Lendit in June began only on 9 June 1053 [SD:lendit].
- **Tolls.** Every toll in the district of Paris, from Saint Denis's Mass until the market ends, is the abbey's: on ships, harbours, carts and pack beasts, and the wheel, bridge and gate tolls [T 55, T 77].
- **The quarrel.** In Charles Martel's day the count began taking four pennies a head from every free merchant, and five from a slave, who first had to swear he was one. In 753 the monks said the merchants were staying away, and King Pippin struck the tax down [T 55]. In 710 the count had claimed half the toll and lost [T 44].
- Saint-Germain's traders went toll-free at Rouen, Quentovic, Amiens, Maastricht, Dorestad and Paris (27 March 779) [T 81].
- The edict of 864: a new penny, the king's monogram on one side, the city's name and a cross on the other; and no mail coat, weapon or horse to a Northman, on pain of death [Pîtres 11, 25].
- 866: to pay the Northmen 4,000 pounds of silver the king took, besides a levy on every farm, a tenth of all that merchants were seen to have; part was paid in wine [AB 866].
- Ships: the charters say only "boats" [T 55].

## 5. The years 793 to 1066

- 793: Fardulf abbot [W:Fardulf]. 800, summer: Charles passes through [ARF 800].
- 802: Fardulf and Stephen are the king's envoys for Paris, Meaux, Melun, Provins, Étampes, Chartres and Poissy [Capit 802]. 803: Stephen has the new chapters of Salic law read in the public court at Paris; all agree and sign [Capit 803].
- 821/822: the Seine frozen [Repo:chronicle].
- 827: Greek envoys bring the emperor a book of Dionysius the Areopagite; Hilduin has it put into Latin [W:Hilduin].
- 834: the Seine in flood; on 1 March Louis the Pious is given back his arms in the church of Saint-Denis [AB 834].
- 845, March: a hard winter; 120 ships reach Paris with no one resisting; the king pays 7,000 pounds [AB 845].
- 856, 28 December: Danes take Paris and burn every church but Saint Stephen's, Saint Vincent's and Saint Denis's, which pay "a great sum" [AB 857].
- 858: Abbot Louis and his brother Gozlin are seized; Saint-Denis alone gives 685 pounds of gold and 3,250 of silver [AB 858, note]. 859, 21 September: the martyrs' bones are carried to Nogent [AB 859].
- 861, January: Paris and Saint-Germain burned; merchants fleeing upriver by boat are chased and caught [AB 861].
- 865, 20 October: Northmen enter Saint-Denis and stay about twenty days, carrying plunder to their ships. 866, June: they leave their island by the abbey [AB 865, 866].
- 867, 9 January: Abbot Louis dies; the king keeps the abbey, and keeps Lent and Easter there [AB 867, 869].
- 885, 24 November: the great fleet arrives. 6 February 886: flood breaks the Little Bridge and its tower is burned. 16 April: Gozlin dies. November: Charles the Fat promises 700 pounds and lets them winter in Burgundy [Abbo, W:Siège 885].
- 888: Odo is king; Saint Germain's relics go home [W:SGP].
- 978, autumn: Otto II camps on Montmartre, burns a suburb, and leaves on 30 November [W:978].
- 987: Hugh Capet is king. 1007: Bouchard dies a monk; no more counts [W:Bouchard].

## 6. Faith and custom

- Saints and days: Denis with Rusticus and Eleutherius, 9 October; Genevieve, 3 January; Germain, 28 May, and his moving, 25 July; Marcel, 1 November [W:Denis, W:Geneviève, W:SGP, W:SG-Aux, W:Marcel].
- Burial is beside the saints. Kings lie at Saint-Denis: Dagobert, Charles Martel (741), Pippin (768) [SD:abbey, W:St-Denis].
- In danger the monks carry the saints away: Germain and Genevieve in 845, Denis in 859. In the siege Germain's body was carried round the walls [W:Siège 845, AB 859, Abbo].
- A monk's clothes by the rule of 816-817: a pelisse to the heels, gloves in summer and sheepskin mittens in winter, two pairs of shoes [SD:shoemaker].
- Law: Salic, read in the count's public court before the law-finders [Capit 803].
- The old gods: I found none worshipped here in these years. Montmartre is the hill of Mars or of the Martyrs; the texts change the name in the 9th century [W:Montmartre].

## 7. Stories and oddities

- **The informer.** Fardulf was a Lombard, once the favourite of the Lombard king whom Charles overthrew. He told Charles of his own son's plot and was given Saint-Denis [W:Fardulf, ARF 792].
- **The slave's oath.** At the fair a slave paid five pennies, one more than a free man, for swearing that he was a slave [T 55].
- **101 windows.** The description of 799 is copied from one of Saint Martin's at Tours with the numbers made bigger: 245 feet for Tours's 160, 101 windows for its 55 [SD:799].
- **No wine.** In 865 two hundred Northmen came to Paris for wine, found none, and went back unharmed [AB 865].
- **The barrel.** Charles the Bald died in the Alps on 6 October 877, asking to lie at Saint-Denis. His men gutted him, filled him with wine and spices, and at last put him in a pitched barrel wrapped in hides. The smell beat them; they buried him, barrel and all, at Nantua [AB 877].
- **The hawks.** When the Northmen fired the tower in 886 the twelve loosed their hawks so the birds should not burn. They had one flask to throw water with, and it slipped from their hands [Abbo].
- **The head.** That Denis walked from Montmartre carrying his head is first written in Hilduin's Passion of 835-840 [W:Denis]. In 793 he is a martyred bishop with two companions.
- **The saint who fought back.** In 845 sickness struck the Northmen at Paris; a captive told them to fast, and it passed. Their leader is said to have wept at the Danish court, saying only the dead Saint Germain had resisted him [EW:845].

## 8. What we do not know, and what the writer must not claim

- Not known: the number of people; where boats lay; where the mint stood; any named merchant, moneyer, toll man or reeve.
- Where the fair stood in 793. The judgment of 710 says it had been moved "because of a disaster" from Saint-Denis to Paris, between Saint Martin's and Saint Laurence's; the charters of 753 and 775 speak of the market and of the city both [T 44, T 55, T 77]. Its length is given two ways; the repo's table has 28 days.
- Not yet in 793: towers on the bridges; any wall but the island's; the castellum (869); the Lendit (1053); Notre-Dame as it stands (1163); the water-merchants' company (1121) [W:Histoire]; a king living here; Normandy (911).
- Do not call Paris the capital. Do not name Ragnar Lodbrok as fact: the annal of 845 names no leader [AB 845]. The 700 ships and 40,000 men of 885 are Abbo's; modern guesses are 200 to 300 ships [W:Siège 885]. The 111 men hanged "for Odin" I saw only in an article [EW:845].
- 845: the repo's chronicle dates the sack 28 March; the English article has Easter Sunday, 29 March. Say "at Easter".
- 857: the annal's "church of Saint Peter and Saint Genevieve" is the Holy Apostles, not Montmartre as one article has it; its "church of Saint Denis" may be the abbey or a church in the city [AB 857, W:856].
- Offa's charter of 790 for Saint-Denis, naming Abbot Maginarius and land "in the port called Lundenuuic", is a forgery, perhaps on a true base [S 133].
- From memory, unchecked: the forged charter of Dagobert that names honey, madder and men of Rouen and Quentovic at the fair (?); the Roman boatmen's pillar found under the cathedral in 1711 (?). The spine sets deported Saxons "by Parisius" in 804-815; I found no source for that.
