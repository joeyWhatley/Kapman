# The Franks (Rhine to Loire) and the Aquitanians, 793 to 1066

For dialogue writers. Sources in brackets, key at the foot; (?) is unchecked; quoted English is public-domain translation.

## 1. Who they are

- **793-814.** One kingdom under "the lord king Charles" (emperor from 800). No capital: the court moves. In 793 the king is at Regensburg digging a canal from the Rezat to the Altmühl; Saxons have destroyed a Frankish force at Rüstringen in Frisia; Saracens have beaten the counts near Narbonne (ARF 793).
- **814-843.** Louis the Pious, then his sons' war. At Strasbourg (842) the armies swear in two tongues, *romana* and *teudisca* (Nithard III.5). Verdun (843) makes three kingdoms; Frisia falls to Lothar's.
- **843-911.** Northmen on every river. Count Odo holds Paris (885-6) and is king in 888.
- **911-1066.** Rollo gets Rouen (911). Saxon kings rule the east from 919; "Franks" there now means the men of the Rhine and Main. In the west the Capetians reign from 987 and "Francia" shrinks to the country round Paris.
- **Aquitanians** (Loire to Garonne) are not Franks and say so: Romance speech, Roman law, old walled towns. A kingdom under a Frankish prince from 781; from the 10th century the counts of Poitou are its dukes. South of the Garonne live the Gascons, a third people (E 5).

## 2. How they live

- **House.** Timber, thatch, an open hearth, a hedged yard. Stone is for churches and palaces: the royal estate of Asnapium had one stone house and "17 other houses built of wood" (Ogg no. 19).
- **Food and drink.** An estate keeps "smoked meat, salt meat ... mustard, cheese, butter, malt, beer, mead, honey" (DV 34). The king eats four courses and a roast off the spit, and "abominated drunkenness" (E 24). Wine grows from the Rhine and Seine south; north of the vines, ale.
- **Dress.** "A linen shirt and linen breeches", tunic, leg bands, cloak, sword; otter or marten skin in winter (E 23). The old cloak was long, white or blue (N I.34).
- **Work by season,** in Charles's month names (E 29): *Brachmanoth* (June, fallow ploughing), *Heuvimanoth* (hay), *Aranmanoth* (harvest), *Windumemanoth* (vintage). The tenant Bodo owes his abbey two days' work a week, three fowls and fifteen eggs (Irm).

## 3. Law and rank

- **Each man carries his people's law:** Salic or Ripuarian for Franks, Roman for Aquitanians, Frisian for a Frisian.
- **Wergild.** A free Frank 200 *solidi* (8,000 pence); a king's man 600 (LS 41); half to the sons, half to the kin (LS 62). Among the Rhine Franks a Frisian is priced at 160 (?), and goods may stand for silver: a sword with scabbard 7, a mail coat 12 (LR 36).
- **Court.** The count's *mallus* with seven doomsmen (*scabini*); the king's *missi* hear complaints against counts (C802). Proof is the oath with oath-helpers, or the ordeal. A perjurer "shall lose his right hand" (C802 36).
- **Rank.** King; counts and bishops; free men who owe the host; tenants (*coloni*); slaves, sold before a bishop or count and never beyond the frontier (Herstal 779). Every man down to twelve years old swears fealty to the emperor (C802 2).
- **Women.** "Of Salic land no portion of the inheritance shall come to a woman" (LS 59); other property they hold, give and sell. The queen's orders bind the stewards (DV 16). Dhuoda ran the march at Uzès and borrowed from Christians and Jews (Liber manualis X.4). Estate women spin, dye and weave (DV 43). Women trading in their own names: not attested (?).

## 4. Faith

- Christian since Clovis. **There are no pagan Franks in 793.** What is attested of older ways is a church list (about 743) of things forbidden: February feasts, wood shrines called *nimidas*, amulets, the need-fire (Indiculus). A Frankish pantheon of Wodan and Donar is later guesswork.
- **Saints.** Martin of Tours (11 November, the season's end), Denis (9 October), Remigius; in Aquitaine Hilary and Radegund at Poitiers, Martial at Limoges.
- **Rules that bite.** Tithes; Lent and Friday fasts; on Sunday no "rustic labours", court or hunt (capitulary of 827, in Power), and no market (809) (?); no lending at interest (Admonitio generalis 789).
- **Later:** Aquitaine's duke founds Cluny (910); its bishops proclaim the Peace of God (Charroux, 989).

## 5. Trade

- **Coin.** One coin, the silver penny, heavier from 793-4 (about 1.7 g). Twelve make a *solidus*, 240 a pound: counting words, not coins. Nobody may refuse a good penny (Frankfurt 794). Aquitaine's silver is mined at Melle.
- **Lawful prices** (Frankfurt 794): a *modius* of oats 1 d, barley 2, rye 3, wheat 4.
- **Toll.** A tenth at Dorestad, Quentovic and the Alpine gates, even from merchants holding the emperor's letter (Praeceptum negotiatorum, 828).
- **Forbidden.** Selling mail and swords to Slavs or Northmen (Thionville 805; Pîtres 864, on pain of death). Buying grain at harvest only to sell it dear (Nijmegen 806).
- **Fairs.** St Denis, a month from 9 October: wine, honey, cloth; Saxons and Frisians come (Power).
- **They sell** wine, grain, Mayen quernstones, Rhenish pots, sword blades, Loire salt. **They want** furs, wax, amber, English cloaks, Frisian cloth, slaves, and silk and spices from Venetians and Jews (N I.16, II.17).
- **Gifts go upward:** to the count before business, to the king at the May assembly. **Credit** is a pledge and a kinsman's oath; interest is sin.
- **Hosts.** Frisians have their own quarter at Mainz (burnt 886, Annals of Fulda).

## 6. Ships

A river people. Barges go down on the current and are towed back up by men on the bank (Miracles of St Goar) (?). Charles's war fleet was built "on the rivers that flow from Gaul and Germany into the Northern Ocean" (E 17); in 811 he inspected it at Boulogne and relit the old lighthouse (ARF). Sea carrying is left to Frisians. A Frank knows a raider "by the build of the ships and their speed" (N II.14).

## 7. Names

Two parts, often one from each parent: *Adal-* (noble), *Bert-* (bright), *Ger-* (spear), *Hild-* (battle), *Megin-* (might); men end in *-hard*, *-ric*, *-ulf*, *-win*; women in *-trud*, *-gard*, *-hild*, *-rada*. Short forms are everyday (Odo, Bero, Bodo, Bertha). Karl, Pippin, Hludowic and Lothar are the royal house's: never a boatman's. Aquitanians also bear Roman and saints' names.

- **Men.** Eggihard, Anselm, Hruodland (E 9); Meginher, Otulf, Unruoch, Burchard, Hatto, Rihwin, Ercangar, Bero, Hildiger, Rocculf (E 33); Drogo, Hugo (E 18); Odo, Robert, Ragenar, Ebolus (Abbo); Bodo, Wido, Gerbert, Frambert, Ermoin, Ragenold (Irm). Aquitanian and Gascon besides: Hunold, Waifar, Lupus (E 5).
- **Women.** Berthrada, Hildegard, Fastrada, Liutgard, Hruodrud, Bertha, Gisela, Theoderada, Hiltrud, Ruodhaid, Regina, Ethelind, Adaltrud (E 18); Adelaide, Atula, Guntrada, Berthaid (E 19); Ermentrude (Irm); Dhuoda (her own book); Irmingard (ARF 818); Richildis, Theutberga, Waldrada (AB); Gerberga (Nithard I.5); Ingelberga (Cluny's charter, 910).

## 8. How they speak

1. "Have the Frank for your friend, but not for your neighbor." A Greek proverb (E 16).
2. "What is the good of those little napkins? I cannot cover myself with them in bed ..." Charles, on the Frisians' short cloaks (N I.34).
3. "These ships are not filled with merchandise, but crowded with our fiercest enemies." (N II.14)
4. "One of you has given a Jew all this silver for a painted mouse." (N I.16)
5. "... let us go hunting and kill something ..." (N II.17)
6. "Oh the iron! Woe for the iron!" (N II.17)
7. "I will treat this my brother as one's brother ought to be treated ..." The oath of Strasbourg (Nithard, in Ogg).
8. "According to their custom the Northmen plundered eastern and western Frisia ..." (AX 846)
9. "The heathen from the North wrought havoc in Christendom as usual ..." (AX 849)
10. "I should deserve that my head be cut off and thrown to the dogs." Siegfred the Dane at Paris (Abbo, in Ogg).

## 9. Oaths, insults, jokes

- **Oaths** are sworn on relics. The words of 842 survive in both tongues: *Pro Deo amur et pro christian poblo* and *In Godes minna ind in thes christianes folches*, "for the love of God and the Christian people".
- **Insults with a price** (LS 30): "fox" or "hare" costs 3 shillings; saying a man threw away his shield, 3; "spy" or "perjurer", 15; calling a woman harlot without proof, 45.
- **A traveller's phrasebook** (Paris Conversations, 10th or 11th century): *Gimer min ros*, "give me my horse"; *Undes ars in tine naso*, "a dog's arse in your nose" (?).
- **Jokes** are stories with a moral: the bishop and the spiced mouse; courtiers made to hunt in the rain in their Pavia silks (N I.16, II.17).

## 10. Neighbours

- **Of Frisians:** sharp traders who sell less cloak for the same money (N I.34); useful subjects.
- **Of Saxons:** "a fierce people, given to the worship of devils" (E 7).
- **Of Northmen:** "the heathen", bought off at Paris in 845 (AB).
- **Of the English:** kin in faith; but about 790 Charles shut his ports to them (Gesta of Fontenelle), and in 796 complained that their cloaks had grown short (Alcuin, letter 100).
- **Of Aquitanians:** vain and fickle; about 1000 a northern monk mocks their shaved necks and short clothes (Glaber III.40) (?).
- **Neighbours of them:** the Greek proverb. The Northmen called Charles "the Hammer" (N II.14).

## 11. Burial

In the churchyard, in a shroud, head to the west, nothing in the grave; weapon graves ended about 700. The great lie in abbey churches: Charles was buried at Aachen "the same day that he died" (E 31). What counts is alms, masses and a name in the monks' book of the dead.

## 12. What a Frisian merchant must know

1. You are the king's subject, not a stranger. Say "the lord king"; after 800, "the lord emperor".
2. Pay the tenth at Dorestad. Elsewhere ask whether a toll is old: new ones are unlawful (Thionville 805).
3. Gift first, business after.
4. Sell long cloaks.
5. Take the new pennies; use the king's measures.
6. No Sunday trade; fast when they fast; swear only on relics.
7. Never carry mail or swords towards Danes or Slavs.
8. On the Rhine they half understand you. In the west (?) and Aquitaine they speak Romance: bring a clerk with Latin.
9. Never call an Aquitanian a Frank.
10. After 843, know which brother's land you are in.

## 13. Ten things they never say or do

1. Worship Wodan or any old god.
2. Say "France", "Germany" or "Holy Roman Empire". It is the kingdom of the Franks.
3. Say "Charlemagne" or "Vikings": he is the lord Charles; they are Northmen, Danes, heathen.
4. Talk of knights, tournaments, coats of arms or stone castles before about 1000.
5. Pay in shillings, pounds or gold: only pennies exist.
6. Sing of Roland: before about 1100 he is one line in Einhard.
7. Cite the Salic law against a woman ruling: that reading dates from 1317 (Ogg, note 67).
8. Lend at interest openly.
9. Call Paris the capital.
10. As Aquitanians: speak Frankish, or sing troubadour songs (the first is born in 1071).
