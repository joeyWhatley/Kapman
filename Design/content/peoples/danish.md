# The Danes of Jutland and the isles, 793 to 1066

For the writers of dialogue. Keys in square brackets: see Sources. DR = a stone's number in *Danmarks Runeindskrifter*; a stone named without a number is cited from its Wikipedia page. (?) = not checked against the source on 4 Oct 2026. Quotations are public-domain translations; runic lines are our own plain renderings.

## 1. Who they are, and how that changes

Danes (*Dani*); their land is *Denamearc* about 890 [Oht]: Jutland north of the Eider, Funen, Zealand, the small isles, Scania.

- **793 to 854.** One strong house facing the Franks: Sigfred, Godfred (killed 810), Hemming, Harald Klak against Godfred's sons, Horik I [RFA; Rim 24]. The Eider is the border from 811 [RFA 811]. Often two kings at once.
- **854 to c. 940.** Kings nearly leave the books [AF 873]; the fighting men are abroad, in Frisia, on the Seine, in England from 865 [ASC].
- **c. 940 to 1014.** The Jelling kings: Gorm; Harald Bluetooth, who "won all Denmark, and Norway, and made the Danes Christian" [DR 42]; Sven Forkbeard, who takes England in 1013 [ASC].
- **1014 to 1066.** A Christian kingdom with coin, bishops and new towns (Roskilde, Lund). Cnut rules England 1016 to 1035. Hedeby burns about 1050 and again in 1066 (?).

## 2. How they live

- **House.** A fenced farm in a village; one long house, people at one end and cattle at the other, a hearth in the middle [Vorbasse (?)].
- **Food.** Barley and rye as porridge and flat bread; butter, pork, beef, herring. Hedeby eats mainly fish [Tar]. Ale and mead daily; bought wine goes to their heads [ASC 1012].
- **Dress.** Men: tunic, trousers, a cloak pinned at the right shoulder. Women: a strap dress held by two oval brooches, until about 1000. Both paint their eyes [Tar]. Hair long over the eyes and shaved at the neck [Ælfric, c. 1000 (?)].
- **The year.** Sow and launch in spring; sail, trade and raid until harvest; slaughter in autumn; weave sailcloth, build boats and feast in winter.

## 3. Law and rank

No Danish law is written before about 1200: never quote the Law of Jutland (1241). What the period shows:

- The king decides with his chief men; his officer keeps Hedeby [Rim 24, 31].
- **Wergild and oath.** A slain Dane or Englishman: 8 half-marks of pure gold; a Danish freedman: 200 shillings. A man clears himself by oath with eleven or twelve others. A buyer of slaves, horses or oxen must know his warrantor; trade across a truce line needs hostages [AG 2 to 5].
- **Punishment** is the axe or slavery; a beating is the shame, and the condemned must look cheerful [Ad IV.6 (?)].
- **Ranks on the stones:** king; thegn (a settled landed man); dreng (a young man in a lord's or a ship's company); steward; skipper; smith; *gode* (priest-chief); partner (*felagi*) [DR 1, DR 209; Randbøl].
- **Slaves.** They sell captives, and one another [Ad IV.6 (?)]. Toke the smith raised a stone to the man "who gave him gold and freedom" [DR 58 (?)].
- **Women** raise stones in their own names and name their own kin: Asfrid Odinkar's daughter, Ragnhild Ulf's sister [DR 2, DR 230]. A wife may leave her husband [Tar]. **In trade:** no Danish woman merchant is named anywhere. Cloth and sailcloth are women's work, so a woman selling cloth is safe; a woman skipper is not.

## 4. Faith

- **Attested.** Thor is the only god called on in stone: "Thor hallow these runes" [DR 209; Sønder Kirkeby; Virring]. Hammers are worn as amulets and cut on stones [Læborg]. A *gode* serves a people or a holy place [DR 209; Helnæs]. Whoever sacrifices sets the beast on poles at his gate [Tar]. The sick sacrifice for health; the gods are angry when a strange god is let in; a church bell is unlawful [Rim 24, 31, 32]. At Lejre every ninth year, 99 men with horses, dogs and cocks: a German bishop's late hearsay [Thietmar I.17 (?)].
- **Later invention, or not Danish.** The ordered gods and Valhalla of the Icelandic Eddas (13th century); Saxo (c. 1200); Ragnar Lodbrok; the blood eagle; skull cups; horned helmets.
- **Conversion,** slowly and from the top. Harald Klak is baptised in 826 [Rim 7]. Churches at Hedeby about 850 and Ribe about 860 [Rim 24, 32]. Merchants take the sign of the cross to deal with Christians and put baptism off to the deathbed [Rim 24]. Harald Bluetooth is baptised about 965, after the priest Poppo carries hot iron [Widukind III.65 (?)].
- **Saints and feasts.** No Danish saint yet (St Cnut dies in 1086). Yule becomes Christmas; stones ask "Christ and Saint Michael" for help [Tillitse]. Hamburg's St Ansgar is kept on 3 February.

## 5. How they trade

- **Places.** Ribe in the west, a market since about 705, sailing to Frisia, England and Saxony [Ad IV.1 (?)]. Hedeby on the Schlei, 15 km overland from the Treene and the North Sea; Godfred moved Reric's merchants there in 808 [RFA 808].
- **Silver.** At Ribe small Woden-and-beast pennies pass by count from about 720 into the early 800s (?). Hedeby copies Dorestad's pennies from about 825. Elsewhere, above all from 850 to 1000, silver goes by weight: folding scale, lead weights, cut rings and dirhams, a knife-nick to test; about 25 g to the *øre*, 8 to the mark (?).
- **Partners and credit.** The *felag*: men who put goods or a ship together. "He owned a ship with Arne" [DR 68 (?)]. Written credit is not attested; a debt stands on witnesses.
- **Gifts and peace.** Ansgar won Horik "by gifts and by any possible kinds of service" [Rim 24]. In 873 King Sigfred asks that Danish traders may cross the Saxon border [AF 873].
- **They sell** slaves, cattle, hides, amber, furs, wax, whetstones. **They want** wine, Rhineland querns, glass, cloth, salt, silver, and Frankish blades and mail, whose sale abroad Charlemagne forbade in 779 (?).

## 6. Ships

Clinker-built of oak, one square woollen sail, a steering oar on the right side, oars.

- Ladby, c. 925 (?): a 21 m ship in a chief's grave.
- Skuldelev, sunk c. 1070 [Sk]: no. 1, a sea trader of 16 m and some 20 tons, crew 6 to 8; no. 2, a 30 m long ship built at Dublin in 1042.
- Alfred's ships were "not shaped either after the Frisian or the Danish model" [ASC 897].
- Kaupang to Hedeby: five days [Oht]. Hedeby to Truso: seven days and nights [Wul].
- Game: `norse_small_boat`, `norse_allround_ship`, `norse_longship`.

## 7. Names

**How they are built.** Two parts joined (Thor-kil, Ragn-hild) or, very often here, a short form: Toke, Tue, Tove, Åse. Toke is the commonest name on the stones. A man is "Toke, Gorm's son", a woman "Asfrid, Odinkar's daughter". Bynames are plain: Asgot Red-shield, Toste the Sharp [Rønninge; Glemminge]. No family names; no saints' names before about 1050.

**Men (25).** Godfred, Hemming, Sigfred, Harald [RFA 777 to 826]; Osfrid, Hebbi, Urm [RFA 811]; Horik, Hovi [Rim 24, 31]; Rorik [AF 850]; Gorm [DR 42]; Sven, Thorulf, Erik, Skarde [DR 1, DR 3]; Alle, Sote [DR 209]; Gunulf [DR 230]; Toke [DR 295]; Tue [Bække 1]; Roulf, Gudmund [Helnæs]; Odinkar [Gørlev 1]; Asgot [Rønninge]; Eskil [Tillitse].

**Women (25).** Thyre [DR 42]; Ragnhild [DR 209]; Asfrid [DR 2]; Tove [Sønder Vissing 1]; Vibrog [Bække 2]; Thorgunn [Randbøl]; Sasgerd [Skern 2]; Asbod [Ravnkilde 1]; Åse [Sjørring]; Thora [Tillitse]; Thjodvi [Gørlev 1]; Gyda [Øster Bjerregrav 2]; Tonna [Sövestad 2]; Kata [Rydsgård]; Ketilø [Stora Herrestad]; Gunhild [ASC 1045]; Estrid [Ad II.54 (?)]. Thinner ground, all (?): Gunnor (duchess at Rouen); Tola (wife of Cnut's man Urk); Arngunn (a comb from Lund); Santslaue (Cnut's sister, a Wendish name); Sigrid, Ingerid (later sagas); Botild (queen after 1086); Margareta (Estrid's baptismal name). For invented people use the first sixteen.

## 8. How they speak: ten lines

1. "These were the first ships of the Danish men that sought the land of the English nation." [ASC 787]
2. "...and swore with oaths on the holy bracelet, which they would not before to any nation..." [ASC 876]
3. "They were also much drunken; for there was wine brought them from the south." [ASC 1012]
4. "This year died King Hardacnute at Lambeth, as he stood drinking." [ASC 1042]
5. "Their King Godfred ... looked upon Saxony and Frisia as his provinces." [Ein 14]
6. "I have gone through this washing business here twenty times already ... but a sack like this is more fit for clodhoppers than for soldiers." [an old Northman at his baptism; Not II.19]
7. "...many who had already become Christians and had been baptized in Dorstadt or Hamburg..." [Hedeby; Rim 24]
8. "...they judged that it was to their advantage to be baptised at the end of their life." [Rim 24]
9. "...the port which they call Haddeby ... belongs to the Danes." [Oht]
10. "No need to fight if ye agree — we'll make a peace for gold." [the raiders' herald; Maldon]

Hávamál is Icelandic: never "a Danish saying". The Danes' own voice is the stones: short, proud, understated. "Few are now born better than he" [DR 230]. "He did not flee at Uppsala" [DR 279]. "They lie in London" [DR 337].

## 9. Oaths, insults, jokes

- **Oaths.** On a holy ring [ASC 876]. On their weapons; twelve great men of each side swear a peace [RFA 811]. Hostages stand behind the oath [ASC 876].
- **Insults.** The worst is sorcerer or unmanly: stones curse whoever harms them to become a *ræte* (outcast) or a *seid*-man [DR 209; Skern 2]. The best praise: "least a nithing of all men" [DR 68 (?)].
- **Jokes.** Understatement ("did not flee"). A rhyme-charm: "thistle, mistletoe, little chest" [Gørlev 1]. The Northman baptised twenty times for the clothes (line 6).

## 10. Neighbours

What Danes think shows in deeds; they left few words.

- **Franks:** rich, to be met as equals [Ein 14]. **Saxons:** the enemy at the wall, the Danevirke [RFA 808].
- **Frisians:** the men they trade with and the coast they tax: 200 ships and 100 pounds of silver in 810 [RFA 810 (?)]; Danish lords in Dorestad from 850 (Rorik) [AF].
- **English:** a land that pays from 991, then a crown, 1016 to 1042.
- **Wends:** tributaries, in-laws (Tove, Mistivoj's daughter, Harald's wife [Sønder Vissing 1]), later raiders.
- **Swedes and Norwegians:** kin to fight; Norway is "won" [DR 42].

What neighbours say: they are heathen men and oath-breakers [ASC]. They sell one another and will not weep, even for their dead [Ad IV.6 (?)]. Their singing is a growl like dogs [Tar]. They comb their hair daily and bathe on Saturdays [John of Wallingford, 13th century (?)].

## 11. Burial

Heathen: fire or earth, both. Stones set as a ship round the grave [Glavendrup; Tryggevælde]; a mound and a stone. The great lie in a ship (Ladby, with eleven horses). Christian, from about 965: churchyard, no goods, "God help his soul", a bridge built for the dead man's soul [Källstorp; Sandby 3].

## 12. What a Frisian merchant must know

1. Ribe is your door; its small pennies are the old Frisian kind.
2. Hedeby is the other: up the Eider and Treene, then 15 km by cart. A Dane says Hedeby; a Frank says Sliesthorp [RFA 804].
3. Outside those two markets your pennies are only silver. Carry a scale and let him nick the coin.
4. Give before you ask, and see the king's man first.
5. Your faith is no bar: Hedeby's best men were baptised in your own town [Rim 24]. Do not ring a bell.
6. Bring wine, querns, glass and cloth; blades sell best and are forbidden you. Take home slaves, hides, amber, furs and wax.
7. Do not plead, weep or shout. Ask for an oath before witnesses; for a large matter, a hostage.
8. The man who buys your wine in 830 may burn Dorestad in 834; from 850 your own lord is a Dane.

## 13. Never

1. Call themselves "Vikings". *Viking* is a thing done: "he died in the north on viking" [DR 334]. They are Danes, men of Jutland, Funen, Zealand or Scania.
2. Wear horned helmets, drink from skulls, cut blood eagles, or name Ragnar Lodbrok as a known man.
3. Swear or bless by Odin. On stone it is Thor only.
4. Speak of Valhalla or Ragnarok as common belief.
5. Take coins by count outside Ribe and Hedeby before about 1000.
6. Weep, beg, or accept a flogging [Ad IV.6 (?)].
7. Read a letter, cite a law book or sign anything: they cut runes and swear.
8. Say "the king of Denmark" as one man over one realm before Harald Bluetooth; say "a king of the Danes".
9. Name a Danish saint, a Danish bishop's seat before 948 (?), Danegeld before 991 or the Danelaw before 878.
10. Send a woman to sea as skipper or to war as a "shield-maiden" (Saxo, c. 1200), or toast with "skål".
