# The continental Saxons, 793 to 1066

A bible for the dialogue writers. Keys in square brackets: see Sources. (?) = not checked against a source on 4 Oct 2026. The English call them Old Saxons [ASC]; they are not the English.

## 1. Who they are, and how that changes

- **Before.** Westphalians, Angrians, Eastphalians, and Nordalbingians beyond the Elbe [ARF 775]. No king: "several lords", a war leader chosen by lot [Bede]. A yearly meeting at Marklo on the Weser, twelve men of each rank from every district [VLeb] (?).
- **772–804, the war.** Charles fells the Irminsul (772), beheads 4,500 at Verden (782), stands godfather to Widukind (785) [ARF]. In 793, the game's first summer, Saxons destroy Count Theoderic's force at Rüstringen and burn churches (`chronicle_793.json`). In 804 ten thousand are carried off "with their wives and children" [Einh]; Nordalbingia goes to the Abodrites.
- **804–850.** Bishops at Bremen, Münster, Paderborn and five more; Corvey abbey 822; Hamburg 831, burned by Danes 845 [Rimb 16]. In 841 the Stellinga, free men and liti, rise for their fathers' heathen law [Nith] and are cut down.
- **850–1024.** Liudolf's house holds the march and founds nunneries (Gandersheim 852). Henry I is king in 919, Otto I emperor in 962. A Saxon monk now calls Franks and Saxons *quasi una gens*, as it were one people [Wid I.15].
- **1024–1066.** Frankish kings again; Billung dukes; Bremen great under Archbishop Adalbert [Adam] (?).

In 793 a Saxon is a beaten man, baptised under threat. His great-grandson is the emperor's countryman.

## 2. How they live

- **House.** A long timber hall, cattle under the same roof; sunken weaving huts beside it (Warendorf) (?). No towns in 793: forts, then bishops' seats.
- **Food.** Rye, oats, barley, honey, beef, pork; ale and mead. Fines are priced in a yearling ox, oats, rye, "clean barley", honey [CS 11]. Meat eaten in Lent out of contempt is death [CPS 4].
- **Dress.** Cloak, hair loose over the shoulders, long spear, small shield, a big knife (*sahs*) at the hip [Wid I.7, I.9]. Straw hats in summer: a whole army wore them in 946 [Wid III.2].
- **The year.** The ox is stalled in autumn, out in spring [CS 11]. Horses and bees are wealth: 300 horses was the old yearly tribute [ARF 758]; stealing a horse, or a hive from inside a fence, is death [LS 29–30].

## 3. Law and rank

- **Ranks.** *Edhilingui*, *frilingi*, *lazzi*: nobles, free, half-free liti [Nith]; slaves below.
- **Wergild.** Noble 1,440 solidi, litus 120, a slave killed by a noble 36 [LS 14–17]; the free man's is not written (240?).
- **Solidus.** A count, not a coin: a yearling ox or twelve pennies; in the south 40 measures of oats or 20 of rye, in the north 30 or 15 [CS 11].
- **Oaths.** A man clears himself "with twelve hands", his own and eleven helpers' [LS 16–18]. A knowing false oath is death [LS 21].
- **Court.** The all-Saxon meeting is banned; "each count shall hold judicial assemblies" [CPS 34]. Neighbours may burn the house of a man who defies the court [CS 8].
- **Slaves** are given and sold [LS 62].
- **Women.** Bride-price 300 solidi to her kin [LS 40]. A widow passes to the guardianship of her stepson or brother-in-law [LS 42]. Daughters inherit only where there are no sons [LS 41, 44]. A Westphalian wife takes half of what the pair earned; elsewhere only her dower [LS 48]. No source shows a Saxon woman merchant; noblewomen run estates, as Liutbirg did for Gisla [VLiut].

## 4. Faith

- **Attested.** The Irminsul, a great wooden trunk under the open sky, "the universal column" [TSA], with gold and silver by it [ARF 772]. Three gods in the baptismal vow: Thunaer, Uoden, Saxnote [Vow]. Vows at springs, trees and groves, meals for "demons", burning the dead, mounds, diviners, fear of man-eating witches [CPS 6–7, 21–23]; grove rites (*nimidas*), funeral feasts (*dadsisas*), need-fire, idols of dough and rags [Ind].
- **Invented later.** Rudolf's other details come from Tacitus. Ostara (Grimm, 1835), Krodo (1492), the Externsteine, the Irminsul as world tree, Widukind's white horse, "Wotan": none is in a source of the time.
- **Conversion** by the sword: death for refusing baptism [CPS 8], a tithe from every rank [CPS 17], softened in 797 [CS]. Preachers: Lebuin, Willehad, Liudger. About 830 the *Heliand* tells the Gospel as a lord and his thegns [Hel].
- **Saints.** Vitus at Corvey from 836 (15 June): since he came, the Franks' fortunes waned and the Saxons' grew [Wid I.34]. Liborius at Paderborn (836), Alexander at Wildeshausen (851) [TSA], Pusinna at Herford (860). Feasts in the law: Easter, Pentecost, Christmas, St Mary, St John, St Peter, St Martin [LS 23].

## 5. How they trade

- **Money.** No mint in 793: debts run in cattle, grain and honey, twelve Frankish pennies to the solidus [CS 11]. Mints at Corvey 833 (?), Bremen 888 (?). Saxon pennies from c. 930–940 (Magdeburg): nameless, high-rimmed, bitten to test, weighed as plain silver by Slavs. Otto-Adelheid pennies of Harz silver soon after 983 [Coin].
- **Where.** From 805 trade with Slavs only at Bardowick, Magdeburg and other posts, and no arms (`chronicle_793.json`). Hamburg men go to Sliaswich [Rimb 24].
- **They sell** horses, cattle, hides, rye, honey, wax, flax, oak, bacon; later salt, Harz silver, Slav captives (?). **They want** wine, quernstones, Rhenish pots, cloth and, once baptised, books, relics, church plate (`goods_793.json`).
- **Bargains.** A false sworn claim falls to two or three witnesses of the same province, "and more is better" [LS 39]. Inherited land may go only to church or king, or be sold in famine [LS 62]. On hosts, credit and merchants' gifts the sources are silent.

## 6. Ships

River people. Logboats, and barges like the Bremen boat "Karl": oak, about 20 m by 2.3 m, flat-bottomed, clinker sides pegged with wood, moss caulking, no mast, poled; a plank felled in 808 [Karl]. Game: `logboat`, `logboat_expanded`, `river_barge`, `skiff_punt`. Frisians carry their sea trade; yet Old Saxony beat a Danish fleet twice in 885, Frisians beside them [ASC].

## 7. Names

Two stems: Liud-, Thiad- (people), Hathu- (battle), Thanc- (thought), Hrot- (fame), with -olf, -mar, -ric, -bern for men and -hild, -burg, -gard, -mod, -suith for women. Kin share a stem (Liudulf, Liudgard). Short forms: Oddo, Hessi, Oda. No surnames.

**Men.** Widukind, Abbio, Hessi, Brun [ARF 775–785]; Hathagat, Liudulf, Oddo, Thiatmar, Thiadric, Immed, Reginbern [Wid I]; Thancmar, Wichmann, Herimann, Gero, Sigifrid, Asic, Maincia [Wid II]; Ecberht, Hosed, Warin, Bovo [Wid III]; Unwan, Bernhard [VLiut]; Waltbert [TSA].

**Women.** Mahthild, Liudgard, Gerberga [Wid]; Oda, Aeda, Christina [Hrotsvit, *Primordia*]; Hrotsvit, Rikkardis [her preface]; Hathumod [Agius]; Liutbirg, Gisla, Bilihild, Hruothild [VLiut]; Haduwy [Herford, 860]; Frederuna [Kemnade, c. 960]; Imma (?); Hathui, Hatheburg, Godila, Swanhild, Kunigunde [Thiet] (?); Reinhild [Vita Mathildis] (?); Marcsuith (Schildesche, 939) (?); Helmburg (Fischbeck, 955) (?); Ida [Vita Idae] (?).

## 8. How they speak in the sources

In public-domain translation:

1. "We are of one blood and one bone." Saxons to the English, via Boniface c. 738 [Kylie].
2. "Those Ancient Saxons have no king, but several lords that rule their nation." [Bede]
3. "...on whomsoever the lot falls, him they follow and obey during the war." [Bede]
4. "No war ever undertaken by the Frank nation was carried on with such persistence and bitterness..." [Einh]
5. "...had it not been for the faithlessness of the Saxons." [Einh]
6. "...shall have wished to remain a pagan, let him be punished by death." [CPS 8]
7. "...to the church cemeteries and not to the mounds of the pagans." [CPS 22]
8. "We have forbidden that all the Saxons shall hold public assemblies in general..." [CPS 34]
9. "...the Saxons had the victory. There were the Frieslanders with them." [ASC]
10. "Some of them they sold to pagans, whilst others they enslaved, or sold to other Christians." [Rimb 38]

Their own words have no public-domain English (?); quote the original, gloss afresh:

- *ec forsacho ... Thunaer ende Uoden ende Saxnote*: I forsake Thunaer and Uoden and Saxnote [Vow].
- *that ist thegnes cust, that hie mid is frâhon samad fasto gistande*: a thegn's choice is to stand fast beside his lord [Hel 3995–96].
- *vincere velle aut certe vivere nolle*: to win, or else not to live [Wid I.9].

## 9. Oaths, insults, jokes

- **Oath.** Nordalbingians chained Christian captives who had fled the heathen and sold them. Ansgar denied the sellers oath and witnesses and left them to "the judgment of Almighty God" [Rimb 38].
- **Insult.** Hugh the Great, 946: he could swallow seven Saxon spears at one gulp. Otto: he would bring more straw hats than Hugh had ever seen [Wid III.2].
- **Joke.** A starving Saxon sells his gold to a Thuringian for a lapful of earth. Both camps laugh. He strews it thin over the fields and holds them as land bought [Wid I.5]. Hrotsvit renders her own name "the strong voice of Gandersheim".

## 10. Neighbours

- **Franks:** conquerors, tithe-takers; by 950 "one people".
- **Frisians:** fellow rebels in 793 (`chronicle_793.json`), shipmates in 885 [ASC].
- **English:** kin, and their preachers; villagers killed the two Hewalds and their lord burned the village [Bede].
- **Danes:** Widukind's refuge in 777 [ARF]; later the burners of Hamburg.
- **Abodrites:** the king's allies, given Saxon land in 804; later tributaries.

## 11. Burial

Heathen: burning and burial side by side, mounds, horses in their own graves (Liebenau) (?). From the 780s burning is death and Christians go to the churchyard [CPS 7, 22]. By c. 850 rows lie west to east without goods (?).

## 12. What a Frisian merchant must know

1. Until 804 Saxony is a war: an army most summers, districts emptied.
2. Bring pennies; expect oxen, rye, honey, horses at the law's rates [CS 11].
3. Take two or three local witnesses [LS 39]. Never buy inherited land [LS 62].
4. Ask whose man he is: a lord answers for his litus [LS 50].
5. Three solidi stolen is death; so is one horse [LS 29, 35].
6. Keep Lent and Sunday in plain sight [CPS 4, 18].
7. No swords or mail past Bardowick after 805.
8. They half-understand Frisian (`npc_text_encounters.md` 2.6); you meet them at Daventre.
9. Beyond the Elbe a runaway is goods [Rimb 38].
10. They want relics and books above all (`goods_793.json`).

## 13. Ten things never to write

1. A Saxon king, or a duke before c. 850; Widukind is a Westphalian noble.
2. Odin, Thor, Valhalla, rune magic, "Wotan": only Thunaer, Uoden, Saxnote are known.
3. Ostara, Krodo, the Externsteine, the white horse.
4. Open heathen worship after 785 without fear.
5. A free all-Saxon assembly after 782.
6. Saxon sea-raiders or longships: four centuries too late.
7. Saxon pennies before c. 930.
8. "Germany", "Lower Saxony", the Hanse; Saxony meaning Dresden.
9. "Anglo-Saxon" for them; the "Saxons" of the Saint-Denis fair are English.
10. Love of King Charles from a commoner in 793; hatred of him in 960.
