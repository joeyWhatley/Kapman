# The Svear, the Götar and the Gotlanders, 793-1066

For the writers of dialogue. `(?)` = not checked tonight. **late** = written c. 1220-1280 (law books, Guta saga, Snorri): custom and colour, not record. Rune stones by Rundata number (U344 = U 344); most are of 1000-1100. Game ids: people `svea`, `gute`; ports `birka`, `helgo`, `uppsalir`, `paviken`.

## 1. Who they are, and how that changes

Three peoples, not one kingdom.

- **Svear** (Sueones, Sweon): round the Mälar bay, then an arm of the sea. Market: Birka (c. 750-975). Kings' seat and great thing: Uppsalir.
- **Götar** (Beowulf's Geats): inland farmers of Västergötland and Östergötland, with their own law.
- **Gotlanders** (Gutar): island farmers who are also the merchants. No town, only beach harbours like Paviken. Own tongue, own law.
- **793-829:** heathen. Birka men sail to Dorestad; dirhams arrive from about 800.
- **829-865:** Anskar at Birka under kings Björn and Olof: a few Christians, no lasting church (R11, 26-28).
- **c. 890:** Gotland, Öland and Blekinge are "subject to the Sweons" (line 9). Gotland pays 60 marks of silver a year and goes toll-free (GS, **late**).
- **936-980:** Archbishop Unni dies at Birka (Adam I 62). Birka is empty by 975; Sigtuna is laid out about 980.
- **c. 995-1022:** Olof Skötkonung, king of Svear and Götar, baptised about 1008; a bishop at Skara (Adam II 58).
- **1022-1066:** Anund Jacob, Emund, Stenkil. Men take geld in England (U344), serve in Greece (U112), die with Ingvar in the east, 1041 (Sö179). Uppsala still sacrifices (Adam IV 27).

## 2. How they live

- **House:** a long timber hall, hearth in the floor, smoke through the roof.
- **Food and drink:** barley bread and porridge, pork, pike, herring; ale and mead. Wine is bought and rare: Frideburg of Birka kept some three years for her deathbed (R20).
- **Dress:** wool and linen. Women: a strap dress on two oval brooches. Rich Birka men: eastern coats with silk bands and rows of small bronze buttons (Geijer 1938) (?), the one place a button is right.
- **The year:** winter is for sledge travel and furs. The market and thing at Uppsala fall in "Goe month" (late February); after the conversion, at Candlemas (Hkr, St Olaf 76, **late**). Ships go out in May and are home by autumn.

## 3. Law and rank

- **Rank:** king, jarl, lawman, free farmer (*bonde*), freedman, thrall. At Birka the king's man is the prefect (R11).
- **The king** is taken and can be cast out: "Sveær egho konong at taka ok sva vrækæ" (VgL Rb 1, **late**). King Olof says as much in 852 (line 1). The thing decides by voice and asks the gods by lots (R19, 27).
- **Wergild, Västergötland (late):** a Västgöte 21 marks (9 to the heir, 12 to the kin); a Svea 13⅓; a Dane or Norwegian 9; "a southerner or an Englishman" 6; a thrall 3 (VgL Md 1, 5).
- **Gotland (late):** a Gotlander 3 marks of gold (24 of silver); any other man 10 of silver; a slave 4½ in coin (GL 15).
- **Law in runes:** the Forsa ring (Hs7, 10th century): one ox and two öre, doubled at each repeat.
- **Slaves** are bought, born or taken; on Gotland they serve a term (GL 2).
- **Women** inherit after sons and through their children (U29), raise stones, build bridges for souls (Sö101, U489) and run farms (Vs24). Frideburg is rich in her own right; her daughter Catla carries her money to Dorestad (R20). Scales lie in women's graves at Birka (?).

## 4. Faith

**Attested:** lots to learn which god is angry (R18, 27, 30); vows and sacrifices in danger (R19); a dead king taken in as a god (line 4); Thor asked to hallow a stone, "þur uiki" (Vg150); holy places called *vi*; groves and mounds (GL 4); on Gotland a shared sacrificial meal, *suþnautar*, men who boil together (GS). Uppsala: Thor, Wodan and Fricco, a feast every ninth year, men and beasts hung in a grove; Christians buy themselves off (Adam IV 26-27). Adam never saw it.

**Later invention, or unsafe:** the golden temple (none has been dug); the Yngling kings; Tjelvar; Sigrid the Haughty; the blood-eagle; speeches about Valhalla.

**Conversion** comes by trade: Birka men were baptised at Dorestad (line 2). From about 1020 Uppland stones carry crosses and "God help his spirit". Gotland's merchants brought priests home; the first church was burnt; Olaf of Norway lay at Akergarn in 1029 (GS).

**Feasts by 1066:** Candlemas, Easter, John's mass, Olaf's mass (from the 1030s), Michaelmas, Yule.

## 5. Trade

- **Silver by weight:** folding scales in a box, small weights, coins cut and pecked. Mark = 8 öre = 24 örtugar. Sigtuna strikes pennies c. 995-1030, then stops.
- **Birka** "contained many rich merchants"; ransomed for 100 pounds of silver, and the Danes said each merchant had more (R19).
- **Gotland:** over 700 silver hoards; Spillings alone held 67 kg.
- **They sell** furs, iron, wax, honey, slaves from the east. **They want** silver, Rhine wine, glass, blades, fine cloth, salt.
- **Partners and hosts:** the *félag* (one purse); 11th-century "guild-brothers" (U379). Refusing a traveller is shameful (Adam IV 21). Gifts first: Ormika gave King Olaf twelve rams and got two cups and an axe (GS).
- **Credit:** none attested. A deal is weighed and witnessed.

## 6. Ships

Clinker-built oak, one square sail, a side rudder. **Mälar boat:** 9-10 m, four or five pairs of oars, light enough to drag (the Viks boat, 9.6 by 2.2 m, c. 1050). **Sea trader:** the Äskekärr ship, Göta river, c. 900-920, about 16 m and 20 tons, sail only. Gotland's picture stones show chequered sails. In the east boats are hauled past the Dnieper falls (G280). A coast guard is "vikinga vorðr", the watch against raiders (U617).

## 7. Names

One name, with the father's when needed: "Ginnlaug, Holmgeir's daughter" (U617). Names have two parts and kin share one: Rodvisl and Rodälv name a son Rodfos (G134). Men end in -björn, -fast, -sten, -ulf, -vald, -mund; women in -lög, -vi, -frid, -gärd, -dis, -älv. Bynames are blunt: Strabain, "straw-legs".

**Men:** Björn, Anund, Olof, Hergeir, Erik (R11, 19, 26); Varin, Vämod (Ög136); Alrik (Vg119); Ingvar, Harald (Sö179); Ulf (U344); Jarlabanke (U127); Ingefast (U142); Östen (U136); Ragnvald (U112); Torsten, Sven, Öpir (U104); Torbjörn, Torkel (U379); Assur (Ög81); Holmgöt (Vs24); Djärv, Värmund (Sigtuna box); Ofeg (Hs7). Gotland: Rodvisl (G134); Hegbjörn, Vivil, Ravn (G280); Ottar, Liknat (G113); Avair, Botair, Ormika (GS).

**Women:** Frideburg, Catla (R20); Tola (Sö179); Gerlög, Inga (U29); Estrid (U136); Fastvi (U112); Ingetora (U104); Kättilö, Jorunn (U142); Ragnfrid (U148); Gyrid, Gudlög (U328); Gillög (U489); Ginnlög (U617); Sigrid (Sö101); Odindisa (Vs24); Torgärd (Ög81); Tyrvi (Vg150); Tora (Vg67); Ingegerd, Holmfrid (Hkr). ## 8. How they speak: ten lines

1. "the control of public business of every kind should rest with the whole people and not with the king." King Olof, 852 (R26).
2. "Some of us who on various occasions have been to Dorstadt have of our own accord adopted this form of religion" An old man at the Birka thing (R27).
3. "we cannot be sure that our gods will be favourably disposed" The same (R27).
4. "if you desire to have more gods and we do not suffice, we will agree to summon your former King Eric" The gods' message (R26).
5. "there are here but few poor" Frideburg, sending her alms to Dorestad (R20).
6. "they drowned five kings in a morass at the Mula-thing" Thorgny the lawman, warning his king (Hkr, St Olaf 81, **late**).
7. "the burning of the dead continued, long after that time, to be the custom of the Swedes" (Hkr, preface).
8. "for these Swedes to be sitting at home killing their sacrifices" A Norwegian king's jibe (Hkr, Olaf Tryggvason 114, **late**).
9. "Gotland, all which territory is subject to the Sweons" Wulfstan, c. 890.
10. "so I deem it sure / that the Swedish folk will seek us home" A Geat (Beowulf 2999-3001).

In runes (gloss ours): "Þæiʀ foru drængila fiarri at gulli", they went like men far after gold (Sö179).

## 9. Oaths, insults, jokes

- **Oath:** "Sva se mær guþ hull", so may the gods be kind to me: a heathen plural kept in Christian law (VgL Md 1).
- **Insults at law**, each fined 48 örtugar (VgL Rb 5): "bitch-whelp"; "I saw you run from one man with your spear on your back"; to a woman, "I saw you ride the pen-gate, hair loose, in a troll's shape, when night and day were equal."
- **Curses:** "God betray those who betrayed him" (G134); on a stone-breaker, may he become an *arg* woman (Vg67); on a thief, the raven grows fat on him (Sigtuna scale-box).
- **Jokes:** *The player's right* (VgL): a wounded fiddler gets a wild heifer with a greased tail; if he can hold her she is his, to enjoy "as a dog enjoys grass". Gotlanders in Greece asked to stay "for the waxing and waning of the moon", then explained that this is for ever (GS).

## 10. Neighbours

**They of others.** Danes: rivals who can be hired; an exiled king led them against Birka (R19). Curonians: rebels made to pay "half a pound of silver for each individual man" (R30). Franks and Frisians: wine, blades, baptism.

**Others of them.** Franks: the "Rhos" at the emperor's court in 839 proved to be Sueones and were held as likely spies (Annals of St Bertin). Bremen: hospitable, unmoved by gold and furs, as many wives as a man can keep (Adam IV 21) (?). An Arab on the Volga, 922: tall as palms, dirty (Ibn Fadlan).

## 11. Burial

The Svear burn the dead with their gear under a low mound (line 7). The great lie unburnt in boats (Valsgärde) or, at Birka, in timber chambers with weapons, horses and scales. Christians lie unburnt with little. After 1000 the rich raise a rune stone by the road, not on the grave. On Gotland a picture stone stands for the dead.

## 12. What a Frisian merchant must know

1. They know Dorestad (lines 2, 5). In the 11th century Sigtuna's sworn merchants are "the Frisians' guild" (U379), though their names are Norse.
2. It is far: "nearly twenty days in a ship" (R26); the convoy of 829 was stripped by pirates (R10).
3. Bring scales. Before 995 no coin passes by count.
4. Your life is cheap: 6 marks in Västergötland, 10 on Gotland, against 21 and 24 for their own. Find a host first.
5. The king settles nothing alone. Wait for the thing, and the lots.
6. A hammer and a cross can hang on one neck. Mock neither.

## 13. Never

1. "Sweden" as one realm before 1000, or "Swede" for a Gotlander.
2. "Viking" for themselves: it is the raider they watch for.
3. A king who commands. He asks.
4. A bishop, a bell or a stone church among the Svear before 1000.
5. Visby, Stockholm or Lödöse; Birka after 975; Sigtuna before 980.
6. Coin counted out before 995, or Swedish coin after 1030.
7. Saints Erik, Eskil, Botvid or Bridget; Lucia with candles.
8. Horned helmets, skull cups, blood-eagles, "Valhalla awaits".
9. The golden temple described by one who has seen it.
10. Mock-saga speech, "skål", thee and thou; "Rus" or "Varangian" used at home.
