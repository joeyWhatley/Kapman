# The Frisians, 793 to 1066

The player's own people, from the Zwin (Sincfal) to the Weser. Keys in square brackets: see Sources. (?) = not checked against a source on 4 Oct 2026. Quotations are public-domain translations; Latin glosses are ours.

## 1. Who they are, and how that changes

- **Three Frisias** in their own law: West (Zwin to Vlie), Middle (Vlie to Lauwers), East (Lauwers to Weser), each with its own fines and shilling [LF I].
- **793.** No king since Radbod (d. 719): counts rule for King Charles. West and Middle are two generations Christian. The East, conquered in 785, rose about 793 under Unno and Eilrat and burned its churches; Liudger was back within the year [VL I.22]. That summer Saxons wiped out Count Theoderic's force in Rüstringen (`chronicle_793.json`).
- **810 to 885, the Danish time.** Godfred's 200 ships take 100 pounds of silver [RFA 810 (?)]; Dorestad is sacked from 834 (?); Danish lords hold Frisian counties (?). Frisians win their own fights: Oostergo 873, the West 876, Norden 884 [AF].
- **After 885.** Gerulf, "count of the Frisians" [Reg], founds the house later called Holland. Tiel, Deventer, Utrecht and Stavoren take dead Dorestad's trade. Sea dykes begin about 1000 (?); before that every house stands on a mound.

## 2. How they live

- **House.** A mound (terp) of turf and dung on the salt marsh; on it long turf-walled houses, people at one end, cattle at the other (?).
- **Water.** "The want of fresh water, which throughout almost all Frisia occasions the greatest difficulty both to men and to beasts" [Wb 9].
- **Food.** Butter, cheese, porridge, honey, beef, fish; ale and mead. "Frisia pours neither oil nor wine" [Alc].
- **Cattle** measure land: "pasture for forty beasts", "land of ten oxen" [TF 57, 72].
- **Dress.** Wool; their cloaks, "white, grey, red and blue" [Not II.9], are a gift for the king of Persia, and for the lower servants of the palace at Easter [Not II.21].
- **The year.** Easter to Martinmas the boats are out; summer is hay and shearing; winter is floods, stalled cattle, the loom and the nets.

## 3. Law and rank

- **Ranks:** noble, free, *litus* (half-free, under a lord), slave.
- **Wergild** in old pennies: noble 11 pounds, free 5 1/2, litus 2 pounds 9 ounces, slave 1 pound 4 1/2 ounces [LF XV]; two thirds to the heir, a third to the kin. The law's shilling is 3 pennies in the Middle, 2 1/2 West, 2 East [LF Add. III], not the Frankish twelve.
- **Proof** is the oath on relics with helpers of your own rank, "twelve-handed" for a killing. Perjury costs your wergild, and a second to buy back your hand [LF X]. Cases come to a public meeting before judges [LF XIV.4]; peace-money (*freda*) goes to the king.
- **Ordeals** [LF XIV]: Middle, lots at the altar (two twigs, *tenos*, one cut with a cross, wrapped in clean wool, drawn by a priest or an innocent boy); West, boiling water; East, the duel.
- **Slavery.** A free man may give himself up as a litus "from need" [LF XI]. Selling a slave to heathens costs your own wergild; selling a free man abroad is paid as a killing [LF XVII, XXI].
- **Women.** Same wergild as a man of her rank [LF Add. V]. A woman "who makes *fresum*" (the cloth) has her hand valued a quarter higher, like a harper's or a goldsmith's [LF Add. III]. Women give land in their own names; Diethilt's rent is "six ounces or twenty cloaks" [TF 72]. No source shows a woman skipper: write her as owner, weaver, lender, host.

## 4. Faith

- **Attested of the old religion** (all from Christian pens): shrines [LF V]; a god Fosite on an island towards the Danes, his spring drawn only in silence [VL I.22]; the shrine-robber led to the sand the tide covers, his ears slit, gelded and "offered to the gods whose temples he violated" [LF Add. XI]; a newborn drowned only before it has tasted food (Liafburg was saved by a neighbour's honey) [VL I.6-7].
- **Not attested:** any Frisian naming Woden, Thunor or a hall of the slain. Radbod at the font is a monks' tale (?). **Later invention:** Stavo, Fosta, Meda (?), the Oera Linda Book, Fosite as "god of law".
- **Conversion:** Willibrord from 690 (Utrecht); Boniface killed near Dokkum, 5 June 754; Liudger (d. 809), the first Frisian bishop; the East baptised under arms in the 780s.
- **Bernlef**, blind singer of Helewyret, "loved by his neighbours because he was affable and knew well how to sing the deeds of the men of old and the wars of kings" [VL I.25].
- **Feasts:** Martin 11 November (Utrecht), Boniface 5 June, Willibrord 7 November.

## 5. How they trade

- **Sell:** cloaks, salt (?), fish, hides, cheese; carried: Rhine wine, quern stones, pots, glass, slaves. **Want:** grain, wine, timber (the marsh has no trees), iron, furs, amber.
- **A price:** in 945 Fulda took from its Frisian lands 35 pounds of tested pennies or 600 grey cloaks [TF c.37]: 14 pennies a cloak.
- **Coin.** Frankish pennies, 240 to the pound, struck at Dorestad; a tenth in toll there (?). Rents run in ounces of silver or in cloaks [TF]. Under Danish lords silver also goes by weight (Westerklief hoards (?)).
- **Abroad:** a Frisian quarter, "the best part of Mainz" [AF 886]; York, until a Frisian merchant killed an earl's son and all fled [VL I.11]; a Frisian guild at Sigtuna, 11th century [U 379].
- **Hosts.** A merchant away needs one: "black Hrotberct" of Dorestad, the "greedy merchant", gave Alcuin's verses no roof [Alc].
- **Pooled silver.** At Tiel about 1020 merchants pool money, share it out for gain and feast on it [Alp (?)].

## 6. Ships

- Alfred's ships "were not shaped either after the Frisian or the Danish model" [ASC 897].
- The Utrecht ship: 17.8 m, a hollowed oak log widened with planks, felled 997-1030, about 13 tons [Utr]; Dorestad's pennies show the same round keel-less hull (?). For the flats, flat bottoms that sit upright at the ebb (?). One mast, a square sail, a side rudder; towed upriver on a long rope [Alc].
- Game: `logboat_expanded`, `river_barge`, `north_sea_merchant_ship`. The player's `gislinge` is a later Danish find standing in: nobody names her type.

## 7. Names

Two stems: Thiad-grim, Liaf-burg, Folc-lint. Men's endings: -grim, -braht, -bod, -ger, -gisl, -lef, -rad, -ulf. Women's: -burg, -gard, -hild, -lint, -suit, -trud, -birn, -wih. Short forms abound (Ado, Abba, Bubo, Unno, Tetta, Imma); a man may carry both: "Wrssing, called Ado". No surnames. Fulda's scribes wrote in their own German (Diethilt for Thiadhild).

**Men.** Radbod, Aldgisl (kings) [Bede V.9, V.19]; Bubo (duke, d. 734) [Fred (?)]; Wrssing, Nothgrim, Thiadgrim, Nothrad, Willibraht, Thiadbraht, Liudger, Hildigrim, Bernlef, Landric, Unno, Eilrat [VL]; Abba (count at Dokkum) [Wb 9]; Hrotberct (merchant of Dorestad) [Alc]; Wlemar, Saxmund (lawmen) [LF]; Albdag (count, 873) [AF]; Gerulf, Gardulf, Waltger [Reg 885, 898]; Wulfheard, Ebbe [ASC 897].

**Women.** Theudesinda (Radbod's daughter, 711) [LHF 50 (?)]; Adalgard, Adelburg, Liafburg, Heriburg, Meinsuit [VL]; Gisuhild, Habuku [Run]; Tetta, Folclint, Diethilt, Imma, Uodalwih, Geiltrud, Frigart and her daughters Friduwic and Friburch, Sigibirn, Engilburc, Althilt, Helmsuint, Hera daughter of Habo [TF 7-93]; Brunihilt [TF 96 (?)]; Wilfsit (nun at Egmond) (?); Geva (wife of Count Dirk I) (?).

## 8. How they speak in the sources

1. "Dear is the welcome guest to the Frisian wife, when the vessel stands ... she calls him in, washes his weedy garment, and to him gives new raiment." [Exeter Gnomic Verses, Thorpe]
2. "Fin, Folcwald's son, the race of Fresns" [Widsith, Thorpe]
3. "What is the good of those little napkins? I cannot cover myself with them in bed ..." [Charlemagne, on Frisians selling short cloaks at the long price; Not I.34]
4. "Frisian robes, white, grey, red and blue" [Not II.9]
5. "They were not shaped either after the Frisian or the Danish model" [ASC 897]
6. "Wulfheard, a Frieslander; Ebb, a Frieslander, and Ethelere, a Frieslander" [ASC 897]
7. "he sold him at London, to a Freson" [the captive Imma, 679; Bede IV.22]
8. "go with the money to Dorstadt. There are there many churches, priests, and clergy, and a multitude of poor people." [Frideburg of Birka to her daughter Catla; Rim 20]
9. "Some of us who on various occasions have been to Dorstadt have of our own accord adopted this form of religion ..." [a Swede at Birka; Rim 27]
10. "traders both from here and from Dorstadt freely sought to visit this place" [Sliaswic; Rim 24]

## 9. Oaths, insults, jokes

- **Oaths.** "I will swear alone. You, if you dare, deny my oath and fight me" (*Ego solus iurare volo. Tu, si audes, nega sacramentum meum, et armis mecum contende*) [LF XI.3]. An accuser swears holding his man by the hem of his cloak [LF XIV.5].
- **Insults.** The law prices deeds, not words: seizing a man by the hair, 2 shillings; ducking him, 4; a scar seen from twelve feet, *wlitiwam*, three times 4 [LF XXII, Add.]. No Frisian insult word survives: invent none.
- **Jokes.** Alcuin greets Utrecht's bishop as *vaccipotens*, "mighty in cows" [Alc]. At Tiel the man who tells dirty stories loudest over the morning drink is most praised [Alp (?)]. A comb's runes say Aib made it for Habuku [Run].

## 10. Neighbours

- **Franks** are the king, the count, the toll and the Church. They say Frisians cheat on cloaks [Not], love silver more than verse and are rich only in cows [Alc].
- **English** are near in speech (?); Frisians trade in London and York and die in Alfred's ships.
- **Danes** want tribute. Oostergo's answer in 873: "we owe tribute to none but King Louis and his sons". Yet a baptised Northman "who had long lived among the Frisians" led them that day [AF].
- **Saxons:** the next marsh east, fellow rebels in 793.
- What Frisians said of any of them is not recorded. Keep it dry and about prices.

## 11. Burial

Before baptism both burning and burying, in graveyards on the mounds, with brooches, beads and combs, sometimes a horse or a dog (Hogebeintum, Oosterbeintum (?)). The law fines the man who digs up the dead and takes something [LF Add. III.75]. After baptism: graves without goods round a wooden church on the mound (?).

## 12. What a merchant must know among his own

1. Ask which Frisia you stand in: fines change at the Vlie and the Lauwers.
2. Bring kin: without oath-helpers you cannot clear yourself.
3. Never swear lightly on relics.
4. Keep Sunday: 4 shillings, 12 beyond the Lauwers [LF XVIII].
5. Do not block the river's "public way": 12 shillings [LF Add. VI].
6. Pull a man out of the water and you are owed 4 [LF XXII.87].
7. A borrowed thing goes back as it came [LF Add. X].
8. Carry fresh water.
9. Debts run in cloaks or cattle as readily as silver.
10. In the East, in 793, ask before you name a saint.

## 13. Ten things never to write

1. "Free Frisians", Charlemagne's charter of freedom, the Upstalsboom, *asega*, the seven sealands: all 13th century.
2. Oaths by Woden, Thor or Valhalla; Stavo, Fosta, Frya or Wralda.
3. A king after 719.
4. Holland, Dutch, Netherlands; "Viking" for a people.
5. Dykes before about 1000; polders, windmills, clogs or tulips.
6. A guild before about 1020; the Hanse ever.
7. Guilders, stivers or sceattas: it is the penny and the pound.
8. Surnames, or names in -ma, -stra, -inga.
9. Burning the dead or offering a man to the tide after baptism.
10. Black-and-white cows or black Friesian horses: modern breeds (?).
