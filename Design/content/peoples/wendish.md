# The Wends, 793 to 1066: Obodrites, Wilzi, Rani, Pomeranians

A bible of one people for the dialogue writers. Read with `BRIEF.md` and `BRIEF_2.md`. Keys in square brackets are in the source list. (?) marks a thing not checked on 4 Oct 2026. "Later" marks witnesses of 1120–1170. Nearly every word about the Wends was written by an enemy.

## 1. Who they are, and how that changes

- **Four peoples, never one.** "Wends" (*Winedas*, *Sclavi*) is the neighbours' word. Obodrites round Wismar bay, with Wagrians (Starigard) and Polabians: 53 forts [BG]. Wilzi from there to the Oder, "in their own tongue Welatabians" [Einh], 95 forts; called Liutizi from 991 [Lut]. Rani on Rügen, first named 955 [Wid III.54]. Pomeranians beyond the Oder, first named 1046 [Pom].
- **789–844.** Obodrites are the Franks' allies, Wilzi their enemies. Thrasco gets Nordalbingia in 804, loses Reric in 808, is murdered there in 809 (`chronicle_793.json`). In 844 Louis the German kills King Goztomuizli and shares the people among chiefs [AF].
- **929–983, tribute.** In 955 on the Recknitz Stoinef's head is set up in the field and 700 captives are beheaded round it [Wid III.55].
- **983–1043, free.** The Liutizi rise, the bishops' seats burn, Mistivoj burns Hamburg (`chronicle_793.json`). From 1003 they march, idols and all, beside King Henry against the Poles [Lut].
- **1043–1066.** Magnus besieges Jumne [Adam schol. 56]. Gottschalk builds churches; on 7 June 1066 he is killed at Lenzen, Bishop John is offered to Redigast, Sliaswich is destroyed [Adam III.50–51].

In 793 an Obodrite is the emperor's friend. In 1066 his heirs are heathen by choice.

## 2. How they live

- **House.** Forty small houses close together, 4 by 5 m, wattle walls, one hearth; log houses after 900 (Groß Raden) [GR]. Each district has a ring fort of timber and earth in marsh or lake [Adam II.21].
- **Food.** Rye, wheat, barley, oats, millet [Elb]; fish, game, butter, honey, poppy (later) [Herb II.41]. They "neither possess nor desire wine": mead and beer [Herb II.1].
- **Dress.** Linen and wool. Women wear bronze or silver rings at the temples [TR]. Men crop hair and beard (later) [Saxo] (?). Wealth is counted in horses, one to a fighter [Herb II.23].
- **The year.** Spring: the hook-plough, two oxen or one horse [Helm I.12]. Summer: markets and sea raids. After harvest: the god's feast [Saxo] (?). November: herring off Rügen [Helm II.12].

## 3. Law and rank

- **No law book survives,** and no wergild table: do not invent one. One fine is known: Selibur of the Wagrians, fifteen talents of silver to the Saxon duke [Wid III.68].
- **Ranks.** Prince, nobles, priests, free farmers, slaves. On Rügen the priest outweighs the king [Helm I.6].
- **Assembly.** The Liutizi have no lord. All must agree; who speaks against is beaten with rods [Thiet VI.25]. Wagrians meet each Monday at Prove's oaks for judgment; the god's fence shelters anyone in danger of death [Helm I.83].
- **Oaths.** They swear unwillingly: to swear is as good as to forswear, for fear of the gods [Helm I.83]. Peace is made with a lock cut from the crown, grass and the right hand [Thiet VI.25]. The brief's "oaths sworn on water" has no source.
- **Slavery.** Captives are the chief spoil on every side. Ansgar buys Slav boys [Rimb 15].
- **Women.** Wartislaw put away 24 concubines [Herb II.22]. Infant daughters were killed [Ebo II.12]. A widow was praised for burning with her husband [Bon]. Yet a rich widow near Kamień "ruled her house with vigour" [Herb II.23]. No source shows a Wendish woman merchant.

## 4. Faith

- **Attested in the period.** Riedegost of the Redarii: a wooden temple set on beasts' horns, gods in helmet and mail with their names cut, the first called Zuarasici; banners, lots, blood of beasts and men [Thiet VI.23–25]. Adam calls it Rethra and the god Redigast: gold image, nine gates, a lake, four days from Hamburg [Adam II.21]. Before 1000 no god's name is on record: say "the god of this land".
- **Attested later.** Triglav at Szczecin, three heads, eyes and lips covered [Ebo III.1]; a horse led three times over nine spears before a raid [Herb II.33]; Svantevit, Prove, Siwa, Podaga; a cup passed with words to a good god and a "black" one [Helm I.52].
- **Invention.** Belbog; the Prillwitz idols (?); the Book of Veles; Lada; Perun and Mokosh on the Baltic (?); sunken Vineta; the Jomsvikings' brotherhood [Jom].
- **Conversion** did not hold. The bishoprics fell in 983, Kolberg's (1000) by 1005 [Pom], Gottschalk's church in 1066. A Danish king blamed the Saxons' greed for tribute [Adam III.22]. Pomerania follows in 1124–28, Rügen in 1168.
- **Saints.** None of their own. The dead of 1066 are others' martyrs: Gottschalk (7 June), Ansver, Bishop John [Adam III.50–51].

## 5. How they trade

- **Silver by weight,** cut small: 2,203 dirhams in one Ralswiek hoard, the youngest of 847 [Ral]; western pennies after 950 [Pom]. No Wendish coin (?).
- **Linen.** The Rani have no coin: what you buy in the market you get for linen cloth (later) [Helm I.38].
- **The god's share.** On Rügen nobody may buy or sell until he has offered something costly to the god [Helm I.6].
- **Markets.** Reric until 808; Ralswiek; Menzlin; Starigard; Jumne (Wolin), seven days from Hamburg by land, fourteen under sail to Novgorod [Adam II.22]. Szczecin trades twice a week [Herb II.26]. Franks may meet them only at the posts of 805 and sell no arms (`chronicle_793.json`).
- **They sell** slaves, honey, wax, furs, grain, horses, fish, salt, linen. **They want** silver, swords, glass beads, quernstones (?).
- **Hosts.** A table stands laid in every house [Herb II.41]. Who turns a stranger away may have his house burned; what is stolen by night is shared with guests in the morning [Helm I.82].
- **Credit and written bargains:** the sources are silent.

## 6. Ships

Clinker boats like the Norse, but pegged with wooden nails and caulked with moss: class `baltic_coastal_boat` (`ship_classes_793.json`). Four boats at Ralswiek, up to 14 by 3.4 m [Ral]. Witscacus of Szczecin fitted out six vessels against the Danes and ended in their prison (later) [Ebo III.2]. No Wendish ship is recorded in the North Sea (?).

## 7. Names

Two stems make a name: Drag- (dear), Liub- (love), Mil- (kind), Slav- (fame), Rat- (war), Mist- (vengeance), Gost- (guest), Svant- (holy), with -mir, -slav, -gast, -drag, -bor, -voj, -mysl. Women add -a. Short names: Liub, Nakon, Kruto. Latin scribes mangle them: keep one spelling. No surnames: "Kruto, Grin's son".

**Men.** Witzin, Thrasco, Godelaib, Slavomir, Ceadrag, Dragovit, Liub, Milegast, Cealadrag [ARF 789–823]; Goztomuizli, Tabomuizli [AF]; Tugumir, Nakon, Stoinef, Selibur [Wid]; Mistivoj, Mizzidrog, Sederich, Anatrog, Gneus, Ratibor, Blusso [Adam]; Mistizlav [Thiet VIII.5]; Zemuzil [Pom]; Kruto, Grin, Butue [Helm I.25].

**Women.** Twenty-five Wendish women cannot be found: six are named before 1100. Tofa, Mistivoj's daughter (a Norse name) [DR 55]; Hodica [Helm I.13]; Dragomira of the Stodorani (?); Emnildis, Dobromir's daughter [Thiet IV.58]; Santslaue, Cnut's sister [LV]; Slavina, Kruto's wife [Helm I.34]. Slav neighbours of those years: Dobrawa, "the Good" [Thiet IV.55]; Liudmila, Mlada, Strezislava, Bozena, Svatava, Bozetecha (?). Pomeranian charters, 1150–1290: Pribislava, Miroslava, Woislava, Dobroslava, Witoslava [Greif; Samb]; Zwinislava, Damroka (?). Built, not attested: Dragoslava, Liubomila, Radoslava, Gostislava, Svantomira.

## 8. How they speak in the sources

In public-domain translation:

1. "...the Slavs who are commonly known among us as Wilzi, but properly, that is to say in their own tongue, are called Welatabians." [Einh]
2. "...the Slavs kept harassing the Abodriti, old allies of the Franks, by continual raids." [Einh]
3. "And we had Weonodland, on the right, all the way to the mouth of the Vistula." Wulfstan (`texts_793.json`; Bosworth 1859)
4. "And the Wends, the most degraded and depraved race of men, observe the mutual love of the married state with such zeal, that a wife, when her husband dies, refuses to live." [Bon]
5. "He began also to buy Danish and Slav boys and to redeem some from captivity." [Rimb 15]

Wends speaking, in the 1120s:

6. "...the supreme God would never send to us so abject a messenger." Wolin, to a barefoot hermit [Ebo II.1]
7. "If you have so great a desire to preach, preach to the fishes of the sea and the fowls of the air." [Ebo II.1]
8. "Amongst the Christians there are thieves and robbers ... Let such a religion be far from us!" Szczecin [Herb II.26]
9. "He is strong, powerful and rich ... for he has such or such a number of horses." [Herb II.23]
10. "Do not you see how much good and how great wealth our gods have given us?" The widow of Kamień [Herb II.23]

Without public-domain English (?), so glossed freshly:

- *Si igitur canis valens fuerit, magnos morsus dabit*: if the dog is a strong one, he will bite hard. Mistivoj [Helm I.16].
- Pribislav of Wagria, 1156: what is left but to quit the land, take to the sea and live among the waves? [Helm I.83]

## 9. Oaths, insults, jokes

- **Oath.** Mistivoj's kin at Rethra: swear to us that you will leave the Saxons, and we will stand with you. He swore [Helm I.16].
- **Insult.** Saxons call Wends dogs; Wends call Saxons a faithless and greedy people [Helm I.16]. In 955 Stoinef, gnashing his teeth, mocked Gero and the emperor's whole army across a marsh [Wid III.54].
- **Jokes.** Wolin would not kill the hermit: it is not wise "to kill a barefooted stranger" [Ebo II.1]. A priest of Wolgast hid in a thicket at dawn and told a peasant going to market, "I am your god" [Herb III.4]. When Szczecin's temples fell and no god struck, the people took the wood home to cook with [Herb II.31].

## 10. Neighbours

- **Franks:** to Obodrites the paymaster, to Wilzi the invader. Franks say "old allies" [Einh].
- **Saxons:** the tax-taker. Saxons say: hard men, who put every hardship below dear freedom [Wid II.20].
- **Danes:** Godfred hanged Godelaib and burned Reric; later marriage kin (Tofa) and prey. Danish captives sat in fetters in a Wagrian hall [Helm I.83].
- **Each other:** Obodrites and Wilzi are enemies from 789 [Einh]. All fear the Rani for the nearness of their gods [Adam IV.18].
- **Outsiders' praise:** no people is more honourable or kindly in manners and hospitality [Adam II.22].
- **Frisians:** no source (?).

## 11. Burial

Burning. Ralswiek has about 400 mounds, with boat burials and one rider's grave [Ral]; Menzlin has stone ship settings [Men]. Pomeranians bury "in the woods or the fields" and set sticks on the graves [Ebo II.12]. Unburnt burial, with knife, coin and temple rings, comes after c. 950 (?); Wolin has both rites [Wol].

## 12. What a Frisian merchant must know

1. Until 808 Reric is the door; after it, Sliesthorp or the Elbe posts.
2. No swords or mail: the ban of 805 stands.
3. Bring scales. Silver is cut and weighed; on Rügen bring linen.
4. On Rügen pay the god before you open a bale [Helm I.6].
5. Take the host's table. Locked chests astonished the Pomeranians [Herb II.41].
6. Do not press a Wend to swear. Take his right hand [Thiet VI.25].
7. At Jumne keep your Christianity out of sight [Adam II.22].
8. A Frisian understands no Slavic (`npc_text_encounters.md` 2.6). Hire an interpreter.
9. Ask whether he is Obodrite or Wilzian before you praise the other.
10. A stranger without a host is a captive. Rügen's pirates kill those others would sell [Adam IV.18].

## 13. Ten things never to write

1. "We Wends" or "we Slavs" as one nation, or a king of all of them.
2. A Wendish god's name before 1000. Svantevit and Triglav are first written after 1100.
3. Perun, Veles, Mokosh, Lada, Belbog, Baba Yaga.
4. Jomsvikings, Jomsborg's law, Vineta under the sea.
5. Wendish coin, a law book, a written bargain, "Slavic runes".
6. Christian Wends as ordinary before 1043.
7. Wine at a Wendish table; vodka, potatoes.
8. A Wend who charges a stranger for bed or bread.
9. A Wend swearing lightly "by the gods".
10. Russian or Polish modern forms: Ivan, Boris, names in -ski, "tsar", "boyar".
