# The east of the Baltic, 793 to 1066: Prussians, Curonians, Finns, Rus of Ladoga

For the dialogue writers. Keys in square brackets: see Sources. (?) = not checked against a source on 4 Oct 2026. **late** = recorded after 1066: colour, not fact. Quotes from [Adam] and [Can] are fresh glosses of the Latin. Ports: `truso`, `grobin`, `aldeigjuborg`.

**Names that mislead.** Wulfstan's *Este* (his translator's "Estonians") are Prussians. Norse *Finnar* are Sami; the Finns here are the Chronicle's Yam, the Swedes' *Tafeistaland*. *Rus* are at first Northmen among Finns and Slavs; never "Russians".

## 1. Who they are, and how that changes

- **Prussians** (*Este*, *Sembi vel Pruzzi*): Balts from the Vistula to the Neman. A king in every "town" [Wulf]; they "suffer no lord among them" [Adam]. Northmen lie buried at Kaup in Sambia (?). They kill Adalbert in 997 [Can] and Bruno in 1009 (?); free and heathen in 1066.
- **Curonians** (*Cori*, *Kúrir*): Balts of the coast north of the Neman, with five "towns" [Rimb]. Gotlanders and Swedes live at Grobiņa, c. 650–850 (?). About 853 they beat a Danish fleet, then buy off the Swedes at Apuolė [Rimb]. By c. 1050 Denmark keeps a guard against them [Hkr] late (?).
- **Finns:** farmers and trappers; no king, no town, no writing. Round Ladoga live Chud and Ves, who pay the Varangians tribute in 859 and march in every Rus host after; Novgorod beats the Yam in 1042 [PVL]. Swedes die "in Finnland", "in Tafeistaland" [Run].
- **Rus of Ladoga:** Aldeigjuborg stands from 753 (tree-rings); dirhams from 786/7; burned c. 865; Rurik's first seat in one text of the Chronicle [Lad]. From 882 the princes sit in Kiev and Ladoga is Novgorod's sea-gate. Earl Eric burns it in 997; from 1019 it is the Swedish princess Ingigerd's bride-gift, held by Earl Ragnvald [Lad].

## 2. How they live

- **Prussians.** Forts among marsh and wood. Honey and fish in plenty; the rich drink mare's milk, the poor and the slaves mead (see 8); no ale is brewed [Wulf]. They eat horse-flesh; they are "blue, ruddy-faced and long-haired" (eyes or skin: unclear) [Adam].
- **Curonians.** A rich man's farm: many rooms, high palings, a hall where they "ate, drank, and were merry" [Egil] late. "Much gold, the best horses" [Adam].
- **Finns.** Log houses; rye and barley on burnt clearings, cattle, seal, fish; winter is for trapping, on ski and sledge (?). Women wear bronze spirals sewn on the apron (Luistari) (?).
- **Rus.** Log houses; the steam bath (see 9). Each man carries axe, sword and knife; a wife's neck-rings show her husband's silver [IbnF]. Winter: the prince's tribute round; spring: boats down the rivers; summer: sail [DAI] (?).

## 3. Law and rank

- **Prussians:** a king to each fort; rich, poor and slaves [Wulf]. In 1249 they still buy wives, keep several, and leave property to sons only [Chr] late.
- **Curonians:** a town treats as one body: half a pound of silver a head, tribute, thirty hostages [Rimb].
- **Finns:** nothing written.
- **Rus:** kin may avenge a killing; otherwise 40 grivnas, the same for Rus or Slav, retainer or merchant (Yaroslav's law, c. 1016) (?). A Varangian clears himself by oath alone (?). Slaves are the chief export.
- **Women:** Olga rules for her son; in 945 she, Svanhild and Predslava each send an envoy [PVL]. No woman merchant is named.

## 4. Faith

- **Attested.** Prussians: groves and springs barred to Christians [Adam]; Sicco, "priest of idols", strikes Adalbert first [Can]; a harvest idol, Curche, and funeral priests [Chr] late. Curonians: "every house full of diviners, augurs and necromancers" [Adam]. Finns: a Chud magician calls his gods in a trance (1071) [PVL]; no god is named before 1551. Rus: Perun, and Volos "god of cattle" [PVL]; food laid before a wooden post for luck in trade [IbnF].
- **Invented later.** Romuva and its "pope" Criwe (Dusburg, 1326); Widewuto and a three-god shrine (Grunau, c. 1520); the Kalevala (1835); Danes praying to be spared the Curonians (no source found).
- **Conversion.** Prussians and Finns: none by 1066. Curonians: one church c. 1070, built by a merchant whom the Danish king won with gifts [Adam] (?). Rus: Christians and heathens swear side by side in 945, the Christians in St Elias's church; Olga is baptised Helena; Vladimir in 988 [PVL].
- **Feasts** (Greek rite): Elias, George, Sophia (?).

## 5. How they trade

- **Silver by weight:** dirhams whole and cut, folding scales (?). The Rus also reckon in marten skins (*kuna*) (?); tribute was "a squirrel-skin and a beaver-skin from each hearth" [PVL 859].
- **Prussians** hold gold and silver cheap and give marten skins for woollen cloaks, *faldones* [Adam]: bring Frisian cloth. They sell amber, horses, honey, slaves.
- **Curonians:** a market is a truce counted in days [Egil].
- **Finns:** furs for blades, beads and silver, through Swedes and Rus (?).
- **Rus** sell furs, wax, honey and slaves. In Greece they accept strict terms: a certificate from their prince, one gate, fifty men at a time, unarmed [PVL 945]. A Rus merchant asks his post-god for a buyer with many dirhams who will not haggle [IbnF].
- **Credit, hosts, gifts:** the sources are silent for Balts and Finns.

## 6. Ships

Balts: `logboat`, `baltic_coastal_boat`. Finns: logboats, and planks sewn with roots (?). At Ladoga sea ships (`norse_allround_ship`) stop below the Volkhov rapids; goods go on in river boats (?). In 907 Oleg orders "silken sails" for the Rus, linen for the Slavs [PVL].

## 7. Names

Rus names are Norse in Slavic mouths (Helgi is Oleg, Helga Olga, Ingvar Igor); *-slav* names enter the ruling house by 945, baptismal names after 988. Prussian: two Baltic stems (Wisse-gaud) or short forms in -e, -o. Finnish: Iha (joy), Hyvä (good), Lempi (love), Mieli (mind), Valta (power), joined or extended [FiN]. No Curonian is named before 1230 (?).

**Men.** Rus [PVL 912]: Karl, Ingjald, Farulf, Vermund, Hrollaf, Gunnar, Karni, Frithleif, Hroarr, Throand, Leithulf, Fast, Steinvith; merchants [PVL 945]: Authun, Gamal, Heming, Bruni, Svein, Styr, Manni. Prussian: Sicco (997) [Can]; late [Dusb]: Glande, Glappo, Auttume, Diwane, Scumand, Pipin, Codrune, Wissegaud, Nalube, Gedune. Finnic: Tuky (1068) [PVL]; late [FiN]: Kaukovalta (c. 1220), Mielitty, Asikka, Kuningas (1340).

**Women.** Rus [PVL]: Olga, Helena, Svanhild, Predslava, Rogned, Malfred, Anna; Ingigerd [Lad]; Elisaveta, Anastasia, Dobronega (?). Prussian, 13th to 15th century, late [Lewy]: Baide, Barislo, Buite, Drua, Geruscha, Grasute, Nameda, Namego, Patulne, Trichoinne, Wicelle. Swedish wives of men who sailed east [Run]: Sigrid, Sigrud, Ragnfrid. No Curonian or Finnish woman is named in the period: build from the stems; never Aino or Kyllikki (Kalevala).

## 8. How they speak in the sources

In public-domain translation:

1. "...in every town is a king." [Wulf]
2. "...the king and the richest men drink mare's milk, whilst the poor and the slaves drink mead." [Wulf]
3. "...fleet horses in that country are wonderfully dear." [Wulf]
4. "We desire peace rather than fighting..." Curonians at Apuolė [Rimb]
5. "...made a peace for half a month with the men of the land and traded with them." [Egil]
6. "Our whole land is great and rich, but there is no order in it. Come to rule and reign over us." Chuds and Slavs to the Rus [PVL 862]
7. "Drinking is the joy of the Russes." Vladimir [PVL 986]
8. "The gods dare not approach, since you wear a symbol of which they are afraid." A Chud magician [PVL 1071]
9. "Let us not disgrace Rus..." Svyatoslav [PVL 971]
10. "...may we become yellow as gold, and be slain with our own weapons." [PVL 971]

Originals, glossed afresh:

- *Nobis et toti huic regno, cuius nos fauces sumus, communis lex imperat et unus ordo vivendi*: we and this whole realm, whose gateway we are, keep one law and one way of life. Prussians to Adalbert [Can].
- *Hann oft siglt til Sæimgala, dyrum knærri, um Domisnæs*: he often sailed to Semigallia in a costly cargo-ship, round Domesnäs [Run, Sö 198].

## 9. Oaths, insults, jokes

- **Oath.** Heathen Rus swear on their weapons by Perun: the breaker shall "not be protected by their own shields" and "be in bondage forever" [PVL 945].
- **Insults.** "Why did you come hither with this crooked-shanks, you carpenters? We shall put you to work on our houses." A Kiev general to the Novgorod men, 1016 [PVL]. Prussians to Adalbert: think it much that you came this far unhurt; leave tonight or lose your heads tomorrow [Can].
- **Jokes.** The Novgorod bath: they lash themselves with reeds till half dead, then cold water; "not a mere washing but a veritable torment" [PVL]. A Curonian's son finds it too dark to enjoy killing the prisoners and waits for morning; they escape [Egil] late. A Rus calls the Arabs fools for leaving their dead to the worms [IbnF].

## 10. Neighbours

- **Swedes and Gotlanders:** the Curonians' old tribute-takers [Rimb]; elsewhere partners, husbands, hired swords.
- **Germans on Prussians:** "most kindly people", who help those in peril at sea [Adam]. A saint's biographer hears them snarl like dogs [Can].
- **On Curonians:** "a most cruel people, shunned by all" for idol-worship [Adam].
- **On the Rus:** an Arab finds them the finest bodies and the dirtiest men he has seen [IbnF].
- **Rus on Chud:** tributaries, allies, wizards; Yaroslav builds Yuryev in their land in 1030 [PVL].
- **Of Frisians** none of them says a word.

## 11. Burial

- **Prussians.** The dead man lies in his house a month or two, a king half a year, kept by "artificial cold", while his kin drink and play. His goods lie in heaps along a mile, the largest furthest out; the fastest horses win them. Then he is burned with weapons and clothes [Wulf].
- **Curonians** burn their dead, the weapons broken (?).
- **Finns:** burnt bones under stone settings; at Luistari unburnt, in full dress (?).
- **Rus:** high mounds along the Volkhov; boat-burnings at Plakun (?). On the Volga a chief burns in his ship with a slave girl [IbnF].

## 12. What a Frisian merchant must know

1. Bring scales, weights and cloth. East of Hedeby pennies are weighed.
2. Prussians pay marten for wool [Adam]. Amber is cheap at Truso; a fast horse is dear [Wulf].
3. Every Prussian fort has its own king: buy peace at each [Wulf].
4. Keep out of groves and away from holy springs; land no priest [Adam, Can].
5. Come during a wake and nobody trades for a month, but everybody drinks [Wulf].
6. In Courland count the days of the truce; be at sea before the last [Egil].
7. Finland has furs and no market: go with a Swede [Run].
8. At Ladoga your hull goes no further: hire river boats. Ice holds from early winter to after Easter (?).
9. Among the Rus swear on your weapon, or after 988 on the cross; a killing costs 40 grivnas (?).
10. You speak none of their tongues: Norse serves at Truso, Grobiņa and Ladoga until c. 950 (?).

## 13. Ten things never to write

1. "Estonians" for Wulfstan's Este, "Finns" for Sami, "Russia", "Russians", "Vikings".
2. A king of all Prussia, a Curonian or Finnish king, a Finnish town.
3. Romuva, Criwe, Widewuto, a Prussian god by name; Väinämöinen or Ukko on a man's lips.
4. Ale in a Prussian hall, or mead as the rich man's drink there.
5. Teutonic Knights, Riga, Königsberg, crusades to Finland: all after 1150.
6. Baptised Prussians, Curonians or Finns as ordinary people.
7. Coins struck by any of them before c. 988 (?); pennies counted, not weighed.
8. Novgorod before c. 930 (?); the Hanse's yard; onion domes.
9. Slavic-speaking lords at Ladoga in 793, or Norse-speaking ones in 1066.
10. Vodka, tsars, Cossacks; a Rus who will not sell people.
