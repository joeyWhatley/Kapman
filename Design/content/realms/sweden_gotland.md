# The Svear, the Götar and Gotland, 780-1070: as a merchant lives it

Research for the writers, 4 Oct 2026. Every fact here has its date, source and doubt in `sweden_gotland.json` beside this file; custom, law, faith and names are in `../peoples/svea_gotlandic.md`. **late** = written 150 years or more afterwards (Snorri, the Guta saga, the law books). (?) = not checked tonight. R = Rimbert's Life of Anskar, by chapter; Adam = Adam of Bremen.

**What it is.** Three peoples, not a kingdom. The **Svear** hold the Mälar bay: Birka is the market (to about 975), Uppsalir the kings' seat, the temple and the great thing. The **Götar** farm inland and have no port. The **Gotlanders** have no king and no town; they are the Baltic's carriers. Only two stretches are well lit: 829-865 (Rimbert) and 995-1070 (Adam, coins, rune stones). For 780-828 and 866-935 no king's name is safe, and the game should say "the king at Uppsalir".

## The four standing answers

- **Who must be paid.** At Birka the king's prefect, and the king himself with a gift at table: that is how Anskar did it (R 26). No toll list survives (?), but the Gotlanders' treaty frees them "of toll and all charges", so others paid. At Sigtuna (from 980) your host, the lord of the yard you trade in. On Gotland the farmer whose beach it is, and nobody else. At Uppsalir in a ninth year, a Christian buys himself off the sacrifice (Adam IV.27). At sea, whoever catches you: Birka's own price was 100 pounds of silver (R 19).
- **Who protects.** The peace of the market and the thing; the prefect; in the tenth century the fighting men in the hall by Birka's fort; your host, who answers for you; from about 1050 your guild-brothers (the "Frisians' guild" stones at Sigtuna); holy ground, heathen or Christian. At sea nobody: sail in company, as the merchants of 829 did, and it may still not be enough.
- **What is forbidden.** A new god without the leave of the lots and two things (R 26-27). Drawing a weapon at a thing or a vi. Forcing any man's faith, after the pact of about 1014: not even a king or a bishop may. Refusing a traveller a roof (Adam IV.21). Counting coin is not forbidden, only useless: silver is weighed, except Sigtuna's own pennies, 995-1030.
- **When to stay away.** Birka 845-851; the Curonian shore 853-854; the Mälar bay about 975-985; Gotland's coasts 996 and autumn 1007; the Göta border 1015-1019; Skåne and the Sound 1026 and 1043-1047; Sigtuna 1066-1067. Every year the bay is ice from early winter to April: no ship moves, but the late-winter thing and market at Uppsalir are reached by sledge (**late**).

## Decade by decade

- **780s-790s.** Heathen, no coin. Helgö's workshops are fading; Birka, a generation old, takes the trade. Furs, iron, wax and slaves go out; glass, wine, blades and Frisian cloth come in. Bring scales.
- **800s.** The first dirhams come down from Aldeigjuborg. The exiled sons of the Danish king Godfred live at the Svear court (ARF 813).
- **810s.** They go home in 813 and take Denmark: for forty years the Danish kings are men who once lived among the Svear.
- **820s.** 829: Svear envoys ask the emperor for priests. Anskar's merchant convoy is taken by pirates half-way, with "nearly forty books" (R 10). King Björn lets him preach; the prefect Herigar builds a church. Hedeby's new pennies reach Birka and are weighed like any silver.
- **830s.** A bishop, Gautbert, at Birka. In 839 Svear calling themselves Rhos reach the emperor from Constantinople: the east way runs to the Greeks. Dorestad, the other end of Birka's western trade, is sacked four years running (834-837).
- **840s.** About 845 a crowd kills the bishop's nephew and drives the bishop out, "not by command of the king" (R 17). Then the exile Anund comes with 32 ships; Birka pays 100 pounds; the lots send the Danes off to rob a Slav town instead (R 19).
- **850s.** A priest again, about 851. Catla of Birka carries her dead mother's wealth to Dorestad for the poor (R 20). 852: Anskar's second voyage, twenty days by ship; King Olof, the lots and two things say yes (R 26-28). 853-854: the Danes fail in Courland; Olof burns Seeburg and takes Apuolė's ransom (R 30).
- **860s.** Three more priests; one is killed by robbers on the road to Schleswig (R 33). Dorestad ends in 863: a Frisian now ships for Birka from Hedeby. Anskar dies in 865 and the mission lapses for seventy years.
- **870s.** The Spillings silver, 67 kg, goes under a floor on Gotland after 870. Weights made to the dirham's standard become common. (**late**: King Erik and Harald Fairhair quarrel over Värmland.)
- **880s-890s.** Wulfstan, about 890: Blekinge, Möre, Öland and Gotland belong to the Svear. Gotland's treaty (**late**): 60 marks of silver a year, and Gotlanders go toll-free through the Svear lands.
- **900s-920s.** Samanid silver by the Volga Bulghars: the flood. A house of kings from the Svear holds Hedeby, so both ends of the crossing answer to kin. The best years.
- **930s.** 934: Henry I humbles Gnupa at Hedeby. 936: Archbishop Unni dies at Birka; King Ring rules with his sons.
- **940s-950s.** Peak silver about 950. Birka has a rampart, a fort and a hall of fighting men.
- **960s.** The eastern silver thins and worsens. The land is rising and Birka's channels are shoaling.
- **970s.** Birka is empty by about 975. Erik the Victorious is king.
- **980s.** Sigtuna is laid out, a king's town of plots. About 985 Styrbjörn's fleet is beaten on the Fyris fields (?).
- **990s.** Erik is said to hold Denmark for a time (Adam; doubted). About 995 Olof Skötkonung: English moneyers strike his pennies at Sigtuna. Jarl Eirik robs ships off Gotland and burns Aldeigjuborg (996-997, **late**).
- **1000s.** Svolder, 1000. Uppland men come home from England with geld silver. About 1007 the boy Olaf Haraldsson takes tribute of Gotland and winters there. 1008: the king is baptised.
- **1010s.** About 1014 the pact: the king may be Christian in Västergötland (the see at Skara) and must force nobody. 1015-1019: border war with Norway; the thing makes the king choose peace and share his kingship (**late**).
- **1020s.** 1022: Anund Jacob. 1026: the fight at the Holy River, by Åhus; 1027: Cnut calls himself king of part of the Svear. About 1029 an English priest axes Thor's image at the thing and is killed. 1029: Olaf Haraldsson lies at Akergarn on Gotland.
- **1030s.** 400 Svear march to Stiklestad. About 1030 Sigtuna's mint stops: back to the scales. Olaf's mass (29 July) becomes a market day.
- **1040s.** Ingvar's fleet is lost in the east (1041); twenty-six stones round the bay remember the dead. Sven Estridsson fights for Denmark out of Svear harbours.
- **1050s.** Emund the Old keeps his own bishop and turns Bremen's away. His son's host dies in the far north. Halland, Skåne and Blekinge are Danish by now.
- **1060s.** Stenkil: a bishop at Sigtuna, and the king forbids him to burn the temple. 1066: Stenkil dies; two Eriks kill each other; the bishops stay away. About 1070 a king is cast out for refusing to sacrifice.

## Ten scenes

1. **The convoy, 829.** Half-way to Birka. The merchants fight, lose, and wade ashore with what they can carry. A monk has lost forty books and will not turn back (R 10).
2. **The ransom of Birka, about 847.** The king is away. A hundred pounds of silver is on the table; the Danes say every merchant here has more. An exiled king asks the lots whether the town may burn (R 19).
3. **Catla at Dorestad, about 852.** A Svea woman gives her mother's wealth away in the player's own town, spends four pennies on wine, and finds the bag full again, less the four (R 20).
4. **The thing at Birka, 852.** Noise. An old man stands: some of us have been to Dorestad and took this faith there, and the way is now beset by pirates; why refuse what is brought to our door? (R 27)
5. **The lots before Apuolė, 854.** Eight days of fighting, five days from the ships, no god answering. Merchants in the host say: ask the Christians' god (R 30).
6. **Silver under the floor, Gotland, after 870.** A farmer who is also a shipowner buries 67 kg, and among the coins is one that names Moses.
7. **Unni's head, Birka, September 936.** The archbishop is dead. The body stays; the head must reach Bremen. Somebody's ship carries it (Adam I.62).
8. **The moneyer's bench, Sigtuna, about 995.** An Englishman strikes OLAF REX, and for the first time in this land a price is counted, not weighed.
9. **The night of the elf-sacrifice, about 1019.** A Christian traveller in the Götar's woods is turned from one farm door after another: they are sacrificing and want no stranger (Sigvat's verses (?)).
10. **The temple the king would not burn, 1060s.** Two bishops want fire at Uppsalir. Stenkil says they will be killed and he cast out. In a ninth year a Christian pays to stay away (Adam IV.27, 30).

Also good: twelve rams for two cups and an axe at Akergarn, 1029 (**late**); a mother raising a stone for a son who went east with Ingvar; the Frisians' guild cutting a stone for a dead brother.

## Against chronicle_793.json

**It lacks** (all are in the JSON with sources):

1. The ransom of Birka by Anund and the Danes, between 845 and 851 (R 19): the one recorded attack on a Svear port.
2. The bishop driven out of Birka, about 845 (R 17), and the bishop sent, about 832 (R 14).
3. Anskar's second voyage and the two things, 852 (R 25-28); Catla's journey to Dorestad (R 20).
4. Anskar's death, 865, and the end of the mission.
5. The Spillings hoard, after 870; Gotland's tribute treaty (**late**). Gotland appears in one entry only.
6. The Svear house at Hedeby, about 900-934: the 934 entry calls Gnupa the Danes' king and does not say where his house came from.
7. Unni's death at Birka, 17 September 936.
8. The Fyris fields, about 985 (?); Jarl Eirik off Gotland and at Aldeigjuborg, 996-997 (**late**); Olaf Haraldsson's tribute from Gotland, about 1007.
9. Every change of king. No entry carries `"realm": "svear"`: Olof Skötkonung (995), Anund Jacob (1022), Emund (1050), Stenkil (1060), the two Eriks (1066-1067).
10. The king's baptism (1008) and the see at Skara (about 1014); the border war and peace with Norway (1015-1019).
11. The Holy River, 1026: the entries for 1027 and 1028 show Cnut's results without the battle.
12. Olaf at Akergarn, 1029; the end of the Sigtuna mint, about 1030; Ingvar, 1041; the bishop at Sigtuna, about 1060; the temple at Uppsalir still standing in 1070.

**Wrong, or at odds with the tables:**

1. `854-courland-war` burns the port `grobin`, but ports_793.json closes grobin in 850. Let the port run to 854.
2. `975-birka-sigtuna` moves the trade to Sigtuna, but there is no port for Sigtuna: after 975 the Svear have no market on the chart for 91 years.
3. `890-ohthere-wulfstan` names the port `truso`, which ports_793.json closes in 850. It also leaves out what matters most here: Wulfstan says Blekinge, Möre, Öland and Gotland belong to the Svear, the only dated word on who holds Gotland; its regions should add `gotland` and `svealand`.
4. `829-ansgar-birka` says he reached Birka "on foot". Rimbert says on foot and by ship across the waters between (R 11); Birka is an island.
5. `934-gnupa-tribute` is right as far as it goes; add that Adam (I.48) has Gnupa's father come from the Svear.
6. `paviken` has no end year in ports_793.json; the harbour gives way to Västergarn about 1000-1050 (?).
