# The Irish Sea world, 780-1070: a merchant's view

The data is in `irish_sea.json` (rulers, wars, raids, laws, coins, church, trade notes), with the sources named entry by entry. Short names here: **AU** Annals of Ulster; **AI** Inisfallen; **AT** Tigernach; **FA** Fragmentary Annals; **CKA** Chronicle of the Kings of Alba; **ASC** Anglo-Saxon Chronicle; **DB** Domesday Book (Chester). `(?)` marks a doubt. Ports: `duiblinn` (Dublin), `hi` (Iona), and `legacaestir` (Chester), the English door on this sea.

**Not re-checked on the web.** This was written from knowledge of the annals and the standard books, without reading each entry again. The kings of Tara, Dublin and Alba and the dated events are firm. The lesser kings of Leinster and Munster, the Isles before 970 and every Orkney date are weak, and are marked so in the data.

**Four worlds on one sea.** Ireland: some hundred and fifty small kings under five or six great ones, no town, no coin, the great churches as its only markets. The north: Dal Riata and the Picts, made one kingdom by the disaster of 839, called Alba from 900, with no town and no coin in the whole period. Dublin: a ship-fort in 841, a king's harbour from 853, empty from 902 to 917, then the richest town on the sea, under an Irish over-king from 980 and an Irish king from 1052. Man and the Isles: nobody's and everybody's, the road between Norway, Dublin and York.

## Decade by decade

- **780s-790s.** A Frisian has no reason to come. Wine reaches the Irish churches from Gaul by the western road. Iona is the head of a family of churches from Kells to Lindisfarne. The islands are hit in 794, Iona and Rechru in 795, the island off Skerries in 798.
- **800s.** Iona burned (802), sixty-eight killed (806); the community builds at Kells (807-814). Inland, the king of Tara burns Leinster twice in a month (804). Saints' laws are cried province by province and their stewards go round collecting.
- **810s-820s.** Local kings beat raiders (811, 812). Women carried off from Howth (821). Blathmac dies on Iona rather than show where the shrine is (825). A sea full of small fleets: stay out of the Hebrides.
- **830s.** Armagh plundered three times in a month (832). Sixty ships on the Liffey and sixty on the Boyne (837). The kings of the Picts and of Dal Riata fall in one battle (839). In Munster a bishop-king burns churches himself.
- **840s.** The ship-fort at the black pool (841): the raiders stay and sell. Turgeis drowned (845). The Irish win four battles in 848. Kenneth takes the Pictish kingship (about 843); Columba's relics are shared between Kells and Dunkeld (849).
- **850s.** Dark Foreigners against Fair (851-852). Amlaib comes in 853 and everyone pays him. From here Dublin has a king, and a king wants traders.
- **860s.** Dublin's kings rob the Boyne tombs (863), take hostages from the Picts (866), burn Armagh (869). The king of Tara clears every Norse fort from the north coast (866): there will be no second Dublin.
- **870s.** Dumbarton falls (870); two hundred ships of captives come into the Liffey (871). Imar dies (873); his heirs kill each other. Forty quiet years begin.
- **880s-890s.** Dublin splits in two (893). The Law of Sunday arrives with a letter from heaven (887). Silver goes by weight, in arm-rings.
- **900s.** Dublin emptied (902); its people settle Man, the Wirral, the Ribble. Chester is rebuilt (907). Constantine and his bishop swear at Scone (906).
- **910s.** New fleets: a sea-fight off Man (914), Waterford (914), Dublin retaken (917), the king of Tara killed at its gate (919).
- **920s-930s.** One family at Dublin and York. Limerick founded and fought. Chester's mint is the busiest facing Ireland. Brunanburh (937): a bad autumn at Dublin.
- **940s.** Dublin sacked and burned by the Irish (944). Amlaib Cuaran baptised (943). Constantine turns monk.
- **950s-970s.** Three thousand captives out of Kells (951); a sickness in the town. Then Amlaib Cuaran's long reign: the town of plots and plank streets grows. Maccus is king of many islands; Edgar at Chester (973). Limerick sacked by Brian's brother (967).
- **980s.** Tara (980): the Irish slaves freed, old Amlaib dies on Iona. Christmas gold laid on every plot (989). Danes kill the abbot of Iona (986); a thousand dead off Man (987). Keep south.
- **990s.** Sitriuc Silkbeard strikes pennies (about 995-997). Brian and Mael Sechnaill share Ireland (997) and beat Dublin at Glenn Mama (999).
- **1000s.** Brian in Dublin at New Year 1000; king of all from 1002; gold on the altar at Armagh (1005). The English fleet wastes Man (1000). The great Gospel stolen at Kells (1007).
- **1010s.** Clontarf, Good Friday 1014. The town is not taken and goes on trading. Carham (1018).
- **1020s-1030s.** No high king. Sitriuc raids, is ransomed out of his neighbours' hands (1029), goes to Rome (1028) and builds Christ Church. Dublin's pennies go light and stay at home. Man strikes its own.
- **1040s.** Macbeth king of Alba (1040). A sixty-oar longship is built at Dublin of oak felled in 1042. Kings change at Dublin three times.
- **1050s.** Harold Godwinson winters at Dublin (1051-1052). Diarmait of Leinster takes the town (1052). Siward beats Macbeth (1054); Malcolm kills him (1057). A Norwegian fleet in the Irish Sea (1058).
- **1060s.** Dublin takes tribute from Man (1061). Two old kings die pilgrims at Rome (1064, 1065). Harold's sons sail from Dublin against Bristol (1068, 1069).

## Who must be paid

- **In Ireland, to 841 and inland always:** nobody takes a toll, because nobody trades without a patron. The king or abbot who names you his guest takes a gift and first choice. The saint's steward takes his due when the saint's law is out.
- **At Dublin, 853 to 980:** the king of the Foreigners or his man on the strand (the amount is not recorded (?)).
- **At Dublin, 980 to 1070:** the same, and through him the Irish over-king: cattle in 980, an ounce of gold a plot each Christmas from 989, Brian's tribute from 1000, Leinster's from 1052.
- **At Chester:** the king's and the earl's reeve: four pence a load, and the king's first sight of marten skins (DB).
- **In the Isles:** whoever has ships there this summer: the sons of Aralt in the 970s and 980s, the earl of Orkney's men about 990-1014 and again under Thorfinn.
- **In Alba:** a mormaer's steward or an abbot, in kind.

## Who protects

- **A protector's word.** A stranger from over the sea has no honour-price in Irish law. A king's or an abbot's protection gives him one, for a set time.
- **The fair.** Tailtiu at the start of August under the king of Tara, Carman in Leinster: no fighting and no seizing for debt while it lasts. Often not held.
- **The saints.** Church ground is sanctuary, and Adomnan's law guards women, clerics and children. Heathens ignore it and so, some years, do Christian kings.
- **A strong king at Dublin** (853-873, 952-980, 989-1036): his peace on the strand.
- **Edgar's peace** on the whole sea, 973 to 975.

## What is forbidden

- Loading, selling or travelling on a Sunday in an Irish king's land (from 887; with force in Munster from 1040).
- A bargain without sureties, or made with a slave, a son under his father, a monk without his abbot, or a drunk man: void.
- Taking what the sea throws on another man's shore.
- Holding or selling Irish slaves at Dublin after 980.
- Coming up the Dee without leave: forty shillings a man.
- Killing or carrying off women, clerics and children (Adomnan's law, 697): the fine goes to Iona.

## When to stay away

- The Hebrides and the North Channel, 794-840 and 986-989.
- The Liffey in 837, 851-852, 902 (the day itself), 944, 980, 989 (a siege, and no fresh water), midwinter 999-1000, and Easter week 1014.
- Dublin 902-917: nothing there to buy or sell.
- Inland Leinster in 804; the Shannon in 844-845 and 924-937.
- Man in 987 and 1000.
- Any Irish strand where you have no protector.

## The ten best scenes

1. **Iona, 806.** You bring wine for the Mass and find sixty-eight graves. The abbot asks passage to Ireland for books and a shrine, to a place called Kells.
2. **Iona, 825.** Word on the wind of a fleet. Blathmac will not leave and will not say where the shrine is buried. You can carry some monks away, or stay to see.
3. **The black pool, winter 841.** A turf bank, ships drawn up, and men who mean to stay till spring. They have slaves and church silver and want grain and wine. Do you sell?
4. **The Liffey, 871.** Two hundred ships in from the Clyde with English, Britons and Picts in the holds. Slaves are cheaper than hides. A captive calls to you in a tongue you know.
5. **At sea off Cornwall, 891.** A boat of two and a half hides with no oars, three Irishmen in it and food for a week. They do not want rescuing. They want to know which way the wind goes.
6. **Dublin, 902.** The fort is taken as you come in on the tide. Ships left half-built on the strand, people wading out to anything that floats and offering all they have for a place to Man or the Wirral.
7. **Dublin, autumn 937.** Amlaib's ships come home from Brunanburh a few at a time. Five kings and seven jarls are dead in England. Every house wants news of one man and has ransom to raise.
8. **Dublin, 980.** After the battle of Tara the king of Tara stands at the gate and every Irish slave in the town walks out free. You bought six last week. Old King Amlaib asks passage to Iona to die.
9. **Clontarf, Good Friday 1014.** You lie in the Liffey mouth. Sitriuc watches from the wall with his wife, Brian's daughter. The fight runs all day across the Tolka, and at evening the flood tide is between the beaten men and their ships.
10. **The ransom of 1029.** The king of Brega holds Sitriuc's son. The price is twelve hundred cows, six score Welsh horses, sixty ounces of gold, sixty of silver and the sword of Carlus. Dublin must find the horses, and you have a boat and know the road to Anglesey.

Others worth a card: the Boyne tombs robbed (863); the siege with only sea water to drink and the Christmas gold (989); the stolen Gospel under a sod (1007); the kings at Chester (973); the oak felled in 1042 for a sixty-oar ship; Harold Godwinson hiring crews in Dublin (winter 1051-1052).

## Against `chronicle_793.json`

The chronicle follows the annals closely to 814 and by its own rule keeps only main points after. For this sea the main points it has are right in substance. What it lacks, most useful first:

**Events it lacks**

- 825: Blathmac killed on Iona (AU 825.17). A raid on port `hi`.
- 837: sixty ships on the Liffey, sixty on the Boyne (AU 837.3). The first great fleet at `duiblinn`.
- 839: the kings of the Picts and of Dal Riata killed by heathens (AU 839.9). Without it Kenneth's rise (843) has no cause.
- 848: the four Irish victories and the embassy to Charles the Bald (AU 848; AB 848).
- 849: Columba's relics divided between Kells and Dunkeld (AU 849.7; CKA). Changes what `hi` is.
- 851-853: Dark Foreigners sack Dublin; Amlaib comes and takes tribute (AU 851.3, 853.2). The start of the kingdom of Dublin.
- 866: Aed Findliath destroys the Norse forts of the north coast (AU 866.4).
- 873: Imar dies (AU 873.3); 877: Halfdan killed on Strangford Lough (AU 877.5).
- 887: the Law of Sunday (AU 887.3). A trading rule.
- 904: Imar's grandson killed in Fortriu; 906: the oath at Scone (CKA).
- 914: sea-fight off Man and the fleet at Waterford (AU 914.4-5).
- 919: Niall Glundub killed at Islandbridge (AU 919.3). It is only inside the effect text of the 917 entry.
- 934: Aethelstan invades Alba by land and sea (ASC 934).
- 944: Dublin sacked by Congalach (AU 944.3). 951: three thousand captives from Kells (AU 951.3).
- 986-987: Iona's abbot killed at Christmas; the battle of Man (AU 986.3, 987.1).
- 989: the Christmas gold on every plot (AT 989).
- 999-1000: Glenn Mama and Brian in Dublin (AU 999.8). 1002: Brian king. 1005: Brian at Armagh.
- 1000: Aethelred's fleet wastes Man (ASC E 1000).
- 1007: the great Gospel stolen (AU 1007.11).
- 1018: Carham. 1034: Malcolm II dies. 1040: Duncan killed. 1054: Siward and Macbeth. 1057-1058: Macbeth and Lulach killed. Alba has no entry between 973 and 1066.
- 1028: Sitriuc to Rome and Christ Church. 1029: the ransom.
- 1036, 1038, 1046, 1052: the changes of king at Dublin; 1052 above all (Diarmait takes it).
- 1051-1052: Harold at Dublin. 1058: Magnus's fleet. 1061: Dublin takes tribute from Man.

**Entries to correct or tighten**

- `843-kenneth-alba`: he died king of the Picts (AU 858); "Alba" is first used in 900. The id and effect are fine as hindsight, but no card before 900 should say Alba. That he was king of Dal Riata first is from later lists (?).
- `917-dublin-retaken`: Ragnall's fleet had been at Waterford since 914; in 917 he came to it again. "Takes Waterford" should be 914.
- `937-brunanburh`: the Chronicle does not name the Strathclyde king. Owain comes from the Historia regum and is likely, not sure. Add HR to the sources or drop his name from the title.
- `980-tara`: Amlaib Cuaran was not at the battle; his sons led and one, Ragnall, fell. His death on Iona may be 981. Add AT 980, the source for the siege and the freeing of the slaves.
- `1014-04-23-clontarf`: Sitriuc is listed under `who` but stayed inside the town; say so in the note. Add Brodir of Man and Mael Morda's death.
- `973-05-11-edgar-bath`: region `dalriata` is there for the Isles; Maccus, king of many islands, and Kenneth of Alba should be in `who` (John of Worcester).
- `997-dublin-coins`: about 995-997; the standard work is Dolley 1966, with Blackburn.
- `795-rechru-burned` and `795-skye-raid`: already hedged; keep the doubt.
- `841-duiblinn-longphort`, `845`, `870`, `902`: cite the entry numbers (AU 841.4, 845.8, 870.6 and 871.2, 902.2).

**The ports table**

- `duiblinn` is "monastery, size 1" with no end. It needs eras: church (to 841), ship-fort (841-902), empty (902-917), town (917 on; size 3 or 4 by 1000, with a mint from about 995).
- `hi` is realm `dalriata` for ever. Dal Riata has no king after about 843.
- There is no port for Man, Waterford (914), Limerick (922), the Wirral or Dumbarton. Man at least belongs on this chart.
