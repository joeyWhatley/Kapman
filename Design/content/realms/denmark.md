# The Danes, 777 to 1076: the realm as a merchant lives it

The facts, with a source on every one, are in `denmark.json` beside this page (rulers, lords abroad, wars, raids, laws,
coins, church). This page is the same story told from the deck of a Frisian boat. `(?)` marks what could not be checked
or what the sources leave open. Web searching was shut for this session; the dates were checked against the pages listed
in the JSON, and the annals are cited from memory of their year entries.

## The four questions

**Who must be paid.** The king's toll at the landing. Reric paid it to Godfred until 808; after that it is paid at
Sliesthorp. The port has a count of its own (Hovi, in the 850s), and he is the man to satisfy. Goods going by land
pass the one gate in the wall. At home, from 850 to 873 and from 882 to 885, the toll of Dorestad is a Dane's (Rorik,
then Godfrid). In 934, and from 974 to 983, tribute goes south from Hedeby and the emperor's men sit at the border.
From Cnut's time a moneyer takes his cut for changing foreign coin. By 1070 the raiders of Zealand pay the king for
leave to rob, so a pirate may be a taxpayer.

**Who protects.** Your host, first. Then the peace your own king has sworn with the Danish one: 811, 813, 825, 845,
and above all 873, the first that names merchants. From about 850 the church at Sliesthorp, and from about 860 the one
at Ripa: a Christian may trade, hear Mass and be buried there. From 1019 to 1035 Cnut's peace, from Lund to London,
and after 1027 his letter on the roads to Rome.

**What is forbidden.** By your own king: mail coats and swords to foreign buyers (779, 803, 805), on pain of death in
the west from 864. That is exactly what a Dane pays most for. By the Danes: the Christian rite at Sliesthorp before
about 850 and again for a year or two after 854; a church bell until about 856. After about 965, open sacrifice in the
king's towns (?).

**When to stay away.** Summer 804 (unless you sell fodder). 808 to 810. 812 to 815. 845. 854. 934. 974. 983. About
986. Skåne's waters in 1026. South Jutland in 1043. Every open coast in summer from 1048 to 1064. Schleswig in 1066.

## Decade by decade

- **790s.** Sigfred is king, and little more than a name. Danes buy Frankish blades, wine and querns at Ripa and
  Sliesthorp and pay in weighed silver, amber, furs and captives. Ripa's old thick pennies still pass there by count.
- **800s.** Godfred. In 804 his fleet and all his horsemen lie at Sliesthorp and he will not meet the emperor. In 808
  he destroys Reric, carries its merchants to Sliesthorp and shuts the border with a wall and one gate. A Frisian is
  the emperor's man: 808 to 810 are enemy years.
- **810s.** 810: 200 ships on the Frisian islands, 100 pounds of silver paid, and Godfred murdered by his own man.
  811: twelve men a side swear peace on the Eider. 812 to 814: the two royal houses kill each other, 10,940 dead in
  one battle. 815: a Frankish army burns north of the Eider. Nobody can promise a stranger safety.
- **820s.** Two houses share the kingdom and envoys go south nearly every year: quiet, good years. About 825 the
  first Danish coins appear, copied from Dorestad's own pennies. 823 Ebo preaches; 826 Harald is baptised at Mainz;
  827 he is thrown out; 828 a scuffle on the Eider, soon mended.
- **830s.** Horik alone. From 834 to 837 Dorestad is plundered four years running by fleets he says are not his. In
  838 he asks the emperor for Frisia as his reward for hanging the leaders. Home burns; Sliesthorp is safe and full
  of Frisian cloth and Frisian people for sale.
- **840s.** 845: Horik's fleet goes up the Elbe and Hamburg burns; Ragnar's men come home from Paris with 7,000
  pounds and a sickness; in the autumn Horik offers peace, captives and loot. 847: three Frankish kings warn him.
- **850s.** About 850 a church at Sliesthorp: the merchants of Dorestad and Hamburg now come without fear. 850:
  Rorik holds Dorestad, so the Dane is paid at home too. 854: Horik and nearly all his kin die in three days; the
  count shuts the church; the boy king opens it again and allows a bell; a church at Ripa.
- **860s.** January 863: Dorestad's last sack, with many Frisian merchants killed. No Danish king is named after
  864. Anskar dies in 865. The great army goes to England, and Danish buyers have English silver and English captives.
- **870s.** 873: Sigfred and Halfdan make peace with Louis the German, and merchants of either kingdom may cross
  the border and trade in peace. The same June the Frisians of Oostergo kill the raider Rodulf rather than pay him.
- **880s.** Not the kings' war, but Danes: the great army burns Cologne, Nijmegen, Deventer and Duisburg. 882:
  Godfrid is given Frisia; 885: he is killed. Stay off the Rhine. The loot is sold in the north.
- **890s.** 891: the army is broken on the Dyle. About 890 Ohthere and Wulfstan describe the port 'at the Heaths':
  it belongs to the Danes, and Truso is seven days and nights east. Who is king? Helge, some say (?).
- **900s to 920s.** A house from the land of the Svear, Olaf and his sons, rules at Hedeby (?). Nothing is written.
  Thin Hedeby pennies with Dorestad's name on them, long after Dorestad is gone; dirhams from the east by weight.
- **930s.** 934: King Henry beats Gnupa, takes tribute and has him baptised; the sea raids on Frisia stop. Gorm
  rules from Jelling. 936: Archbishop Unni passes through to Birka and dies there.
- **940s and 950s.** 948: bishops are named for Schleswig, Ripa and Aarhus. Gorm dies in the winter of 958/959.
- **960s.** About 965 Harald is baptised after Poppo carries the hot iron. A traveller from Spain finds Hedeby
  large, poor, living on fish, with few Christians. 968: the town's rampart is joined to the great wall.
- **970s.** 974: Otto II storms the wall and leaves a garrison by Hedeby. The road south is the emperor's.
- **980s.** About 980 the ring forts and the long bridge at Ravning: the king buys timber, iron and labour. 983:
  the Danes burn the emperor's fort. About 986 Svein drives out his father, who dies among the Slavs.
- **990s.** The fleets go to England: 10,000 pounds in 991, 16,000 in 994. About 995 the first penny with a Danish
  king's name. English and German pennies everywhere, nicked and weighed.
- **1000s.** 1000: Svolder; the Danish king holds both shores of the Kattegat. 1002: Danes killed in England on
  Saint Brice's day. 36,000 pounds in 1007. Warriors sit around Hedeby (the stones; the year is not known).
- **1010s.** 1013: Svein takes England and dies. Harald rules Denmark; Cnut wins England in 1016, takes 82,500
  pounds in 1018 and comes home to take Denmark in 1019.
- **1020s.** Cnut's peace: one king from Lund to London. Regular pennies at Lund, Roskilde, Viborg, Ripa and
  Hedeby; English bishops. 1026: the fight at the Holy River. 1027: Rome, and freedom from tolls on the road.
  1028: Norway.
- **1030s.** Cnut dies in 1035. Harthacnut cannot leave Denmark for fear of Magnus. The march of Schleswig is the
  Danish king's again; the Eider is the border.
- **1040s.** 1042: Magnus of Norway takes Denmark. 1043: he burns Jumne; the Wends ravage Jutland as far as Ripa
  and are cut down on the heath by Hedeby. War with Svein Estridsson, who is king from 1047.
- **1050s.** Harald of Norway burns along the coasts every summer; about 1050 Hedeby burns from end to end. Trade
  moves to Schleswig across the water, to Roskilde and to Lund.
- **1060s.** 1062: the sea fight at the Nissan; 1064: peace. About 1060 nine bishoprics. Svein's pennies copy
  Byzantine coins and some carry runes. 1066: the Wends destroy Schleswig.

## Ten scenes

1. **Sliesthorp, summer 804.** The king's fleet in the Schlei and every horseman of the realm in the fields; the
   emperor camped across the Elbe; envoys riding both ways. Sell fodder dear, carry a message, or be asked whose man
   you are. (ARF 804)
2. **Reric, 808.** Godfred's men empty the booths into his ships. Go where the king sends you, slip your cable by
   night, or plead that a Frisian is no Wend. (ARF 808)
3. **The Eider, spring 811.** After a winter that shut the roads, twelve men a side swear peace, each by his own
   custom; one of the Danes is Osfrid of Skåne. You ferry a count, or sell to both camps. (ARF 811)
4. **Dorestad and Sliesthorp, 834 to 838.** Home is burnt four summers running. At Sliesthorp your neighbour's
   cloth is for sale, and perhaps your neighbour. The king's man says the fleets were never the king's. (AB 834-838)
5. **Horik's hall, 845.** The Paris raiders are home with silver and a plague; an envoy of King Louis is watching;
   the king orders them seized and the captives sent back. Someone must carry the captives home. (AB 845;
   Translatio S. Germani (?))
6. **The bell at Sliesthorp, about 856.** The count Hovi has shut the church. The boy king opens it and lets it
   ring a bell for the first time, a thing the heathen had held to be an abomination. (Rimbert 31-32)
7. **The sword with the golden hilt, 873.** Halfdan's envoys swear the peace on their weapons. You are the first
   Frisian to try the new peace at the gate in the wall. (Ann. Fuld. 873)
8. **The stranger from Spain, about 965.** A man of Tortosa walks Hedeby: sweet wells, fish at every meal, eye
   paint on men and women, wives who divorce at will, and singing 'like the barking of dogs'. Guide him; his opinions
   are his own. The same year news comes of a priest who carried hot iron before the king. (al-Turtushi; Widukind)
9. **A stone for a partner, about 1000.** Warriors sit around Hedeby. Erik, a steersman, dies, and his partner
   raises a stone that calls him 'a very good man'. The word on it for partner, félagi, is a trader's word. (DR 3, DR 1)
10. **The fire, about 1050, and the end, 1066.** Harald's ships burn Hedeby from end to end; sixteen years later
    the Wends finish it. The heir chooses: rebuild, cross to Schleswig, or go home. (Heimskringla, late; Adam iii.51)

Spare: carrying Archbishop Unni from Hedeby to Birka in 936 (Adam i.60-62); Christmas 1026 at Roskilde, where Earl
Ulf is killed in the church after a quarrel over a board game (late).

## The chronicle (`chronicle_793.json`) against this file

**Wrong, or needing a note**

- `1066-hedeby-slavs` lists the port `reric`, which `ports_793.json` closes in 808. Take it out.
- `ports_793.json` closes Reric in 808, but the chronicle (rightly, ARF 809) has Thrasco murdered 'at the emporium
  of Reric' in 809. The port should end in 809, or that event has nowhere to fire.
- `934-gnupa-tribute` cites Adam of Bremen for Gnupa. Only Widukind names Chnuba; Adam (i.57 (?), not i.59) names
  the beaten king Worm (Gorm), and Thietmar calls him Cnuto. That the king sat at Hedeby rests on the stones.
- `808-summer-danevirke`: the title follows the annals ('along the Eider'), but the wall runs from the Schlei to
  the Treene marshes, well north of the Eider. Its note already says the timbers are of 737; no card should call the
  wall new.
- `958-gorm-jelling` lists the port `ripa`. Jelling is some 60 km from Ripa; the entry is news for the whole realm.
- `965-harald-baptised`: the effect ('the Danish realm Christian: churches in its ports') says too much.
  `965-ibrahim-hedeby`, the same year, has Hedeby with few Christians. Say 'the king Christian'.
- `980-trelleborgs` names four forts. Borgring near Køge, found in 2014, is a fifth.
- `986-harald-dies`: the year is 985, 986 or 987; the day, All Saints, is Adam's.
- `854-horik-killed` has no `ports`; its effect (the church shut) belongs at `sliesthorp`.
