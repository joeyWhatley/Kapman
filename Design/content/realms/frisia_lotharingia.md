# Frisia and the Rhine delta, 793 to 1066: a merchant's account

Written 4 October 2026. The data is in `frisia_lotharingia.json` beside this file (111 rulers in twelve lines, 24 wars, 41 raids, 34 laws, 18 coinages, 23 church events, each with its source). This page tells the same years as a Frisian skipper's house would live them. Codes: AB, AF, AX = the annals of St Bertin, Fulda and Xanten by year; Reg = Regino; Alp = Alpert of Metz; LF = the Frisian law; OSU = the Utrecht charters; "dossier" = `Design/content/dossiers`. (?) = not checked on the day.

**Four things never change.** The king's due is a tenth of the cargo, taken where the rivers meet the sea. The church of Utrecht has a share of it and plots of its own where its men land free. Whoever holds the river mouth takes the due: first the king's agent, then a Dane, then a count. And the protector always arrives after the raid.

## Decade by decade

**793-799. The king's port.** Pay the tenth and the bank due to the king's agents at Dorestad; no name of theirs survives, so "the count" of your debt is a made-up man in a real office [OSU 56; dossier dorestad]. Land on St Martin's plots and you pay the bishop, Hamacar, instead. 793 is a famine year, and the coast east of the Lauwers is in revolt: Count Theoderic's men lie dead by the Weser, the churches are burned [ARF 793]. Stay west of Dokkum until 797. From June 794 only the new heavy penny passes; refusing it is an offence, and bread has a top price [Capit. I 28].

**800s. The law years.** The emperor is often at Nijmegen in Lent. You have sworn to him (802). Your own law is written down: a free Frisian's life is 53 shillings and a penny, the shilling three pennies, and selling a slave to heathens costs your own man-price [LF]. Forbidden: mail coats and swords for the north (805); new tolls; holding grain for a famine price (806); a double cloak above 20 shillings, with half the fine to the informer (808). Godfred burns Reric in 808 and makes Sliesthorp: a new market, a dangerous king.

**810s. The hundred pounds.** In 810 two hundred Danish ships strip the islands; three fights are lost; the Frisians pay 100 pounds of silver [ARF 810]. A cattle plague kills the oxen. Then quiet: Godfred is dead, peace is sworn on the Eider (811), ships are built at Ghent, Charles dies (814). Louis gives the East Frisians back the right to inherit (814) and confirms Utrecht's tenth (815). The penny shows a ship.

**820s. The good years.** Nothing to fear but the weather: in 821 carts cross the frozen Rhine for thirty days. At Martinmas 825 old pennies stop passing. In 826 a ship with two cabins comes down past Dorestad with a baptised Danish king and a monk called Ansgar. In 828 the emperor's own merchants are told they still owe the tenth at Dorestad. This is as rich as the place will ever be.

**830s. The raids.** The emperor's sons put him down; the coast guard stops. In 834 a fleet comes by Utrecht and plunders Dorestad; again in 835; in 836 Antwerp and Witla burn; on 17 June 837 Walcheren falls and its count is killed [AB, AF]. Stay out of Dorestad from Pentecost to Lammas. The emperor comes to Nijmegen each time too late and blames the Frisians. On St Stephen's Day 838 the sea drowns 2,437.

**840s. Three kings and a heathen lord.** Lothar gives Walcheren to the Dane Harald (841): at the Scheldt mouth his men take what they like. Verdun (843) puts the delta in Lothar's kingdom, Ghent in Charles's, Mainz in Louis's: three coinages. Dorestad burns while Lothar watches from Nijmegen; in 847 Danes hold it and the Betuwe [AX, AB]. Nobody protects.

**850s. Rorik.** In 850 Rorik takes Dorestad with a fleet and Lothar makes him his man: he is to pay in the king's dues and keep pirates off [AF 850]. Pay him the tenth; the penny still bears Lothar's name. It works while he is at home. In 857 he sails for Denmark, other Danes storm Dorestad and sack Utrecht, and the bishop leaves for good.

**860s. The last of Dorestad.** January 863, the rivers in flood: Dorestad sacked, Frisian merchants killed in the estate they had fled to; the fleet leaves "on Rorik's advice" [AB 863]. Rorik is baptised now, and an archbishop writes to ask what he knew. In 864 every farm pays 4 pennies to hire another Northman. In 867 the Frisians drive Rorik out. Move the house to Deventer or Tiel.

**870s. A border in the river mouths.** Lothar II dies; at Meerssen (870) Utrecht and two thirds of Frisia go to Louis the German. Rorik swears to each king in turn and is not heard of after 873. That June Oostergo tells Rodulf it owes tribute to nobody but King Louis, and kills him [AF 873]. Dorestad's harbour silts up and is given up about 875. In November 879 the great army lands at Ghent: stay off the Scheldt.

**880s. The worst years.** The army winters in the palace at Nijmegen, then at Elsloo; Cologne and Bonn burn; the emperor pays over 2,000 pounds and makes Godfrid lord of the river mouths (882). Deventer's port is burned. Stay off every river until 885, when Godfrid is cut down at a parley. A Frisian count, Gerulf, holds the coast, and in 889 the king gives him land of his own.

**890s. Dorestad's freedom moves.** Arnulf breaks the Northmen at Leuven (891) and they leave for England. On St John's Day 896 King Zwentibold rules that Utrecht's men at Deventer and Tiel keep the freedom they had at Dorestad [OSU 88]. Pay the king's toll at Tiel or Deventer; the bishop, in exile, sits at Deventer.

**900s-910s. Lords' quarrels.** Zwentibold is killed by his own counts (900). From 911 to 925 the delta's king sits at Laon. The danger now is a count's feud, not a fleet. The Cologne penny is the coin of the river.

**920s-940s. The Saxon kings.** Bishop Balderic brings the see home to Utrecht (about 920) and walls it. Count Dirk gets Egmond (922). Henry I takes Lotharingia (925); Otto is crowned at Aachen (936). Stay off the Rhine crossings in 939, when Duke Gilbert drowns at Andernach. In 948 a charter speaks of "the estate once called Dorsteti, now Wik".

**950s-970s. The bishops' tolls.** In 953 Otto's brother Bruno is archbishop of Cologne and duke at once, and Balderic gets Utrecht's mint and the toll at Muiden: going north by the Vecht you pay the bishop. The peace is long. Dirk II builds in stone at Egmond.

**980s-990s. Pennies north, raiders back.** Tiel and Deventer strike the pennies that fill the hoards of the Baltic. In 985 Dirk II becomes owner of his counties and the king's toll at Medemblik goes to Count Ansfried, bishop from 995. In 991 pirates waste Stavoren; in 993 the West Frisians kill Count Arnulf at Winkel. North of Kennemerland nobody owes the count anything.

**1000s. Tiel and Utrecht burn.** In London the emperor's men trade like Londoners for cloth, pepper, gloves and vinegar twice a year. In 1006 a fleet burns Tiel; in 1007 it comes to Utrecht, where the merchants burn their own port [Alp I.8-10].

**1010s. The count's toll.** Dirk III builds a fort at Vlaardingen and takes toll of every ship between Tiel and England. The merchants of Tiel complain at Nijmegen at Easter 1018; on 29 July the emperor's army drowns in the ditches [Alp II.20-21]. Pay twice: the king at Tiel, the count at the mouth.

**1020s-1030s. Sworn merchants, a prince bishop.** At Tiel the merchants judge their own cases, swear each other clear and feast from a common purse [Alp II.20]. The bishop becomes count of Drenthe (1024) and of Tiel's land (1026). In Frisia the pennies are a Brunswick count's and light. At Pentecost 1039 Emperor Conrad dies at Utrecht.

**1040s. War at the mouths.** Deventer's toll goes to the bishop (1046). The emperor comes by ship against Dirk IV in 1046 and 1047 and loses men in the marsh; Nijmegen's palace is burned; Dirk is killed at Dordrecht on 13 January 1049. Do not lie at Vlaardingen when a fleet is out.

**1050s-1066. A child king.** Floris I is killed under a tree in 1061. In 1062 the boy king is carried off by ship at Kaiserswerth. In 1064 he gives the count's land to the bishop of Utrecht on parchment; on the water Robert the Frisian still takes the toll.

## Ten scenes

1. **The new penny** (dorestad, 794). The mint will take your light pennies and give fewer heavy ones back. Change now, or run them north to a coast that still counts by the old weight. [Capit. I 28; LF]
2. **A hundred pounds** (wijnaldum or doccinga, summer 810). Three fights lost. Each terp must find its share of the tribute by weight; you have silver aboard. Give, lend at a price, or sail. [ARF 810]
3. **The fleet by Utrecht** (dorestad, 834). Word comes down the Kromme Rijn a day ahead. Load and run up the Lek, bury the silver, or stay with the storehouse. [AB 834]
4. **St Stephen's flood** (doccinga, 26 December 838). The sea almost to the top of the dunes; 2,437 counted. A boat is the one thing that floats. Whose cattle, whose child, whose debt. [AB 839]
5. **Rorik's oath** (dorestad, 850). The man whose fleet burned the Betuwe now takes the king's tenth on the dam. Pay and be known to him, or go round by the Waal. [AF 850]
6. **The flood winter** (dorestad, January 863). The merchants flee to a large estate and are killed there. Rorik talks the fleet into leaving. Did he know? A priest asks you what you saw. [AB 863; Flodoard (?)]
7. **Oostergo's answer** (doccinga, June 873). "Tribute to none but King Louis and his sons." Rodulf falls. An old Northman, long a Christian here, bargains the rest out for their treasure. You can carry the silver, or the news. [AF 873]
8. **No wine in Frisia** (noviomagus, June 885). Godfrid has asked for Koblenz and the vineyards; Count Gerulf carried the message. At the island where Rhine and Waal part, Count Everhard strikes first. You are lying upstream with a cargo of wine. [Reg 885]
9. **They burn their own port** (traiectum, 1007). The sails are on the Lek. The merchants fire the waterfront and go into the bishop's fort. Help carry, save your berth, or be the one who talks to the Northmen. [Alp I.10]
10. **Easter at Nijmegen, the ditches at Vlaardingen** (noviomagus, 1018). Speak for the Tiel men before the emperor, or keep the count's goodwill. On 29 July ships are wanted to carry the army. [Alp II.20-21]

## Against `chronicle_793.json`

**Wrong, or to mend.**

- `836-witla-antwerp-burned` cites AB 836. The burning of Antwerp and Witla is in AF 836; AB 836 has only Dorestad and Frisia.
- `837-06-17-walcheren-raid` cites AB 837 alone. The names Eggihard and Hemming and the day are AF 837. AF says Dorestad was laid waste and tribute taken of the Frisians.
- `863-dorestad-last` fires on 1 March. AB puts it in January; the fleet lay by Neuss until about April. Xanten's church was burned on the same raid (AX 864 = 863).
- `1006-tiel-burned` puts Utrecht's burning of its own port in 1006. Alpert has it the year after: 1007.
- `882-07-asselt-godfrid` writes "Asselt (Elsloo)" at 51.2 N, while `881-winter-aachen-cologne` puts the camp at Elsloo, 50.95 N. They are two places on the Meuse about 35 km apart, and the annals' "Ascloha" has been read as either; choose one. The payment is 2,412 pounds in the Mainz text of AF and 2,080 in the Bavarian text; the note gives only the second. What Godfrid got, in AF and AB, is Kennemerland and what Rorik had held; "Frisia" is Regino's word.
- `882-07-asselt-godfrid` and `885-06-godfrid-killed` name the port `dorestad`, which the port table ends in 863.
- `885-06-godfrid-killed`: Herispich (Spijk) is at the fork of Rhine and Waal by Lobith, just east of the Betuwe. Add Count Everhard, who struck first (Regino).
