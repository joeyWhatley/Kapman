# The Norwegian kingdoms, 780-1070: a merchant's guide

For the writers of cards and dialogue. The data is in `norway.json` (34 rulers, 18 wars, 24 raids, 18 laws, 8 coin rows, 18 church rows). **(saga)** = told only by the kings' sagas of about 1190-1230: colour, not fact. **(?)** = not checked against the text on 4 Oct 2026.

**The realm in one paragraph.** There is no Norway in 793. There is a coast road, the *North Way*, and whoever holds a narrow sound on it takes his due. The chart has two Norwegian ports: `skiringssal` in the Vik (a market from about 800 to about 960, probably a Danish king's foundation) and `ogvaldsnes` on the Karmsund (a king's farm through the whole game). They sell whetstones, soapstone pots, iron, furs, walrus tooth, hide rope, down, dried fish and people. They want grain, malt, wine, cloth, glass, silver and the Frankish swords you are forbidden to sell. Silver goes by weight until Harald's penny, about 1050.

## Decade by decade

| Years | Who must be paid, who protects | What is forbidden, when to stay away |
|---|---|---|
| **780s** | A sea-king at Ögvaldsnes, name lost; his forerunner was laid in a ship in the mound Storhaug in 779. In the Vik, kings of Vestfold (Halfdan the Mild, by legend) under the king of the Danes. You pay with a gift; your host protects you, nobody else. | 789: three Hordaland ships kill the king's reeve at Portland. He took them for traders. |
| **790s** | The same. A second ship is buried at Ögvaldsnes about 790-795. | Lindisfarne (793), Jarrow (794), Iona (795): the young men of the west are away all summer and come home with church silver and captives. Buying them is your choice. |
| **800s** | Skíringssalr is laid out in plots about 800 (a jetty timber of 803). Pay the king's man at the hall of Huseby; the thing at Thjodalyng keeps the market peace. | Frankish law forbids you to sell swords or mail (805). |
| **810s** | The Danish kings Harald and Reginfrid. | **Stay away in 813:** their army is in Vestfold to make it obey. |
| **820s-830s** | The chiefs of Vestfold (Gudrod the Hunter, Olaf Geirstad-Alf, by legend). The Vik is rich: in late summer 834 a great lady is buried in the Oseberg ship with silk and fine cloth. | Nothing new. |
| **840s** | The same. Dublin is a ship-fort from 841. | 843: men of Vestfold storm Nantes. What they bring home is on the market in autumn. |
| **850s** | Danish kingship breaks in 854: Vestfold is its own master. A Frankish or Frisian trading household lives at Skíringssalr. | An Olaf, 'son of the king of Laithlind', takes tribute in Ireland (853). |
| **860s** | The same; the Frisians at Skíringssalr drift away as Dorestad fails (863). | In the west Harald's wars begin (saga): ask whose man a skipper is. |
| **870s** | **Harald Fairhair** after Hafrsfjord (872 by tradition, perhaps 885-900): pay his steward at Ögvaldsnes. | Five ounces of silver a head to the king for every man you carry to Iceland (landaurar). |
| **880s-890s** | Harald on the west coast; Ragnvald in Vestfold. Ohthere sails a month from the far north to Skíringssalr and five days on to Hedeby. | The Finns' tribute (furs, down, walrus-hide rope) is the northern chiefs': you buy from them, not from the Finns. |
| **900s-920s** | Harald; in Vestfold his son Bjorn the Merchant, who keeps trading ships (saga). The Gokstad chief is buried about 901. | Skíringssalr thins from a town to a summer market. |
| **930s** | Eric Bloodaxe (932-934) kills Bjorn the Merchant; then **Hakon the Good**, raised in England. The Gulathing has one law for the west by 930. | Stay out of Vestfold while the brothers fight. |
| **940s** | Hakon; Jarl Sigurd in the north. A quiet decade. | In a levy summer ships and men are called out; malt must be brewed for Yule (saga). |
| **950s** | Hakon. | **953: the sons of Eric land at Ögvaldsnes itself.** When the beacons burn, stay in harbour. |
| **960s** | Harald Greycloak and his brothers (961-970). | Famine: snow at midsummer. Grain is worth more than silver. Skíringssalr's market ends about 960; after it the chart has one Norwegian port. |
| **970s** | **Jarl Hakon** of Lade; over him Harald Bluetooth, who 'won all Norway' (the Jelling stone) and holds the Vik. Good seasons. | 974-975: war at the Danevirke, then Danish ships burn the west coast (saga). Heathen feasts are kept: do not mock them. |
| **980s** | Jarl Hakon alone. | 986: a Danish fleet is beaten in Hjorungavag. Olaf Tryggvason raids Frisia about now. |
| **990s** | Jarl Hakon to 995, then **Olaf Tryggvason**. Nidaros is laid out as a market (997); the first Norwegian penny is struck. | **Baptism or no sailing** for heathen crews. Sacrifice is forbidden. |
| **1000s** | Jarl Eirik in the west under Sweyn of Denmark; on the spot, Erling Skjalgsson of Sola. English pennies everywhere. | Nothing: fifteen easy years. |
| **1010s** | The jarls to 1015; then **Olaf Haraldsson**, who has raided the Frisian coast (1009), London and Canterbury and been baptised at Rouen. | 1016: battle off Nesjar in the Vik. 1016-1019: the border with the Svear is shut. |
| **1020s** | Olaf and his stewards. An Icelander pays one toll, half a mark, and no other. | **No grain north out of the south-west (1022).** No trade or loading on Sundays and feasts, no meat on Fridays (Moster, 1024). The Bjarmaland trade is the king's (1026). 1028: Cnut comes; Erling is killed off Bokn. |
| **1030s** | Svein and his mother Alfiva for Cnut (1030-1035); then **Magnus the Good**. Pilgrims come to Saint Olaf from 1031. | **The worst years:** a room across every outbound ship is the king's; nobody leaves without leave; one Dane's oath outweighs ten Norwegians. Eased from 1035; the feuds closed about 1038. |
| **1040s** | Magnus, king of the Danes too from 1042; **Harald Sigurdsson** with him from 1046, alone from 1047. | Danish waters are at war every summer from 1043. |
| **1050s** | Harald. Oslo is his base in the Vik. | Hedeby burned (1049 or 1050). **After about 1055 the king's penny is two-thirds copper:** take weighed silver or goods. Grain to Iceland by the king's leave and at his price. |
| **1060s** | Harald to 1066; then his sons Magnus and **Olaf the Quiet**. | Sea-fight at the Nisa (1062), peace with Denmark (1064). 1066: about 300 ships sail for England; 24 come home. After it, peace. |

## Ten moments that would make the best scenes

1. **The sound and the two mounds** (Ögvaldsnes, 793-795). You come up the Karmsund and a boat puts out from the king's farm. On the ridge a new mound is rising over a ship, beside the old one of 779. The lord's man asks what you carry. *True:* the two ship graves and their years (tree rings). *Ours:* the meeting.
2. **Home from the Loire** (Skíringssalr, autumn 843). Men of Vestfold are back from Nantes with a bishop's church plate and captives who speak Romance. You are a Christian from Francia: buy, ransom, or look away. *True:* the Annals of Angouleme name the takers of Nantes as men of Vestfold.
3. **The day after Hafrsfjord** (the Karmsund, 872 by tradition). Beaten ships pass north, the wounded lying in the bilge. Within the month chiefs are selling land and stock for a passage west, and the king's man is counting heads at five ounces each. *True:* the battle (Harald's own poet); the toll (Ari).
4. **Ohthere's cargo** (Skíringssalr, about 890). A rich man from the far north, on his way to Hedeby and King Alfred, with walrus tooth and ropes of hide. He owns six hundred reindeer and twenty cattle and ploughs with horses. 'He said that no man abode north of him' (Bosworth 1859, already in `peoples/norse.md`).
5. **Herring for a cloak-pin** (the west coast, about 965-970). It snows at midsummer. The king's own poet sells his arrows, and the silver pin the Icelanders sent him, for herring. Your hold is full of rye. *True:* the poet's own verses.
6. **Easter at Ögvaldsnes** (998). King Olaf Tryggvason keeps the feast. Sorcerers are bound on a skerry as the tide comes in; an old one-eyed guest keeps the king awake with tales of the dead king Ogvald and his cow. Your heathen deckhand must take the first signing or the ship does not sail. *(saga)*
7. **The steward and the sail** (Ögvaldsnes, about 1022-1023). The king has forbidden grain to go north. His steward Thorir Sel seizes Asbjorn's corn, bought from a great man's thralls, and takes his good sail too. At Easter Asbjorn walks into the king's hall and Thorir's head lands on the table in front of the king. *(saga)* The player can be the next skipper the steward stops.
8. **A room for the king** (any Norwegian harbour, 1030-1035). Alfiva's law: a space across your ship is the king's on every voyage out, and in a suit the oath of one Dane outweighs ten Norwegians. A Dane claims your cargo. *(the sagas' list of the laws; the years are sure)*
9. **The thin pennies** (Nidaros or Oslo, about 1055). The king pays in his own new coin, more copper than silver. His old comrade Halldor throws his pay on the floor. You have sold a cargo for a price counted in those pennies. *True:* the coins themselves.
10. **Three hundred out, twenty-four home** (Ögvaldsnes, autumn 1066). Every farm on the sound sent a man. The heirs' game ends here: who is left to buy, and what do the widows sell? *True:* the Chronicle's numbers.

## Against `chronicle_793.json`

**Wrong, or to mend**

- `843-06-24-nantes-sacked`, note: the name *Westfaldingi* is in the Annals of Angouleme (Ann. Engolismenses 843), not the Annals of Saint-Bertin, which say only Northmen. The (?) can go once the note is mended.
- `1028-cnut-norway` lists the port `skiringssal`, which `ports_793.json` closes in 960.
- `872-hafrsfjord` lists the region `vestfold`: the fight and its result touch the west coast only; Harald's hold on the Vik is the sagas' claim.
- `995-olaf-tryggvason` puts the founding of Nidaros in its effect line; by Snorri that is 997. It has no port: `ogvaldsnes` fits.
- `1047-10-25-harald-hardrada` reads as if Harald came to power in 1047; he shared the kingship from 1046.
- `1050-hedeby-burned`: 1049 or 1050; this file uses 1049. Either is defensible; they should agree.
- `1000-09-09-svolder`: not wrong, but 999 is also argued.

**Lacking** (each is in `norway.json` with its source)

- 789: the three Hordaland ships at Portland (backstory, before the start).
- 779 and about 790-795: the ship graves at Ögvaldsnes; 834: the Oseberg burial; about 901: the Gokstad burial (evidence entries like `803-kaupang-jetty`).
- 853: Amlaib, son of the king of Laithlind, takes tribute at Dublin.
- About 880-930: landaurar, the king's toll on the Iceland passage.
- 934: Hakon the Good king; **953: the battle at Ögvaldsnes**; 961: Fitjar and his death.
- About 960: the end of the market at Skíringssalr (to match the port's last year).
- About 965-970: famine in Norway under Harald Greycloak; 970: his killing, and Harald Bluetooth's lordship over Norway (the Jelling stone).
- 986: Hjorungavag. 997: Nidaros laid out. About 995-1000: the first Norwegian penny.
- 1015: Olaf Haraldsson comes home; 25 March 1016: Nesjar; 1016: Borg built.
- 1022: the grain ban (saga); 1024: the Christian law at Moster; 1026: the Holy River.
- 21 December 1028: Erling Skjalgsson killed off Bokn. 1030-1035: Alfiva's laws.
- 3 August 1031: Olaf's body taken up and declared holy (the Stiklestad entry only says 'soon').
- 1035: Magnus made king; 1042: king of the Danes too; 1046: Harald shares the kingship.
- About 1055: the penny debased. 1058: the fleet from Norway in the Irish Sea.
- 9 August 1062: the Nisa; 1064: peace with Denmark. 1066: Fulford and Scarborough stand only in a note.

**For the spine.** After 960 `ports_793.json` has no Norwegian port in the Vik, and none at all for Nidaros (997), Borg (1016) or Oslo (about 1050): for the last hundred years of the game Norway is one king's farm on the Karmsund.
