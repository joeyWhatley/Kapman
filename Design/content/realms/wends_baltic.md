# The Wends and the Baltic shore, 780 to 1070: the realm as a merchant lives it

The facts, with a source on every one, are in `wends_baltic.json` beside this page (rulers, overlords, wars, raids,
laws, coins, church). This page is the same story from the deck of a Frisian boat. Five peoples share it: the
Obodrites (Wismar bay to the Trave), the Wilzi, later called Lutici (east of them to the Oder), the Pomeranians (Oder
to Vistula), the Prussians (Wulfstan's Este, east of the Vistula) and the Curonians (north of them). `(?)` marks what
is uncertain or was written from memory of the source and not checked against an edition. No web search was made for
this page: it rests on the JSON, whose pages were read on 4 Oct 2026.

One thing shapes all of it. Almost nobody on this shore writes. Every name and date comes from a Frank, a Saxon, a
Dane, an Englishman or an Arab looking in. So the game can say who rules the Obodrites in most years, the Wilzi in
some, and the Prussians and Curonians in none; where the JSON says "(not recorded)" the game should say so too.

## The four questions

**Who must be paid.** At Reric to 808, the Danish king's toll, though the town is Obodrite. After 808 the same toll,
at Sliesthorp. On the Elbe from 805, the king's man at Bardowick or Magdeburg; from 965 Magdeburg's toll, mint and
court belong to the church of Saint Maurice. Among the Obodrites, the prince, when there is one: after 844 "several
dukes", each with a hand out. At Wolin, the elders of the town, and from 967 to about 1005 the Polish duke as well.
Among the Prussians, the lord of whichever fort you lie under. Among the Curonians after 854, nobody, if you come from
Birka or Gotland; your luck, if you come from Denmark. The Wends themselves pay silver west every year from 929 to
983 (the Obodrites to 1066), and that is why they sell.

**Who protects.** Your host. On this shore that is the whole law: Adam says no people is more honest or open-handed
to a guest than the men of Wolin, and that the Prussians put out to help ships in trouble. Among the Lutici a peace is
made with a lock of hair, a tuft of grass and the right hand, and it is kept. From 789 to 838 a Frankish merchant has
more: the Obodrite and Wilzi kings answer to the emperor's court, and know it. From 1043 to 1066 Gottschalk, a
Christian prince who served Cnut in England, keeps one peace from the Trave to the Peene.

**What is forbidden.** By your own king: mail and swords to the Slavs (779, 803, 805), and crossing the Elbe to
trade at all (805). Found out, you lose everything you carry. By the Wends: the cross shown openly at Wolin; a priest
landed among the Prussians (997: gone by nightfall or lose your head); a Christian near a Prussian grove or spring;
speaking against the assembly among the Lutici. Nobody forbids what you most expect: there is no ban on a heathen
buying or a Christian selling.

**When to stay away.** Reric in 808 and 809. The Elbe mouth in 817. The Curonian coast in 853 and 854. The middle
Elbe in 929, 939, 955, 983 to 997, 1033 to 1035 and from 1056 on. The Oder mouth in 963 to 967. The whole Obodrite
land in 1018, and the road from Hamburg to the Trave about 1028 to 1030. Wolin and south Jutland in 1043. Everything
between Hamburg and Schleswig in the summer and autumn of 1066.

## Decade by decade

- **790s.** Witzan, then Thrasco: the Obodrites are the Frankish king's friends. Reric on Wismar bay is their market
  and the Danish king takes its toll. Witzan is killed at the Elbe crossing in 795; in 798 Thrasco cuts down the
  Saxons at Bornhöved. Captives, wax, honey, furs and horses for Frankish cloth, querns, wine and, quietly, blades.
- **800s.** 804: the emperor gives Thrasco the Saxon land beyond the Elbe. 805: the border posts. 808: Godfred
  storms the Obodrite forts, hangs one duke, drives out the other, breaks up Reric and ships its merchants to
  Sliesthorp. 809: Thrasco comes back and is murdered at Reric by a trick. The Wends have no market of their own again
  until Wolin.
- **810s.** The Saxons get their land back and a border is drawn (the Limes). 815: Obodrites march with the Saxons
  over the Eider. 817: King Sclaomir, told to share his rule, calls a Danish fleet into the Elbe; 819: he is exiled.
- **820s.** The palace years. Ceadrag is accused before the emperor three times and keeps his seat. In 823 two
  Wilzi brothers bring their quarrel to Frankfurt and the emperor gives the kingship to the one the people want.
  Quiet, and a merchant's complaint is heard.
- **830s and 840s.** 838: both peoples fall away while the emperor's sons fight. 844: Louis the German kills King
  Goztomuizli and sets up several dukes. No king of all the Wilzi is ever named again. Dirhams reach the coast in
  quantity: the hoard at Ralswiek on Rügen, some 2,200 coins, is laid down about now.
- **850s and 860s.** The far shore. About 853 the Curonians destroy a Danish fleet; about 854 the Svear burn Seeburg
  and besiege Apulia until it pays. 862: the Obodrite duke Tabomuizli gives his son as hostage. The Elbe posts work
  when the king is strong.
- **870s to 920s.** The free years. Arnulf fails against the Obodrites in 889 and takes gifts in 895. No tribute, no
  bishop, no named prince. About 890 Wulfstan sails seven days and nights from Hedeby to Truso and writes down the
  Este: a king in each fort, mare's milk for the rich, mead for the poor, and a dead man's goods run for on horseback.
  Hack-silver and the folding scale are the only mint.
- **920s and 930s.** The winter of 928 to 929: King Henry takes Brandenburg over the ice, every people to the Oder
  is made to pay, the Redarii rise and are destroyed at Lenzen on 4 September. 939: Margrave Gero kills some thirty
  Slav princes at a feast. Tribute silver goes west; captives go west and south.
- **940s and 950s.** Bishops at Havelberg and Brandenburg (948). Magdeburg grows into the gate of the Wends, and the
  rimmed "Wendish" pennies are struck for the trade. 955: Otto crosses the Raxa; Stoinef's head is set on a pole and
  700 captives are beheaded round it. Nakon keeps Mecklenburg by paying.
- **960s and 970s.** The best years to trade. Ibrahim ibn Yaqub finds Nakon's land cheap and full of horses, and a
  great town by the sea with no king: Wolin. 967: Mieszko of the Poles breaks the Wolinians and takes the Oder mouth.
  968: an archbishop at Magdeburg, a bishop at Oldenburg. 975: Magdeburg men trade toll-free everywhere but Mainz,
  Cologne, Tiel and Bardowick.
- **980s and 990s.** 983: the Lutici take Havelberg and Brandenburg in three days; Mstivoj burns Hamburg. War every
  year to 997. The inland road shuts; the coast does not. About 986 Harald Bluetooth dies a fugitive at Wolin. 997:
  Adalbert is killed by the Prussians a week after landing.
- **1000s and 1010s.** 1000: a bishop for the Pomeranians at Kołobrzeg, who throws anointed stones into the sea;
  within seven years the Pomeranians are rid of him and of the Poles. 1003: the Christian king takes the heathen
  Lutici as allies; they march behind their idols. About now Truso ends in fire and the dirhams stop. 1009: Bruno
  beheaded. 1018: the Obodrites drive out their Christian prince and burn every church.
- **1020s and 1030s.** Udo, "a bad Christian", is killed by a Saxon; his son Gottschalk turns robber, is caught, and
  goes to serve Cnut. Ratibor rules. The Lutici take Werben (1035) and pay a heavier tribute (1036). English and
  Saxon pennies fill the hoards.
- **1040s and 1050s.** 1043: Magnus burns much of Wolin; Ratibor is killed; a Wendish army wastes Jutland to Ribe and
  dies on Lyrskov Heath. Gottschalk comes home and builds churches. 1056: the Lutici destroy a Saxon army at
  Pritzlawa. 1057: they fight each other, and Gottschalk, the Saxon duke and the Danish king sell them peace for
  15,000 pounds of silver.
- **1060s.** Three bishops in Gottschalk's land about 1060. On 7 June 1066 he is killed at Lenzen. Ratzeburg's monks
  are stoned, Bishop John's head goes to Rethra on a spear, Hamburg is wasted, Schleswig destroyed. Kruto the heathen
  rules. In the winter of 1067 to 1068 a Saxon bishop rides home on the god's horse.

## Ten moments for scenes

1. **Reric, summer 808.** The Danish king's ships in the bay, the town coming down, and every merchant told to load
   and sail for Sliesthorp. The player may be one of them. Go, hide, or run for the Elbe.
2. **Reric, late 809.** Thrasco is back and the booths are up again. The player is in harbour the day he is killed
   "by a trick". Who saw what, and who asks.
3. **Bardowick, any year after 805.** A Wend offers three times the price for a mail coat, with the king's man
   Hredi forty paces off. The game's cleanest forbidden-goods card.
4. **Frankfurt, May 823.** Two Wilzi brothers before the emperor, each with his own interpreter and his own gifts.
   A merchant who has sold to both is asked which one the people want.
5. **Apulia, about 854.** Eight days of siege. The Svear cast lots and no god answers; the merchants in the army say
   try Christ. The player can be one of those merchants (Rimbert says they were there).
6. **Truso, about 890.** A dead trader lies cold in his house while his kin drink his goods away; on the last day
   what is left is set out in heaps along a mile and ridden for. He owed the player money. The player may enter a
   horse.
7. **The Raxa, 17 October 955.** The morning after: a head on a pole, 700 dead round it, and Saxon lords with
   captives to sell and no carts. A scene about what the slave trade is.
8. **Wolin, about 986.** A wounded Danish king is carried ashore in a town where a cross must stay inside the
   shirt. Vulcan's pot burns on the headland. News of his death is worth a great deal at Sliesthorp if the player
   gets there first.
9. **The Prussian market, April 997.** A duke's boat lands three priests and leaves. The crowd gives them until
   night. The player's boat is the only other one on the beach.
10. **Mecklenburg and Schleswig, 1066.** A queen driven out on foot, a bishop led from fort to fort, and at
    Schleswig a harbour full of merchants with a day's warning or none. The last great card of the game on this shore.

Spare: Gero's feast (939); the Circipani counting out 15,000 pounds (1057); Reinbern's stones in the sea (1000);
the horse at Rethra asked whether the Lutici march (1005).

## Against `chronicle_793.json`

The chronicle is thick to 815 and thin after, by its own rule. For this shore it holds 20 entries: 795, 798, 804
(two), 805, 808 (four), 809 (two), 810, 811, 812, 815, 854, 890, 983, 1043 and 1066, with Wolin in a note under 986.

**Wrong or doubtful**

- `983-slav-rising` and `1066-hedeby-slavs` both list the port `reric`, which the ports table ends in 808. They need
  a living Obodrite place (Starigard/Oldenburg or Mecklenburg, neither yet in `ports_793.json`) or no port.
- `1043-09-28-lyrskov`: "Magnus also burns Jomsborg (Wolin)". Jomsborg is the sagas' name, two centuries later; the
  source of the time says Jumne. The entry also leaves out that Ratibor was killed that year and his eight sons fell
  on the heath, which is why Gottschalk could come home.
- `854-courland-war` says Grobin is "taken and burned" in 854, but `ports_793.json` closes Grobin in 850. And
  `890-ohthere-wulfstan` sends Wulfstan to Truso forty years after the same table closes it. The ports table is the
  one that is wrong (see `needs` in the JSON).
- `1066-hedeby-slavs` fires on 1 July with no day. Gottschalk dies on 7 June, Ratzeburg falls on 15 July, Bishop
  John is killed on 10 November; Hamburg and Schleswig come after Lenzen, in that order in Adam. One entry hides
  five months.
- `986-harald-dies` has no port or region for the place he died; Jumne is only in the note.
- `965-ibrahim-hedeby` keeps Hedeby and Mainz and drops what he says of Nakon, of the kingless town by the sea and
  of Mieszko, which is the only eyewitness account of the Wends in the whole period.
- Spelling: "Abodrites" in the entries of 795 to 809, "Obodrite" in 815, 983 and 1066. Pick one.
- `1000-09-09-svolder`: the regions leave out the Wendish coast, where the sagas put it. The note already says Adam
  puts it in the Øresund; a `(?)` on the place would be honest.
