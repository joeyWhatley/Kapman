# Francia, 780 to 1070: the realm as a merchant lives it

Companion to `francia.json` (rulers by part, a port-by-port lordship table, wars, raids, laws, coins, church; every row sourced there). Annals by year: AB Saint-Bertin, AF Fulda, AX Xanten, WJ William of Jumièges, WP William of Poitiers. (?) = not checked against the text.

## The four questions

- **Who must be paid.** To 840 the king: a tenth at the two sea gates, Dorestad and Quentovic, which no charter waives (828, 831); elsewhere toll only where a bridge, ferry or market serves you (805, 809). A tenth of Dorestad's toll is the bishop of Utrecht's (815). From 843 three kings; from 850 at Dorestad a Dane. In tribute years the king taxes merchants as a class: houses and gear valued (860), a tenth of all stock (866). After 900 whoever holds the bridge: count, bishop or abbot, each with his own penny.
- **Who protects.** To 830 the king: coast guard (800), fleet (811), and the emperor's letter that keeps your ship from being pressed (828). In Frisia 841-885 a Danish lord set to keep other Danes out. From the 880s a count behind a wall (Flanders, Paris); from 911 the count of Rouen. From 989 the bishops' peace, which names merchants from 1023; from 1047 the duke's truce in Normandy.
- **What is forbidden.** Mail and swords out of the realm (779, 805); from 864 a mail coat, weapon or horse to a Northman is death. Slaves beyond the march. Trade past the nine eastern posts (805). Usury, and buying corn at harvest to sell dear (806). New Sunday markets (809). Refusing a good penny; old pennies after Martinmas 825 and 864.
- **When to stay away.** Dorestad in the summers of 834-837, in 846-847, 857 and January 863. The Seine 841, 845, 851-866, 876-877, 885-889. The Loire 843-873 and 903-937. Flanders and the Meuse 879-892. Normandy's roads 1035-1047. The Flemish coast in 1049.

**Frisia inside the realm.** One king to 840. Lothar's middle kingdom 843-869, with Danish lords at Walcheren (841) and Dorestad (850). Split at Meerssen (870), all eastern from 880. A western king again, Charles the Simple, 911-925. German for good from 925: after that the count in West Frisia and the bishop of Utrecht are the lords a ship meets.

## Decade by decade

- **780s.** One king. The Saxon war shuts the east; Frisians beyond the Lauwers rise (784). Abbey ships pass Dorestad and Quentovic free (779); you pay the tenth.
- **790s.** A royal quarrel nearly closes the Channel to the English (about 790); the letter to Offa reopens it (796). Famine, capped grain prices and a heavier penny (793-794). First raiders off Aquitaine (799).
- **800s.** An emperor. Nine gates to the Slavs, famine caps, cloak prices (805-808). The Danish king takes 100 pounds from the Frisian islands (810). Ships are built on every river.
- **810s.** Peace on the Eider. Louis confirms Utrecht's tenth at Dorestad (815). The safest years to trade.
- **820s.** Thirteen pirate ships beaten off (820). One penny for the whole empire (825). A Danish king baptised and given Rüstringen (826). The emperor's letter for merchants (828). Dorestad at its height.
- **830s.** The sons put their father down (833); nobody watches the coast. Dorestad sacked four summers running; Antwerp and Witla burned (836); Walcheren taken (837). The monks leave Noirmoutier. The sea drowns 2,437 in Frisia (838).
- **840s.** Three kingdoms (843): Dorestad is Lothar's, Quentovic and Paris are Charles's. Rouen (841), Quentovic (842), Nantes (843), Paris for 7,000 pounds (845), Bordeaux (848). Dorestad burned (846) and held (847).
- **850s.** Rorik, a Dane, is lord of Dorestad and takes the king's dues (850); while he is away others storm it and the bishop flees Utrecht (857). Brittany is a kingdom with Nantes (851). Fleets live on the Seine and Loire. Peasants who band against them are cut down by their own lords (859).
- **860s.** Dorestad's last sack (863). New penny and the death law at Pîtres (864). A tenth of merchants' stock for tribute (866). Baldwin, who stole the king's daughter, gets Flanders (863). Rorik driven out for a time (867).
- **870s.** Lothar's kingdom cut in two (870); Rorik serves both kings. Dorestad's harbour given up (about 875); trade goes to Deventer and Tiel. Oostergo's Frisians refuse tribute and kill Rodulf (873). The great army lands (879).
- **880s.** The worst decade: Ghent, Arras, Cologne, Aachen, Nijmegen, Deventer. A Danish duke in Frisia (882), murdered (885). 12,000 pounds (884). Paris holds (885-886). The empire ends; a count is king (888). Flanders builds forts.
- **890s.** The army is beaten on the Dyle (891) and starved out to Kent (892). Two kings in the west. Deventer and Tiel get Dorestad's old freedom (896).
- **900s.** The count of Flanders has an archbishop killed and keeps his county (900). Tours burns (903). Kings give mints and markets away.
- **910s.** Rollo, beaten at Chartres, is given Rouen (911). Frisia and Cologne take the western king (911). Brittany overrun; Nantes is the Northmen's (919).
- **920s.** The western king is deposed and jailed (922-923); Frisia goes to the German king (925). The last tributes (924, 926); Rouen's men burn Amiens and Arras (925).
- **930s.** Normandy reaches the Cotentin (933). The Bretons rise (931); Alan retakes Nantes (937). Flanders and Normandy fight for the Canche.
- **940s.** The count of Rouen murdered at a parley (942). Danes seize the king of the Franks (945). Two kings fail before Rouen (946).
- **950s-970s.** The duke of the Franks outweighs the king. Quentovic dies; Montreuil takes its place. Rouen calls in Danes against Blois (960s) (?). The king takes Arras from a child count (965). A raid on Aachen, an emperor before Paris (978).
- **980s.** Hugh Capet is king of Paris and Orléans (987). The first Peace of God (989).
- **990s.** The pope makes peace between Rouen and the English king (991). Norman peasants who met to make their own rules lose hands and feet (WJ V.2).
- **1000s.** Rouen wine and Flemish cloth pay toll at London. Danes may sell plunder at Rouen (?). Tiel burns (1006); Utrecht burns its own quay (1007).
- **1010s.** An English king in exile at Rouen, a Norwegian sea-king baptised there (1013-1014). The count at Vlaardingen beats the emperor's army for his toll (1018).
- **1020s.** Knights swear by name not to rob merchants (Beauvais, 1023). Father and son fight for Flanders (1028-1030).
- **1030s.** Three famine years, then crowds for Jerusalem (1033). A boy duke (1035). Queen Emma lodges at Bruges (1037-1040).
- **1040s.** The Truce of God in Flanders and Normandy (1042-1047). Nijmegen's palace burned (1047). English plunder sold in Flanders (1048). Two fleets shut its coast (1049).
- **1050s.** Earl Godwin winters at Bruges (1051-1052). The king is beaten twice in Normandy (1054, 1057).
- **1060s.** The count of Flanders rules France for a child king (1060-1067). Every hull in Normandy is wanted (1066). Flanders goes to Robert "the Frisian" (1071).

## Ten moments for scenes

1. **Dorestad, summer 834.** Sails come by Utrecht in the year the emperor got his sword back. Run upriver, bury the silver, or stay with the house. (AB 834)
2. **Quentovic, first light, 842.** Only the houses that were bought off are left standing. What is yours worth, and to whom do you pay? (AB 842)
3. **Paris, Easter eve 845.** The town is empty; 111 captives hang in sight of the king's army; then 7,000 pounds are weighed out. A man with scales is wanted by both sides. (AB 845; Translatio S. Germani)
4. **The Seine above Paris, January 861.** Merchants flee upriver by ship; the Danes row after them and take them. The game's chase, as the annal tells it. (AB 861)
5. **Dorestad, January 863.** A winter of floods. The Frisians shelter on a large estate; the Danes find it and kill many merchants. Rorik, your own lord, advises them to leave. (AB 863)
6. **Angers, winter 873.** The beaten Northmen ask for an island in the Loire until February, and a market. Buy what was stolen upstream, or sell them corn, with the king's leave. (AB 873)
7. **Herispich, 885.** The Danish duke of Frisia wants Koblenz, Andernach and Sinzig "for the wine" his land does not grow. A wine ship is welcome; then comes the parley where he is cut down. (Regino 885)
8. **Nantes, 937.** Alan cuts his way to the cathedral through thorns. The first salt ship up the Loire finds no market, no toll and no roof. (Chron. Nantes)
9. **Rouen, about 1003-1014.** Danes sell English silver and captives on the quay; an Irishman buys back his wife; an English king lodges in exile. (WJ V.7; Warner, Moriuht)
10. **Saint-Valery, September 1066.** The fleet waits fifteen days for a south wind; the saint's body is carried out; the duke pays for what he eats. Sell, be pressed, or carry the news to Bruges first. (WP II.2-6)

## Against `chronicle_793.json`

**Wrong or too sure**

- `845-03-28-paris-sacked`: Easter Sunday 845 was 29 March, not 28 (golden number 10; full moon Friday 27 March). The Translatio has them arrive on Easter eve and plunder on Easter day.
- `836-witla-antwerp-burned`: cites AB 836. Antwerp and Witla are in AF 836; the AB have only Dorestad and Frisia.
- `843-06-24-nantes-sacked`: "led by the rebel count Lambert", marked sure, rests only on the 11th-century Chronicle of Nantes. "Westfaldingi" is from the Annals of Angoulême, not the AB.
- `863-dorestad-last`: fires 1 March; the AB place it in January. It lacks the merchants killed on the estate.
- `1006-tiel-burned`: joins two years: Tiel 1006, Utrecht 1007 (Alpert).
- `885-11-24-siege-paris`: Sigfrid took 60 pounds and left in spring 886 (Abbo); the siege went on without him.
- `885-06-godfrid-killed`: "back under the East Frankish king": in 885 one emperor ruled east and west.
- `882-07-asselt-godfrid`: the two versions of the AF give 2,080 and 2,412 pounds.
- `ports_793.json`: Quentovic has no end year (last coin about 980). Herio's "raided in 799" is a guess: Alcuin names no island.
