## What the change is

1. He drops the h from the small common words: 'e, 'im, 'is, 'er, 'ere, 'ome, 'ad, 'ave, 'as, 'ow, 'ear, 'eart. It is kept on names and on words that would be hard to read without it (hill, hall, hand, hold, house, help).
2. He clips -ing: lookin', goin', mornin'. And he shortens as people do: I'll, don't, that's, we're. Nobody else in the game shortens a word, so this alone sets him apart.
3. A handful of country words, the same ones every time: 'tis and 'tisn't, me for my (me knee, me father), nowt, owt, summat, aye, afore, them for those, were for was (I were, it were), "a man as" for "a man who", reckon, mebbe, yon (once).
4. The old words stay clean. A riddle, a verse, a vow or what a rune stick says is given word for word, as he was taught it: the one thing he says properly.
5. In the grave scenes the same habits stay but there are fewer of them, and nothing is made funny by it.
6. Nothing else is changed: every fact, name, figure and the order of what he says is as it stands in the game now.

Apostrophes: 7 in all the lines now, 441 with the change (about 4.6 a line).
