# Your own company: the fellowship

The user, 7 Oct 2026: "a grand campaign should take a really long time; as the game goes on it'll become a multiple ship
fleet manager trading sim", and "Form your own 'company' or whatever term works best for the period".

This builds on the plan already written, Design/scale_to_fleets_plan.md ("From one boat to a house of ships"), which
has the stages, the numbers and the screens. What is new here is the name, the act of founding it, and how it sits in
the frame. Written from memory of the sources: each term needs its source checked before it goes in the wiki.

## The word

There was no "company" in 793. What there was:

| Term | Tongue | What it was | Use in the game |
|---|---|---|---|
| **félag** ("fellowship") | Old Norse: fé, goods, and lag, a laying together | Two or more men who put goods or a ship together for a venture and shared what it made; a partner is a félagi. Cut on rune stones of the tenth and eleventh centuries for men who traded together. | **The name of your company.** In English text: "the fellowship". A partner: "a fellow", or félagi in a Northman's mouth. |
| **gild** | Old English, Old Frisian, Frankish | A sworn brotherhood that feasted together and stood by each other in loss, at law and at burial. Charles forbade sworn gilds in 779, which shows they existed. Frisian merchants abroad had gilds: rune stones at Sigtuna in Sweden were raised by "the Frisians' gild-brothers". | **The later, larger thing**: a brotherhood of merchants across several ports that you may found or join. Not a firm: it owns nothing, it backs you. |
| **the house** | plain English | You, your kin, your hall or yard, your heir. | Stays as it is: what you are born into and pass on. The fellowship is what you make. |
| a share | plain | A half or a quarter of a ship or a cargo. Ships were owned in parts. | How partners are in a ship: "a half of the knarr". |

So the three rings: **your house** (kin, and it inherits) inside **your fellowship** (partners, ships, shares: the
company) inside, late, **a gild** (sworn merchants of many ports).

The words by people, for flavour in talk (English everywhere else): a Northman says félag; an Englishman or a Frisian
says gild for the sworn brotherhood and "fellowship" for the venture; a Frank's clerk writes societas.

## Founding it

It is an act, with a day, a place and witnesses, not a menu.

- **When it can be done**: you own your boat free of the debt, you have standing in at least one port, and one person
  who trusts you (a bond at "trusted" or better) will lay goods or silver beside yours.
- **How**: a card at a quay or a hall. Each partner names what he lays in (silver, goods, a share of a boat, his own
  work as a shipmaster). The shares are cut on a tally stick, one half kept by each. A Christian swears on a relic or
  before a priest; a man of the old gods on a ring; mixed partners must find an oath both hold, which is its own small
  story. From that day the frame's House workspace has a Fellowship panel.
- **What it gives**: a second boat under a shipmaster; goods bought in one port and sold by a partner in another; a
  loss shared; a partner's standing where you have none.
- **What it costs**: gains are split by shares at each reckoning (Michaelmas); a partner may cheat, die, quarrel, or
  ask for his share out; an oath-breaker's name travels as news does.

## How it grows into a fleet game (the long campaign)

The user plays mostly at 2x or 3x and wants a grand campaign that takes a really long time. The first hours are one
boat and every plank of her. The fellowship is what turns the game, by steps, into running ships you are not aboard:

1. **One boat** (the first season or two): you are the shipmaster. Everything in the frame is about her.
2. **A partner's boat**: you agree with him, face to face, where he will go and what he will carry. After that you
   know only what word reaches you. His boat is a mark on the chart where she was last seen.
3. **Shipmasters you hire**: orders are words, given at a quay or sent by another ship, and they are as old as the
   ship that carried them (the rule of the whole game). A good shipmaster uses his own head when the word is stale.
4. **Factors ashore**: a trusted man or woman at a far port who buys low for you all winter and holds goods for your
   ships. A storehouse, a plot.
5. **The fellowship as a power**: many ships, a hall, a place in a gild, loans to lords, a say in a town. The player
   now reads reports and gives orders, and sails himself when it matters, or because he wants to.

At each step the frame gains panels, never a new screen: Fellowship (partners, shares, the next reckoning), Ships (each
with where she was last seen and how old that word is, her orders, her shipmaster), Orders (words on their way),
Factors, the Gild. The brief face of Ships is one line a boat. The view of the ship shows whichever boat you are aboard.

## What needs deciding later (not now)

- Whether the player may leave his own boat for good and run the fellowship from a hall.
- Whether partners can be played against each other, and how far a partner may ruin you.
- How an heir takes over a fellowship whose partners swore to the dead man, not to him.
