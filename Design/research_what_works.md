# Kapman: what other games teach us

**The short answer.** Slow travel is loved when the player has a light job to do, company to listen to, and a reason for the trip. It is hated when the player only watches. Every game studied points this way. Kapman already has the right numbers. The risks are the minutes between ports and the size of the plan.

**How sure is this?** The web-search budget was used up, so the research read pages directly: Wikipedia, Steam stores and reviews, Valve's guides, a few design talks. Each review fetch gave only a handful of reviews, so the quotes show what people complain about. They are not statistics. Points marked "unchecked" were not re-checked. There is no hard data on how long people play this kind of game in one sitting, and small sailing games from 2024-26 are barely covered.

## 1. What works

1. **Slow travel works with a job, company and a purpose.** *Kapman: the real-speed voyage.* Euro Truck Simulator 2 (over 22 million sold) and Sailwind (about 89% positive) keep the hands busy: lane-keeping, or trimming and star sights. ETS2 players say "suddenly hours have passed".
2. **Your scale is already ETS2's.** *Kapman: the 1:40 map and 30-90 minute crossings.* ETS2 shrinks Europe 1:19, and a job runs about 20-60 minutes (unchecked). Build the ordinary voyage as "ETS2 at sea in 793": a cargo, a buyer, a date, small costs for ordinary trouble, and light and weather as the show.
3. **Written riddles give sailing a purpose.** *Kapman: hard riddles on the chart.* Sea of Thieves fixed its lack of purpose with riddle stories (Tall Tales). Return of the Obra Dinn checks answers only in groups of three, which stops guessing. Outer Wilds' log pins each clue to where you found it.
4. **Start small and short, then widen.** *Kapman: Dorestad and the Rhine.* Dredge (over 1 million sold, small team) polished its first area for six months, then added each new area in two to three months.
5. **Heirs work best when each life has an end.** *Kapman: heirs to 1066.* Sid Meier's Pirates! ages your captain, then ranks the life from "beggar" to "King's advisor". Crusader Kings III (over 4 million sold) runs on families.
6. **A few clear traits, shown loudly, make crew stories.** *Kapman: crew and the council.* CK3 adds stress when a character acts against his nature. Darkest Dungeon (2 million sold) shows breakdowns with big sound and art. Saltsea Chronicles makes you pick who goes ashore.
7. **Trade stays alive when news is old and unsure.** *Kapman: news by ship, the ledger.* In Pirates!, tavern rumours decide where you sail. In Taipan!, price news on arrival opens sudden chances. One ship and news by ship is Kapman's edge.
8. **Story in small pieces brings people back.** *Kapman: the crewman's reading.* Hades gives a bit of story after every run. 80 Days shows only about 2% of its writing per trip.
9. **Be real where the player decides, plain where it is bookkeeping.** *Kapman: real goods, prices and trade practice.* Pentiment kept period detail but added a glossary. Manor Lords put play ahead of exactness "in some cases" and sold 3 million. This matches your call that period labels in the UI were "obnoxious".
10. **Something on the horizon pulls the eye.** *Kapman: AI fleets and the chronicle.* Sea of Thieves marks world events with a sign in the sky you see from far off. Black Flag's horizon full of sails is what players remember (unchecked).

## 2. What does not work

1. **Watching a boat crawl.** This is the biggest risk, because you chose no steering. Sunless Sea: "minutes staring at a tiny boat crawling across a pitch-black screen". *Avoid it:* keep no-steering, but add optional jobs at sea and a steady drip of things to see. A first target to test: something to notice every 2-4 minutes, a small choice every 10-15, a real event every 30-45. In play-tests, log the minutes since the last choice.
2. **A dead world.** Sailwind's top complaint after bugs: "The world is dead... from one static NPC to another to deliver boxes". Skull and Bones (Metacritic about 60) felt lifeless and grindy. *Avoid it:* the people (AI fleets, port folk, crew, encounters, reputation) are the selling point. Build them before more ships, ports or polish.
3. **"Wreck = game over, but save any time."** In practice that means reload. Storms become reload chores, and a player who forgot to save loses an evening. *Avoid it:* storms you can read hours ahead, a way out, and damage short of a wreck (cargo thrown over, a lost mast, a man overboard). Then settle the save rule (question 1).
4. **Money worries and spreadsheet trade.** Sunless Sea: "you never have enough money". Sailwind: "bulk trading is nigh unprofitable". Port Royale 4: "unnecessarily convoluted". *Avoid it:* keep food and pay a small, felt cost, show the price drop before you sell, and make one ordinary leg pay enough to matter.
5. **The same thing twice.** Sunless Sea players kept "reading the same things multiple times". Boredom comes on the tenth crossing, not the first. *Avoid it:* never show the same encounter text twice in one life, and make every repeated activity change something lasting.
6. **Chores that are not the dream.** Wind Waker's wind change, with an animation you could not skip, was "a tedious nuisance" (IGN). Sailwind boats get "stuck to the dock" in a headwind, and "oars don't do anything". *Avoid it:* one key per order, acting at once; oars that really move the ship; auto-docking for the last 50 m.
7. **Too much at once.** Stonehearth ended badly after early access. Sailwind is still in early access five years after aiming for Christmas 2021. Valve: "Do not ask your customers to bet on the future of your game". Kapman has already weighed Unreal against Unity and HDRP against URP, and it is spread over four project folders. *Avoid it:* finish one region first, and lock the engine after the sail tank and look test.
8. **A slow first two hours.** Steam refunds anything played under 2 hours, and a new player may finish only one crossing in that time. *Avoid it:* script the first two hours: a short river leg taught by the steersman, a profitable sale, a crew hire, an encounter, a chart note, a weather change, a glimpse of danger.

## 3. How to make it more enjoyable

Ranked by value for effort, cheapest big wins first.

1. **Every voyage has a buyer and a date.** Add a deadline and a named person to the commissions you already have.
2. **Stop the clock in the market, ledger and stowage screens.** Dredge players love that "time only passes when you actually do stuff". It costs almost nothing.
3. **Drop to 1x on any event, then go back.** Builds on the 1-4x speed.
4. **Never repeat a text.** "Seen" flags and cooldowns on the encounters. This removes the top complaint about text games.
5. **Show how old every price is.** "Lundenwic wool, 9 days old" in the ledger, plus the price drop before you sell. Builds on news by ship.
6. **A ship's log after every voyage.** What sold, what went wrong, who did what. Sunless Sea players missed a "record of how you failed", and Sailwind players beg for "a JOURNAL? WE CAN WRITE ON?". Builds on the chart notes.
7. **Signs on the horizon.** Smoke from a raided village, a fleet's sails, beacon fires, birds over a shoal, a wreck's mast. Builds on the AI fleets and chronicle, with no timers.
8. **Optional jobs at sea.** Trim and reef in gusts, set the rowing pace, bail, sound the shallows, send the lookout, trail a fishing line. Put them in the isometric ship view so that mode earns its place.
9. **A small event pacer.** It weighs risk (cargo, raiding season, weather) and time since the last event, then picks an event for the place, season and date. "Fair winds" and "saga" presets, like RimWorld's storytellers, set how busy the sea is without touching the clock.
10. **Crew with 2-3 traits, one want and one fear.** Overruling a man's faith or nature makes him uneasy. At the peak he deserts, fights or swears a vow. Tie wants to ports ("home to Ribe for my sister's wedding") so they set routes.
11. **Storms that warn and wound.** Hours of warning, a way out, damage short of a wreck. Builds on the full storms.
12. **The reading in parts.** Beowulf has about 43 numbered sections: give one per milestone, read in the ship view while you sail. The player finishes it by choosing from words he has learned, not by typing. Audio only from public-domain recordings such as LibriVox (check each one).
13. **Merchant ranks and an end-of-life scorecard.** Named ranks change who will deal with you (Patrician). At death or 1066 the chronicle ranks the life, and the heir inherits things you can see: the boat's name, a debt, a feud, the chart notes.
14. **The hold as a puzzle.** Stowing the Gislinge's 0.9 t for trim and freeboard makes each departure start with a choice, as in Dredge. More work, high value.
15. **A hired skipper for routine runs (later).** Like ETS2's hired drivers, for hours 10-40 when routes repeat. No time warp: you just choose which voyages to live.

## 4. Questions for the designer

1. **Wrecks and saving.** Keep "wreck = game over, save any time"? Pass the family to the heir on a wreck? Or offer two modes, as Sunless Skies does (a line of captains, or reload)? The three research passes did not agree.
2. **First release.** One finished region (Dorestad, the Rhine, the Frisian coast, one crossing to Lundenwic), with other seas added later the way ETS2 adds countries?
3. **Lock the engine.** After the sail tank and look test, which project and which pipeline (HDRP or URP) is final?
4. **Hands at sea.** With no steering, which three or four jobs should the player do?
5. **Money pressure.** Should food, water and pay be a small felt cost or a real squeeze?
6. **Time in menus.** Does the clock stop in the market and ledger, or does each action cost a fixed time?
7. **Routine runs.** May the merchant later send a skipper on runs he does not want to sail himself?
8. **Who is it for first?** Sailing purists, or Crusader Kings and ETS2 players who want to unwind? The research points to the second, plus an optional navigation and storm assist.
