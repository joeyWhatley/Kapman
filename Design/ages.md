# The seven ages, 793 to 1066

4 October 2026. The facts, rulers, stories and sources are in `Assets/Kapman/Game/Data/story/ages_793.json`
(seven entries, each with `ports.rise`, `ports.fall` and `ports.lacking`). This page says how each age should
feel at the table and what the ports table needs. A draft for you to overwrite. `(?)` marks what I could not check.

## How each age should feel

| Age | In a line | What changes for the player |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The emperor's peace** 793-833 | A rich, ordered world. Learn the trade. | One penny, one toll law, honest measures. News is slow but true. The day stick's threats are weather, a famine year (793, 805) and the toll-man. Raids are rumours from far away. Dorestat is home and the biggest panel in the game. North of the Eider silver is weighed, not counted. |
| **2. The raiding years** 834-864 | Home burns. | The best word on any item is where the fleet is this summer. A river mouth can be shut for a season; winter camps (Thanet, Sheppey, Oissel) come and go as places that sell loot and captives cheap, at a cost to your name. From 850 the toll at Dorestat is paid to a Dane. Pennies get worse, then are called in (864). By 863 the house has to move. |
| **3. The great armies** 865-899 | The lords change under you. | A kingdom can be gone between two voyages, and a dead king's charter is worth nothing. Armies sit on the chart as moving markets. Two purses: counted pennies in the west, weighed silver in the Danish lands. Walled towns give a safe berth, a gate shut at night and a toll at it. The chart grows: Iceland, the far north. |
| **4. Walled towns and eastern silver** 900-954 | The best markets belong to yesterday's raiders. | Trade is lawful only in a town, before a reeve; every town has a mint. Silver is plentiful and the long road east (Birka, Ladoga, the Volga) pays best and costs a whole season. York and Dublin swing with each new king. Inland the Magyars ride: the river is safer than the road. |
| **5. The quiet years** 955-990 | Build. | Few raid cards. Profit is in margins, law and standing: change your silver at each recoinage, pay the bishop's toll, trade by charter. The winter choice matters most now (a ship, a factor, a share, a gift to a church). The eastern silver stops and Birka dies inside one heir's life. From 980 the day stick shows strange sails again. |
| **6. Tribute, then one king** 991-1041 | Two games in one age. | To 1016 the English coast is the dark edge: fleets as chase cards, towns burned when you arrive, high prices for whoever gets in. After 1016, the safest seas in the game: low tolls, the biggest ships (60 tons), fellowships to join (the merchants of Tiel, the Frisians' guild at Sigtuna). Nearly everyone on the quay is baptised. |
| **7. The last years** 1042-1066 | Everything points at one autumn. | Wool out, cloth back, Flanders in the middle. A war each summer shuts a strait; ships are called out by kings. Hedeby burns (1050). The last notches on the day stick: the star after Easter 1066, then Stamford Bridge and Hastings, arriving as news as old as the ship that brought it. The game ends on news, in a port panel. |

**Home through the ages.** Dorestat under the count (793) and under Rorik the Dane (850); gone by 863. The house
moves to Tiel or Deventer. Frisia is a Danish duchy for three years (882-885). The bishop is back in Utrecht about
920 (?). Tiel is at its height from 975 to 1020, is burned in 1006 and fades after 1050. In the 1040s the Frisian
counts strike their own pennies at Dokkum, Leeuwarden, Stavoren and Bolsward.

## Ports the table lacks, most needed first

1. **Tiel** on the Waal (about 850 to 1100): Dorestat's heir. Without it the house has nowhere true to move.
2. **Hamburg** (831) and **Bremen** (787): the gap between Emuthon and Ripa.
3. The later northern towns: **Sigtuna** (980), **Roskilde** (c. 980), **Lund** (c. 990), **Schleswig** (1050), Aarhus (948), Nidaros (997), Oslo (c. 1048).
4. The east: **Wolin** (c. 800 to 1043), **Novgorod** (c. 930), **Kiev** (882), Gdańsk (c. 980).
5. England: **Sandwich** (851), **Norwich**, **Lincoln**, Winchester, Thetford, **Bristol** (c. 1020), Exeter; Hastings and Pevensey for 1066.
6. The Irish Sea and the far north: Waterford (914), Limerick (922), Orkney, a landing in Iceland (874), Greenland (986), Vinland (c. 1000 to 1021).
7. Flanders and Francia: **Bruges** (a fort c. 880, a port by 1037), Saint-Omer, **Montreuil** (Quentovic's heir, c. 900), Boulogne (811), Saint-Valery (1066).
8. Frisia and the delta: **Stavoren** (991), Vlaardingen (1018), Dordrecht (1049), Groningen (1040), Leeuwarden, Bolsward, Middelburg, Maastricht.
9. The winter camps as places that open and close: Thanet (851), Sheppey (855), Oissel (856-861), Torksey (872).

## Dates in the table to look at

- **Truso** ends in 850, but its own note has Wulfstan sailing there, which was about 890. End it about 980 (?).
- **Quentovic**, **Hamwic**, **Sliesthorp**, **Hleiðr** have no end. Quentovic fades about 900 to 950; Hamwic's open wic is given up about 880 for the walled Hamtun; Sliesthorp is burned in 1050 and left after 1066; Hleiðr gives way to Roskilde about 990.
- **Lundenwic** moves inside the Roman wall in 886: the same port with a new name (Lundenburh) and a quay for a beach. **Eoforwic** is the Danes' Jórvík from 866. **Duiblinn** is a ship-fort from 841 and empty of Northmen from 902 to 917.
- **Lindisfarena**, **Herio** and **Dommoc** lose what made them ports: the monks leave in 875 and 836, the bishops end about 870.

## What else the ages ask of the tables

- Realms that do not exist in 793 need ids: England, Danish York, Dublin's Northmen, Normandy, Flanders, West Frisia, the eastern and western kingdoms after 843, Norway, Iceland, the Poles. In the ages file `realm` is filled only where a 793 id fits.
- `ship_classes_793.json` gives no years to its classes and has no later ships: the sailing cargo ship of 20 to 60 tons (Äskekärr c. 910, Hedeby 3 c. 1025) and the great longship (Skuldelev 2, 1042). The ages file lists them under `new`.
- The words to keep out of the early ages: tribute is `gafol`, not Danegeld; "Danish law" only after 878; "guild" only after about 1000.
